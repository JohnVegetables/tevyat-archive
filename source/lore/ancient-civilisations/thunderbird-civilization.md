## summary
### LITE
The Thunderbird Civilization was the ancient tribe of Tsurumi Island, a fog-bound island off Inazuma. The people worshipped a thunder bird, Kanna Kapatcir, and offered her blood sacrifices. A boy named Ruu, whose song caught her attention, was sacrificed to please her. She saw it as the murder of an innocent and a broken promise, destroyed the tribe, and cursed the island to repeat its last day until she heard his song again. Only the descendant family of Kama survives. In the World Quest series Through the Mists (2.2) the Traveler ended the loop.
### STANDARD +
The tribe is one of Inazuma's lost peoples and is modelled on Ainu culture; its place names and script follow Ainu words. Its story is told through the Thundering Fury artifact set, the Through the Mists quests and the Seirai Island thread that came before them. The tribe's own people are gone: what players meet on the island are looping illusions and the boy Ruu.

## established
### LITE
- The tribe lived on Tsurumi Island and worshipped the Thunderbird Kanna Kapatcir, using her feathers to make instruments (Maushiro) that carried sound through the island's fog.
- Ruu, child of the last priest Mata, befriended the bird with his singing, promised to sing again, and was then sacrificed by the tribe.
- Kapatcir destroyed the whole tribe in rage and cursed the island to relive that day.
- The Raiden Shogun later killed Kapatcir on Seirai Island. In The Sun-Wheel and Mt. Kanna, Ruu sang to her remains and the curse ended.
### STANDARD +
- Religion and society: the tribe held a strict hierarchy with priests and shamans at the top. Shamans offered blood to the Thunderbird for protection and forgiveness, and the people read her unpredictable behaviour as revelation. An amethyst-and-gold hourglass kept by a shaman counted down to her arrival, which was marked by a festival.
- Isolation: people were taught to stay inside the island. Those who left were believed lost in the "sea of darkness" beyond the fog and never returned. The tribe believed the Thunderbird raised the fog to protect them.
- Writing: the tribe used the Ishine Script, handed down through the priestly line, which is why Tsurumi's names sound strange to outsiders.
- Descendants: a family that left before the end survives as the Kama clan; the present Kama (a boatman who carries travellers to Tsurumi) is the only person alive who knows how to make a proper Maushiro.
- The Thundering Fury artifact lore is the main in-game record of the sacrifice. The goblet "Omen of Thunderstorm" is described as the cup that held the offered blood.
### LARGE +
- The sacrifice: Ruu offered himself because his friends had begun leaving the island and he hoped it would restore things; Mata also thought the Thunderbird favoured the boy. Ruu died at the Moshiri Ceremonial Site. When Kapatcir came back to meet him, she found his blood in a golden cup on the altar at Mt. Kanna.
- Aftermath: Kapatcir razed the mountain with lightning, killed the tribe and left a curse that would hold until she heard Ruu's song once more. The Survivor of Catastrophe lore adds that Ruu had kept a feather she dropped; Thunderbird's Mercy describes a flower that survived the ash.
- Her end: years later she was hunted down and slain as a monster. The wiki, citing the story teaser Promise of a People's Dream, attributes the killing to the Raiden Shogun (Ei) and call Kapatcir an obstacle to progress; her residue was sealed at Asase Shrine on Seirai Island and took form as the Thunder Manifestation (seen in Seirai Stormchasers).
- The loop: the curse made the island replay the tribe's last ceremony. Illusions of the villagers repeat it without awareness. Ruu alone can see and speak with them and with outsiders, and he has "died" in the ceremony many times.
### ULTRA +
- Why the Traveler could end it: feathers that fell from the Thunderbird were the original Maushiro, and a feather picked up on Amakumo Peak (the Peculiar Pinion) lets players trigger carvings on the island and see her memories. Her memory fragments show she first heard Ruu's song from her nest through rain and found it unlike any other sound on the island.
- Resolution: Ruu could not leave Tsurumi on his own, so the Traveler used the feather's power to take him to Seirai Island. Because Kapatcir was already dead, the Traveler and Paimon told him she had moved there. Ruu saw the remains of Amakumo Peak, understood the truth, did not blame them for the lie, and sang. A new feather appeared as a sign she had heard him; the curse lifted.
- After the curse: spectral villagers and a "Boatman" appear on the island to take the spirits to a golden land. The Traveler can help them find closure through hidden objectives such as Boatman's Task, and each spirit boards the boat once settled. These are exploration objectives; canon names several villagers (Abe, Ipe, Kina, Kito, Makiri, Una and others) without a full history.

## accounts
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Three views of the Thunderbird exist. The tribe saw a protector and oracle who needed blood. The artifact lore of the Thunder Summoner's Crown calls her a monster who cared nothing for human worship. Her own memories, seen through the feather, show a lonely creature who noticed one boy among the many she overlooked. The tribe believed the sacrifice was an offering that served the good of the people; Kapatcir read it as murder.
### LARGE +
Ruu's account in the loop is also partial. He knows only the ceremony and believes it must be completed to calm the Thunderbird, and does not remember why it goes wrong. The Thundering Fury lore stresses that he considered his sacrifice an offering, and Mata's role is told by wiki summaries more than direct dialogue. Kama's family tradition, told through Sumida's novel-writing commission, explains the curse mainly through what Kama learned from his ancestors.

## entry: Ishine Script
@keys: Ishine Script, Ishine
@keys2: Tsurumi, Thunderbird, Kama, Mata
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
Ishine Script is the writing system of the Thunderbird Civilization, handed down through the tribe's priestly line. Ruu studied it under Mata. Many of its words are modelled on Ainu, which is why Tsurumi's names (Mata, Kama, Ruu, Kapatcir) sound unlike the rest of Inazuma. Kama says his name means "a very big rock." Rocks with carved Ishine marks are used in puzzles around the island.

## entry: Maushiro
@keys: Maushiro, Peculiar Pinion
@keys2: Tsurumi, Thunderbird, Sumida, Kama
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
The Maushiro is a traditional wind instrument of the tribe, used to communicate through the island's fog; the original ones were made from the Thunderbird's feathers, and wooden imitations came later. Its name is based on an Ainu word for whistling. The Peculiar Pinion the Traveler finds on Amakumo Peak is one of the original feathers and can activate carvings on the island. Kama inherited the knowledge of making a Maushiro and is the only living person who can.

## entry: Ruu's sacrifice
@keys: Ruu's sacrifice, Omen of Thunderstorm, Thundering Fury
@keys2: Tsurumi, Thunderbird, Kapatcir
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
Ruu was the child of the last priest, Mata, and was shy about his singing, often going into the fog to sing alone. His song caught the Thunderbird's notice; he gave her the name Kanna Kapatcir, meaning something like "Great Eagle of the Storm," and promised to sing for her again. When island life worsened and his friends began leaving, he agreed to be a living sacrifice. The Omen of Thunderstorm goblet is said to have held his blood, offered on the altar at Mt. Kanna.
### LARGE +
Thundering Fury lore says Ruu believed he gave himself for the good of the tribe and the Thunderbird's blessing. Kapatcir, who returned and saw only a dead child and a broken promise, killed everyone. Details of Mata's own feelings come mostly from the wiki; the artifact text focuses on Ruu and the bird.

## entry: The loop of Tsurumi
@keys: Tsurumi loop, Thunderbird curse, time loop, cursed island
@keys2: Tsurumi, Ruu, Kapatcir
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
Kapatcir's curse made the island relive the last ceremony endlessly. Illusions of the villagers go through it unaware; Ruu is the only one who perceives change, including hilichurls and rifthounds that did not exist in his day. Adventurers hired by Sumida to obtain a Maushiro were drawn into the loop, and the item they brought back vanished because it came from the illusion. The Traveler learned the truth from Kama, destroyed the perches that anchor the ceremony, and finally took Ruu to Seirai Island to sing, which lifted the curse.
### LARGE +
The ley lines of the island seem to replay Kapatcir's will at the height of each loop; red lightning and a red sky appear in The Sun-Wheel and Mt. Kanna. Places on the island where no lightning falls are where Ruu hides. Canon does not explain the mechanism beyond Kama's remark that the ley lines reproduce the bird's will.

## entry: Kama of Tsurumi
@keys: Kama, Kama clan, Kama's ancestor
@keys2: Tsurumi, Thunderbird, Maushiro, Sumida
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
Kama is a quest-only NPC who ferries travellers to Tsurumi Island for the writer Sumida. He is descended from a villager, also called "Kama" in the loop, who left Tsurumi Island to follow the Thunderbird's light and lived a long, happy life; his only regret was not going back for Ruu. Kama says "Kama" is a clan name and that his ancestor was from the island. After the quests he travels with Sumida in Liyue and Mondstadt.
