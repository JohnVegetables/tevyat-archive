## summary
### LITE
King Deshret's civilization was the desert kingdom of ancient Sumeru, ruled by the god-king Deshret, Lord of Deserts, alongside two companion gods: Nabu Malikata, the Goddess of Flowers, and Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, the earlier Dendro Archon. After Nabu Malikata's death Deshret ruled the desert alone and pursued a utopia, Aaru, where no one would suffer age, hunger or loss. His research into forbidden knowledge destroyed his royal city and ravaged his people. He died sealing the disaster. Today's Eremites descend from his subjects, and Aaru Village and the ruins of the Great Red Sand preserve his legacy.
### STANDARD +
The kingdom's remains dominate southern Sumeru: the Mausoleum of King Deshret, Khaj-Nisut, Primal Constructs, Jinn lore and the unfinished afterlife Aaru. Because Aaru was reprogrammed after the burning of Irminsul (Archon Quest Song of the Welkin Moon, Act X "Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana", Version 6.6), his work now holds the world's stored memory in place of the old world tree.

## established
### LITE
- Deshret was Lord of Deserts and one of the Three Eternal Companion-Gods, with Nabu Malikata and Rukkhadevata. He died thousands of years ago, in the Archon War era.
- He wanted an eternal paradise for his people and turned to forbidden knowledge, which polluted the desert.
- He gave his life to stop the disaster, while Rukkhadevata nearly exhausted her own power helping (Nahida, Where the Boat of Consciousness Lies).
### STANDARD +
- **The three gods.** The three ruled together as oathbound friends. Legend gives each a domain: the banquet to Nabu Malikata, authority to Deshret, life to Rukkhadevata. The wiki says this period was remembered as peaceful and prosperous, and that Deshret and Nabu Malikata built the city of Ay-Khanoum, "City of the Moon Maiden" in the Jinn tongue.
- **The split.** Deshret's ambition was great enough to defy the Heavenly Principles. Rukkhadevata would not join the plans and left to build her own civilization in the rainforest. Nabu Malikata died, and Deshret grieved; he used the power of a Celestial Nail of the sands and the Jinn to raise the Eternal Oasis as her resting place, guarded by Ferigees.
- **His rule.** Alone, Deshret sought vassal-kings and priests with incorrupt hearts and founded a royal city, Akhtamun. He was called the master of the thousand and one Jinn and "king of warriors, horticulturists and sages", and was also known as the Mourning King for his grief and nostalgia.
- **Apep.** Before building his kingdom he struck a bargain with the Dendro Dragon Apep, who claimed the desert: she would leave his people in peace if she could consume him after his death and take his knowledge.
- **Gurabad.** The city of brass rose in the desert after Nabu Malikata's death and is tied, in different accounts, to Deshret's guidance. Liloupar engineered its destruction; Deshret, enraged, had her spirit split into seven parts and hidden by his seven sages.
- **Aaru and the fall.** After Gurabad he doubled down on a plan for Aaru, an artificial afterlife for desert dwellers, made possible by the knowledge from Celestia that Nabu Malikata had given him. Accounts of her death differ: one says the Heavenly Principles executed her for this transgression, another that she gave her life in a ritual so he could receive that knowledge. His forbidden research released the knowledge that became the Withering; Rukkhadevata aided him, temples of life-power were raised, and Deshret finally sacrificed himself. Apep ate his body as agreed and was corrupted.
### LARGE +
- **Culture.** Deshret Script is the kingdom's writing; obelisks at the Mausoleum, Opet Hall and Khaj-Nisut spell a word read as Akhtamon. His symbol is a sun and eye, seen on desert architecture. A mechanism at Khaj-Nisut let anyone who recited a prayer and sat on his throne enter a "golden slumber".
- **Primal Constructs.** The wiki says these were built to rebirth the paradise Deshret dreamed of; Faruzan spent a century in stasis in related ruins.
- **Seven Pillars.** The wiki lists Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf among Deshret's Seven Pillars; he imprisoned these three in Aaru, to be freed only by answering three questions about rise and fall, love and hate, and truth and falsehood. In the Version 6.6 Archon Quest (Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana) Nefer gave the intended answer, "time", and the three were freed.
- **Descendants.** Eremites descend from his subjects; some still worship him and want him back. The people and Guardians of Aaru Village, such as Candace, are called his metaphorical descendants.
### ULTRA +
- **Aaru's design.** The wiki describes it as a final land of dreams for desert dwellers, resembling the Akasha System in architecture and arguably its predecessor. Disembodied souls could enter it even while it was closed, as in the world quest series Golden Slumber.
- **Gurabad's line.** Parvezravan, his son Shiruyeh and the Brass Mask rebellion belong to Gurabad's story. According to the wiki, after Gurabad's fall and Deshret's death later 'kings of kings' rebuilt Gurabad to claim his legacy, and the Cities of the Seven Towers (among them Tulaytullah, Saleh and the old city of Ay-Khanoum) rose and fell in that era. See their entries.
- **Inspirations.** Wiki trivia notes that Deshret's lore echoes the Solomon and One Thousand and One Nights traditions (Jinn in bottles, riddles). Treat this as real-world inspiration, not in-world fact.

## accounts
### STANDARD
- **Nahida** presents the disaster as forbidden knowledge polluting the desert, repelled thanks to Deshret's self-sacrifice and Rukkhadevata's near-exhaustion.
- **Apep** holds that Deshret meant her corruption as a fail-safe and says she thought little of him, though other texts call her his friend.
- **Ormazd** credited Deshret for Gurabad's prosperity and honoured him with temples, while **Liloupar** says Deshret charged Gurabad to guard the Eternal Oasis; other accounts credit Liloupar and say Ferigees defied Deshret.
- **Eremites** remember him as a god-king to restore.

## open_questions
### STANDARD
- Where Deshret came from, and when exactly his kingdom rose and fell.
- Whether Nabu Malikata returned his love.
- How much of Gurabad was his doing.
- Whether he foresaw Apep's corruption.

## entry: Aaru
@keys: Aaru, far side of Eternity
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Aaru is the unfinished artificial afterlife Deshret designed for desert dwellers so they could escape the dangers of Teyvat and its gods. Long closed off and unfinished, it held three imprisoned Pillars. After Nahida burned the corrupted Irminsul with the Pyro Gnosis in the Archon Quest act Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana (Version 6.6), Aaru was reprogrammed to store the ley lines' information. The wiki adds that the Heavenly Principles lost direct control over fate as a result.

## entry: Akhtamun and Ay-Khanoum
@keys: Akhtamun, Akhtamon, Ay-Khanoum
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Akhtamun is the royal city Deshret founded in the desert and the place his forbidden research destroyed. Ay-Khanoum, "City of the Moon Maiden" in the Jinn language, is the older city he and Nabu Malikata built together; the wiki later records it as one of the Cities of the Seven Towers.

## entry: Deshret's Seven Pillars
@keys: Seven Pillars, Thoth, Sobek, Heryshaf
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Deshret's court of chief servants. Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf were locked in Aaru until the three questions were answered; Nefer's answer, "time", freed them in the Version 6.6 Archon Quest, and they agreed to unseal Aaru's Shut so Sumeru could modify it.

## entry: The Withering
@keys: Withering, Eleazar
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The wiki traces the Withering, and its human form Eleazar, to the forbidden knowledge Deshret unleashed. Rukkhadevata aided him with temples of life-power, but the threat persisted until his death temporarily ended it. See the forbidden knowledge entry for what that knowledge is.
