## summary
### LITE
The unified civilization was a single human culture that once covered all of Teyvat, in what Fontaine's histories call "the first era". It arose after the Heavenly Principles came from beyond the world, defeated the Seven Sovereigns (the dragon rulers of the old world) and, according to an Enkanomiya chronicle, created humanity, who made a covenant with them. Angels, divine envoys sent from Celestia, lived among mortals, guided its kingdoms and taught them. It ended about 6,000 years ago in the war of funerary flame, when the dragon king Nibelung returned and the Heavenly Principles cast down Celestial Nails that destroyed many of its cities. Its ruins lie under every modern nation.
### STANDARD +
Known parts of it include Arcadia and Sal Vindagnyr (Mondstadt), Lang-Gan (Liyue), Delphi Pytho, whose sunken remnant is Enkanomiya (Inazuma), Ys and Khab (Sumeru), Meropis (Fontaine), a civilization under the Lord of the Night (Natlan) and Hyperborea, the Golden City (Snezhnaya). Few people today know the era existed: the heavens seem to have suppressed its memory, and much of what survives comes from forbidden chronicles, artifact legends and the angels themselves.

## established
### LITE
- It was one world-wide culture: Eboshi of Enkanomiya says that before Byakuyakoku fell into the deep sea, "the whole world had a single unified culture."
- Angels governed and taught it. They were created by the Heavenly Principles and sent as envoys of Celestia.
- It ended in the war of funerary flame about 6,000 years ago.
### STANDARD
- **Origin.** Before Sun and Moon, the chronicle in The Byakuyakoku Collection, tells it this way. When the "eternal throne of the heavens" came, the Primordial One fought and defeated the seven dragon-lords of the old world and made four shining shades of itself. The seven nations "submitted to the heavens". Then the Primordial One and one of its shades made animals, plants and finally humans. Humanity made a covenant with the Primordial One and "entered into a new age".
- **A guided paradise.** The same chronicle describes years of plenty. If people were hungry, the heavens sent food and rain; if they were sad, the heavens answered. The one taboo was to give in to temptation. Istoria, a philosopher of that age, remembers it as a "mortal paradise" in which the angels answered humanity's practical problems.
- **Angels.** Mortals could not bear to look directly at them and called them angels. They had pale silver wings and crowns of "sevenfold radiance". The crowns carried a command to love all beings of the earth equally, or perhaps a mandate to rule over "the nations of the earth" (Deep Gallery's Lost Crown). Divine envoys "spoke openly with the people then, bringing them the word from the heavens" (Amethyst Crown). Each region had its own envoy; Zibai, for example, watched over Lang-Gan.
- **Its end.** Eboshi says a great war broke out "in which the heavens capsized and the earth was ripped asunder". Byakuyakoku's chronicle calls it the coming of the "second throne of the heavens" in the Funerary Year.
### LARGE +
- **What the angels taught.** Nicole's elders recall that the angels kindled fire in their palms and taught mortals to use it against the night, for cooking and against beasts. They twisted hemp into rope for climbing, clothing, shoes and river craft. They also taught "thoughts profound and ideals pure": steadiness of heart, endurance in hardship, and "dreams that belonged to mankind alone". Zibai taught Lang-Gan to till and weave, and its people worshipped her as the God of Soil and Grain.
- **Worship.** Besides the "heavenly god" (the Heavenly Principles) and the Four Shades, peoples of this age worshipped the Three Moons. Artifact text says believers loved the moons' gentle light more than the "wrathful and merciless Formidable Father". Pilgrims climbed Vindagnyr with laurel-crowned verses, carried carved jade to Lang-Gan's altars and took golden boughs from Arcadia's sacred oak groves. Some envoys, such as Zibai, also served as envoys of the moons; the wiki calls them Moon Envoys.
- **Rules.** The heavens set limits on knowledge. "To probe the heavens was forbidden, for every quest beyond the appointed rules led only to destruction" (Moonlit Offering's Libation). The eldest angel, Koitar, gave the law as: the laws shall not be tested, the strictures not questioned, the scriptures not infringed, and every transgression will be punished.
- **Writing.** The old script found on ruins, ley-line seals and divine sites across Teyvat (fans call it "Abyss language"; the wiki calls it the Ancient Teyvat Script) is mostly found in inscriptions of this civilization.
- **Ruins.** Ruins in a shared architectural style survive in every nation. This shared style is so noticeable that even a Snezhnayan newspaper contributor builds a theory on it.

## regions
### LARGE
Known parts of the civilization, by present-day territory. Canon does not say whether they formed one realm or many kingdoms under one faith; the sources speak of "nations of the earth" and city-states with their own envoys.
- **Mondstadt:** Arcadia, which worshipped Istaroth and Asmoday and also made offerings to the Three Moons, and Sal Vindagnyr on the mountain now called Dragonspine.
- **Liyue:** Lang-Gan, a realm of Sand Crystals and gemstones under the angel Zibai, where the Chasm is now.
- **Inazuma:** Delphi Pytho, part of which sank and became Enkanomiya (Byakuyakoku). There were also ruins of a moon-worshipping civilization on Tsurumi Island.
- **Sumeru:** Ys, a rainforest harbour now under the Great Red Sand, and Khab, a tower civilization beneath Chatrakam Cave.
- **Fontaine:** Meropis, at what is now the Sea of Bygone Eras, guarded by the angel Sybilla.
- **Natlan:** an unnamed civilization guided by Yohualtecuhtin, the Lord of the Night.
- **Snezhnaya:** Hyperborea, the Golden City, under Koitar, the first angel.

More detail:
- **Sal Vindagnyr's** founders fled a land of snow and strife and settled on a green mountain. A Celestial Nail, the Skyfrost Nail, later fell there, and the mountain became today's frozen Dragonspine.
- **Arcadia** included the city-state of Elysium at what is now Windrest Peak.
- **Lang-Gan** was tainted by Abyssal malice from the war, and Celestia sent a Nail to purge it. Zibai defied heaven to hold the Nail back long enough for survivors to flee. Its ruins are the Inverted City under the Chasm, and the survivors settled mostly in Chenyu Vale.
- **Delphi Pytho:** in the funerary war its people and land fell into the depths, where they warred with the Dragonheirs of the Depths and lived in darkness until the sage Abrax built the artificial sun.
- **Tsurumi Island:** underground ruins there have murals of Celestia and the Three Moons. A Nail is said to have struck the island, after which the fog began.
- **Khab's** tower and others like it were "man-made towers that could touch the skies", built all across the land; they collapsed under their own weight into the depths (Steeple of Ignorance).
- **Natlan:** the civilization there kept ritual sites where sages studied flame and the transformation of Phlogiston.

## fall
### LARGE
The ending, as canon pieces it together:
1. **Transgression.** Over time, some peoples took their knowledge past the limits the heavens set. Meropis's descendants stopped heeding the oracles. In Hyperborea, a youth possessed by the star-voyager Anacharsis questioned why seeking knowledge was a sin. Koitar fell in love with him, broke her crown, and the two led Hyperborea in rebellion against the Heavenly Principles.
2. **War.** Nibelung, the dragon king of the old world, returned in the Funerary Year and fought the Heavenly Principles, bringing the corruption of the Abyss. The Goddess of Flowers remembered "invaders" from beyond the firmament who overturned rivers and spread plagues, and who also brought "illusions that could break through shackles".
3. **Nails.** Afraid of the "rising tide of delusion and breakthroughs", the master of the heavens sent down divine nails to "mend the land", laying waste to the mortal realm. Hyperborea, Lang-Gan, Sal Vindagnyr and Ys are among the places struck.
4. **Exile of the angels.** Divine envoys lost their connection to heaven. The angels were cursed; many lost their minds and forms and became Seelie.
5. **Aftermath.** The heavens appear to have tried to erase the era from memory. Eboshi says the heavenly order "did not wish for those who remembered all this to remain on the earth", so Byakuyakoku was denied any road home until "heaven's powers of prohibition" weakened much later. A new order followed under gods rather than angels, leading to the Archon War.
### ULTRA +
Survivors scattered. Frost Moon worshippers from Hyperborea fled to what is now Nod-Krai and became the Frostmoon Scions. Lang-Gan's people went east toward Chenyu Vale. Ys's survivors probably became the desert's nomadic tribes. Fontaine's survivors lived "shorn of all knowledge and wisdom" among forests and rivers until Remus founded Remuria on Meropis's ruins. Natlan's angels were almost all devoured by dragons after the war, and its last envoy, the Lord of the Night, rebuilt a ley-line network as the Night Kingdom. Asmoday's watchers carried some people of the age, among them the philosopher Istoria, to the Temple of Space as living memory.

## angels_parables
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### ULTRA
Nicole's character stories (6.6) record three cautionary tales the elder angels told her about cities that overstepped:
- A city that bound fire to its rule so that its light never dimmed, then turned that sun-bright fire on its own people and reduced countless lives to bone and ash. The heavens put out the flame and later raised new life on the dead ground.
- A city that wove everything from rope and tried to climb to "the most sacred heavens" by golden cords, binding them into an upside-down tower to reach a fruit it imagined there. The heavens cut the cords and sealed the trespassers inside their own canvases, bound by the cords forever.
- A city that pushed knowledge to its limit, cast off its bodies as burdens and sank into the lightless sea to explore further. Before it could look on the dark gulf beyond the world, the heavens boiled the sea away, purged the intellect that drove it and returned its people to their first shape.
These three match the gifts the angels taught (fire, rope, profound thought), turned into overreach. Canon does not name these cities or date their punishments.

## accounts
### LARGE
- **Enkanomiya's chronicle** (Before Sun and Moon) is written by descendants of the civilization who fell into the depths. It is reverent toward the Primordial One but records being abandoned and kept from home. The book is "forbidden" to ordinary readers.
- **Fontaine's history** (Pulteney, The History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria) describes a heaven-blessed "first era" ending in floods sent against people who rejected their fate.
- **Angels** (Zibai, Nicole, the Goddess of Flowers via Amethyst Crown) remember it from the inside, with grief and warnings.
- **The Byakuyakoku chronicle's own guesses:** after the war, its writers assumed the Primordial One "must have defeated the Second Who Came", because the ban on returning home stayed in place.
- **The Goddess of Flowers** (Nabu Malikata) warned King Deshret never to seek the Master of the Four Shades or ask about the mysteries of sky and abyss; he chose to ignore her.
- **Istoria,** an Uplifted philosopher at the Temple of Space (6.5), says the priests and angels gave his people the answers to practical problems, and that the songs of the angels could cure melancholy.
### ULTRA +
- **Snezhnaya Vestnik:** a contributor's "Theory of Historical Echoes" (7.x) argues that the shared ruin style everywhere proves the "unified civilization" was Emperor Monomakh's tsardom, and that other histories are rewrites of his conquest. This is in-universe pseudo-history and should be treated as such.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- How long the civilization lasted, and whether it was one state or many kingdoms under one faith.
- Exactly what the heavens forbade, and why some cities were destroyed before or apart from the war.
- Patraipolis is named by an Uplifted from King Deshret's era; where it stood, and whether it belonged to this civilization, is unknown.
- Whether all angels and envoys were the same kind of being, and how many regions had one.
- How far the heavens' erasure of the era reached, and what was lost.

## connections
### STANDARD
Successor to the old world of the Seven Sovereigns; ended by the war of funerary flame; followed by the age of gods and the Archon War. Closely tied to the angels, the Four Shades, the Three Moons, Irminsul trees (angels were linked to them; Sybilla and Koitar each lived by one) and the Celestial Nails.
### LARGE +
Modern echoes: the Seelie are cursed angels; Enkanomiya and Watatsumi descend from Delphi Pytho; Chenyu Vale's people descend from Lang-Gan; the Frostmoon Scions descend from Hyperborea; the Snowland Fae were first made in Hyperborea; the Spiral Abyss is said to have been an underground Hyperborean city-state.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): ordinary people in present-day Teyvat do not know the unified civilization existed. They see old ruins, strange script and monsters, not a lost world culture. Scholars, Uplifted, angels such as Nicole and Zibai, and people of Enkanomiya know more. Treat the era as hidden, fragmentary and dangerous to dig into.
### LARGE +
Do not give the era a single capital, ruler, name or calendar; canon gives none. Do not present it as a technological empire everywhere; Hyperborea's advanced devices came after Koitar and Anacharsis rebelled. Keep the tone of a lost, guided paradise with strict, deadly limits.

## entry: Meropis
@keys: Meropis
@version: 6.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Meropis was the unified-civilization city at what is now Fontaine's Sea of Bygone Eras. From its harbour, a great silver ship carried souls to the moons' golden halls, and its people offered songs and dances to the Three Moons. The angel Sybilla guarded its Irminsul tree. When its descendants rejected the oracles and tried to escape their fate, heaven sent giant waves and a hundred days of rain. No city was left above the waters, and the survivors lost their knowledge. Ages later, Remus found the ruins, met Sybilla (by then reduced to a golden bee) and built Remuria there.

### STANDARD
Ys was a harbour city in the rainforest that once covered what is now Sumeru's Great Red Sand. After the war of funerary flame, a Celestial Nail fell, the forest became desert, and the city sank beneath the sands. Its survivors probably became the desert's nomad tribes.

Weapon text from a limited-time reward (Silver Radiance of Gradlon, 7.0) says a divine envoy of the high heavens gave the city's high priest that claymore, granting the right to lead the harbour.

## entry: Patraipolis
@keys: Patraipolis
@version: 6.5
### LARGE
Patraipolis is an ancient city named only once, by the Uplifted architect Vitruvius at the Temple of Space in Unofficial Chronicle of the Desert Pavilion: his chosen epitaph begins "Please, tell my love in Patraipolis that..." before he turns to sand mid-sentence. Vitruvius himself lived in King Deshret's era, after the war of funerary flame, so canon does not show that Patraipolis belonged to the unified civilization; the wiki merely lists it alongside Arcadia, Lang-Gan and Delphi Pytho. Nothing else is known about it.

## entry: Natlan before the Funerary War
@keys: Lord of the Night, Yohualtecuhtin
@keys2: unified civilization, angel, envoy, Funerary
@version: 5.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
A civilization in Natlan was guided by an angel now known as Yohualtecuhtin, the Lord of the Night. Its sages studied the mysteries of flame and Phlogiston at ritual sites. When Nibelung returned, the Pyro dragon Xiuhcoatl awoke and destroyed it, and dragons devoured most of its angels. Because the land was corrupted by the Abyss, a Celestial Nail fell there; the Basin of Unnumbered Flames is believed to be its crater. The surviving envoy rebuilt a ley-line network from fragments, creating the Night Kingdom.

## entry: Forbidden chronicle of Byakuyakoku
@keys: Before Sun and Moon, Byakuyakoku Collection
@version: 2.4
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Before Sun and Moon, Vol. 2 of The Byakuyakoku Collection, is a chronicle that ordinary folk were forbidden to read. It is the fullest native account of the unified civilization. It covers the Primordial One's victory over the dragons, the creation of humans and their covenant, the "Year of Jubilee" when the heavens met every need, the "Funerary Year" when the second throne came, the fall into darkness and the building of the artificial sun. It also records Istaroth's secret name, written backwards, because it was taboo to speak it.
