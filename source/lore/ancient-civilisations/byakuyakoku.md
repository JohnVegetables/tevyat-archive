## summary
### LITE
Byakuyakoku, also called Tokoyokoku and originally Delphi Pytho, was an ancient human civilisation that belonged to the single world-spanning culture of the first era. In the war of funerary flame its land fell into the sea and was cut off from the surface. Its people, surrounded by darkness and by the vishaps called Dragonheirs of the Depths, built an artificial sun, the Dainichi Mikoshi, which gave them the cycle of Whitenight and Evernight. Later the fallen god Orobashi became their patron and led them back to the surface as the Watatsumi people. The place is now called Enkanomiya.
### STANDARD +
Its memory survives in a forbidden chronicle, Before Sun and Moon, in the five-volume Byakuyakoku Collection, and in the ruins beneath Watatsumi Island. Players meet it in the 2.4 Enkanomiya content.

## established
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
- Origin: before its fall, the people of Delphi Pytho were part of the unified civilisation that spanned the world. The chronicle describes the Heavenly Principles defeating the Seven Sovereigns and the creation of humanity under a covenant, and calls the supreme being "the Primordial One", with four shining Shades.
- Fall: in the Funerary Year the second throne of the heavens came, war was rekindled, and the chroniclers' ancestors and their land "fell into this place". The chronicle calls the second throne's coming only that; the wiki identifies it with the war of funerary flame, when Nibelung returned.
- Years of Darkness: in the depths they met the Dragonheirs of the Depths, who loved the dark and shunned light. Lanterns kept them back only partly, and the prayers of the people went unanswered by the Primordial One and three of its Shades.
- Istaroth: only the Ruler of Time, whom the chroniclers call Kairos or Tokoyo Ookami (and write backwards as "Htoratsi"), answered. In the Year of Blindness the sage Abrax (Aberaku in Narukami-style naming) woke to wisdom and led the building of Helios, the divine chariot of the sun, later the Dainichi Mikoshi.
- Years of Sun and Moon: when Helios was finished the Whitenight came and Evernight was banished. The people tried to find the way home but a ban from the first throne barred them. Abrax was imprisoned by order of the Sunchild, whom the people had chosen as king.
- Names: book 3 notes that its own title changed over the years from "Aphotic Earth" to "Tokoyokoku" and then "Byakuyakoku" (byakuya means white night); the wiki reads this as the land's successive names, and volume 1 does use Tokoyokoku for the land. After Watatsumi came, the land was renamed Enkanomiya and the surface home Sangonomiya.
### LARGE +
- Geography: it had no mountains. It lay on the border of three realms, which the wiki gives as human, light and void, and both wind and water powers were still felt there. Three corners mark its borders, and Towers of the Three Realms stood there to steady its winds and waters, hidden by secret arts that only shrine maidens and the Vassals of Watatsumi can summon. Their sites are now the three Loci.
- The Serpent's Heart was built where space overlaps; it was used to guard secrets, imprison criminals and worship the imagined cosmic serpent Ouroboros. Its early name was Delphi, "land of snakes". Ouroboros was drawn as a scaleless serpent and Orobashi later as a coral-adorned one.
- Dainichi Mikoshi: Helios was a high tower, said in book 3 to stand for earth, the element between wind and water. The book says it was likely the sun the sage displayed, as prophecy suggested, and that, as the prophecies also suggested, it was later used to cast a great shadow over the nation.
- Livestock: the riddles say domestic animals (aux, hors, forest boar) died out within two generations once the land sank, so later generations knew them only from texts.
### ULTRA +
- The parables in the chronicle: the Parable of the Sun (people who mistake a torch for the sun, after which someone monopolises the flame), the Lethied Lotus (voyagers tempted to stay and forget the surface) and the Tree (the God of Moments can carry seeds of a moment into past and future). Read these as the chroniclers' commentary on their own history, not as separate events.
- The chronicle ends in the Tenth Year of Sun and Moon with the scribe hearing armour outside and stopping, presenting himself as a scribe of Tokoyo Ookami.
- The wiki, summarising quest text, adds that corrupt nobles had the sun worshipped as a god and that Aberaku died in the Tenth Year, his body and clothes enshrined in several places, which left his soul scattered until the Hyperion's Dirge quest.
- Vol. 5 (a children's riddle book from the transitional period) shows Narukami names written with Byakuyakoku readings, and records that Orobashi forbade one riddle about the "mother of fourteen billion years", Tokoyo Ookami.

## accounts
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
- The Byakuyakoku chronicle (Before Sun and Moon) is the voice of the old civilisation. It is pious toward the Primordial One and bitter about being abandoned. The wiki says Celestia wanted this history concealed, and Orobashi later banned the book as well.
- Watatsumi tradition (Sangonomiya Chronicles) tells the same history from the viewpoint of the people saved by Orobashi: a child asks a great serpent to be their god, the Sunchild's rule falls, and coral from the god's body makes the road to the surface.
- Present-day Watatsumi practice (the Goryou Matsuri quests) holds that every few thousand years Enkanomiya bleaches Watatsumi's soil and risks releasing the Dragonheirs, and the Goryou Matsuri ritual, using coral from the depths, is how the island guards against this. At the end of the limited-time 2.4 event Three Realms Gateway Offering, Kokomi resolves to find another method so the vishaps are no longer disturbed.
- Enjou of the Abyss Order claimed the book Before Sun and Moon would prove the gods came from outside Teyvat.
### LARGE +
- Experimental records in volume 4 (written under Orobashi's direction) are the official Watatsumi view of the Bathysmal Vishaps: not killed, studied, grafted with Omikami's blood, with a prophecy that the Dragon Sovereign of Water will be born in human form.
- A person from Byakuyakoku would speak of the Primordial One and the Four Shades; a modern Watatsumi person would speak of Orobashi and Sangonomiya; neither side uses Celestia's wording.

## open_questions
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
- The surviving text does not say how many generations lived under the Sunchildren, nor how long passed between the fall and Orobashi's arrival.
- It is not stated what the "Second Who Came" is; the chroniclers only guess it was defeated.
- It is unknown why only Istaroth heard the people, or what she wanted from helping them.
- Whether the vishap-people exist is left open.
### LARGE +
- Many appendix names are illegible; do not invent the missing ones.
- Treat links to hilichurls, Mondstadt's "thousand winds" and Hyperborea as the wiki's inferences, not as established facts about Byakuyakoku.

## connections
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
- Parent culture: the unified civilisation (Delphi Pytho is listed there as a member).
- Setting that survives it: Enkanomiya, the Dainichi Mikoshi, Watatsumi Island.
- Patron: Istaroth (as Tokoyo Ookami), then Orobashi (Watatsumi Omikami).
- Founding sage: Aberaku. Other figures: Antei (Antigonus in the old naming) and Spartacus (Supada). The Yuna Clan of Watatsumi, later home of the shrine maiden Mouun, is among the old houses that came up from Byakuyakoku.
- Opponent: the Bathysmal Vishaps; modern Abyss interest: Enjou.
### LARGE +
- The Watatsumi ritual that guards the island against what lies below, the Goryou Matsuri, is the modern echo of the ancient vishap problem.
- On the last day before the people moved to the surface, Khaenri'ahn envoys and a Ruin Guard came for the book Before Sun and Moon and Antigonus fought them off, per the wiki's summary of the Antigonus quest.

## entry: Sunchild and the Sunchildren
@keys: Sunchild, Sunchildren, children of the sun
@keys2: Byakuyakoku, Enkanomiya
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
The Sunchild was the king of Byakuyakoku. The book says the people chose him, crowned him and worshipped him, and that he ruled harshly and framed the righteous; the wiki, summarising quest text, says he was a child puppet of corrupt nobles who played on his fear of Aberaku. The line of Sunchildren ended when Orobashi came.

## entry: Helios, Hyperion and the Dainichi Mikoshi
@keys: Helios, Hyperion, Byakuya no Hikari, divine chariot of the sun
@keys2: Byakuyakoku, Enkanomiya, Aberaku
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
The artificial sun of Byakuyakoku, built under Aberaku with knowledge from Istaroth. Its light drove back the Dragonheirs and brought Whitenight; its absence is Evernight. The wiki says Orobashi later strengthened it so day and night could be switched. Its names changed with the culture: Helios and Hyperion in the old tongue, Dainichi Mikoshi in the Watatsumi era.

## entry: Dragonheirs of the Depths
@keys: Dragonheir, Dragonheirs, Dragonheirs of the Depths
@keys2: Byakuyakoku, Enkanomiya, Orobashi
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
The old name for the Bathysmal Vishaps that ruled the depths when the land fell. They loved the dark, were driven back by Helios, and were later sealed and studied under Orobashi. Book 5 notes that they had evolved to wield other elements, which is why the Dragon of Water was not expected to arise from them.

## entry: Whitenight and Evernight
@keys: Whitenight, Evernight, Sinshades
@keys2: Byakuyakoku, Enkanomiya, Dainichi Mikoshi
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
Whitenight is the lit state of the land, Evernight the dark state. Book 5 says the land's two odd phenomena, the mirages and the Sinshades, were at first both called Eidolons; only after Orobashi came were they told apart, and the mirages of Whitenight were named Sunfire Phantasms and those of Evernight Ghostfire Phantasms (later all called Sunfire Phantasms). In the 2.4 content, the spectral Sinshades (Shades of Tokoyo) appear during Evernight.

## entry: Tokoyo Ookami in Byakuyakoku
@keys: Tokoyo Ookami, Kairos, Htoratsi, Ruler of Time
@keys2: Byakuyakoku, Enkanomiya, Istaroth
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
In Byakuyakoku, Istaroth was worshipped as Tokoyo Ookami, "the thousand winds", Kairos and God of Moments. Her power was named the undying wind and treated as one of the land's two great forces, the other being water. The chroniclers dared write her true name only once, reversed. Her wider story is in her own entry.
