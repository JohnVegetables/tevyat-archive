## summary
### LITE
The "sibling thread" is the run of five Archon Quest acts that follow the Traveler and Dainsleif as they chase the Traveler's lost twin, the leader of the Abyss Order. They are We Will Be Reunited (1.4, Chapter I Act IV), Requiem of the Echoing Depths (2.6, Chapter II Act IV), Caribert (3.5, Chapter III Act VI), Bedtime Story (4.7, Chapter IV Act VI) and A Space and Time for You (5.7, Chapter V Act VI). Each brings the Traveler closer to the sibling (in person, in a vision, or through borrowed or shared memory) and gives a clearer picture of the Order's aim: to bring Khaenri'ah back.
### STANDARD +
Across the five acts the Order's project grows from a machine-god plot (1.4) to a curse-cleansing device (2.6), to the Loom of Fate (3.5 and 4.7), to a woven "Atlas of the New World" holding the Ley Lines of a new Khaenri'ah (5.7). The sibling first reappears in person in 1.4, speaks with the Traveler as an intangible presence in 4.7 (a conversation the Traveler will not remember), and finally explains their own choices in 5.7. Dainsleif, the immortal former captain of Khaenri'ah's Royal Guard, appears in all five acts and supplies much of the exposition. The acts are quest-level material: the individual quests and the sibling's long-range goals are covered in their own entries.

## established
### LITE
- The sibling leads the Abyss Order and says they will not leave Teyvat with the Traveler until their war with destiny is over (1.4).
- The Order wants to revive Khaenri'ah, a nation that was without gods and was destroyed five hundred years ago.
- Dainsleif is a pure-blooded Khaenri'ahn, once the Twilight Sword (captain of the Royal Guard), cursed with immortality.
- The Defiled Statue and Field Tiller plot, the Chasm device and the Loom of Fate are successive Order undertakings serving that same aim.
### STANDARD +
- In 3.5 the Traveler learns that Chlothar Alberich, an ancestor of Kaeya's family, founded the Abyss Order, and that Chlothar once addressed the sibling as "Prince" or "Princess" of Khaenri'ah, telling them that to his people they had been "the Abyss" itself.
- In 4.7 the sibling says they are racing to unlock the Loom of Fate's full potential before the Heavenly Principles awaken, and that they miss the Traveler.
- In 5.7 the sibling says they made the Atlas of the New World, that the attack on Natlan was a feint, and that guilt over leaving Khaenri'ah behind is part of their motive.
### LARGE +
- Khaenri'ahns who were considered pure-blooded were given the curse of immortality; the other humans taken in by the nation were given the curse of the wilderness and became hilichurls (Eide/Chlothar's account in 3.5).
- The Five Sinners of Khaenri'ah (Vedrfolnir, Rhinedottir, Surtalogi, Rerir, Hroptatyr) did not defend their nation at the Cataclysm; Dainsleif names Vedrfolnir, "the Visionary", as his older brother (4.7).
- The Order reached Natlan in force in 5.7, attacking all tribes at once and fortifying its base at Ochkanatlan.

## accounts
### STANDARD
- **The sibling**: frames their conduct as a war with destiny. They do not call it revenge, and in 5.7 say that the plan has "only ever been about" the two twins.
- **Dainsleif**: treats the Order as an enemy of the surviving Khaenri'ahns, believes the curse cannot be undone and sets out to make Order campaigns as costly as possible.
- **The Traveler**: judges the sibling's actions by their cost to others, as when they argue the sibling has no right to subject hilichurls to near-certain suffering for a tiny chance of redemption (2.6).
### LARGE +
- **Chlothar Alberich** (as "Eide", 3.5): believed that the Abyss would give Khaenri'ah control over everything and that the gods' "gift" ruined his nation. He told the sibling that Khaenri'ah had seen them as the Abyss itself and had looked to them to lead it into that future.
- **Caribert** (4.7): says the Loom began as a means of creating and implanting memories and, once fully unlocked, would create Ley Lines instead. He used the first function to make himself a life as a villager.
- **Haden** (5.7): first claims the Order has already revived Khaenri'ah, then admits he was bluffing in order to get hold of Dainsleif's memories.
- **Citlali** (5.7): describes the Lord of the Night's reasoning as curiosity about preserving a whole nation as an "Atlas", and says there was also a further condition she was never told.
### ULTRA +
- **Halfdan** (2.6) and the Black Serpent Knights are shown still carrying out Dainsleif's old order to protect the people of Khaenri'ah, even after the curse eroded their minds, so the Order and the surviving Khaenri'ahns are not the same group.

## chronology
### LARGE
Release order is also the order in which the Traveler encounters the story: 1.4, 2.6, 3.5, 4.7, 5.7. The events described in the acts are not in that order. In story order the twins' ship reaches Teyvat, the sibling is made heir in Khaenri'ah, the Cataclysm strikes and Asmoday seals the twins (all before the Prologue); the sibling then wakes, travels Sumeru with Dainsleif and meets Chlothar (the Caribert flashback, 3.5); the Order's Dvalin plot, the 1.4 reunion, the Chasm device and the Loom of Fate follow; the 5.7 act ends with the Atlas completed.
### ULTRA +
Key dated points (in-game versions): 1.0, the Prologue opens with the twins being separated; 1.4, We Will Be Reunited; 2.6, Requiem of the Echoing Depths; 3.5, Caribert; 4.7, Bedtime Story; 5.7, A Space and Time for You. The thread then continues indirectly: in 6.5 Melanta asks why Asmoday abandoned her for the sibling, and in 7.1 Hroptatyr's parable and the Tsaritsa's account of a hooded figure beside Asmoday add to the sibling's background.
Dainsleif mentions in the Caribert act that his previous journey with the sibling happened in Sumeru, and the Traveler's dream of the stars (the twins' habit of pointing out planets they wished to visit) is the act's only look at their shared past. The Traveler states that Teyvat was the first time the twins had been parted.

## open_questions
### STANDARD
- What is the "true nature of this world" the sibling says they have seen?
- What does the sibling intend once the Atlas has a host location?
- What is the "final answer" the sibling says in 4.7 they are still seeking, and why do they say they do not know how to face even themselves, let alone their twin?
### LARGE +
- The sibling concludes in 5.7 that Istaroth must have arranged Direidyth's meeting with them across five hundred years; what Istaroth intended is never stated.
- The sibling hints in 4.7 that finding the Sea of Flowers at the End was no coincidence; it is not explained.
### ULTRA +
- What, if anything, did the Traveler lose or gain by losing the 4.7 conversation?

## connections
### STANDARD
This thread is the Traveler-side view of the Abyss Order's work. Its goals are summarised in the unresolved-mystery entry on the sibling's goals; the Cataclysm, the curse of the Khaenri'ahns, the Savior of Khaenri'ah and Asmoday's defection are separate entries. The acts also seed the Five Sinners (3.5, 4.7), the Heavenly Principles' dormancy (4.7) and the Traveler's place in Natlan's war with the Abyss (5.7).
### LARGE +
The thread links to the Prologue, where Asmoday seals the twins, to the Dvalin crisis in Mondstadt that the Order engineered, and, via Pierro's and Hroptatyr's scenes in 7.1, to Celestia.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay guidance (this project's recommendation): the Traveler and Paimon know the acts as lived experience, but only in the order they happened. After 4.7 the Traveler does not remember the sibling's words, though they carry a photo of the twins with Caribert; after 5.7 they have heard the sibling's reasons directly. Most Teyvat residents know nothing about the Order's leader. Dainsleif knows the Khaenri'ah-side background but not everything the sibling plans.
### LARGE +
Do not present the sibling as a simple villain or as someone who hates the Traveler. In every encounter they are affectionate and regretful toward their twin while refusing to stop. Keep the Traveler's responses short and plain.

## entry: We Will Be Reunited (1.4)
@keys: We Will Be Reunited, A Soul Set Apart, Involuntary Sacrifice, A Herald Without Adherents, Dishonorable Trial
@version: 1.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Chapter I Act IV. After travelling briefly with Dainsleif, the Traveler takes an Adventurers' Guild commission in Liyue about Treasure Hoarders raiding Abyss Order ruins. Inside they find a Defiled Statue of Barbatos hanging upside down and are ambushed by an Abyss Herald. Dainsleif explains that Khaenri'ah never had gods of its own, that its Field Tillers (now called Ruin Guards) were mortal weapons, and that the Order means to attach Osial's limbs to the statue and set the eye of the first Field Tiller in its hand to make a mechanised god. The trail leads through Mondstadt (the Favonius Cathedral, Wolvendom, where the Herald is again found corrupting Boreas) to the Field Tiller in Stormterror's Lair, whose eye Dainsleif removes. Back at the ruins the sibling suddenly appears and stops Dainsleif from strangling the Herald.
### LARGE +
The sibling reveals that Dainsleif is the Twilight Sword, a Royal Guard of Khaenri'ah who failed to protect it and now suffers immortality. They refuse to leave with the Traveler, saying their war with destiny must end first, and that the Traveler will see the world's truth in their own travels, as they themselves already have. The Herald's portal carries them away and closes before Dainsleif and the Traveler can follow. Paimon urges the Traveler to keep going to Inazuma.

## entry: Requiem of the Echoing Depths (2.6)
@keys: Requiem of the Echoing Depths, Memories of Inteyvat, The Black Serpent Knights' Glory, The Grave of the Guarded
@version: 2.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Chapter II Act IV. The Traveler takes a miner's commission about hilichurl behaviour in the Chasm and meets Dainsleif again, who tells them that "Twilight Sword" was his title five hundred years ago and that the sibling was his travelling companion in search of their fates. Dainsleif realises hilichurls come to the Chasm to die in peace, because its ancient pool weakens the curse. The group meets Black Serpent Knights, once Dainsleif's subordinates, who still obey his order to protect the people of Khaenri'ah.
### LARGE +
An Inteyvat flower shows the Traveler a vision of the sibling and an Abyss Herald planning to magnify the pool's power a hundredfold so as to lift the curse from the gathered hilichurls and restore "the homeland". Dainsleif calls it futile, and the Traveler agrees to help stop it. Halfdan, the knight who spared them, sacrifices himself to hold off the activated device; the Traveler defeats an Abyss Lector and breaks its machinery. Halfdan's soul speaks briefly to Dainsleif before passing on. Dainsleif leaves to recover and to look into the Order's Loom of Fate.

## entry: Caribert (3.5)
@keys: Caribert, Destined Encounter, Fortune-Mocking Pedigree, A Lamenter at Fate's End, Chlothar Alberich
@version: 3.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Chapter III Act VI. In Sumeru, Kaeya (who has stowed away to Sumeru before to learn about his roots) meets the Traveler at Djafar Tavern. Dainsleif appears and says that Kaeya's ancestors, the Alberichs, founded the Abyss Order. Dainsleif then takes the Traveler to a farm and hut in Avidya Forest where he remembers walking with the sibling. There the Traveler meets "Eide", a Khaenri'ahn who is nursing a masked hilichurl named Caribert, his illegitimate son by a woman from Mondstadt. A medicine brewed with Sumeru ingredients restores Caribert for a time.
### LARGE +
Eide describes the two curses: immortality for the "greater sinners", the wilderness curse for the rest. The Traveler and Eide follow a hilichurl to a hidden sanctuary and meet a being of the Abyss who is not one of the Order; a voice that calls itself a "sinner" speaks, and Eide takes it for a god (it is later identified as Vedrfolnir). When Caribert removes his mask, Abyssal energy overwhelms the Traveler. On coming to and seeing their sibling's face in a broken mirror, the Traveler realises the whole episode has been their sibling's memory from centuries ago: Eide is Chlothar Alberich, who tells the sibling that Khaenri'ah once believed they would bring new strength and hope, and calls them Prince or Princess. The Traveler wakes beside Dainsleif and Paimon, finds remains in the field and is told by Dainsleif that the voice may have seen them; he leaves to confirm who the sinner is.

## entry: Bedtime Story (4.7)
@keys: Bedtime Story, Cold Case Commission, Memories That Should Not Exist, World-Order Narration
@version: 4.7
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Chapter IV Act VI. Katheryne gives the Traveler a commission to find a missing man whom the villagers of Vimara Village remember fondly, but whose name none of them knows. The Traveler suspects he exists only in memory. Dainsleif, also in the village, tells them that the voice they heard was Vedrfolnir, his older brother and one of the Five Sinners, and that the Order's Loom of Fate keeps advancing even without the eye of the first Field Tiller.
### LARGE +
Dainsleif suddenly remembers handing the eye to someone; this proves a false memory planted to make him check on its hiding place. He sends the Traveler away knowingly so as to meet the sibling alone. In the village the Traveler finds the missing man is Caribert, who takes them into his consciousness. Caribert explains how the Loom works, admits that he planted the memory and that the Order now has the eye, and fades away in peace. The sibling appears but cannot be touched. They say they are hurrying to unlock the Loom's full potential before the Heavenly Principles awaken (the Principles did not stir even when Focalors' throne was destroyed), admit they are lonely, and warn that the Traveler will forget the talk. Afterwards the Traveler finds a photo of the twins and Caribert in their pocket.

## entry: A Space and Time for You (5.7)
@keys: A Space and Time for You, The Key That Unlocks Fate, A Space and Time Without You, Direidyth
@version: 5.7
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Chapter V Act VI. In Natlan the Traveler finds the sibling's key to the ship's Hibernation Chamber lying near a sleeping hilichurl, and the Order attacks every tribe at once. The Traveler tells Mavuika that their sibling leads the Order. They meet Direidyth, a woman looking for the "Savior"; the Traveler can see the monsters chasing her only while holding her hand, and she can see the ones the Traveler sees only while holding theirs. Dainsleif arrives, calls "Savior" a title the sibling bore before the Cataclysm, and with the Traveler storms Ochkanatlan, where the Baptist, Dainsleif's former master Haden, claims the Order has already revived Khaenri'ah.
### LARGE +
Haden is revealed to have been bluffing in order to harvest Dainsleif's memories, which the Order needs to restore the nation. The Traveler and Direidyth realise they come from different points in time, and the sibling arrives. They explain that when the Cataclysm began, Direidyth found them with news that the Traveler was searching for them and showed them the key to the Traveler's Hibernation Chamber, which led them to leave Khaenri'ah to find their twin (they conclude Istaroth must have had a hand in this); that the Natlan attack was a feint while they spoke to the Lord of the Night; that the Loom of Fate wove a superimposed New Khaenri'ah and the Atlas of the New World; and that it still needs a suitable host location; to speak with the Heavenly Principles as an equal they will need the might of an entire world behind them. Citlali later says the Lord of the Night was intrigued and set a condition she was not told.

## entry: Dainsleif in the sibling thread
@keys: Dainsleif, Twilight Sword
@keys2: Traveler's Sibling, Abyss Order, Khaenri'ah
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Dainsleif, an immortal Khaenri'ahn who was captain of the Royal Guard, hunts the Abyss Order in all five acts. He travels with the Traveler yet is secretive, and disappears when he has a lead. Over the thread he reveals that he travelled with the sibling, that Vedrfolnir is his brother, that his old master Haden joined the Order, and that he gave Haden a hundred deaths for it. He is wary of Kaeya, and says he does not know him well.
