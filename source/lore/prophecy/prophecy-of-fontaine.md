## summary
### LITE
The prophecy is a doom foretold for Fontaine. Every Fontainian is born with a sin that no trial can absolve. One day the waters will rise, the people will dissolve into them, and only the Hydro Archon will be left, weeping on her throne. Only then will their sins be washed away. Rumour links it to Egeria, the first Hydro Archon. For years it was a gloomy rumour, until the sea really began to rise and people really began to dissolve. Its truth and resolution are the climax of the Fontaine Archon Quest (Version 4.2).
### STANDARD +
The fans' name for it is "the prophecy of Fontaine"; in game people just call it "the prophecy". Lyney, who first explains it to the Traveler, says "prophecy" is almost the wrong word: what it describes is already happening.

## established
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The truth, revealed in Masquerade of the Guilty (4.2): Fontainians descend from Oceanids, Egeria's water familiars. Because they longed to live as humans, she gave them human bodies by pouring Primordial Seawater into their "blood vessels". She had no permission from the Heavenly Principles to create a new human race. Celestia judged this a sin, and Egeria and all her creations were branded with an "original sin". Primordial Seawater dissolves a Fontainian back into an Oceanid; non-Fontainians are not dissolved.
### STANDARD +
Focalors, an Oceanid made human by Egeria, inherited the problem when she succeeded Egeria as Hydro Archon. She decided to deceive the Heavenly Principles: let the prophecy seem fulfilled while saving everyone. She split her divinity from her human self, Furina, and had Furina play the Hydro Archon for about 500 years. In 4.2, Fontaine put Furina on trial, and the Oratrice sentenced "the Hydro Archon" to death. Focalors executed herself with the Oratrice, destroying the Hydro Archon's divine throne and returning its Authority to Neuvillette, the Hydro Dragon Sovereign. Neuvillette then declared the people's sins forgiven. The flood still came, but nobody dissolved, and the waters receded.
### LARGE +
Behind the scheme:
- Focalors hid her divinity, with the Hydro Gnosis, inside the Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale.
- The trials at the Opera Epiclese turned the people's belief into Indemnitium. Focalors used only a small part of it to power Fontaine and stored the rest for 500 years, building enough power to destroy a divine throne.
- She cursed Furina so that she could not die, or freely live for her own happiness, while Focalors existed.
- She had arranged for Neuvillette to serve as Iudex so that centuries among humans would make him unwilling to declare them guilty.
- Once his Authority was restored, Neuvillette's verdict made the Fontainians true humans, and he could separate the Primordial Sea's power from the All-Devouring Narwhal.

## the_prophecy_text
### LARGE
The prophecy as Fontaine tells it (Lyney, Soloist's Prologue, 4.0):
1. Everyone in Fontaine is born with sin, and no trial can absolve it.
2. One day the water will rise and slowly drown the sinners.
3. The people will all dissolve into the waters, leaving only the Hydro Archon weeping on her throne.
4. Only then will Fontaine's sins be washed away.

The prophecy names no date and no cause. Nobody knew what the sin was, so at first most people ignored it. Then, in the years before 4.0, sea levels rose and places were submerged. In 4.0, Neuvillette notes that two parts had already come true: the rising sea, and the fact that Fontainians can be dissolved (the serial disappearances case showed that).

## accounts
### STANDARD
Who claims what:
- Lyney (4.0): some say Fontaine's ancestors stole the power of the seas and roused its wrath; others say the people ignored the first Hydro Archon's warnings and offended Celestia. Lacking evidence, Fontaine treats both as conjecture.
- Neuvillette (4.0): rumour roots it in the last words of the previous Hydro Archon, Egeria. He suspects it is the "conundrum" she left to Furina.
- Augereau, a Fontainian in the 4.0 opening: an old story says people once lived in the ocean and grew blood vessels to carry the sea inside them. To him, dissolving would be like going home.
### LARGE +
- Wriothesley (4.1) does not much believe in prophecies, but he found that this one kept proving accurate. He describes the waves of panic a prophecy causes: first when people hear it, again when they see signs, and again when it happens.
- Mona (4.2) came to Fontaine to speak on the prophecy as an astrologist. She says it concerns the fate of Fontaine and perhaps of all Teyvat, and must come from a powerful visionary.
- N of the Hexenzirkel (4.2) tells the Traveler that prophecies are "the history of the future" and will be fulfilled. She also hints that prophecies show the future as the gods see it, and that things happen in corners where the gods do not look.
- Focalors (4.2): Egeria knew the prophecy would come to pass, and knew that defying the Heavenly Principles is dangerous. Focalors calls the theft of the Primordial Sea's power Fontaine's "original sin", and the people's right to exist its "original justice".
### ULTRA +
- Nahida (voice-over, "About Furina", after 4.2): the prophecy was carved into Irminsul long ago, yet it seems everything has changed; she says "we both know her" to be smart enough to turn the fate of the world around.
- The Fontaine glider Wings of Merciful, Wrathful Waters tells another version. Celestia's envoy made a new "heart" for the Primordial Sea, Egeria, after the first heart, the first Hydro Dragon, was removed. Egeria's compassion led her into "all the sins" that beings of pure water must beware. The item presents these as tales told by vishaps, Oceanids and scholars, and warns that they may not be true.

## slates
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The prophecy slates are four stone slates, set out as if as an offering in ancient ruins near Poisson. Navia, Neuvillette and the Traveler found them in Meeting Is Also Parting (4.2). Only three were there; the first was missing. An inscription below reads, roughly: reason dictates that this nation be destroyed, so its future history is recorded here in its past. The slates call themselves a "history of the future". Navia wondered whether this place was where the prophecy began.
### LARGE +
What each slate shows:
1. Egeria using divine power to turn Oceanids into people. Freminet, sent by the Knave, recovered it from the sea floor and brought it to the trial in Apocalypse.
2. Egeria kneeling with her people before a floating island in the sky, as if confessing, as judgement falls on them.
3. The Hydro Archon (it looks like Furina) sinking into water, surrounded by people.
4. The scene the prophecy describes: the people dissolved and the Hydro Archon alone, weeping on her throne.

The order of slates three and four troubled everyone. Neuvillette later read the third slate symbolically: the people in it are humans, not dissolved Oceanids, so the water stands for judgement. In other words, the people of Fontaine would put the Hydro Archon on trial. Their trial meant to escape the prophecy was itself part of it.
### ULTRA +
Neuvillette sensed a second layer of information etched into the slates with "a different power source". He could not read it with only three slates. Once the set was complete, he decoded the true history of the Fontainians' creation and announced it in court. Who carved the slates is unconfirmed; the Traveler later thinks Vedrfolnir likely did (see uncertainties).

## fulfilment
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Before the end, the prophecy's signs kept arriving:
- In 4.1, Wriothesley showed the Traveler a great sluice gate under the Fortress of Meropide, holding back the Primordial Sea. Its water had risen dangerously high, and Wriothesley built the airship Wingalet in case the gate failed.
- In 4.2, Primordial Seawater leaked around Poisson and some residents dissolved.

At the trial in the Opera Epiclese, the All-Devouring Narwhal, which had fed on the Primordial Sea, broke in, drawn by the Fontainians. Childe, in his Foul Legacy form, and Neuvillette drove it back.
### LARGE +
After Focalors's execution, Neuvillette and the Traveler fought the Narwhal in the Primordial Sea. The battle's Hydro energy then flooded Fontaine, so that only part of the Opera Epiclese stood above the water. Furina wept alone on the defendant's stand, her "throne", as the water reached her feet. Navia in a boat, Wriothesley's Wingalet, and Lyney, Lynette and Freminet pulled people from the water. Then the flood receded. Survivors shouted that the prophecy had been wrong, and Furina, freed from her curse, walked out into the sunlight as a human.
### ULTRA +
Those who had already dissolved earlier were not restored. Skirk shrank the subdued Narwhal into her hand and called it her master Surtalogi's "pet". Afterwards, Neuvillette gave the Hydro Gnosis to the Knave as a diplomatic gift. She had earlier received the Tsaritsa's permission to use the Gnosis against the prophecy first, and she passed it on to the Tsaritsa. After the prophecy came to a head, the amount of Primordial Water on Teyvat's surface greatly diminished.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- Who carved the slates, and why? The Traveler suspects Vedrfolnir (4.7), but nobody confirms it.
- Did the prophecy come true or fail? It was fulfilled in form but defeated in effect, and canon keeps both readings.
- How a prophecy "carved into" Irminsul could be outwitted, and what deceiving the Heavenly Principles means for the world's fate in general, are not explained.
- Whether Egeria herself spoke the prophecy, or only knew it, is unresolved.
- What the second, hidden layer in the slates was made with is not explained beyond "a different power source".

## connections
### LARGE
Remuria, Fontaine's earlier civilisation, lived under its own prophecy of doom. Lore texts say Sybilla foretold that Remuria's desire for justice would bring it down. Remus sought out the imprisoned Egeria, guarded by the dragon Scylla, for a way out, and she gave him a goblet of Primordial Seawater. Remuria sank anyway. Wriothesley knew a theory that the sea which swallowed Remuria would rise again, and that the prophesied flood was part of the same idea.

- The prophecy explains why Fontaine is obsessed with trials, why its god was an actress, why the Oratrice existed and why Neuvillette was Iudex.
- The Narzissenkreuz Ordo was founded to help Fontaine survive a coming apocalypse that its founders, Rene and Jakob, had predicted.
- The Fatui's interest in Fontaine was partly about this prophecy.
- Focalors destroyed the Hydro Archon's divine throne and Celestia took no action; in 4.7 the Traveler's sibling cites this as proof of the Heavenly Principles' dormancy.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay guidance (this project's recommendation, not canon text). Before 4.2, Fontainians knew the prophecy as a common, uneasy rumour. Most of them did not know they were made from Oceanids, why they could dissolve, or that Furina was not a god. Those truths came out publicly at the trial in 4.2.
### LARGE +
After 4.2 the danger is over. Fontainians are true humans and no longer dissolve, the Hydro Archon's throne is gone, and Furina lives as an ordinary person. People remember the flood and the "miracle" of the receding water. Characters who were not present (for example, most people outside Fontaine) would know only rumours. Do not have the prophecy threaten Fontaine again, and do not treat Vedrfolnir's authorship as fact.

## entry: Prophecy slates
@keys: prophecy slates, prophecy slate, history of the future
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Four stone slates in ancient ruins near Poisson depict Fontaine's prophecy as a "history of the future". They show:
1. Egeria turning Oceanids into people.
2. Egeria and her people kneeling before Celestia as judgement falls.
3. The Hydro Archon in water, surrounded by people, which Neuvillette read as the people putting her on trial.
4. The people dissolved and the Archon weeping on her throne.

Three were found in 4.2; Freminet recovered the missing first slate from the sea floor. Neuvillette decoded a hidden layer that revealed the true origin of the Fontainians. The Traveler suspects Vedrfolnir made them (unconfirmed).

## entry: Original sin of Fontaine
@keys: original sin, born with sin, Fontaine's original sin
@keys2: Fontaine, Fontainian, Fontainians, Egeria, Oceanid, Hydro Archon, Neuvillette
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Fontaine's "original sin" was Egeria's theft of the Primordial Sea's power to make her Oceanids human without the Heavenly Principles' permission. Celestia branded her and all her creations with it. The sin meant that Primordial Seawater would dissolve them back into Oceanids. In 4.2 Neuvillette, with his restored Hydro Authority, declared the people of Fontaine forgiven and made them truly human. Focalors called their right to exist Fontaine's "original justice".

## entry: Focalors' deception
@keys: Focalors's plan, deceive the Heavenly Principles, deceiving the Heavenly Principles
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Focalors's 500-year plan to make the prophecy look fulfilled while saving Fontaine had five parts:
1. She split her divinity from her human self, Furina, and had Furina play the Hydro Archon.
2. She hid herself and the Hydro Gnosis in the Oratrice, storing the Indemnitium raised by trials.
3. She brought Neuvillette in as Iudex so he would come to understand humans.
4. When the people tried "the Hydro Archon", she executed herself and destroyed the divine throne.
5. Neuvillette, restored to his Authority, absolved the people.

Furina knew only that she had to keep up the act; if her identity were exposed, everything would be lost. The epilogue sums it up: Focalors deceived the Heavenly Principles and used Furina's endless performance to save Fontaine.
