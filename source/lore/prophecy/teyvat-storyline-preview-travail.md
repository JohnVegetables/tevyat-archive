## summary
### LITE
Travail is an official preview video for the Archon Quest, released on 27 September 2020 and narrated by Dainsleif. He gives each nation one or two ominous lines, shows one character per nation, and ends by speaking to the Traveler in a field of flowers. Its opening claim is that the war "has already begun" as "a continuation of past battles" (which the wiki links to the Cataclysm).
### STANDARD +
Each nation from Mondstadt to Snezhnaya appears as an "Act" with a Latin motto (labelled by the wiki as Ancient Teyvat Script) and a title that became the matching Archon Quest chapter title; a final unnumbered act ("ACT.??") covers Khaenri'ah without a motto. It laid out the planned run of the story from Mondstadt to Snezhnaya, with Khaenri'ah last. Some of its lines only became understandable years later.
### LARGE +
The video is best treated as in-world-flavoured foreshadowing delivered by Dainsleif, not as a neutral summary. It shows what he wants the Traveler to consider, not the full truth of any nation's story.

## established
### LITE
- Dainsleif narrates. The Traveler (Aether and Lumine, both shown) and a field of Inteyvat flowers frame the video.
- Opening: the war has already begun, a continuation of past battles. The gods tempt mortals with seven "treasures" and "the doorway to divinity", while burnt remains lie buried deep in the world as a warning to trespassers. "That throne in the sky is not reserved for you."
- Each act pairs a nation with one featured character and a short line about its god.
### STANDARD +
Act by act, as the video gives them:

- **Prologue, Mondstadt** (Diluc; "The Outlander Who Caught the Wind"; motto: my freedom is my song). The dragon who defended Mondstadt for a millennium finally faces his perturbation. What does freedom mean when a god demands it?
- **Chapter I, Liyue** (Ningguang; "Farewell, Archaic Lord"; motto: let the contract be made, though the heavens fall). The God of Contracts is slaughtered as his people watch in horror, and in the end signs "the contract to end all contracts".
- **Chapter II, Inazuma** (Kamisato Ayaka; "Omnipresence Over Mortals"; motto: perpetual, perennial, impervious). The immortal Shogun's Bakufu rules eternal. What do mortals see of the eternity their god pursues?
- **Chapter III, Sumeru** (Cyno; "Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana"; motto: light of wisdom under the flowers). The God of Wisdom's enemy is wisdom itself. In the city of scholars there is a push for folly, and the god makes no argument against it.
- **Chapter IV, Fontaine** (Lyney and Lynette; "Masquerade of the Guilty"; motto: justice conquers all). The God of Justice lives for the spectacle of the courtroom and seeks to judge other gods, yet knows not to make an enemy of the divine.
- **Chapter V, Natlan** (Iansan; "Incandescent Ode of Resurrection"; motto: rise, O strong man, and go to your destined victory). The rules of war are woven in the womb: victors burn bright, losers turn to ash. When the God of War shares this secret with the Traveler, she has her reasons.
- **Snezhnaya** (Pulcinella and followers; "Everwinter Without Mercy"; motto: I, nigh unmatched Queen by my glory, shall be guide). A god with no love left for her people, nor they for her. Her followers hope only to be on her side when she rebels against the divine.
- **Khaenri'ah** ("The Dream Yet to Be Dreamed"; Dainsleif is shown). Most people in a sheltered eternity are content to live and not to dream, but in places the gods do not watch there are those who dream of dreaming. Dainsleif says humans have their humanity and will defy the world with a power from beyond.
### LARGE +
The ending is addressed to the Traveler as the one "who has set foot in this world". A figure stands in silhouette in the Sea of Flowers at the End. Dainsleif says the journey has reached its end but one doorway remains, and invites the Traveler to step forward if they have understood the meaning of the journey. He asks to be defeated, or commanded to step aside, as proof that the Traveler is worthier than he to rescue "her", and then says the threads of all fate will be theirs to re-weave. His last line says his memory has all but faded but he will always remember how much she, too, loved these flowers.

The video opens with Aether saying there was no time to say goodbye, so let's not call it that, while two shooting stars cross the sky.
### ULTRA +
Details worth keeping straight:
- The wiki notes that Diluc, Ningguang and Kamisato Ayaka each have a residence that can be entered in game (the Dawn Winery, the Jade Chamber and the Yashiro Commission headquarters), while Lyney and Lynette's home (the Hotel Bouffes d'ete, visible in the open world) cannot be entered and Iansan's residence is not in the open world. This is a wiki observation about game design, not story canon.
- The video introduced a musical motif for each nation that the game reused later, according to the wiki.
- The line about the "throne in the sky" plays on the Chinese use of the word for "throne" in constellation names; the English cannot carry the pun.
- The Sumeru act title is a Purana-style title in Chinese (about the "Akasha Calamity Ash"), and the "folly" line puns on the Fatui's Chinese name. Both wordplays were lost in the early English version.

## accounts
### STANDARD
Dainsleif is the only voice. He speaks as someone who disapproves of the gods' order and, in the Khaenri'ah act, counts himself among the "we" who will defy the world. He frames the gods as offering rewards while keeping mortals from the divine throne, and humans as people who should defy that. The video shows no counter-view, and later canon complicates several of his claims, so he should not be treated as an objective narrator.
### LARGE +
The Chinese original is more literal than the English release. Examples from the wiki's unofficial translation: the gods "encase the outlines of desire in seven types of brilliant light" to show their authority can be exceeded; the Mondstadt dragon is a giant who guarded the city of freedom for thousands of years rather than "a millennium"; and the Heavenly Principles are the one thing the Fontaine god cannot make her enemy. The Natlan and Snezhnaya acts are phrased differently in Chinese ("rules of war carved into the bodies of all living things"; "a god whom no one will love ever again").

The Snezhnaya act says her followers follow because they believe she will one day raise a flag of rebellion against the Heavenly Principles. This is how Dainsleif describes it, not necessarily how the Fatui would describe it.

## connections
### STANDARD
- The acts line up with the Archon Quest chapters named in the video.
- The Fontaine act's courtroom drama and judging of gods fits Fontaine's central story, covered in the Prophecy of Fontaine entry.
- The opening ("past battles", which the wiki links to the Cataclysm) and the Khaenri'ah act tie the nation acts to the Khaenri'ah thread, and the ending's challenge to "rescue her" points toward the Traveler's journey's end; who "her" is stays unstated (see open questions).
### LARGE +
Reading the lines against later content is possible but should be labelled as a reading. The "senselessly slaughtered" line fits the Liyue chapter's staging of the God of Contracts' death; what the "contract to end all contracts" refers to is left to interpretation. The Natlan line fits Mavuika sharing an Ancient Name with the Traveler, as the wiki links it. The Snezhnaya line fits the Fatui's focus on the Tsaritsa's rebellion. None of these matches is spelled out by the video, and how they play out belongs to the quest entries.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- Which lines were meant literally and which as poetry cannot be told from the video itself.
- Who "her" is in the closing lines is not stated in the video. This archive does not pick a reading.
- Whether Dainsleif's challenge ("defeat me ... to rescue her") promises a literal later fight is not established by the video. His full role belongs in his own entry.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay guidance (this project's recommendation, not canon text). The video was an out-of-game release, so characters would not know it as a document. It is a good flavour source for how Dainsleif speaks (formal, aphoristic, pointedly addressed to the Traveler). Do not give characters knowledge of later chapters merely because the video hints at them.
### LARGE +
In scenes with Dainsleif, the tone of the closing address (challenging, weary and elegiac) is canon-consistent. Avoid having him spell out the meaning of the acts, since the video deliberately leaves them veiled.

## entry: Travail video

@version: 1.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Travail is an official preview video, released 27 September 2020 and narrated by Dainsleif. It runs through the Archon Quest nations one act at a time, each with a Latin motto, one featured character and a short ominous line about the nation's god. It opens with the statement that the war has begun again and that the divine throne is not for mortals, and closes with Dainsleif's challenge to the Traveler in the Sea of Flowers at the End.
### LARGE +
Featured characters: Diluc (Mondstadt), Ningguang (Liyue), Kamisato Ayaka (Inazuma), Cyno (Sumeru), Lyney and Lynette (Fontaine), Iansan (Natlan), Pulcinella and Fatui followers (Snezhnaya), and Dainsleif (Khaenri'ah).
