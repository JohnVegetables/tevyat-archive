## summary
### LITE
The Withering is a blight in Sumeru that kills living land and sickens people. It shows up as dark, hostile Withering Zones: patches of dead ground where monsters gather and a "tumor" sits at the centre. Its human form is the illness Eleazar. It is tied to forbidden knowledge and, after the 3.2 Archon Quest, stopped spreading, but zones that already existed remain a danger.
### STANDARD +
Sumeru's people have met it at two moments: in the ancient desert kingdom of King Deshret, and again during the Cataclysm 500 years ago, when the pollution of Irminsul made new zones appear faster than the Forest Rangers could clear them.

## established
### LITE
- It is a climate-like blight of Sumeru, named the Withering in game. Aranara call it Marana and say it can turn forest (Vana) into desert (Valuka); an old book calls it the Black Tide.
- Zones are harmful to everything living; humans without a Vision can barely endure them.
- Eleazar is the Withering's manifestation in human bodies.
### STANDARD +
- A Withering Zone is centred on a Tumor of the Withering, ringed by Withering Branches and sometimes Fetid Boughs (budding, flower-like growths that spit at intruders). Dendro power destroys the branches; the tumor then calls up a stronger "Disciple of Decay"; once that is beaten the tumor can be destroyed and the zone ends for good. Creatures such as Tri-Lakshana animals sit scorched and unresponsive inside until it is cleared.
- Zones start as small nascent zones that draw a few monsters; if left, they grow, gain branches and boughs, and begin to affect people. Typical inhabitants are Fungi, Ruin Machines and Riftwolves; no ordinary wildlife or harvestable plants survive within.
- Rana, who did not yet hold a Vision, spent only a few minutes in a zone and left sick and exhausted, then fell into a coma; Arana bubbled her to keep her safe (as told on the wiki page).
### LARGE +
- The book The Folio of Foliage (pre-3.2 text) treats the Black Tide as a flood of decay that swallowed fields, villages and cities, whose root lay underground.
- Forest Rangers carrying Visions were the main force against zones, but after the Cataclysm new ones appeared faster than they could be cleared.
- Gameplay note (not in-world fact): the game models exposure as a Decay gauge that lowers resistances and Max HP and kills the party if it stays full; Candles of Life, Flames of Life and some waypoints relieve it.

## accounts
@version: 3.6
### LARGE
- Wiki Lore summary: King Deshret introduced forbidden knowledge to his people; the land weakened and the blight afflicted those who wandered in. Greater Lord Rukkhadevata checked it, but the effort cost her much of her power and left her child-sized.
- Homecoming (3.6): Apep says she was polluted by forbidden knowledge; elemental lifeforms born from her fled her body, and the wiki reads the Withering as their "death", with the lifeforms turning into Tumors. After a zone is cleared, any life essence left in the tumor can be reclaimed by a living elemental lifeform.
- Aranara folklore (Arakarman): Marana is an old enemy of the hero Aramuhukunda, alongside giant iron machines.

## history
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
It first appeared in King Deshret's desert kingdom, was halted by Rukkhadevata at great cost, and returned with the Cataclysm 500 years ago.
### LARGE +
Rukkhadevata sacrificed herself to stop it spreading across Sumeru. Cases fell, but her taint in Irminsul kept feeding it, so it still spread. After Nahida removed Rukkhadevata from Irminsul (3.2), the Withering stopped expanding and Eleazar disappeared, though existing zones stayed.
### ULTRA +
Homecoming (3.6) then gave a second angle: the blight as the death of the elemental lifeforms that escaped Apep, which Nahida resolved by healing Apep and keeping Rukkhadevata's promise to those lifeforms. Alhaitham had already noticed in Through the Predawn Night (3.2) that the Withering, Eleazar and the sandstorms resembled the ancient desert pollution.

## status
@version: 7.1
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Withering no longer spreads, but old Withering Zones can still be met in Sumeru. Forbidden knowledge is considered erased from Teyvat.
### STANDARD +
No canon after 3.6 revisits the Withering directly in the sources checked. Do not place new zones in a post-3.2 scene as spreading naturally; a lingering zone is fine.

## connections
### STANDARD
Closely linked to forbidden knowledge (cause), Eleazar (human form), Rukkhadevata and Irminsul (why it spread), and Apep (Homecoming).

## roleplay
### LARGE
For scenes: describe zones as silent, airless patches of dead growth with a faint sour taint, watched by monsters; do not make them spread beyond what canon states. Characters like Tighnari or the Forest Rangers speak of them as a known hazard; most Sumeru residents rarely enter.

## entry: Withering Zone
@keys: Withering Zone, Withering Zones, Tumor of the Withering, Disciple of Decay
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A Withering Zone is a patch of corrupted ground in Sumeru with a Tumor of the Withering at its centre. Dendro power breaks its Withering Branches, the tumor summons a Disciple of Decay, and destroying the tumor ends the zone. Inside, exposure sickens and can kill; Visions let humans resist. Wildlife and plants do not survive within.

## entry: Marana
@keys: Marana, Black Tide, Valuka
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Aranara call the Withering Marana, saying it can turn forest into desert. The Folio of Foliage calls it the Black Tide, a dark flood of decay from the Cataclysm era.
