## summary
### LITE
Forbidden knowledge is a kind of knowledge that does not belong to Teyvat. Greater Lord Rukkhadevata called it a "truth" no one in the world can understand, which came from the very bottom of the Abyss. The world keeps rejecting it, and that rejection causes disasters: madness, a scale-like illness (Eleazar), and decay of the land (the Withering). It mostly struck Sumeru. Nahida and the Traveler erased it from Irminsul in 3.2.
### STANDARD +
Canon records it in King Deshret's desert kingdom thousands of years ago, in the dragon Apep, and in Irminsul and Rukkhadevata herself during the Cataclysm 500 years ago. Nahida later said the Abyss takes whatever form is most devastating to each land: in Sumeru it was forbidden knowledge, in Natlan it was monsters.
### LARGE +
In 7.1 the Traveler told the Fatui Harbingers that forbidden knowledge tainted by the Abyss should no longer exist anywhere in Teyvat. When the Harbingers unsealed the notebook of the Khaenri'ahn Sinner Hroptatyr, most of its pages had gone blank.

## established
### LITE
- It is foreign to Teyvat and comes from the Abyss. Even the God of Wisdom could not understand it.
- People exposed to it hear "crazed whispers", go mad and grow dark grey scales. Land exposed to it loses its vigour.
- Gods and dragons can be polluted too: Apep and Rukkhadevata both were.
### STANDARD +
- Rukkhadevata warned that if it polluted Irminsul, all of Teyvat might fall apart.
- Its traces could be suppressed with divine power, contained, or altered into a more stable form, but it could not simply be destroyed. Rukkhadevata's own memory in Irminsul kept it alive after her death.
- Removing it from Irminsul (3.2) ended Eleazar within days and stopped the Withering from spreading.
### LARGE +
- Its effects were kept from the public. Alhaitham, despite his time at the Akademiya, had never been told that it could drive people insane, and suspected the Akademiya had never understood it.
- Nahida told the Traveler that they have "an amazing resistance" to it (Homecoming, 3.6).

## nature
### LARGE
Canon describes it more by its effects than its content. The terms used for it include "knowledge that did not belong to this world" (Kasala), a "truth" that cannot be understood (Rukkhadevata), "the apocalypse" (Apep and the elemental Fungi of the Chasm), and, in King Deshret's weapon text, a "dark poison" and a "sin running deeper than the sky". Those who sought it wanted power to defeat or escape the order of the Heavenly Principles. It was not a written book of spells: it spread "like a plague", and through Irminsul it could pollute memories.

Nahida treats it as a regional manifestation of the Abyss. The Abyss "manifests its power in a manner that is most devastating to the place in question", she said; for Sumeru, a nation of wisdom, that meant knowledge. Item text pairs it with the tearing-down of the sky's barriers, and the Heavenly Principles answered it with Celestial Nails.

## history
@spoiler: major
### LITE
It first afflicted the desert of King Deshret thousands of years ago, then returned with the Cataclysm 500 years ago and polluted Irminsul.
### STANDARD +
- The desert: Deshret brought it into his kingdom. Rukkhadevata saved what she could and was shrunk to a child's form by the effort; Deshret sacrificed himself, letting Apep swallow him (own entries below).
- The Cataclysm: Rukkhadevata, not summoned to Khaenri'ah because she had to protect Irminsul, felt the pollution arrive with the disaster. She built the Akasha, which used people's dreams and wisdom together with her power, and cleared almost all of it from Irminsul, but she herself was already polluted and died.
- 3.2: Nahida, the purest branch of Irminsul Rukkhadevata chose as her next incarnation, used two Gnoses to reach her predecessor's consciousness and removed Rukkhadevata from Irminsul. Everyone forgot Rukkhadevata, except the Traveler.
### LARGE +
- Before the desert: Apep says the dragons once believed only forbidden knowledge could give them the power to defeat the Heavenly Principles. Nibelung, the Dragon King, brought "the power of darkness from outside of this world" and led them in the war of funerary flame. After he died, Apep kept gathering it as the world neared collapse until a Celestial Nail fell and turned her forest into dunes (3.6).
- The Fungi of the Chasm: after Deshret's death the "apocalypse" passed into Apep, and elemental lifeforms born from her fled it. Rukkhadevata promised one of them she would erase it. Nahida kept the promise after 3.2 and healed Apep in Homecoming (3.6).
- Before 3.2: in Through the Predawn Night Alhaitham pieces together that the Withering, Eleazar and the sandstorms resemble the ancient desert pollution, and that Irminsul's illness may come from it.
### ULTRA +
- Rukkhadevata explained that she could not remove herself, since that was a paradox, so she left a trail of clues for her successor. Removing her meant she would never have existed. Nahida protested and then did it.
- The Akademiya's Divine Knowledge Capsules held knowledge taken from scholars who went mad connecting to Irminsul, which still carried Rukkhadevata's polluted consciousness (wiki, Akasha System). The Gnosis gave Nahida some protection when she entered that consciousness.

## status
@version: 7.1
### LITE
As of 7.1 forbidden knowledge is considered gone: erased from Irminsul in 3.2, so the Withering and Eleazar no longer spread. The Abyss itself remains active in other forms.
### STANDARD +
In Etched in Time (7.1), the Traveler argued that Hroptatyr's sealed notebook was no longer dangerous for this reason. Pierro agreed, and after the Harbingers voted to unseal it, he found most of its pages blank, "presumably forbidden knowledge that has been erased". What was left were Hroptatyr's personal thoughts and research notes from the Universitas Magistrorum.
### LARGE +
Its after-effects outlive it. Those who suffered from Eleazar recovered, but the dead did not return. People's memories of Rukkhadevata were rewritten, and Apep is only now recovering.

## effects
### LITE
- Eleazar: dark, hardened scales, numbness, nerve damage, immobility, coma, death. It was unique to Sumeru and had no cure until 3.2.
- The Withering: zones of decay in Sumeru's forests.
- Madness: "crazed whispers" in people's minds.
### LARGE +
- Oozing Filth: dark sludge in the Chasm's Underground Mines in Liyue, where forbidden knowledge was altered by the power of the Spiritstone (a Celestial Nail) and stored in a more stable but still dangerous form.
- In Apep it amplified her hatred and rage until it was purged.

Eleazar was positively correlated with a person's elemental content, and the forest eased it. In Homecoming, the Withering is shown to be tied to the deaths of elemental lifeforms that escaped Apep's corrupted body; the creatures that stayed in the Chasm took monstrous forms to survive.

## accounts
### LARGE
- Kasala, a priest of King Deshret (recorded memories, 3.1): the Scarlet King brought it into the world. It spread like a plague; Rukkhadevata saved the survivors, and the king gave his life.
- Greater Lord Rukkhadevata (3.2): knowledge from the bottom of the Abyss, a "truth" no one can understand, that the world keeps rejecting.
- Nahida (3.2 onward): two pollutions, the desert and the Cataclysm. Later (5.x voice line) she calls it Sumeru's form of the Abyss.
- Apep (3.6): the dragons sought it to fight the "usurper". Nibelung brought it, and she kept collecting it until the nail fell. Deshret's corrupted knowledge became her "apocalypse", and she calls herself the "final fail-safe" in his plan.
- King Deshret (Staff of the Scarlet Sands text): the "dark poison" penetrated the earth and the spikes fell to heal the world; taking poison is a sin "running deeper than the sky", yet its whispers are sweet.
- Nabu Malikata (Amethyst Crown text): invaders from beyond the firmament brought war and "illusions that could break through shackles"; the master of the heavens, afraid of "the rising tide of delusion and breakthroughs", sent the nails. She warned Deshret not to inquire into the sky and the abyss, and he silently resolved to defy her.
- Pierro (7.1): Hroptatyr's notebook held material that would have driven him mad and could bring national disaster; he would not let Snezhnaya repeat Khaenri'ah's mistakes.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- What it actually is: canon never shows its content, only its effects.
- How it reached Irminsul during the Cataclysm, and whether Khaenri'ah's "secrets from beyond the skies" or the Sinners' Abyssal power were the source.
- Exactly what Al-Ahmar obtained with the Goddess of Flowers' help.
- Whether everything Hroptatyr wrote has truly been erased, or only what Irminsul could reach.

## connections
### STANDARD
Source: the Abyss. Opponent: the Heavenly Principles and its Celestial Nails. Medium: Irminsul, which records knowledge and memory. Victims: King Deshret's civilisation, Apep, Greater Lord Rukkhadevata and Sumeru's Eleazar patients. Ended by: Nahida and the Traveler. Related phenomena with their own entries: the Withering, Eleazar, Abyssal corruption, the Akasha, the war of funerary flame, the Cataclysm.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): keep its content unknowable. Never write out what forbidden knowledge "says"; show its weight instead: whispers that half make sense, a scholar who will not stop writing, scales spreading on skin, colour draining from leaves. Present-day (7.1) Sumerians may know Eleazar is gone and the Withering has stopped, but most do not know why, and no one remembers Rukkhadevata.
### LARGE +
Scenes set before 3.2 can include Eleazar patients, Withering zones and Akademiya secrecy. Scenes after 3.2 should not have new cases. Desert people of Aaru Village can carry Kasala's story of the king's sacrifice; Alhaitham, Nahida and the Traveler know the full account. Only the Traveler remembers that Rukkhadevata existed.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- It is not ordinary "banned research" or Akademiya censorship: it is Abyssal corruption that harms whoever holds it.
- Do not have it still spreading in Sumeru after 3.2, or have anyone except the Traveler remember Rukkhadevata.
- Do not present the Khaenri'ah connection as confirmed; canon only implies it.
- "The apocalypse" in Homecoming means this corruption inside Apep, not the end of the world.

## entry: King Deshret's forbidden knowledge
@keys: Kasala, Scarlet King, Al-Ahmar, King Deshret
@keys2: forbidden knowledge, plague, scales, whispers
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Thousands of years ago King Deshret (Al-Ahmar) brought forbidden knowledge into his desert kingdom while trying to defy the Heavenly Principles. It spread like a plague: crazed whispers, dark grey scales, dying land. His old friend Greater Lord Rukkhadevata had the priests build temples and filled them with the divine power of life. This slowed the disaster, but exhausted her until her form became a small child's. The survivors' descendants live in Aaru Village. The king finally sacrificed himself. The priest Kasala's recorded memory of this, shown in Secret of the Scorching Desert (3.1), told the desert people that the two gods had been allies, not enemies.
### LARGE +
By agreement, Apep swallowed Deshret after his death to gain all his knowledge. She gained "the apocalypse" instead, and believes he used her as a fail-safe.

## entry: Apep's apocalypse
@keys: apocalypse, Oozing Filth, Oozing Concretion
@keys2: Apep, Fungi, Chasm, forbidden knowledge, Nahida
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In Nahida's Story Quest Homecoming (3.6), Apep tells how she was polluted. The dragons once believed only forbidden knowledge could defeat the Heavenly Principles; after Nibelung's death she kept collecting it until a Celestial Nail stopped her. Swallowing King Deshret gave her his corrupted knowledge, "the apocalypse", which magnified her hatred. Elemental lifeforms born from her fled; their deaths are tied to the Withering. In the Chasm, the Spiritstone's power had turned the apocalypse into Oozing Filth, more stable but still dangerous. Nahida and the Traveler healed Apep. She grew calmer but still despises humanity.

## entry: Rukkhadevata and Irminsul's pollution
@keys: Akasha, Rukkhadevata, Greater Lord, Irminsul
@keys2: forbidden knowledge, pollution, Cataclysm, erase, Withering, Eleazar
@version: 3.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
When the Cataclysm struck 500 years ago, forbidden knowledge polluted Irminsul. Greater Lord Rukkhadevata stayed behind to protect the tree and built the Akasha to clear it using the wisdom in people's dreams together with her own power. Almost all of it was removed, but she was polluted herself; even dead, her memory in Irminsul kept the corruption alive, causing the Withering and Eleazar. She chose the purest branch of Irminsul as her next incarnation, Nahida, and left her clues. In Where the Boat of Consciousness Lies (3.2), Nahida and the Traveler used two Gnoses to reach her and removed her from Irminsul. Everyone except the Traveler forgot her, and the disease and decay ended.

## entry: Hroptatyr's notebook
@keys: Hroptatyr's notebook, The Wise's notebook, notebook of The Wise
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Before he abandoned his humanity, the Khaenri'ahn Sinner Hroptatyr ("The Wise") gave his fellow student and rival Eftirvit (both studied under Professor Mimameith), later Pierro, a notebook. When Pierro eventually read it, he realised its contents would drive him mad and could bring disaster on a national scale. With the Tsaritsa's approval the Harbingers sealed it deep in the Armory Palace archives, to be opened only by a unanimous vote of all active Harbingers. In Etched in Time (7.1), the Traveler explained that forbidden knowledge had been erased from Irminsul. The Harbingers voted to unseal it, and most pages were blank. What survived included Hroptatyr's notes on extracting the Crimson Moon's authority over Death, a discovery he first named the "blessing of immortality" and then renamed the "Curse of Immortality".

## entry: Secrets from beyond the skies
@keys: secrets from beyond the skies
@keys2: Khaenri'ah, Khaenriah, Irmin, Anfortas, forbidden knowledge
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
An unsigned, unfinished letter in the Khaenri'ahn ruins under Gavireh Lajavard (3.6), addressed to a Marshal, says the "four pillars" of the kingdom prospered by "spying upon secrets from beyond the skies". That knowledge let their machines throw off the shackles of this world's laws. Worried that the technology was imperfect, the writer asks the Marshal to petition the King to preserve some machinery built to the old standard. The wiki links this to forbidden knowledge, but canon does not confirm the link.
