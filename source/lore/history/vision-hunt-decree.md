## summary
### LITE
The Vision Hunt Decree was an order in Inazuma to confiscate Visions from their bearers and inlay them on the Statue of the Omnipresent God, in front of Tenshukaku. It was issued in the name of the Electro Archon, the Raiden Shogun (Ei), and enforced by the Tenryou Commission. By the time the Traveler arrived about a year later, 99 Visions had been taken. In Archon Quest Chapter II the Traveler and Yae Miko led Ei to abolish it, and the Visions were returned.
### STANDARD +
The decree is a cause of the resistance movement of the Watatsumi Army and a central subject of Chapter II. It was also a Fatui scheme (see `accounts`), though Ei knew of that and approved it anyway.

## established
### LITE
- The decree ordered Vision bearers in Inazuma to hand over their Visions. They were inlaid on the Statue of the Omnipresent God, built in front of Tenshukaku as the "Symbol of Eternity."
- The Tenryou Commission, one of the three Commissions, carried it out. The Raiden Shogun's government issued it.
- It began about a year before the Traveler's arrival, around the same time as the Sakoku Decree. Refusal and concealment were punished severely.
- The Traveler met the decree in Chapter II, Act II (2.0): during the Vision Hunt Ceremony, the Traveler intercepted Thoma's Vision. After the Traveler and Ei fought in the Plane of Euthymia in Act III (2.1), Ei abolished it.
### STANDARD +
- By Chapter II, Act II, 99 Visions had been seized; the 100th was meant to be Thoma's, at a public Vision Hunt Ceremony marking the milestone.
- Seized Visions could not be used by their owners, and many bearers lost their ambition along with their Visions, a side effect described in canon. Arataki Itto and Kuki Shinobu, whose Visions were seized, seem to have been unaffected.
- People who opposed the decree but not the government made decoy fake Visions. The Watatsumi Army on Watatsumi Island, which fought the Shogunate, drew members who had lost or feared losing their Visions, and also those who opposed the Sakoku Decree. It also sheltered people unwilling to fight.
- Kamisato Ayaka, Kamisato Ayato, Yae Miko, Sayu and Kujou Sara kept their Visions in public without incident. Sara served the Shogun directly; Thoma kept his until the ceremony. The Tenryou Commission dismissed Kurosawa Kyounosuke for poor performance and then took his Vision, so service was no permanent protection.
### LARGE +
- Kaedehara Kazuha took his friend's Vision after the friend was killed, before it could be seized. Seizures were attempted against Kazuha, Hanshirou, Thoma, Yoimiya and Kazuha's friend. Kazuha's arrest warrant was cancelled after the decree ended.
- Seized Visions named in canon include those of Arataki Itto, Kuki Shinobu, Domon, Tejima, Iwakura Mitsunari and Kurosawa Kyounosuke, plus many Watatsumi Army fighters. After the decree ended, all seized Visions were returned to owners who were alive and reachable.
- The decree is what first set the Raiden Shogun against the Traveler: at the ceremony the Traveler used elemental power without a Vision, and the Shogun called them an exception: "Exceptions... the enemy of Eternity."
- The Balladeer says generating conflict creates a big market for Delusions.
### ULTRA +
- Because of the danger in Inazuma, even people without Visions avoided travelling there (per Alrani, Atsuko and Bibo).
- The reaction abroad: at Liyue Harbor, the scholar Alrani (World Quest Endless Research, 1.1) mentions the decree as rumour, linking it to the year with no new Electro Visions, and decides not to go to Inazuma at the time.

## accounts
### STANDARD
Three accounts of why the decree existed:
- **The Fatui plot.** In "Delusion" (Chapter II, Act III) the Harbinger known as the Balladeer states the Fatui worked hard to make the decree happen. The Fatui hoped to produce unrest and build a market for Delusions in Inazuma, and they say it was in their interest.
- **Takayuki's deal.** In "Blind Loyalty, Reckless Courage" the Tenryou Commissioner Kujou Takayuki admits to a bargain with Snezhnayan diplomats: generous rewards if he kept the decree enforced. He wanted to protect the Kujou Clan's position and believed the Fatui could not threaten Inazuma. He reported to the Shogun that the decree was proceeding without obstacle (see the Official Tenryou Commission Document).
- **Ei's reasoning.** Ei, in the Plane of Euthymia, says she gave tacit approval. She says Fatui actions so far were no threat to eternity, that individual ambition cannot coexist with eternity, and that nobody lost their life for having a Vision taken; those who died, she argues, died pursuing aspirations. Her pursuit of eternity follows her past losses, including the end of the previous Shogun, Makoto.
### LARGE +
Yae Miko, who knows Ei well, disagrees. She tells Ei that a nation without ambition or the potential for change is a hollow shell, and that what her people want from her is attention, not promises. Sara, once she learns of Takayuki's deal, says she had already doubted the decree.
Characters weigh responsibility differently: Sara blames Takayuki, the Traveler points to the Fatui, and Yae Miko challenges Ei's own rigidity. Ei says she was well-informed about the decree.

## open_questions
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Canon does not give the exact date the decree began, or say why no new Electro Visions appeared during it. Ei denies controlling Visions, and Alrani and the Akademiya scholars suspected otherwise; the matter is left unresolved. See `uncertainties` in the header. Do not invent a link between the decree and the origin of Visions.

## connections
### STANDARD
- **Sakoku Decree**: issued about the same time and enforced by the same government. Abolishing the Vision Hunt Decree did not end the Sakoku Decree at once; restrictions on foreigners were relaxed, and Ei lifted the Sakoku Decree later, after her duel with the Shogun puppet in Transient Dreams (Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter, Act II).
- **Statue of the Omnipresent God**: after Chapter II, Act III the Visions were removed from the statue and returned.
- **Watatsumi Army and Sangonomiya Kokomi**: the resistance fought the decree and the Shogunate.
### LARGE +
- **Thoma, Ayaka and the Kamisato Clan**: Thoma was scheduled to lose his Vision at the 2.0 ceremony; Ayaka wanted to rescue him herself, but intervening would have compromised the Yashiro Commission's neutrality, so the Traveler went instead and retrieved the Vision.
- **Kujou Clan**: Sara and Takayuki's conflict over the decree is a major storyline in Chapter II.
- **Snezhnaya**: the Fatui say the decree served their interests, and Takayuki dealt with Snezhnayan diplomats.

## timeline
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
- About a year before Chapter II: the decree and the Sakoku Decree take effect in Inazuma.
- Chapter II, Act II (2.0): the Vision Hunt Ceremony, Thoma's Vision, the Traveler's fight with the Raiden Shogun, and the order for the Traveler's arrest.
- Chapter II, Act III (2.1): Delusion and Blind Loyalty, Reckless Courage; in The Omnipresent God Ei admits her tacit approval and, defeated, abolishes the decree. The Visions return to their owners.
- Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter, Act II (after Chapter II): Ei abolishes the Sakoku Decree.
- No later canon through 7.1 reinstates the decree.

## entry: Vision Hunt Ceremony
@keys: Vision Hunt Ceremony
@version: 2.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A public event at the Statue of the Omnipresent God to mark the 100th Vision seized under the decree, held in Chapter II, Act II (Stillness, the Sublimation of Shadow). The target was Thoma, who was bound on an execution block. The Traveler intercepted his Vision as it flew toward the Raiden Shogun, who pulled the Traveler into another dimension and quickly knocked them out. As she raised her sword over the unconscious Traveler, Thoma freed himself, threw a polearm at her and fled with the Traveler. The Shogun ordered the Traveler seized "under the decree" and vowed to "strike twice" next time, and the Tenryou Commission added the Traveler to its wanted list. Thoma then went into hiding at the Komore Teahouse and the Traveler joined the Watatsumi Army.

## entry: Statue of the Omnipresent God
@keys: Statue of the Omnipresent God, Symbol of Eternity
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
The statue in front of Tenshukaku was built after the decree to hold confiscated Visions. Its Chinese and Japanese names mean "Statue of the Thousand-Armed, Hundred-Eyed God." After Chapter II, Act III, the Visions were removed and returned to their owners.

## entry: Takayuki and the Fatui bargain
@keys: Takayuki, Kujou Takayuki
@keys2: Vision Hunt Decree, decree, Fatui
@version: 2.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Kujou Takayuki, Tenryou Commissioner and head of the Kujou Clan, told Kujou Sara and the Traveler that Snezhnayan diplomats offered rewards in return for upholding the decree. He judged the Fatui could not harm Inazuma while the Shogun's Musou no Hitotachi existed. Sara disagreed, and the Traveler and Yae Miko used the exposed evidence to press for the decree's end.
