## summary
### LITE
The Sakoku Decree ("country-locking order") closed Inazuma's borders. It was issued in the name of the Raiden Shogun and enforced by the Kanjou and Tenryou Commissions, with a ring of constant thunderstorms around the archipelago. Outsiders could not leave Ritou without a travel permit, and trade collapsed. It was issued about a year before the Traveler arrived (2.0), together with the Vision Hunt Decree. Ei abolished it in the Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter, after her duel with the Shogun puppet.
### STANDARD +
Where the Vision Hunt Decree was how the Shogunate treated its own people, the Sakoku Decree was how it treated everyone else. The two are linked in the story: the Watatsumi Army drew people who opposed either one.

## established
### LITE
- The Shogun, Ei's puppet, ordered Inazuma's borders shut. Anyone entering or leaving legally had to pass strict checks by the Kanjou Commission, which handles Inazuma's finances, customs and borders.
- Outsiders who were not Inazuman could not go beyond the port town of Ritou without a Travel Permit.
- The Tenryou Commission also helped enforce the decree.
- Storms were set around Inazuma to keep ships away. Few ships were able or willing to brave them.
### STANDARD +
- The decree was in force when the Traveler arrived by ship in Setting Sail (Chapter II, Act I, 2.0). Thoma, the Traveler's contact, takes them to the border checkpoint first and warns that evading inspection is not possible.
- The Kanjou Commission's Outlander Affairs Agency took advantage of the stranded outsiders. Yurika first asked the Traveler for two million Mora; Thoma negotiated it down to 600, and says such fleecing is common. Foreign merchants at the International Trade Association are shown struggling.
- Inazuma's economy suffered. Exports, imports and domestic trade dropped; ships that crossed the storms often lost goods, and fishing declined because the Kanjou Commission treated all vessels as suspect.
- Restrictions did not at first apply to the Fatui and Snezhnayan merchants, because of their diplomatic ties.
- In-game, the storm appears as the climate A Nation Closed by Thunder; its description says Ei has closed Inazuma and no one may enter or leave without good reason. It is cleared when Transient Dreams is completed.
### LARGE +
- The decree was issued about a year before the Traveler's arrival. Chapter II explains it as part of the Fatui plot (see `accounts`), which used the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions to destabilise Inazuma.
- When the Vision Hunt Decree was abolished (Chapter II, Act III, 2.1), the Sakoku Decree did not immediately follow. Restrictions on foreigners were relaxed and the Kanjou Commission was purged of corrupt officials; the Outlander Affairs Agency later became more helpful, but the decree was still formally in place.
- After her duel with the Shogun puppet in Transient Dreams, Ei abolished the decree, thanking the people for their support. Visitors poured in and foreign trade returned to normal. Several NPCs in Ritou and Inazuma City gain new dialogue about it afterwards.
### ULTRA +
- The Inazuma culture article records that life was healthy before the decrees; Paimon says Inazuma had no problems in the year before she began travelling with the Traveler. Once the decree ended, Inazumans could trade with other nations, host visitors and travel abroad; those who leave to train sometimes call themselves "shugenja".
- The name echoes the historical Japanese Sakoku policy, and Ritou resembles Dejima, per the wiki's trivia. This is a real-world reference, not an in-universe fact.

## accounts
### STANDARD
- **The Fatui plot.** In Chapter II the Fatui conspire with the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions, using the closure and the Vision Hunt Decree to cause instability and create a market for Delusions. Hiiragi Shinsuke supplied the Fatui with Crystal Marrow and Kujou Takayuki fed the Shogun false reports of the war (per the Inazuma culture and history pages).
- **Ei's endorsement.** Ei knew the Fatui were involved but did not see them as a threat, and the policy suited her ideal of eternity at the time (see Vision Hunt Decree). She lifted it only after the Traveler and Yae Miko led her to reconsider.
### LARGE +
- **The people's view.** The Watatsumi Army fought the Shogunate partly over this decree and the Vision Hunt Decree. Merchants at the International Trade Association describe being treated harshly under the Kanjou Commission.

## open_questions

## connections
### STANDARD
- **Vision Hunt Decree**: issued at the same time; ended earlier.
- **Kanjou Commission and Ritou**: the border authority and the one open port.
- **Fatui and Snezhnaya**: the plot behind the decrees, and the people exempt from the closure.
- **Watatsumi Army**: the resistance that formed partly in opposition.
### LARGE +
- **Tatarigami**: the war the decrees inflamed led Watatsumi Army fanatics to break the wards on Yashiori Island, which unleashed the Tatarigami.
- **Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter**: Ei's Story Quest, in which the decree ends.

## timeline
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
- About a year before the Traveler's arrival: the Sakoku Decree and the Vision Hunt Decree take effect.
- Chapter II, Act I (2.0): the Traveler crosses the storm, registers at Ritou and meets the Outlander Affairs Agency.
- Chapter II, Act III (2.1): the Vision Hunt Decree is abolished and the Kanjou Commission is purged.
- Imperatrix Umbrosa Chapter, Act II (Transient Dreams): Ei abolishes the Sakoku Decree.
- No canon through 7.1 reinstates it.

## entry: Outlander Affairs Agency
@keys: Outlander Affairs Agency
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
A branch of the Kanjou Commission in Ritou that processes foreign visitors. Under the decree it overcharged stranded outsiders; after the commission was purged, it became more helpful.

## entry: A Nation Closed by Thunder
@keys: A Nation Closed by Thunder, storm around Inazuma
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
The storm belt around Inazuma. Entering it by sea sends travellers back to their starting port, so in play Inazuma is reached only through the story or by teleportation. It clears after Transient Dreams.
