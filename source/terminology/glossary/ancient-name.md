## summary
### LITE
An Ancient Name (tocaitl) is a title held by certain people of Natlan's six tribes, along with a small obsidian stone that represents it. Each Name stands for a quality such as blessing, devotion or resilience, and carries the remembered deeds of everyone who bore it before. The tribe's guardian spirit, its Wayob, picks who receives one. Before the war ended, bearers were expected to enter the Pilgrimage and to fight the Abyss in the Night Warden Wars.
### STANDARD +
The Name is both an honour and a way of keeping a tribe's history alive: each bearer's deeds become part of what the Name means for the next. Most Names are not tied to a single tribe. Natlan's people treat receiving one as a special honour, and bearers are the only people whom the Ode of Resurrection was originally meant to revive.
### LARGE +
The Ancient Names carried by the six Heroes of Natlan are special: they are linked to a hero of the past, and a modern hero who truly connects with theirs gains that earlier hero's memories and knowledge. The Traveler received a Name of their own in Incandescent Ode of Resurrection (5.3), the first ever given to an outsider.
### ULTRA +
Most Ancient Names do not give powers. Pacal says the bearer must earn the Name's meaning by their own deeds. The Names are made in the syllabic form of the Ancient Teyvat Script, and the wiki traces the known words to Swahili; this is an out-of-universe note.

## receipt
### STANDARD
Each tribe's Wayob is mainly responsible for granting Ancient Names to worthy tribe members. Xilonen says the recipient must have done something in their life that embodies the spirit of the Name, though the recipient may not understand why they were chosen. A Name is not usually limited to one tribe, apart from those of the Heroes of Natlan and Names bound to a particular tribe's own tradition.
### LARGE +
The Pyro Archon can instead commission a new Name for someone they judge worthy, though strict conditions apply. A skilled smith trained as a Name Engraver does the work, and forging costs the engraver part of their lifespan. Xilonen says the forging technique is not hard but gathering the facts of the recipient's life must be careful and exact: any falsehood in it would make the Name defective and could cause cognitive problems for its bearer.
### ULTRA +
A Name can also be forged to record a deserving person's life after they have died, and the Wayob then give it to the first worthy recipient. It is unclear whether the Archon commissions these or how common they are. Normally only Natlanese memories are recorded by the Night Kingdom, so historically only people of Natlan received Names. For the Traveler, Mavuika promised one in recognition of rescuing Kachina and helping against the Abyss, and the Lord of the Night's Pilgrim's Chronicle solved the problem of recording their deeds (see the Traveler's Name below).

## stones
### LARGE
Each bearer receives an obsidian stone shaped like a feather. Glowing characters in the Ancient Teyvat Script appear on both faces and spell the Name in syllables. Bearers usually carry the stone, but this is not required: when Mualani led the rescue of Kachina from the Night Kingdom she chose to leave hers behind. Stones are made by commissioned Name Engravers such as Xilonen, and formerly her mentor Tlazolli. Forging also engraves the deeds of the Name's first bearer into the stone, though these marks are not visible on the finished object. The story implies that a Name must be stored in such a stone and cannot exist alone.
### ULTRA +
The wiki suggests the feather shape may echo the bird-like design of Wayob Manifestations. This is a guess, not stated in canon. Xilonen says that using an Ancient Name to commit a crime was unheard of, until Tlazolli was caught trying to overwrite Nepecha's memories with those of his late daughter Nechca, a case the Children of Echoes had not yet resolved when it was recorded.

## duties
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
While the Night Warden Wars lasted, bearers had to take part in the Pilgrimage of the Return of the Sacred Flame, and those who won a place were normally sent to fight the Abyss.
### LARGE +
Team leaders were usually bearers, and a team with several bearers was uncommon because it could look like cowardice. The Archon could release a winner, as happened to Vichama after an injury. Illness or old injuries could bar a bearer from the Pilgrimage, as with Atea and Chanca. If at least one member of a team returned victorious, the Ode of Resurrection could revive the fallen. If a whole team fell, none came back, their deeds were forgotten and their Names lost, which is why the saying goes that victors burn bright while the defeated turn to ash.
### ULTRA +
Bearers could keep competing as long as they felt able. The most famous holder of Uthabiti had fought in twenty-seven Night Warden Wars and was past sixty when Pacal described him, which Pacal called exceptional. A Name can also be lost by being unclaimed: Mavuika says the exile Tenoch lost his while still alive. New Names forged over time have replaced those lost in the Wars.

## known_names
@version: 5.3
### LARGE
The Names with known meanings, each with its current bearer in the story: Baraka (blessing, Xilonen), Bidii (devotion, Ororon), Kiongozi (leader, Mavuika), Malipo (the Turnfire, Kinich), Mwongozo (guidance, bearer unknown), Tumaini (hope, the Traveler), Ukumbuko (memory, Citlali), Umoja (unity, Mualani), Undugu (kinship, Tlazolli), Uthabiti (resilience, Kachina), Uwezo (power, Iansan) and Vuka (transcension, Chasca). A thirteenth Name meaning "seek" was last held by Vichama; he died in the Archon Quest and no successor has been named.
### ULTRA +
Earlier bearers listed by the wiki: Baraka, Sundjatta, Sakkuk and Ixquieh. Bidii, Sanhaj Kompore. Kiongozi, Xbalanque. Malipo, Burkina and Yupanqui. Ukumbuko, Huitzilin and the master of Maghan. Umoja, Tupac and Uenuku. Uwezo, Wanjiru and Ahpub. Vuka, Menilek and Lianca. Known bearers whose Names are not given include Cauich, Chanca, Kibangu and Kaiulani (current), and Atea, Mallko, Pisac and Tenoch (former). These attributions come from a wiki table that cites individual quests and weapon texts; treat Ukumbuko's and Undugu's as medium confidence.

## six_heroes
@version: 5.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Six Names belong to the Heroes of Natlan: Baraka, Bidii, Malipo, Umoja, Uwezo and Vuka. Their bearers 500 years ago were Sundjatta, Sanhaj Kompore, Burkina, Tupac, Wanjiru and Menilek. Today they are held by Xilonen, Ororon, Kinich, Mualani, Iansan and Chasca. Each modern hero was recognised through the Name before the Wayob formally acknowledged them, as part of Mavuika's plan to end the war.
### ULTRA +
Mavuika told the Traveler that after the Cataclysm the tribes and their Wayob were weakened, and a Wayob picking a bearer showed the tribe was strong again. By the time the Traveler arrived, Kinich, Iansan and Xilonen were ready; Mualani made a fourth, and the story later brought in Ororon and, after her sister Chuychu died in the invasion, Chasca. The wiki says Name Engravers adjusted these six Names so that connecting with one brings back the memories of its earlier holders.

## travelers_name
@version: 5.3
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Mavuika arranged for Xilonen to forge a Name for the Traveler. Xilonen at first called it impossible because the Night Kingdom does not record an outsider's deeds. With help from Citlali and the Lord of the Night, who made a parchment called the Pilgrim's Chronicle to record the Traveler's past and future deeds in Natlan, she forged Tumaini, "hope". The Traveler's soul proved so strong that testing it drew in many stray souls, including Vichama.
### ULTRA +
When the Traveler embodied Natlan's hope in the final battle, a projection of the Name appeared and the Lord of the Night acknowledged it with a mantra, unlocking their Nightsoul's Blessing. The mantra speaks of someone setting themselves aflame in darkness so that the universe could know light.

## mantras
@version: 5.0
### ULTRA
Every Name has a mantra, usually revealed in a dream to a tribe's chief by a Wayob. Those known outside the Heroes of Natlan are paraphrased here. Tumaini is about the first person to burn in the dark so that light could exist. Ukumbuko, probably Citlali's, speaks of a bird-like soul that travels through forest, sea, fire and ore, whose paintings become gifts for the dead. Undugu, probably Tlazolli's, says the old gives rise to the new as flowers protect roots, until all return to the soil. Uthabiti tells of one who stands like a mountain, meets the sun's gaze and burns away what is petty. The wiki marks Ukumbuko's and Undugu's as likely rather than confirmed.

## status_as_of_7_1
@version: 5.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
After the final battle in 5.3 the Night Warden Wars are over. The Pilgrimage is now purely a competition, and bearers no longer have to take part in it. Ancient Names still exist and are still carried and honoured.
### LARGE +
The Traveler holds Tumaini; Chasca, Xilonen, Ororon, Kinich, Mualani and Iansan keep the Heroes' Names, and Mavuika bears Kiongozi. Vichama's Name has no bearer.
### ULTRA +
Canon through 7.1 shows no further change in how Names are made. The Hope Name was a one-time exception, and no other outsider has received one.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Natlanese characters treat Ancient Names as meaningful and a little personal. They may mention their Name's meaning with pride, but few explain the mechanics to strangers. Those who knew the pre-5.3 war speak of Names in terms of duty and the risk of being forgotten. Do not call a Name a "class" or a "power-up". Do not let outsiders claim a Name, other than the Traveler. Characters who were not present for 5.3 should not know the Traveler holds Tumaini.

## entry: Ancient Name stone
@keys: Ancient Name stone, Ancient Name stones
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
A palm-sized obsidian feather inscribed with the Ancient Teyvat Script. Glowing syllables on both faces spell out the Name. It is made by a Name Engraver, who also engraves the first bearer's deeds into it, and bearers usually keep it on their person.

## entry: Name Engraver
@keys: Name Engraver, Name Engravers
@version: 5.1
### STANDARD
A smith trained to forge Ancient Name stones. Xilonen, who bears Baraka, and formerly Tlazolli hold the role. Forging a Name takes part of the engraver's lifespan, and the engraver must research the recipient's life carefully, since any false detail would damage the Name.
### LARGE +
In The Rainbow Destined to Burn the Lord of the Night confirms to Citlali that forging takes a toll on the engraver, in connection with the Traveler's Name.

## entry: Uthabiti
@keys: Uthabiti
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
Resilience. Kachina bears it. The Name's most famous earlier holder fought in twenty-seven Night Warden Wars. Pacal, chief of the Children of Echoes, cited this when explaining how bearers are remembered.

## entry: Tumaini
@keys: Tumaini
@version: 5.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Traveler's Ancient Name, meaning hope. It was forged by Xilonen in Incandescent Ode of Resurrection and is the first Name ever given to an outsider. The Lord of the Night's Pilgrim's Chronicle stood in for the Night Kingdom's missing record of the Traveler's deeds.

## entry: Lost Ancient Names
@keys: lost Ancient Name, Ancient Name forgotten, forsaken Ancient Name
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
Before the Wars ended, a team that was wiped out left no one to carry its tale. Its members could not be revived, their deeds were forgotten and their Names were lost. A Name can also be lost if its holder is exiled, as Mavuika says of Tenoch.
