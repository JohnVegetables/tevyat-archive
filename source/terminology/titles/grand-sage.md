## summary
### LITE
"Grand Sage" is the title of the central leader among the six Great Sages who head the Sumeru Akademiya's Darshans. It is held in addition to leading one Darshan, and the Grand Sage is the absolute leader of the Akademiya. Azar held it until Chapter III; Naphis holds it in the later Sumeru story.
### STANDARD +
The title is separate from the Dendro Archon's authority: the Akademiya was founded by Greater Lord Rukkhadevata and Lesser Lord Kusanali (Nahida) remains its god, but the day-to-day running of the Akademiya, and much of Sumeru's government, falls to the sages and their Grand Sage. At least in Azar's time the Grand Sage's office was above the House of Daena.
### LARGE +
The office changed hands three times in the story: Azar (corrupt, removed in Chapter III), Alhaitham (acting, briefly, as a stopgap), and Naphis (the honest Amurta Sage, appointed afterwards).

## definition
### LITE
The Akademiya has Six Great Schools, now nearly the same as the six Darshans, and each is led by one Great Sage. Among these six there is one central leader, the Grand Sage, as Alhaitham explains in A Toast to Victory.
### STANDARD +
The Six Great Schools were established with the Akademiya by Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, each representing a type of wisdom. Other Darshans have been created over the centuries, but only the original six survived, so "Six Darshans" and "Six Great Schools" are today nearly synonymous. Sages are chosen by a set of criteria and hold administrative power over the Akademiya and a seat on advisory councils.
### LARGE +
The Grand Sage keeps a sage's own Darshan. Azar was the Rtawahist Sage and Grand Sage; Naphis is the Amurta Sage and Grand Sage. Researchers bring matters to the Grand Sage: Alhaitham mentions Kshahrewar applying for funding from the Grand Sage, and in Pride and Prejudice Siraj presented a thesis to the Grand Sage.

## powers_and_limits
### LARGE
Canon does not spell out what extra authority the title carries beyond the ordinary sage's administrative powers. It is clear that under Azar the Grand Sage could steer the whole Akademiya: he kept desert-folk from the entrance exam, threatened Nilou and banned public performances at the Sabzeruz Festival, and had the two dissenting sages locked away.
### ULTRA +
The limits were political, not formal. Even Azar gave Naphis some leeway because of his legendary temper, yet still imprisoned him. The Grand Sage cannot overrule the god: once Kusanali was freed in Chapter III, Azar and his accomplices were removed and she personally oversees the selection of new sages.

Cyno, as General Mahamatra, answers to the sages in principle but not blindly. When he asked to investigate anomalies in the data he submitted to the Akasha System, Azar refused and told him he had no right to judge him, and Cyno resigned in protest. Cyno's character stories say that under the six ancient sins even sages are not exempt from judgment.

## holders
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Azar, Rtawahist Sage, was Grand Sage during Chapter III and led the plan to make an artificial god. Naphis, the Amurta Sage and Tighnari's former master, is the current Grand Sage in the later Sumeru story. Alhaitham served as Acting Grand Sage in between, for a short time.
### LARGE +
Tighnari describes Naphis as a man of integrity who sticks to his beliefs, and his short temper is called legendary. Alhaitham was offered the post of Grand Sage and refused, saying he was neither suited to being a sage nor interested in being one, but he handled the Akademiya's important affairs until new sages took office.
### ULTRA +
The other sages shown in 6.6 are Baladhuri of Vahumana, Alkindi of Spantamad, Firnas of Kshahrewar, Shakir of Rtawahist and Rushd of Haravatat. The wiki's table also lists former sages such as Cyrus (Spantamad, retired) and Khajeh (Haravatat, exiled). Nothing in canon says who held the Grand Sage title before Azar.

## history_of_the_office
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
After Greater Lord Rukkhadevata's death, the sages, disappointed by the newly born Lesser Lord Kusanali, sought a replacement god. When Il Dottore returned to Sumeru and offered to make one, Azar and three other sages agreed; Naphis and Baladhuri opposed it and were locked up. Azar oversaw the effort, including the dream samsara powered by the Akasha System, and used Scaramouche, supplied by Dottore, as the vessel.

Under Azar's rule the Akademiya banned the transmission of information from outside sources and required plays to follow approved themes, before banning public art performances outright. After the Traveler and Nahida defeated Scaramouche in Chapter III, Azar and his accomplices were removed, made to repent before the Archon and exiled to the Avidya Forest, where they now spend their time cultivating their wisdom.
### ULTRA +
Naphis and Baladhuri were released after Azar's fall. Alhaitham became Acting Grand Sage after A Toast to Victory (3.2) and, in Pride and Prejudice (3.4), says he is resigning to return to being Scribe. By 6.6 (A Divergence Leading to Oneself, Song of the Welkin Moon) Naphis is Grand Sage; he tells Tighnari he has only just been appointed and admits he handled the Azar affair badly, since his protest only got him locked away.

## status
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the last confirmed Grand Sage is Naphis. Of the other five sages shown in 6.6, Naphis and Baladhuri are the old guard who opposed Azar; the other four took office after the old sages fell.
### LARGE +
In the Irminsul crisis of 6.6 Naphis leads the sages' council at Caravan Ribat, approves Tighnari's plan to take the Dendro Archon to Apep, and arranges for scholars and sages to relocate to the Temple of Silence in the desert while Eremites escort civilians.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Treat the title as a heavy responsibility rather than a throne. Naphis is dignified, quick to anger, honest and protective of his students. Address him as "Grand Sage", or "Master" if he is the speaker's old teacher, as Tighnari does.

Characters who lived through Chapter III remember Azar's era as authoritarian, and the Akademiya's old contempt for the arts still lingers in Sumeru. Do not make the current Grand Sage a villain, and do not imply the Grand Sage controls Nahida.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
Do not say Azar is still Grand Sage, or that Alhaitham permanently holds the title. Do not invent a vote, ritual or term length. Do not give Naphis powers over the Akasha System or Irminsul beyond what the quests show.

## entry: Azar's tenure as Grand Sage
@keys: Azar
@keys2: Grand Sage, Akademiya, Sumeru, Rtawahist
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Azar, the former Rtawahist Sage and Grand Sage, led the sages who wanted a new god to replace Kusanali, accepted Il Dottore's offer, imprisoned the dissenting sages Naphis and Baladhuri and banned public performances at the Sabzeruz Festival. After Chapter III he was removed and exiled to the Avidya Forest.

## entry: Alhaitham as Acting Grand Sage
@keys: Acting Grand Sage
@keys2: Alhaitham, Scribe, Akademiya
@version: 3.2
### STANDARD
After Azar's fall Alhaitham was offered the Grand Sage position and refused, but acted as Acting Grand Sage to handle the Akademiya's important affairs until new sages took office. In Pride and Prejudice he says he has submitted his resignation to return to being the Scribe, because he fears "acting" would become permanent.

## entry: Naphis as Grand Sage
@keys: Naphis
@keys2: Grand Sage, Amurta, Tighnari, Akademiya
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Naphis, Amurta Sage and Tighnari's former master, is the current Grand Sage. He was imprisoned for opposing Azar's plan, freed in Chapter III, and by 6.6 leads the council of six sages during the Irminsul crisis. He is known for a legendary temper and honesty.
