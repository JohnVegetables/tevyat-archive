## summary
### LITE
Celestia is the floating island that hangs in the sky above Teyvat, visible from most of the world. People call it the realm of the gods. It is the residence of the Heavenly Principles and the Four Shades, and each Archon of The Seven holds a divine throne there. The Heavenly Principles have been silent since the Cataclysm 500 years ago, but Celestia itself still watches over the world's laws. No one in the story has visited it on screen, and ordinary people know it only through legend.
### STANDARD +
Popular belief holds that great heroes can ascend to Celestia and become gods. Venti told the Traveler that every Vision holder is an "allogene" who could one day ascend. An Archon's Gnosis resonates directly with Celestia.

## established
### LITE
- Celestia is a real, visible place in the sky, not a metaphor. In-game it floats roughly west-southwest of Mt. Mingyuan in Liyue, at a very high altitude.
- It is controlled by the Heavenly Principles, who have lain dormant since the Cataclysm.
- The Four Shades (Naberius, Ronova, Istaroth and Asmoday) reside there and serve the Heavenly Principles.
### STANDARD +
- In Ending Note (Prologue, 1.0) Venti explains that the Archons are not powered by Visions; each has an internal focus, the Gnosis, that resonates directly with Celestia.
- In the Sumeru Archon Quest (3.2), Nahida describes the Heavenly Principles as long silent and the Gnoses as symbols of their control over Teyvat and its laws.
- The Celestial Nails (also called nails of retribution or divine nails) are cast down from the heavens at the Heavenly Principles' command; they have devastated civilizations such as Hyperborea and Lang-Gan.
- Celestia keeps records of things that were erased from Irminsul. Rhinedottir says she once read Celestia's records about the Third Descender, whose existence had been wiped from the world's memory.
### LARGE +
- The Four Shades hold periodic meetings in Celestia. The official teaser "The Gods' Limits" (July 2025) shows three of them meeting there; Asmoday has been missing since the Cataclysm and has not attended.
- According to Rhinedottir (7.1), the Third Descender ascended to Celestia to bargain with the Heavenly Principles, offering his body (split into seven pieces, later the Gnoses) in exchange for Anastasya's resurrection and protection for his descendants, the Khaenri'ahns.
- In the 7.0 Archon Quest, Ronova and Pierro both state that Paimon is a creation of Celestia (own entry).

## monitoring
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
As revealed in the 7.1 Archon Quest "A Rekviem for the Underworld", Celestia acts as a regulator even while the Heavenly Principles sleep. It runs a "monitoring cycle" set up by the Heavenly Principles that watches events across Teyvat and the conduct of the Four Shades. When it detects something that requires action, it issues a directive that the relevant Shade feels directly: Rhinedottir describes one "echoing" in her mind, ordering her to use the Authority of Life to undo Ronova's killings. After Ronova broke the rules, Celestia reclaimed her Authority of Death.
### LARGE +
- Thresholds: Celestia does not react to everything. A monster that appeared in Snezhnaya was "not on Celestia's radar"; Naberius says such a case would simply be classed as spontaneous monster evolution. The Tsaritsa's use of six Gnoses to slow time in her hall was, in Naberius's words, a "rebellion" that Celestia cannot judge and that falls far short of the threshold for casting down a nail of retribution.
- The loophole: Rhinedottir and Ronova had agreed on a "window of resurrection" to slip past the monitoring system. The Tsaritsa slowed time around Rhinedottir so the window passed, Celestia detected the missed resurrection, and Ronova's Authority was reclaimed. Rhinedottir remarks that Celestia "gravely underestimated" the Tsaritsa's determination to rebel.
- Aftermath: Rhinedottir says that because she and Naberius could be considered accomplices, Celestia is watching them closely, and she would rather not lose the Authority of Life too. Asmoday's Authority of Space has not been rescinded despite her defection.

## appearance
### LARGE
Seen from below, Celestia is a cluster of landmasses rather than a single island: one large central rock surrounded by seven or eight smaller "satellite" rocks, linked by a ring-shaped structure and arches. The rocks look torn from the earth, with grass and forest on top and jagged undersides. White, marble-like buildings stand on the central mass, including a tall spire crowned by a huge golden construct resembling an astrolabe. Streams pour from the rock and dissolve into mist as they fall.
### ULTRA +
Up close the white structures show wear, with broken-off pieces on the spires and bridges. The manga "Prologue: Songs of the Wind" shows the inside of the central spire during Vennessa's ascension. Venti, the only Archon to comment on daily life there, jokes that the water in Celestia tastes foul and the fruit is bland, so there is no cider, and he would not go even if invited.

Murals depict Celestia in ancient ruins: one on Tsurumi Island, in a cave below Shirikoro Peak, and a worn one in the Mural Room of the Entombed City that shows Celestia "moored" above the Skyfrost Nail. The base of the Anemo Archon Statue in Mondstadt bears the words "Gateway to Celestia" in Teyvat's common script.

## accounts
### LITE
Celestia is an enigma to Teyvat's people. Most believe chosen mortals ascend there to become gods and watch over the world.
### STANDARD +
- Mondstadt: Dr. Edith says only mortals who perform great, heroic feats can ascend and achieve godhood. Legend says Vennessa ascended from Windrise, where the great oak (the Symbol of Mondstadt's Hero) now grows, and became the Falcon of the West of the Four Winds.
- Liyue: some say adepti ascend to Celestia; others say adepti descended from Celestia and return there when they leave this world. Weapon lore claims the adeptus Guhua ascended instead of dying.
- Inazuma: a scholar's study of Sangonomiya folk belief records that gods defeated in the Archon War were slain "in accordance with the law of the divine realm", and that Orobashi may have gone to his death on heaven's order.
### LARGE +
- Dainsleif on Vennessa: the Falcon of the West "ultimately does naught but hover under the light of the gods", implying that the ascended remain beneath Celestia's gods.
- Venti: in the manga, when the Traveler asks whether those who ascend truly become gods, Venti answers only with myths from "other, distant worlds" about primeval beings whose bodies became the world. He visibly avoids the topic.
- Anastasya (7.1) describes Celestia as holding "absolute power" and treating its rivals without respect; Rhinedottir denies that Celestia recognizes Snezhnaya as a rival at all.

## history
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
- After seizing Teyvat from the dragons and remaking the world, the Heavenly Principles ruled from the heavens through envoys. Item text describes a calm era in which divine envoys (the angels) spoke openly with people, "bringing them the word from the heavens."
- The War of Funerary Flame ended that era. The heavens sent down the Celestial Nails to purge Abyssal corruption, which also devastated the mortal world, and the envoys were exiled and cut off from heaven.
- In the Archon War, the victors claimed the seven divine thrones; scholars in Teyvat widely believe Celestia instigated the war and set its rules.
### ULTRA +
- Long before the Cataclysm, the Third Descender ascended to Celestia to make his bargain with the Heavenly Principles (per Rhinedottir, 7.1).
- More than 500 years ago, before the Cataclysm, Pierro (by his own account in 7.0) turned Khaenri'ahn sky-pulling technology on the heavens, and Paimon descended from Celestia in response.
- The Heavenly Principles have been dormant since the Cataclysm. After hearing Rhinedottir's account (7.1), Anastasya concludes that their part in the Cataclysm triggered the Third Descender's curse, which put them to sleep; Rhinedottir does not dispute it.
- In Lang-Gan, the angel Zibai defied Celestia by delaying the descent of a Celestial Nail so the people could flee.
- After Koitar's rebellion in Hyperborea, according to artifact text, the rebels' ambitions were "brought down from the heavens" together with deep blue crystal spikes, and the angels were cursed.

## status
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Heavenly Principles are still asleep. Celestia's monitoring still works: it reclaimed Ronova's Authority of Death in the Snezhnaya Archon Quest, and Ronova died soon after.
### STANDARD +
Snezhnaya openly prepares to challenge Celestia. Pierro (7.0) says kuuvahki alone cannot break through "Celestia's defenses", and Project Mistilteinn uses "anti-Celestia energy" reverse-engineered from Stellar Linchpins. Pierro says that Snezhnaya achieved its goal the moment Celestia reclaimed Ronova's Authority. In the act's closing scene, the Tsaritsa seems to be looking toward Celestia.
### LARGE +
Before dying, Ronova infused the Eitr into the nail of retribution over the Cryo Dragon's corpse in Snezhnaya and it crumbled; afterwards huge beams of light appeared in the sky above the other Celestial Nails across Teyvat. What this means for Celestia is unresolved as of 7.1 (see the world quest "The Anomaly").

## open_questions

- Whether ascension really makes mortals gods, and what happened to those said to have ascended, such as Vennessa.
- What Paimon was in Celestia before she arrived in Khaenri'ah.
### ULTRA +
- How much of Celestia's activity is the sleeping Heavenly Principles and how much is an automatic system; canon only shows the monitoring cycle, directives and records.
- Whether the glowing Celestial Nails after Ronova's death will provoke a response from Celestia.
- Who besides the Heavenly Principles and the Shades lives there. Venti jokes about its food and water but also doubts that even he could fly that far.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): treat Celestia as a distant, silent presence in the sky that everyone can see but no one visits. Ordinary people speak of it with awe or superstition and know nothing of the monitoring cycle, the Shades' meetings or Paimon's origin; those are secrets known to a handful of gods, Harbingers and the Traveler's party. Do not stage scenes inside Celestia as if canon had described its interior, and do not make the Heavenly Principles speak or act directly as of 7.1.
### ULTRA +
Mortals who invoke it usually say "the heavens" or "the gods". Scholars, priests and adepti may repeat the conflicting ascension legends. Gods and Archons tend to deflect questions about it, as Venti does. A sudden directive or reclaimed Authority is something only the Shades experience; outsiders see only results, such as a Shade weakened or the dead returning.

## entry: Monitoring cycle
@keys: monitoring cycle, Celestia's radar, window of resurrection
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The "monitoring cycle" is the Heavenly Principles' system for watching Teyvat and the Four Shades, revealed in "Black Feathers Scattered at Parting" (7.1). Incidents it detects trigger directives that echo in the minds of the responsible Shade. Minor events (a monster in Snezhnaya, the Tsaritsa's time-slowing trick) stay below its threshold. Rhinedottir and Ronova arranged a "window of resurrection" to bypass it, but the Tsaritsa's manipulation of time made the window lapse, Celestia detected the incident, and it reclaimed Ronova's Authority of Death.

## entry: Divine thrones of Celestia
@keys: divine throne, divine thrones, divine seat, seven thrones
### STANDARD
The Seven Archons each hold one of seven divine thrones (seats) in Celestia, won at the end of the Archon War together with a Gnosis. An Archon's elemental power comes from the throne's Authority, not from a Vision; the Gnosis is the focus that resonates with Celestia. Archons keep their thrones and power even after losing their Gnosis. The Hydro throne was destroyed in Fontaine.

## entry: Ascension to Celestia
@keys: ascend to Celestia, ascended to Celestia, allogene, allogenes
### STANDARD
In Teyvat's folk belief, mortals of great heroic deeds can ascend to Celestia, become gods and watch over their people. Venti tells the Traveler (Ending Note, 1.0) that every Vision holder is an "allogene" with the potential to ascend. Vennessa is said to have ascended at Windrise and become the Falcon of the West; Liyue lore says some adepti ascended, such as Guhua, though others believe adepti came from Celestia. Dainsleif implies that the ascended still live beneath the gods. Canon has never shown an ascension except Vennessa's in the manga, so its true nature is unconfirmed.

## entry: Paimon as a creation of Celestia
@keys: creation of Celestia, anti-Celestia energy
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In the Snezhnaya Archon Quest (7.0, "Mistilteinn"), Ronova calls Paimon a creation of Celestia that has none of its power or authority. Pierro explains that he first used the Fatui's sky-pulling technology in Khaenri'ah over 500 years ago, aiming it at the heavens, and Paimon came down from Celestia in response. She lived in his palace until the Cataclysm; Pierro says her mind on arrival was like a newborn's. Paimon reacted to the Armory Palace's "anti-Celestia energy". She remembers none of this, and what she was in Celestia is unknown.
