## summary
### LITE
The Celestial Nails (also Nails of Retribution) are huge broken pillars, stone outside and blue crystal within, cast down from Celestia by the Heavenly Principles in the ancient past. Legend says they were sent to purify a world poisoned after the Funerary Year; they also wiped out or crippled civilizations such as Sal Vindagnyr, Lang-Gan and Hyperborea. Each reshaped climate and ley lines around it. In 7.1 a dying Ronova fuses the Eitr with the Snezhnaya nail and it begins to crumble, after which the other nails give off strange pillars of light.
### STANDARD +
Most people in Teyvat have never heard of them. Where a nail fell, mortals tend to remember only odd weather, ruins or a withered Irminsul.

## established
### LITE
- Appearance: a broken pillar with a stone surface, blue crystal visible at the break, carved decoration and a Celestia motif at the top.
- Origin: cast down by the Heavenly Principles after the Funerary Year. The Staff of the Scarlet Sands and Amethyst Crown texts say they were meant to mend a wounded world, yet laid waste to the mortal realm.
- Effect: they bend ley lines and local climate. Dragonspine became snowbound, Tsurumi Island fogbound and the Desert of Hadramaveth stormy.
### STANDARD
- Known nails: the Skyfrost Nail on Dragonspine; the Spiritstone in the Chasm (Lang-Gan); one beneath Sumeru's Mt. Damavand and Eternal Oasis; one on Tsurumi Island (not visible in game); one over Natlan's Night Kingdom; and the nail in Snezhnaya at Hyperborea's site. The 7.1 sky-beams mark these six.
- Impact leaves a crater: the pit around the Spiritstone and the depression in Mt. Damavand are examples. Four sites also have a withered Irminsul tree nearby (Dragonspine, the Chasm, Tsurumi Island, Mt. Damavand).
- Purification: Nahida says a nail can purify and stabilize powers that should not exist in Teyvat, such as forbidden knowledge. In the Chasm, Lumenstone and Lumenspar that appeared after a nail fragment was found repel Abyssal Dark Mud, and the Spiritstone's domain wipes out Abyssal power.
- Use as punishment: they were employed as weapons of retribution even before the War of Funerary Flame and the Abyss's arrival. Canon of the Three Moons says the Heavenly Principles cast them down to punish civilizations. In 7.1 Naberius says the Tsaritsa's "rebellion" falls far short of the threshold for casting down a nail of retribution, implying such a threshold exists.
### LARGE +
- Material robustness: no nail is shown being destroyed before Ronova fused the Eitr with the Snezhnaya nail and it began to crumble.
- Nail fragments can move: the Skyfrost Nail split into three pieces after landing, and the Spiritstone had a detached piece (the Wishstone) in the Chasm's mines.
- Nails are named locally rather than uniformly; Liyue people, for example, call the Chasm one the Spiritstone.
### ULTRA +
- The "Nail of Retribution" label is used by the Anomaly quest descriptions, by Naberius and by the 7.1 cinematic text; "Celestial Nail" is the encyclopedic name. Item and book texts use "divine pillar" and "spikes".

## accounts
### STANDARD
- Heavenly view (item texts): the Staff of the Scarlet Sands says the world's barriers were torn and dark poison seeped in, so spikes descended to heal it. Amethyst Crown says the master of the heavens, afraid of rising delusion and breakthroughs, sent a nail to mend the land and laid the mortal realm waste. Both are in-world artefact texts and their tone differs.
- Three Moons view: in The First Sliver of Moonlight, Canon says the ruler of the firmament feared life that sought power and cast down nails to punish civilizations; she tried to help the punished escape, but too many lives were lost. Columbina notes the Frostmoon Scions' ancestors were among those she helped. This was before the War of Funerary Flame.
- Albedo's belief (7.1): he had assumed the Skyfrost Nail's purpose ended once it destroyed Sal Vindagnyr, and says the new change shows it is not so simple.
### LARGE +
- Sumeru legend: the first divine pillar buried a green land under sand (Shadow of the Sand King text). King Deshret is said to have found a nail and, with the jinn, made the Eternal Oasis as a resting place for Nabu Malikata; Paimon's guiding notes say the oasis is built upon a divine nail.
- Tsurumi Island: the Thunderbird is told that strange objects fell from the heavens and one landed on the island, after which storms cleared and fog began. Sumida guesses it may have caused a ley line disorder that mixed with the Thunderbird's power; this is speculation.
### ULTRA +
- Liyue: the Chasm is said to have come from a nail falling on Lang-Gan, whose guiding Angel Zibai delayed the descent so the people could flee to Chenyu Vale. A different artifact text blames the Solar Chariot's fall.

## entry: Skyfrost Nail
@keys: Skyfrost Nail, Peak of Vindagnyr
@version: 1.2
### STANDARD
The Skyfrost Nail fell over the summit of the mountain of Sal Vindagnyr (now Dragonspine), turning a green land into a blizzard. It split into three pieces, one of which fell on the Irminsul there (now the Frostbearing Tree). The priest Varuch died trying to reach the summit; the princess died trying to graft the tree. In the quest In the Mountains the Traveler thaws the shards with Scarlet Quartz; they rejoin the main nail, which rises to hover above the peak. In 7.1 it changes in the same way as the other nails, and Albedo stays to observe its effect on the surrounding ecosystem.
### LARGE +
Ancient Rime blocked the summit paths until the quest, and a Mondstadt aristocratic expedition about a thousand years ago never returned, sabotaged by Eberhart according to the A Land Entombed quest.

## entry: Spiritstone and Lang-Gan
@keys: Spiritstone, Lang-Gan, Wishstone
@version: 2.6
### STANDARD
In Liyue the Chasm's nail is known as the Spiritstone. Quests state Lang-Gan was destroyed by a nail. Miners found a fragment (the Wishstone) in the Stony Halls that gave hallucinations of wishes, and Lumenstone began to spread as Dark Mud appeared. In The Chasm Delvers the Traveler purifies the mechanisms feeding it and Yelan brings the nail down on the Ruin Serpent Haftvad; it then floats back lower than before. Later quests show its domain annihilates Abyssal power but doomed the Millelith soldiers and the yaksha Bosacius who sealed themselves inside.

## entry: Hyperborea Nail and Ronova
@keys: Nail of Retribution, Hyperborea nail
@keys2: Ronova, Eitr, Holy Soil
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Hyperborea, a golden city of the Unified Civilization, was destroyed by a nail because of the Cryo Dragon, whose latent power spread Holy Soil. The city fell into the Shadow Realm while the Sovereign's corpse and the nail remained above ground. In the 7.1 Archon Quest ending, a dying, stripped Ronova fused the Eitr with this nail, intending to spread the sensation of death and Holy Soil; the nail began to crumble as she disintegrated. Afterwards the remaining nails emitted beams of light, and the World Quest chain The Anomaly follows.

## open_questions
### LITE
Canon does not say whether the nails are meant to remain in place, why they now glow, or what the Eitr will do after the Snezhnaya nail's collapse.
### STANDARD +
Canon has not explained how many nails exist, whether Fontaine was ever struck, or whether Celestia can still recall them. The reading that Fontaine was never struck is a wiki observation from the absence of an Anomaly quest, not canon.
### LARGE +
It is unresolved whether the nails' purifying power and their destructive power are the same function seen from two sides, as the Heavenly Principles' own texts imply.

## connections
### STANDARD
Celestia and the Heavenly Principles cast them. The Abyss and forbidden knowledge are what they purify. Irminsul trees withered where nails fell. Ronova and the Eitr broke the Snezhnaya nail in 7.1. Dragonspine, the Chasm, Mt. Damavand, Tsurumi Island, the Night Kingdom and Hyperborea are the sites.
