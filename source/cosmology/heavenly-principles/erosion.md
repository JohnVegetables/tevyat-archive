## summary
### LITE
Erosion, which some call "the curse of time", is the slow wearing-away that time works on long-lived beings. It is best known for eating away memory, but canon also describes it wearing down the body, the will and the spirit or soul. Gods, elemental dragons, adepti and the cursed, deathless Khaenri'ahns all suffer it. It does not strike everyone equally. The more powerful the being, the more dangerous its erosion becomes. Azhdaha's slide into madness, and the fear of erosion that drove the Raiden Shogun Ei toward "eternity", are the classic examples.
### STANDARD +
The Chinese and Japanese terms literally mean "abrasion" or "wear". The idea is grinding down, not sudden corruption. No one in canon has found a way to stop it: Morax's attempt to halt Azhdaha's erosion failed, and Ei could only try to wall herself off from it.

## established
### LITE
- Erosion affects memory first and most visibly: beings forget faces, promises and even their own past decisions.
- It also wears down the body, the will and the spirit, and can turn a gentle being savage.
- It is tied to the passage of time; characters treat it as part of the natural order that cannot be stopped.
### STANDARD +
- Kun Jun (Azhdaha's benevolent consciousness) calls erosion "the world's greatest destroyer of memories" and says only memories rooted in powerful emotion survive for long, and even those fade (Zhongli's Story Quest, 1.5).
- Zhongli: "The greater the power, the greater the danger erosion may bring about." He calls sealing away his old friend Azhdaha one form of erosion he has endured himself, and he wonders whether giving up what one loves in order to keep to the right path is the erosion the Heavenly Principles impose on him. The Traveler suspects erosion played a part in his decision to step down as Geo Archon, and Zhongli does not deny it.
- The Raiden Shogun (the puppet) says that "as long as the Ley Lines flow, the relentless erosion of time will never stop" (2.5).
- Dainsleif: the curse of "immortality" denies death, yet body and soul keep being eroded until they are virtually nonexistent, "even if 'death' is not the form that this erosion takes" (2.6).
### LARGE +
- Erosion is not the same as aging. Ei's puppet does not age, and Dainsleif's ring keeps resetting his body, but the mind can still erode. The Shogun was built to withstand the body's wear, while Ei hid her consciousness away to protect it.
- Will can resist it for a long time. Dainsleif called the Black Serpent Knights' will, surviving 500 years of erosion, "a miracle born from hopelessness."
- Erosion can be invoked as an accusation. The Shogun tested whether Ei's changed outlook was real growth or "irreversible erosion" (Farewell to the Past, 2.5).

### ULTRA +
- Translation note: the Chinese term is 磨损 (mósǔn) and the Japanese 摩耗 (mamou), both meaning abrasion or wear. In Zhongli's line, the English "Heavenly Principles" replaced an earlier "natural order", which had echoed Kun Jun's "it is the natural order, an unstoppable force". The wiki notes this revision broke the visible link between the two remarks.
- Memory and the Ley Lines: Haden describes Ley Lines as "the collection of memories concerning a given place". Re-weaving Khaenri'ah's Ley Lines would need almost all of the old memories, and after 500 years most Khaenri'ahn memories have faded. Erosion is therefore what made Dainsleif's comparatively intact memories so valuable to the Abyss Order (5.7).

## kinds
### LARGE
Canon and the wiki's reading of it distinguish four overlapping kinds. Mental erosion is loss of memory and growing confusion; Azhdaha forgot Morax's face, Liyue Harbor and the reason he had accepted his seal. Physical erosion is decay of the body; Ei's puppet exists to withstand it. Erosion of the will weakens resolve until a being abandons what it once pursued; Ei claims her will cannot be eroded. Erosion of the spirit is the loss of one's sense of self, ending in savagery or madness; Yae Miko says the spirit is susceptible to it, which is why Ei placed her consciousness in her sword. A single being can suffer several kinds at once: Azhdaha's boss description says he has been disfigured by "the various kinds of erosion" during his long imprisonment.

## accounts
### STANDARD
- Azhdaha, speaking through Kun Jun: Morax shared some of his power to slow the erosion, but it was futile, because "everything returns to dust" by the natural order.
- Zhongli: erosion is a burden of the long-lived and powerful; he hints it is imposed by the Heavenly Principles.
- Ei and the Shogun: erosion is the enemy of eternity; everything the Shogun does is "for the sake of resisting erosion."
- Yae Miko (voice-over "About Us: Erosion"): shutting yourself away from the world may only accelerate erosion, because nobody is left to reflect you back at yourself. Companions should "shine a light for each other." This directly questions Ei's method of isolation.
### LARGE +
- Makoto, Ei's twin and the previous Electro Archon, understood erosion as well as Ei did but chose to focus on the present: knowing a scene is fleeting is a reason to enjoy it more. Ei, by contrast, felt "the agony of erosion" on the day the dying Makoto passed her the blade and she became the true Raiden Shogun. Both details come from the same Raiden Shogun character story (Character Story 4).
- Dainsleif and Haden (Khaenri'ahns): erosion is the price of the curse of immortality, which spares them death but not decay.

## azhdaha
@version: 1.5
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
Azhdaha is the clearest case of erosion. The ancient Earth Dragon (a vishap) was befriended by Morax, given sight and allowed to live above ground, and helped humanity. Erosion slowly ground away his memories of his friend and of protecting Liyue Harbor. When miners disturbed the Ley Lines of the Chasm, his pain drove him to attack the people he had protected, and Morax and the adepti sealed him. In a last moment of clarity he agreed to be sealed, but erosion later took that memory too, so he came to believe Morax had betrayed him.

## ei_and_eternity
@version: 2.5
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Ei's pursuit of "eternity" was in large part a defence against erosion. Fearing that her spirit would erode, she created the Shogun, a puppet meant to withstand wear, to rule Inazuma in her place. She then withdrew into the Plane of Euthymia, her inner world, to meditate undisturbed with her consciousness housed in her sword. The Shogun's voice-over says her body exists "to withstand wear and tear, that the one within might achieve Eternity."
Her first puppet, later known as Kabukimono and eventually the Wanderer, was an experiment to see whether erosion could be escaped; she judged him too fragile when he shed tears. Ei made the Shogun's rules hard to change, even for herself, in case she herself became an enemy of eternity. In the Raiden Shogun's Story Quest (2.5) the Shogun turned this against her, suspecting that Ei's new openness was erosion. Ei won their duel inside Makoto's consciousness. The Shogun then accepted that her present will was "not the product of erosion", though it noted that "a will that cannot be eroded has no way to embrace the future", and agreed to become her kagemusha.

## khaenriahns
@version: 5.7
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
After the Cataclysm 500 years ago, Khaenri'ahns were cursed with deathless "immortality". Dainsleif names Ronova as the one who inflicted this Curse of Immortality on him (Black Feathers Scattered at Parting, 7.1). It does not protect them from erosion. Many became hilichurls or Abyss monsters, and the Black Serpent Knights became Shadowy Husks, though some, like Halfdan, held on to their captain's face and their duty for centuries. In A Space and Time Without You (5.7), Haden says that his memories, spirit, emotions and personality have long been eroded and fragmented. Dainsleif's body is kept unchanged by a ring left to him by his elder brother Vedrfolnir, which keeps resetting its condition, but Haden says Dainsleif's memories are still subject to erosion. Even so, Dainsleif remembers more than any other surviving Khaenri'ahn, which is why the Abyss Order wanted his memories for the Loom of Fate.

## curse_of_time
@version: 6.3
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In The Realm of Rocks, Act II of the Lantern Rite story A Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon (6.3), Zizhi, the "Second Deadly Self" of the White Horse Adeptus Zibai, speaks of "the curse of time" and "the memories this body holds". Xiao guesses she means erosion. Madame Ping recalls that ancient mountain clans regarded the White Horse Adeptus as a "daughter of Time". She remarks that time does not treat everyone equally: the longer one dwells in the world, the more it takes. The story first ran as a limited-time event and can now be replayed as a Story Quest. In it, Zizhi says the one who governs Time (Istaroth) froze the moment of Zibai's death, and that this power has been both a blessing and a prison to her ever since.

## open_questions
### STANDARD
- Whether the Heavenly Principles impose erosion deliberately, or whether it is simply the natural order: Zhongli only wonders aloud.
- How erosion relates to Istaroth and to time itself (see uncertainties).
### LARGE +
- Whether anything can truly halt it. Morax's power failed for Azhdaha; Ei's isolation was challenged by Yae Miko; Dainsleif's ring preserves only the body.
- Whether beings outside Teyvat's records are affected. The wiki suggests erosion may touch only those recorded in the Irminsul, but no character says so.

### ULTRA +
- Whether the wiki's suggested cases are really erosion. The wiki proposes that Kapatcir, the Great Thunderbird of Tsurumi Island, forgot her true name and later, after Ruu's sacrifice, descended into madness through erosion. It also says the Viridescent Venerer "underwent erosion": her artifact text (Labyrinth Wayfarer) says she forgot "time itself" in her endless hunt and was drenched in black blood until Roland slew her as a monster. Neither text names erosion.
- Whether the "Rift of Erosion" in the Fortress of Meropide has anything to do with this concept. It is only a guarded point of interest described as a potential danger; nothing ties it to time's wear.

## connections
### STANDARD
Related concepts: the Heavenly Principles, the Ley Lines and Irminsul, Istaroth and time, the curse of the Khaenri'ahns and immortality. Key figures: Azhdaha, Zhongli, Ei and the Shogun, Yae Miko, Dainsleif, Haden, Zibai/Zizhi.
### LARGE +
Not to be confused with Abyssal corrosion or Gosoythoth's English title "Dragon of Manifest Erosion" (Natlan). Those concern the Abyss's contamination, not time's wear. Nor is it the same as Holy Soil, which is literally soil erosion around Watatsumi.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): erosion is slow and personal. Show it as a long-lived being losing the names of old friends, repeating grudges whose reasons are gone, or growing colder or harsher across centuries. It is not a visible magical effect. Mortal humans do not talk about "erosion" in these terms; it is a concern of gods, adepti, dragons and the cursed Khaenri'ahns, and most ordinary people would not know the word in this sense.
### LARGE +
Long-lived characters handle it differently: Zhongli with weary acceptance, Ei once with fear and isolation and now with resolve, Dainsleif with grim endurance. Do not invent a cure, a precise timescale or a fixed age at which it begins; canon says it varies. Do not present the wiki's links to Istaroth or the Irminsul as established fact.

## entry: Azhdaha's erosion
@keys: Azhdaha, Kun Jun
@keys2: erosion, eroded, forgot, memories
@version: 1.5
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Azhdaha, the ancient Earth Dragon of the Chasm, was Morax's friend and a protector of humans until erosion wore away his memories and reason. Morax shared his own power to slow it, without success. After miners damaged the Chasm's Ley Lines and Azhdaha ran amok, Morax and the adepti sealed him. In a last lucid moment he consented to the seal, but erosion stole that memory too, and he came to see the seal as betrayal. His benevolent consciousness, appearing as Kun Jun, told the story in Zhongli's Story Quest (1.5).

## entry: Ei, the Shogun and erosion
@keys: Ei, Raiden Shogun, Shogun puppet, Plane of Euthymia
@keys2: erosion, eroded, eternity
@version: 2.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Ei feared that her spirit would erode. She built the Shogun puppet to rule in her stead and to withstand bodily wear, and she meditated in the Plane of Euthymia with her consciousness in her sword. In 2.5 the Shogun suspected that Ei's change of heart was erosion. Ei proved in a duel that it was not, and the Shogun became her kagemusha. Yae Miko warns Ei that isolating herself may only speed erosion up.

Haden admits his memories, spirit, emotions and personality are eroded and fragmented (5.7). Dainsleif's brother's ring resets his body, but his memories still erode, though he remembers more than any other surviving Khaenri'ahn.
