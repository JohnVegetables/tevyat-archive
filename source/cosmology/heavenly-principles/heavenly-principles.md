## summary
### LITE
The Heavenly Principles is the god who rules Teyvat from Celestia: the First Descender, a being from the sea of stars beyond the false sky. It conquered the dragon Sovereigns, remade the world, created humanity, and governs through "laws" enforced by its four Shades, the Archons' thrones and the Gnoses. Before Sun and Moon calls it the Primordial One and suggests the name Phanes. It has slept since the Cataclysm 500 years ago, but Celestia still acts in its name.
### STANDARD +
Ordinary people know only a distant heaven. Dragons and rebels call it a tyrant or usurper, and the Tsaritsa's Fatui, the Abyss Order and the Traveler's sibling all work toward opposing it.

## established
### LITE
It made four Shades, copies of itself: Naberius (Life), Ronova (Death), Istaroth (Time), Asmoday (Space). It took the Sovereigns' Authorities, which became the Archons' Authorities, and it enclosed Teyvat under the false sky. About 6,000 years ago it defeated the dragon king Nibelung in the War of Funerary Flame but was badly wounded.
### STANDARD +
Celestial nails punish lands that defy or test its order. It distrusts forbidden knowledge, the Abyss and beings who seek power beyond their station. It edits the world's records through Irminsul. Descenders stand outside its laws, and Irminsul does not record them.
### LARGE +
- It used Phlogiston, Teyvat's primordial energy, as the basis for modern elemental energy, split like light into seven colours, to develop a power better suited to countering the Abyss (Lord of the Night, Natlan Archon Quest, 5.1).
- Apep says Irminsul existed before the Heavenly Principles arrived. The Heavenly Principles "usurped and refashioned" it to serve humanity and to anchor the world's fate.
- Its curses act on fate itself. In 7.1, Ronova's curse of immortality on Khaenri'ahns outlasted the loss of her Authority because the Heavenly Principles had authorised it. Her curse of death on Snezhnaya ended with her Authority because the Heavenly Principles never sanctioned it.

## nature
### LITE
It is said to be winged, crowned and androgynous, of "gender unknowable". It is absolutist: it loves humanity on its own terms and strikes hard at threats to its order.
### LARGE +
The moon goddess Canon (Frost Moon) said it held an "almost paranoid hostility" toward lifeforms seeking power, or perhaps feared them. Rhinedottir says it has "a deep love for humanity", so killing humans without its approval is a grave sin. The Frostmoon Scions' old hymn calls it "Father of All and Mother of the Gods" and says no one dares speak its name. Neuvillette sneers that the world is ruled under "a cluster of filthy feathers", and he and Jakob both call it a pheasant. Rhinedottir says it had "zero tolerance" for uncertain variables. Yet it forbids its servants to kill the Traveler outright, and Ronova admits she does not know what it values in Descenders.

## history
@spoiler: major
### LITE
It conquered the dragons, rebuilt the world and created humans, who lived in a unified civilisation under the heavens. After winning the War of Funerary Flame it cast down the celestial nails and set up the Gnoses and seven thrones. It slept after the Cataclysm.
### STANDARD +
Before Sun and Moon dates the conquest to forty years and the creation of life and humans, with the Shade Naberius, to four hundred years after it. Humans made a covenant with it, and their one taboo was temptation. In the war it shattered the Eternal and Iridescent Moons and flung the Frost Moon beyond the sky. Its nails devastated Sal Vindagnyr, Hyperborea and Lang-Gan, and it banned the history before the war.
### LARGE +
- Arrival: the moon goddesses, who kept Teyvat running for Nibelung, at first lived alongside it "like a guest". They slowly realised it meant to dominate the world. Aria and Canon waited to judge its order, while Sonnet opposed it from the start (The First Sliver of Moonlight, 6.3).
- Wounds: Neuvillette's Vision text says the war "ruined" its functions, so it could no longer suppress the world's original order alone.
- Archon War (about 6,000 to 2,000 years ago): gods fought over the seven thrones of its new order.
- Fontaine: Egeria turned Oceanids into humans without its permission. Its judgement became the Prophecy of Fontaine (4.2).
- Cataclysm: it sent The Seven (except Rukkhadevata) and the Shades to Khaenri'ah. On its judgement, Ronova cursed pure-blooded Khaenri'ahns with immortality. The wiki's reading of the 6.3 Archon Quest says it also hid the twins' spaceship on the Frost Moon, beyond the false sky.
### ULTRA +
- Arrival detail: by the wiki's summary of the Nod-Krai world quests, it first landed on the Frost Moon, destroyed the dragons' Moongazer laboratory, and forced the dragon Lusalim to hand over cloaking ("Blackbody") research that later became the false sky.
- Natlan: Natlan's dragons say the "heavenly reaver" saw the Phlogiston-rich soil as a Holy Soil phenomenon and cast nails to destroy it, and that the "reaver from beyond the skies" froze Xiuhcoatl in lava.
- Angels: after Koitar, the first angel, joined the voyager Anacharsis and Hyperborea in rebelling, the angels were cursed into beasts or Seelie.
- Enkanomiya: when that land fell into the depths during the Funerary Year, its people's prayers went unanswered by the Primordial One and "its three other shining shades" (only Istaroth answered). Later a ban "laid down" by the Primordial One kept them from returning to the surface. Enkanomiya records tie the serpent god Orobashi's doomed war on Inazuma to this ban; the wiki summarises it as a forced sacrifice in exchange for the people's return.

## saarelainens_bargain
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Revealed in 7.1: the Heavenly Principles erased Anastasya's existence for opening a gate to the Abyss. The Third Descender, Saarelainen, then traded his body to become the seven Gnoses. In return it restored her and promised never to harm his descendants, the Khaenri'ahns. Its price was that it wiped him from all memory through Irminsul. He bound the promise with a curse that would strike if it was broken, and Anastasya concludes that this curse is why it has slept since the Cataclysm.
### LARGE +
The Belyi Tsar had incited Anastasya and Saarelainen to open the gate, and Saarelainen made the deal in person in Celestia. Anastasya concluded that harming Khaenri'ah in the Cataclysm triggered the curse, and Rhinedottir did not dispute it. Neuvillette's Vision text tells it from the other side: the wounded "usurper" and "one who came after" made the Gnoses together to keep the world's resentment under control.

## status
@version: 7.1
### LITE
As of 7.1 it is still asleep. Celestia's monitoring cycle keeps watching Teyvat and the Shades, and in 7.1 it reclaimed Ronova's Authority of Death.
### STANDARD +
The Tsaritsa holds six Gnoses and is preparing to face it. The Traveler's sibling says it will certainly wake.
### LARGE +
Pierro's Project Mistilteinn is a weapon meant for that confrontation. In 7.1 Pulcinella explains that a notebook was sealed away for fear that Snezhnaya might become a "Second Khaenri'ah" by drawing the attention of the Heavenly Principles and its sustainer. Earlier upheavals did not wake it: the end of the Hydro throne (4.2) and Nahida burning Irminsul (6.6). In Sumeru, Nahida had wondered whether destroying a Gnosis might wake it. See the separate entry on its dormancy.

## accounts
### LARGE
- Moon goddesses (Aria, Canon, Sonnet; 6.3): a guest who turned conqueror, whose rules brought order at too high a price.
- Dragons (Neuvillette, Apep): "First Usurper", "Usurper-King", "usurper from beyond the heavens". They say its stolen dragon power became the Archons' Authorities (Neuvillette, 4.1). Apep says dragons once believed only forbidden knowledge could beat it.
- Enkanomiya historians (Before Sun and Moon): the reverently named Primordial One, true lord and creator.
- Frostmoon Scions (Hymns of the Far North): Pitkamoonen, Lord of the Seven Calamities, a god to be placated, not invoked.
- Celestia's servants: Ronova was titled its Arbiter, and Asmoday called herself the Sustainer of the Heavenly Principles.
- Nicole (6.2): the moon goddesses could not challenge it, but it tolerated them because they kept the planet alive. It may never have known that Nibelung had imprisoned them, and so treated the moons' weaponisation as a breach of peace.
- Furina (4.2): its will "will have its way". Pierro calls every divine rule, even a god's love for humanity, a curse.
### ULTRA +
Other names: Dottore calls it "Reason", and Ronova's enemy title names it "Paralogism". Earlier English text sometimes translated it as "destiny", "order", "heaven" or "the divine". The Teyvat Chapter Storyline Preview and the "We Will Be Reunited" teaser keep those older wordings.

## connections
### STANDARD
Its seat is Celestia. The Four Shades are its parts, and The Seven sit on its thrones. Its works include the Gnoses, Visions (through the Archon system), the false sky, a refashioned Irminsul, the nails and fate. Its foes are Nibelung, the Abyss, Khaenri'ah, the Tsaritsa, the Abyss Order and Apep.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation): keep it offstage. It is asleep, has no present-day voice, and has never been shown speaking. Show it through Celestia's directives, the Shades, the nails and people's reluctance to name it. Commoners know "Celestia" and "the gods", not this history.
### LARGE +
Do not reduce it to pure evil or pure good. Canon shows both its "deep love" for humanity and its brutality toward civilisations that test its rules.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- The "unknown god" who separated the twins is Asmoday, "Sustainer of the Heavenly Principles", not the Heavenly Principles itself.
- "The Second Who Came" in Before Sun and Moon means Nibelung.
- It is not an Archon, and it is dormant, not dead. Do not give it a fixed gender, a face or dialogue.

## entry: Before Sun and Moon on the Primordial One
@keys: Before Sun and Moon, When the Doves Held Branches, Held Branches
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
Before Sun and Moon, a banned history in The Byakuyakoku Collection, is the fullest in-world account of the Primordial One. When the "eternal throne of the heavens" came, it fought the seven dragon-lords and made four shining Shades of itself. After forty years the Sovereigns fell and the nations submitted. Four hundred years after "the Held Branches", it and one Shade created animals, plants and humans, who made a covenant with it and entered a new age. The author suggests the Primordial One "may have been Phanes", a winged, crowned, androgynous being born from an egg, who used the eggshell to separate the universe from the "microcosm of the world".
### LARGE +
The book records that in the Funerary Year the "second throne" came and war returned, and that after Enkanomiya fell into the depths the Primordial One and three Shades did not hear its prayers. Later a ban from the "first throne" blocked the road home, so the author reasons it must have defeated "the Second Who Came". Possessing the book was forbidden after Orobashi's death.

## entry: The covenant and the Unified Civilization
@keys: unified civilization, sacred covenant, cyclical eternity
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Under the covenant, the heavens answered hunger with food and rain, poverty with riches and sorrow with their voices, and the one taboo was temptation. Angels walked among humans as its envoys. Artifact text (Tiara of Frost, Tiara of Flame) says it also set a hidden rule of "cyclical eternity" under which civilisations flourish and are remade, and that humans who asked the angels about it met only silence.

## entry: Saarelainen's curse on the Heavenly Principles
@keys: Third Descender's curse, Saarelainen's curse
@keys2: Heavenly Principles, Celestia, Phanes
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Saarelainen's curse was the safeguard on his bargain with the Heavenly Principles: it would strike if the promise to spare his descendants, the Khaenri'ahns, was broken. Anastasya concluded in 7.1 that the Cataclysm in Khaenri'ah triggered it, and that this is why the Heavenly Principles sleeps. His body became the seven Gnoses, and the Heavenly Principles erased all memory of him through Irminsul.

## entry: Celestia's monitoring cycle
@keys: monitoring cycle, Arbiter of the Heavenly Principles
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Heavenly Principles set up a "monitoring cycle" through which Celestia watches events in Teyvat and the conduct of the Four Shades, even while the Heavenly Principles sleeps. In 7.1, Ronova and Rhinedottir tried to schedule a resurrection inside a gap in it. The Tsaritsa used her Cryo power and the six Gnoses to slow time in her hall so the window passed, and Celestia reclaimed Ronova's Authority of Death. A directive then "echoed" in Rhinedottir's mind ordering her to undo Ronova's killings. Naberius judged that this act of rebellion fell short of what would warrant a nail.

## entry: Epithets of the Heavenly Principles
@keys: All-Plunderer, Usurper-King, Master of Fortuna, Master of the Four Shades, Lord of Hosts, Lord of the Eternal Heavens, King of Light, Great Judge, Winged One, Sovereign of the Firmament
### LARGE
The Heavenly Principles has many titles in different sources: Usurper-King and First Usurper (Neuvillette), All-Plunderer (the quest Sealed Site of Sacrifice), Master of Fortuna and Master of the Four Shades (artifact texts), Lord of Hosts and Lord of the Eternal Heavens (moon-related artifact texts), Great Judge (a noble's notes) and King of Light (General History of Snezhnaya), Winged One (Scroll of Streaming Song and artifact text), Sovereign of the Firmament (the quest For a Green Island...) and great ruler of the firmament (the moon goddesses). Pitkamoonen, Lord of the Seven Calamities and Lord of the Sky are Frostmoon Scion names. Its Chinese name 天理 means "heaven's law" or natural order.
