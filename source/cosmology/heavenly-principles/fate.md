## summary
### LITE
Fate is the predetermined destiny of Teyvat, imposed by the Heavenly Principles through Irminsul and the stars. Canon also calls it Heimarmene, and in Fontaine and Remuria it was called Fortuna. Vision bearers are tied to named constellations. Descenders are not bound in the same way.
### STANDARD +
Fate is not a god who acts. It is closer to a written script that the world was refashioned to follow. Characters speak of it as something to accept, hide from or break. Hyperborea's Saint rose against it, Remuria and Sumeru built systems meant to stand apart from it, and Durin is the one fate shown on screen being rewritten on purpose.

## established
### LITE
- The Heavenly Principles repurposed Irminsul into the world's anchor of fate. Apep says they usurped and remade the tree and used it to anchor the fate of the world (Song of the Welkin Moon Act IX, 6.6).
- Stars record fate: a Vision bearer's fate is tied to a constellation made of Stella Fortuna.
### STANDARD +
- Nahida says the prophecy of Fontaine was carved into Irminsul long ago, and that Focalors was smart enough to turn the fate of the world around anyway (voice-over about Furina).
- Barbeloth tells the Traveler that a Descender's fate is not anchored in Teyvat. Mona cannot read the Traveler or Paimon in the stars either.
### LARGE +
- Names: Fate, Heimarmene (the Belyi Tsar's notes, the Narzissenkreuz Ordo) and Fortuna (Fontaine, Remuria). Item text also gives wheel of fate, Musica Mundana and Sourcesong.
- In the Chamber of Deliberation records, one of the Thirteen Sovereign Lords (CL-03) abstains from a vote, saying the fate of "these soulless creatures bound to star signs" does not concern them.
- Mona, who reads the reflection of the stars in water, says fate cannot be changed or reversed, only accepted. Astrologers avoid reading their own fate because it can turn fate in on itself, and advising a subject risks tangling the reader's fate with theirs.
- The Narzissenkreuz Ordo taught that to receive a Vision is to sell oneself to the world's bound fate, Heimarmene (their Note #2 in Fontaine).
- Citlali, a Natlan shaman, says the heavens control human fate like a puppeteer and a marionette, so the inauspicious stars have no free will. She seals the power of inauspicious stars into dolls bound with animal bones, and in a birthday letter says her Tzitzimitl Citlalin was made by binding her own Stella Fortuna.
- The Little Witch and the Undying Fire calls the stars of Teyvat the heavenly strings that bind humans, and says other cosmic forces are weakened by a giant sky-shroud, which is the false sky.
- In The Solution of Fate (6.2) Albedo, recalling what Barbeloth told them, says the fate of every living being in Teyvat is woven into the fabric of time, so its most natural path is to proceed along its given track.
- In 7.0, notes at the Pale Crown Palace record that Hyperborea's destruction had been decreed long before Anacharsis, and that the Belyi Tsar feared his conclusion about Heimarmene too terrible to write down.

## names
### LARGE
- Heimarmene: the name used in the Belyi Tsar's notes and by the Narzissenkreuz Ordo. It comes from a Greek word meaning what has been allotted.
- Fortuna: the Fontainian and Remurian word, linked to the harp-string image. It is also the name of an imperial ship in The History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria.
- In the Lightless Silk String item text, ancient Fontainians thought Fortuna ruled the world and was woven from countless fibres like harp strings. Music in harmony would bring happiness to all, and discord would tear the fabric of the universe.
- Remus's Grand Symphony, which Phobos was meant to carry out, is sometimes called Fortuna, and Remus is called the king of Fortuna in one weapon text.
### ULTRA +
- A separate, older order's books, found by Rene after the Cataclysm, use Fortuna for a cycle in which civilisations rise and fall. The Narzissenkreuz Ordo later judged this to rest on myth and assumption, not on proof. Do not treat it as the same thing as Heimarmene without saying so.

## pale_crown_notes
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The notes at the Pale Crown Palace are the Belyi Tsar's private record of his expeditions into the Shadow Realm and the ruins of Hyperborea. They are the longest canon account of how Fate works, and they are also one person's conclusions.
- Note II: he judges that Hyperborea fell to a Celestial Nail sent from above, and he doubts that help from the angels would have changed the outcome.
- Note IV: the records beside the Eitr show that Hyperborea's fate was sealed before The Saint (Anacharsis) arrived. Even the eldest daughter of the angels (Koitar) could not alter the decree, and those who stayed faithful to the future could only stave off its execution for thirty years. He states that his conclusion about Heimarmene may be too terrible to write. He argues that when people anchor themselves to fate, the Abyss will anchor itself to them at the end of time.
- He also writes that the Goddess of Flowers in Sumeru and the woman who prophesied the rising waters in Fontaine each tried to build systems independent of both the Ley Lines and Heimarmene, and that even small rents can loosen the threads of fate. He cites the Natlan ruler who is not a divinely anointed Sacred Lord and the Remurians who evaded the gaze of the heavens.
### ULTRA +
- Note III: he links the Shadow Realm to The Saint's memories left in the void in defiance of the fate the heavens ordained for him, and wonders whether the realm shields Teyvat from the Abyss as an unintended effect. This is stated as his hypothesis.
- Note VI: he warns that the Abyss seeps in so fast that everything within the celestial shell, meaning the false sky, may end within a few centuries.

## attempts_to_escape
### STANDARD
Several civilisations tried to build something that would not answer to Fate. None of them ended well, but each left a mark, according to the Belyi Tsar.
### LARGE +
- Hyperborea: The Saint (Anacharsis) launched an advance against the fate that ruled the world and was punished. Those faithful to the future delayed the decreed end by thirty years, but even Koitar could not alter it.
- Remuria: Remus and Sybilla made Phobos, the Grand Symphony, to avert the prophecy of Fontaine; the Tsar's Note IV says she who prophesied the rising waters sought a system independent of the Ley Lines and Heimarmene. Remurian souls were bound to Phobos and given golem bodies. It failed to save Remuria, but the Tsar writes that the Remurians still eluded the gaze of the high heavens and passed on their arts in distorted form. The wiki links those arts to the Golden Troupe, the Marechaussee Phantom and the Narzissenkreuz Ordo.
- Sumeru: King Deshret's Aaru was meant to be a new afterlife outside the gods' reach, built with celestial technology from the angel Nabu Malikata. The Tsar's Note IV says the Goddess of Flowers sought to build a system independent of the Ley Lines and Heimarmene.
### ULTRA +
- Fontaine, Focalors: to avoid the prophecy carved into Irminsul, Focalors split her divinity from her human half Furina and let the Heavenly Principles and the people believe the Archon's role was filled, while she gathered the power to end the seat of the Hydro Archon. The wiki summarises this as deceiving Fate itself; the scheme is canon, the phrasing is the wiki's.
- Natlan: after Xiuhcoatl died, Ronova showed Xbalanque the arc of fate (A Thousand Blazing Suns). In the 5.3 finale a Voice in the Void tells Mavuika to face her fate and that her destruction is inevitable; she answers by asking why, if she was fated to fail, it fears her.

## durin
@version: 6.2
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
In the Draco Rubedo Chapter the Hexenzirkel's Andersdotter sends a story-born Durin into Teyvat. Albedo explains that the book Durin's story had been grafted onto the fate of the historical Durin, so that one Durin's fate overwrote the other. In The Solution of Fate the Traveler's party faces a trial in the form of "Fate", which says it cannot die and has been decreed by fate. Durin refuses it. Barbeloth afterwards finds that this Durin's fate no longer appears among the stars. Anya says only he can defy the fate of this world. Irminsul at first rejected him; the Hexenzirkel and the Traveler arranged for the Ley Lines to accept him as a separate being.
### ULTRA +
This is the only on-screen case of a fate rewritten on purpose. Albedo stresses that a being's story and its fate are fundamentally different things. Alice had wanted to keep Durin inside the storybook, where her magic could undo fate's curse, but Anya chose to give him ink so he could write his own story in Teyvat.

## irminsul_and_aaru
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In Song of the Welkin Moon Act X (6.6), Nahida and her allies burn the corrupted Irminsul with the Pyro Gnosis, to stop Dottore's experiment. The three Shades of the Heavenly Principles present, Ronova, Istaroth and Naberius, ask whether she is sure, and Nahida answers that humanity's survival matters more than the tree; they let her proceed. The reconfigured Aaru then takes over the role of storing and collecting information, with no lasting harm to Nahida or Nicole.
### ULTRA +
The wiki says that Irminsul's replacement means Celestia can no longer steer Fate as easily. No quest states this directly, and it is recorded as an inference. As of 7.1 no source shows what became of the fate anchored in the old tree, such as the prophecy of Fontaine, which had already been fulfilled or turned away.

## accounts
### STANDARD
- As a script: Mona speaks of fate as fixed and best accepted.
- As a lie: in 7.1 the Tsaritsa (Anastasya) says civilisation is a lie told to us by fate, and the Shades of the Heavenly Principles (Life, Death, Time, Space) are everywhere (Light and Shadow, Intertwined). The Traveler asks whether her authority is rebellion against fate.
### LARGE +
- As something that can be bent: Durin, the Remurians and the Abyss Order treat it as something to loosen, rewrite or weave anew. The Abyss Order's device is called the Loom of Fate, built to weave Ley Lines.
- As a puppeteer's strings: Citlali and the Little Witch text use the image of strings that bind people from the heavens above.
### ULTRA +
- As a risk: Nicole says the fate of Teyvat has been shaken more and more of late (A Window Into the World), and Barbeloth declines to intervene in a prophecy because doing so would only disrupt the path of fate (Revelations by Chance, 6.7).
- As something some can glimpse: the wiki records that Vedrfolnir, one of the Five Sinners of Khaenri'ah, foresaw the kingdom's fall and his own imprisonment, and that augury birds among the Snowland Fae can glimpse their own fate.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- Who or what actually writes each fate, and does any individual choice matter inside the pattern? Canon is not consistent.
- What did Aaru's takeover from Irminsul change for Fate?
- How does Fate relate to the Abyss? The Belyi Tsar's notes say that anchoring into fate draws the Abyss in, but he states this as his own conclusion.
- Why Descenders stand outside Fate, and what that implies for the Traveler.
### ULTRA +
- What Ronova's censored word in Light and Shadow, Intertwined was, and what she meant to suppress.
- Whether major disasters such as the Cataclysm were written in Fate. Canon does not say.

## connections
### STANDARD
The Heavenly Principles and Celestia imposed Fate; Irminsul and the false sky are its instruments. Descenders such as the Traveler stand outside it. Constellations and Stella Fortuna are its visible form, and Visions bind their bearers to it. Remuria, Hyperborea, Sumeru's Aaru, Natlan and Fontaine's prophecy are all stories of struggle with it.
### ULTRA +
The Tsaritsa, the Abyss Order and the Hexenzirkel each relate to Fate in their own way (rebellion, the Loom, and Durin's rewrite). Their goals are separate entries and should not be merged.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): most people in Teyvat do not think of "Fate" as a cosmic system. They say fate, destiny or luck as ordinary words. Only astrologers, scholars of Remuria and Khaenri'ah, Descenders and the very old know that Heimarmene is real and imposed. Do not have common folk lecture about the Heavenly Principles.
### LARGE +
When a character talks about fate, check what they plausibly know. A Mondstadt knight may speak of destiny. A Hexenzirkel witch, Mona, Nahida, Nicole, the Fatui and the Abyss Order can speak of it as a system. Characters should not claim that Fate is broken or destroyed as of 7.1: Irminsul burned and Aaru replaced it, but the stars still stand.

## entry: Heimarmene
@keys: Heimarmene, Fate of Teyvat, predetermined fate
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Heimarmene is the name the Belyi Tsar and the Narzissenkreuz Ordo use for the predetermined fate of Teyvat. They treat it as imposed by the Heavenly Principles, and the Ordo warns that accepting a Vision binds one to it. The Tsar's notes in the Pale Crown Palace (7.0) say its truth was too terrible for him to write down.

## entry: Fortuna
@keys: Fortuna, Grand Symphony, Remus
@keys2: fate, Fontaine, Remuria, harp
@version: 4.2
### STANDARD
Fortuna is the Fontainian word for fate. Ancient Fontainians pictured it as a harp with strings of countless fibres, where harmony brought joy and dissonance tore the world. Remuria, which built its culture around music, used the word for its Grand Symphony and for its prophesied fall, and Remus, the king of Fortuna, made Phobos to escape the prophecy. The sources are the Lightless Silk String item text and The History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria.

## entry: Stella Fortuna and star signs
@keys: Stella Fortuna, star sign, star signs, Vision bearer's constellation
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Stella Fortuna are the stars that make up the constellations, and each Vision bearer's fate is linked to a constellation; astrologers hold that the stars map out a Vision bearer's past, present and future. ## entry: Astrology and hydromancy
@keys: hydromancy, astrologer, astrology, star reading
@keys2: Mona, Barbeloth, fate
### STANDARD
Mona and her teacher Barbeloth read fate in the inverted reflection of the stars on water, which they call hydromancy. Mona says the best water is melted snow, that she will not read her own fate, and that advice tangles a reader's fate with the subject's. She cannot read the Traveler or Paimon.

The six notes of the Belyi Tsar in his mausoleum record his forays into the Shadow Realm and Hyperborea. ## entry: The Loom of Fate
@keys: Loom of Fate
@keys2: Abyss Order, Caribert, Traveler's sibling
@version: 5.7
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Abyss Order uses "Loom of Fate" for an operation and a device. Chlothar first used the phrase for his son Caribert, and later it came to mean a machine that can weave Ley Lines into the planet. The Traveler's sibling is using it, and has used it to weave the Ley Lines of New Khaenri'ah. The name is a metaphor; it does not mean the Abyss Order has taken control of Fate.

## entry: The golden harp of fate
@keys: Etherlight Spindlelute, Golden Strings, titan craftsman
@version: 6.0
@availability: limited
@spoiler: none
### LARGE
Two texts from the limited-time event Clink Clank Clash (6.0) picture fate as music. The Etherlight Spindlelute's story says the titan craftsman of the skies once wove the destinies of all living beings, with a golden harp as the instrument of fate. The Golden Strings material says some in a long-lost civilisation believed the fate governing the world was a song from a colossal harp hung in the heavens, because they had seen angels pluck golden strings from the skies. Both are poetic in-universe accounts, not a description of the mechanism.
