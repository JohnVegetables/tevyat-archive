## summary
### LITE
"Descender" is the term for the rare beings from beyond Teyvat (from the sea of stars) whose wills can rival a whole world. They are not bound by the Heavenly Principles' laws, and Irminsul does not hold their memories. Only four are known: the First, the Heavenly Principles (now dormant); the Second, the dragon king Nibelung (slain); the Third, Saarelainen (killed, his remains made into the seven Gnoses); and the Fourth, the Traveler (alive). The Traveler's twin is not counted as one. Very few people in Teyvat know the term at all.
### STANDARD +
Nahida first learned the term from Fatui intelligence that Dottore gave her in exchange for the Dendro Gnosis (3.2). The moon goddesses, dragons, the Fatui and the Fontainian scholar Rene also use it, each in their own way.

## established
### STANDARD
- **Outside fate's rules.** A Descender is not bound by the laws the Heavenly Principles impose on Teyvat. The Narzissenkreuz called the Traveler a "variable" that had come into the world and one "equal to a world" (4.2).
- **Outside Irminsul.** In 3.3 the Balladeer searched Irminsul for Descenders and found nothing. Nahida concluded that anyone from beyond this world is not counted as part of Teyvat. When Irminsul is rewritten, a Descender's memories stay unchanged. The Traveler alone remembered Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, and later the Balladeer, when everyone else forgot them.
- **Not every outlander qualifies.** Coming from beyond Teyvat is not enough. Rene wrote that the title belongs only to "wills that can rival an entire world". Skirk, who came from another planet, said she is not one, because she is only passing through, while the Traveler is influencing the world's fate (5.7). The Fatui do not count the Traveler's sibling either.
- **The count.** First: the Heavenly Principles. Second: Nibelung. Third: Saarelainen. Fourth: the Traveler.
### LARGE +
- **The First** came from the sea of stars, defeated the Seven Sovereigns and remade the world. Canon, one of the moon sisters, says the sisters fulfilled their duty in the heavens "until the day that the first Descender arrived" (6.3). Nahida had already guessed this in 3.2. *Before Sun and Moon* calls this being the Primordial One and the first "throne of the heavens".
- **The Second** is inferred to be Nibelung, the dragon king and Teyvat's original ruler. He created the three moons to watch over the world and left for the stars, then came back to fight the Heavenly Principles in the war of funerary flame. He was killed, but his enemy was badly wounded.
- **The Third**, Saarelainen, is the source of the seven Gnoses. That the Gnoses are a Descender's remains first came out in Fontaine (4.2). The name Saarelainen comes from the Frostmoon Scions' *Hymns of the Far North* (6.0), which tell of Anastasya's lover; his full story came out in Snezhnaya (7.1).
- **The Fourth** is the Traveler, an outlander who travelled between worlds with their twin. Nahida tells them this in 3.2, and in Nod-Krai the Traveler names themselves a Descender to Lauma.
- **Gods find them hard to handle.** Ronova says a Descender's soul cannot be drawn into the Ley Lines and that, for now, she does not mean to get involved with Descenders (7.0). In 7.1, after killing the Traveler during the curse of death, she says she does not understand what the Heavenly Principles value so much about Descenders, and warns that this tolerance has limits. She points to the Third Descender's remains as the example.
### ULTRA +
- **Divine policy.** In Rhinedottir's account, the Heavenly Principles saw the Third Descender's power, knowledge and final acts as dangerous, and had "zero tolerance" for such uncertain variables. Canon says they wanted to control fate and feared beings who seek power. That explains why Descenders matter to them, though canon never states a full policy.
- **Rival Descenders.** By Lauma's account, the war of funerary flame, in which the three moon goddesses perished, was a war "triggered by the Descenders". Because of this, Paimon broke off before revealing to Lauma where the Traveler comes from. The Traveler then chose to tell her openly.

## accounts
### LARGE
- **Fatui intelligence (3.2):** the Fatui records Dottore traded to Nahida for the Dendro Gnosis count the Traveler as the Fourth Descender and do not classify the sibling as one. Nahida called the Descender intel a very important part of what she received.
- **Rene de Petrichor (Fontaine):** working from Khaenri'ahn, Remurian and Sumeru sources, he concluded that Descenders are wills that can rival a world. He tried to become one so he could save Fontaine from its prophecy, and failed twice. In the end the prophecy was resolved through the Traveler's involvement.
- **Khaenri'ah:** *Perinheri* says the kingdom waited for "beings who could transcend the gods" to come from outside the world, and that none came in its writer's time.
- **Dragons (5.2):** a dragon in the Ashen Realm calls the Heavenly Principles simply "the Descender", the one who brought humans into Teyvat.
- **The moon sisters (6.3):** Canon and Aria say the first Descender was at first a peaceful "guest in our home" who slowly came to want to change and rule the world.
- **Rhinedottir (7.1):** she read Celestia's own records of "the Third Descender incident" and told the Tsaritsa what happened (see the Third Descender entry).
### ULTRA +
- **The Hydro Dragon's view:** Neuvillette's character stories call the Heavenly Principles a "foreign usurper" (A Fontainian Nursery Rhyme). In the Vision story, the usurper was badly wounded in the great war and then made the Gnoses together with "one who came after" (the Third Descender) to keep their order in place.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- Who first set the numbering, and how Nibelung, a native ruler, came to count as a Descender and as Second rather than First.
- How Saarelainen could be erased through Irminsul if Irminsul keeps no records of Descenders.
- Whether a native of Teyvat can ever become a Descender.
- What the Heavenly Principles want from the Traveler, and why Celestia tolerates them.
### ULTRA +
- What the Third Descender sealed beneath the Tsaritsa's palace. As of 7.1 nobody knows.
- Whether other Descenders exist that no record lists. Canon only speaks of the four "known" ones.
- Whether elemental affinity is something all Descenders share or was special to Saarelainen.

## connections
### ULTRA
Cross-references: the Heavenly Principles and their dormancy; the war of funerary flame and the three moons; the Gnoses, Visions and Stellar Linchpins (all tied to the Third Descender); Irminsul and its modifications; the Shadow Realm and the Abyss; the curse that protects the Khaenri'ahns; the Traveler's origin and the sibling thread; the Tsaritsa's plan to gather all seven Gnoses. In roleplay, ordinary Teyvat folk, and even most gods' followers, have never heard the word "Descender". Do not have a random NPC call the Traveler one.

## entry: The First Descender
@keys: First Descender, first throne of the heavens, Primordial One
@spoiler: major
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
The First Descender is the being now called the Heavenly Principles. It came from the sea of stars, defeated the Seven Sovereigns, took their Authorities and rebuilt the world. Nahida guessed this identity in 3.2, and the moon sister Canon stated it in Nod-Krai (6.3).
### LARGE +
The moon goddesses first lived beside it in peace, until it showed a growing wish to change and rule the world and a near-paranoid hostility toward beings who seek power. *Before Sun and Moon* remembers it as the Primordial One, which created four shining shades of itself. It won the war of funerary flame, but its functions were badly damaged. Since the Cataclysm it has been dormant because of the Third Descender's curse (revealed in 7.1).

## entry: The Second Descender
@keys: Second Descender, Second Who Came, second throne of the heavens
@spoiler: major
@version: 6.2
### STANDARD
The Second Descender is generally taken to be Nibelung, the dragon king. He was Teyvat's original ruler and created the three moons and their goddesses to keep the world running. After leaving for the sea of stars he came back and fought the Heavenly Principles for dominance in the war of funerary flame, a war Lauma says was "triggered by the Descenders". Canon never names him "Second Descender" directly; the label is inferred.
### LARGE +
During the war he imprisoned the moon goddesses and turned the moons into weapons. The Heavenly Principles shattered two moons and threw the third beyond the sky. Nibelung was killed, but he wounded his enemy so badly that it could no longer fully suppress the world's original order. *Before Sun and Moon* calls him the "second throne of the heavens" and says the Primordial One must have defeated the Second Who Came.

## entry: The Third Descender
@keys: Third Descender, Saarelainen, Stranger of Winter's Day
@spoiler: major
@version: 7.1
### STANDARD
The Third Descender, Saarelainen, came from beyond Teyvat. He loved Anastasya, the future Tsaritsa, and their descendants became the Khaenri'ahns. Rhinedottir later told the story from Celestia's records (7.1). The Belyi Tsar incited Anastasya and Saarelainen to open the gate to the Abyss, which let in Abyssal disaster, and the Heavenly Principles erased Anastasya as punishment. Saarelainen went up to Celestia and bargained for her. His body, which had an unparalleled affinity for the power of Teyvat's Human Realm and could amplify the elements, would be split into seven pieces, the Gnoses, to support the Archon system. In return the Heavenly Principles would bring Anastasya back and never harm his descendants.
### LARGE +
In exchange, the Heavenly Principles demanded that he be forgotten, and they used Irminsul to erase every trace of him. Only the Belyi Tsar, hidden in the Shadow Realm, kept his memories. Saarelainen made sure the promise would be kept by laying a curse that would take effect if it was broken. Anastasya concludes that when the Heavenly Principles acted against Khaenri'ah in the Cataclysm, the curse struck, and that this is why they have slept ever since. Ronova calls his remains the gods' playthings. Skirk (4.2) had already told Neuvillette that the Gnoses are the Third Descender's remains and that holding one brings misfortune.
### ULTRA +
Fragments of his tale survive in the Frostmoon Scions' *Hymns of the Far North*. Because of the erasure, its details are unreliable.

## entry: Legacy of the Third Descender
@keys: Third Descender's remains, Third Descender's seal, transcription prism
@spoiler: major
@version: 7.1
### LARGE
The Gnoses are not part of Teyvat's own system. They have a strong elemental affinity and amplify their holders' power. The Tsaritsa says they cannot stay in the Ley Lines for long, so a Gnosis absorbed there will be expelled one day. Pierro explains that the Tsaritsa's transcription prisms and Stellar Linchpins carry his power and so are free of some of Teyvat's laws. She records each day's memories in one so that changes to Irminsul cannot touch them.
### ULTRA +
Beneath Zapolyarny Palace is a seal that the Belyi Tsar said was the last thing the Third Descender left before he was killed. The Heavenly Principles apparently never noticed it. Only a Gnosis makes it react, and every attempt to open it has failed. Collecting all seven Gnoses is one reason the Tsaritsa wants them (7.1).

## entry: Stellar Linchpins and the Third Descender
@keys: Stellar Linchpin, Stellar Linchpins
@keys2: Third Descender, Descender, Gnosis, Gnoses
@version: 6.7
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
During the limited-time Sunny Summer Fontinalia event (6.7), Marionette (Sandrone) shared Fatui research: the Gnoses made from the Third Descender's remains consolidate the Archon system, Visions come from that same system and so are partly linked to him, and when a Stellar Linchpin manifests, impurities from his remains are believed to be fixed in place, giving its wielder a power that seems to come from beyond Teyvat.

## entry: The Fourth Descender
@keys: Fourth Descender
@spoiler: major
@version: 3.2
### STANDARD
The Fourth Descender is the Traveler. Nahida told them so in Sumeru (3.2), using Fatui intelligence that did not list the Traveler's twin as a Descender. Since Irminsul holds nothing on the Traveler, they kept their memories of Rukkhadevata and of the Balladeer when everyone else's were rewritten.
### LARGE +
The Narzissenkreuz sensed the Traveler as a new variable in the world-formula (4.2). Skirk contrasted herself with the Traveler: she is passing through, while they are changing Teyvat's fate (5.7). The Traveler told Lauma openly that they are a Descender (6.0). In Snezhnaya (7.0-7.1), Ronova said a Descender's soul cannot be taken into the Ley Lines, and that the Heavenly Principles' tolerance for the Traveler is not endless.

## entry: Would-be Descenders
@keys: become a Descender, wills that can rival an entire world, beings who could transcend the gods
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
Some people have tried to make use of the Descender idea. In Fontaine, Rene de Petrichor of the Narzissenkreuz Ordo studied ancient sources, decided that a true Descender is a will that can rival a world, and tried to become one to save Fontaine from its prophecy. He failed twice. Khaenri'ah, according to *Perinheri*, hoped beings who could transcend the gods would come from beyond the world, and none came in the writer's lifetime. Canon has not confirmed that a native of Teyvat can become a Descender.
