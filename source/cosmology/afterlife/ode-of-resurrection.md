## summary
### LITE
The Ode of Resurrection is the ceremony, held at the Stadium of the Sacred Flame in Natlan, that revives warriors bearing an Ancient Name who have died fighting the Abyss in the Night Warden Wars. An officiator recites a eulogy, the Pyro Archon walks into the Sacred Flame to find the fallen and their Name in the Night Kingdom, and the crowd sings until the Archon steps out again with the revived. It only works if the warrior's team won. It is the reason Natlan could send its best into endless war.
### STANDARD +
It is also called the Rite of Resurrection, and Mavuika's character stories call it the Rite of Rekindling. It heals only the fatal wound, not injuries gathered earlier in the battle or existing illness. After the Abyss was defeated in 5.3 the ceremony was discontinued.

## established
### LITE
- Who qualifies: only Ancient Name bearers. For that reason warriors without a Name were not sent to the Night Warden Wars.
- Condition: at least one member of the fallen warrior's team must return victorious. If an entire team falls, none can be restored.
- Where: at the Stadium of the Sacred Flame, through the Sacred Flame, a conduit to the Night Kingdom.
### STANDARD +
- Those not brought back are stranded in the Night Kingdom, and their Ancient Names are eventually forgotten and lost. Natlan's saying is that the victors burn bright while the losers turn to ash.
- The healing applies only to fatal injuries. It does not mend wounds accumulated during a long battle or pre-existing ailments (Mavuika's character story).
- Warriors who are never lost to the Night Kingdom eventually retire from age or injury, die normally, and their Ancient Name passes to another warrior, so the Names carry Natlan's history across generations.
- Natlanese who die otherwise enter the Night Kingdom, slowly lose their sense of self in the sea of souls, and are in time reborn in Natlan in other forms. The Ode is the exception for bearers who fell in victorious battle.
### LARGE +
- The Ode's power does not belong to the Pyro Archon. Quest text states it comes from Ronova, the Ruler of Death, who let the first Pyro Archon, Xbalanque, borrow it.
- The Night Kingdom typically records only the memories and experiences of Natlanese people, which is why Ancient Names are seen as a Natlanese matter and a bearer's Name is sought there. Its Ley Lines could not take in souls from outside Natlan until Capitano's merger in 5.3; the Traveler's Ancient Name, Tumaini, was a special case forged for them.

## ceremony
### LITE
An officiator opens with a eulogy addressed to the warriors of Natlan, calling on them to heed the call of life as inheritors of memory and legend and naming Natlan's fire as the nation's lifeblood. The Pyro Archon then steps into the Sacred Flame to seek the dead and their Ancient Name in the Night Kingdom. Meanwhile the crowd in the stadium bows its head and sings in Swahili.
### STANDARD +
The song's refrain is that the tale continues on: glory passes through generations, courage lights sky and earth, victory will again herald Natlan's path, and the living will wait and sing until the heroes come back and burn bright once more. The melody and lyrics are an excerpt of the Natlan soundtrack, sung in Swahili. At the end the Archon emerges from the flame with the resurrected person. Mualani describes this as the usual sequence before Kachina's Ode, and later recalls Kachina humming the song during training breaks.
### LARGE +
Because the ceremony is a public rite, the crowd is part of it. Natlanese treat it as a victory celebration rather than a funeral; Mavuika's character story says she thought of it, as a child, as akin to a victory feast.

## history
@spoiler: major
@version: 5.3
### STANDARD
Natlan's tradition holds that the Ode was made possible when Xbalanque borrowed power from Ronova. Through the Pilgrimage the strongest Ancient Name bearers were chosen and sent to fight the Abyss in the Night Warden Wars, and the Ode let them fight knowing they could be brought back.
### LARGE +
In Mavuika's character story, her father won the Pilgrimage and went to war. After a three-day battle in which three of seven warriors fell, he was revived by the Ode but came out of the flame covered with the wounds of the long fight and unable to stand. That was the first time young Mavuika felt the gaze of death, which she saw as something to respect and fear even with the Ode available. Her father told her that everyone fears death, but some still step forward because they have something to protect.
### ULTRA +
When Kachina fell, Mavuika presided over her Ode with Ntotila as officiator. She came out of the flame alone and reported that neither Kachina nor her Name could be found in the Night Kingdom. The crowd, stirred by Tepexpan and Tamuin, speculated that Kachina had not truly been victorious, but Mavuika refuted this and declared Kachina a hero. She suspended the Pilgrimage because heroes could no longer be guaranteed a return. She later told the Traveler, Paimon, Mualani, Iansan, Chasca and Kinich that the Abyss's unrest in the Night Kingdom had prevented her from finding Kachina, and that until it was solved the Ode would fail. Chasca suggested keeping the Pilgrimage going without sending winners to war, but Mavuika pointed out this would cost the contest its prestige and the flow of Contending Fire, so the Sacred Flame would go out.

## in_the_final_war
@spoiler: major
@version: 5.1
### LARGE
Once the six Heroes of Natlan were gathered during the Abyss's invasion, Mavuika awakened the power of the divine throne to extend the Ode's blessings to every Natlanese warrior, whether or not they bore an Ancient Name; later quest dialogue (Citlali, Ronova) treats this as use of Ronova's borrowed power, for which death was owed as the price. From then on, casualties fell effectively to zero, and the Abyss was repelled from the overworld.
### ULTRA +
In the final assault on the Night Kingdom, only Mavuika and the Traveler could withstand the Abyssal energy near Gosoythoth, so ordinary warriors stayed behind. When the Sacred Flame guttered, Kachina spontaneously began the Ode at the Stadium to send the people's support. The flame blazed up again and the Traveler's Ancient Name, Tumaini, was recited by the Lord of the Night, granting a Nightsoul's Blessing. With that, the two drew a weapon from the flame and defeated Gosoythoth, ending the Night Warden Wars. Afterwards Ronova named Mavuika's death as the price for the power she had lent. The Lord of the Night paused, saying another was coming to take Mavuika's place; Capitano then merged his life force with the Lord of the Night, and Ronova withdrew. The ceremony was discontinued because warriors are no longer sent to fight in the Night Kingdom.

## open_questions
### LARGE
- Canon shows the borrowed power but not how Ronova and Xbalanque drew up their terms; do not invent the details of the bargain.
- Canon does not say whether the Ode could be performed again, or what happens to the souls that were never recovered by it.

## connections
### STANDARD
Sacred Flame and Night Kingdom (the ceremony's means and destination), Night Warden Wars and Pilgrimage (the system it supports), Ancient Name (who may be revived), Mavuika (the Archon who performs it), Kachina (its failure and the final use).
### LARGE +
The Ode also links to Ronova's role as Ruler of Death in Natlan's history, to the Wayob who keep the dead in the Night Kingdom, and to the Archon Quest act Incandescent Ode of Resurrection (5.3).

## entry: Ode of Resurrection
@keys: Ode of Resurrection, Rite of Resurrection, Rite of Rekindling
@version: 5.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Ode of Resurrection is Natlan's rite for reviving Ancient Name bearers who fell fighting the Abyss, provided at least one of their team returned victorious. At the Stadium of the Sacred Flame an officiator speaks a eulogy, the Pyro Archon walks into the flame to find the fallen and their Name in the Night Kingdom, and the crowd sings that the tale continues on. It heals only the fatal wound. Its power was borrowed from Ronova, Ruler of Death, by Xbalanque.
### LARGE +
In 5.0 the Ode failed for Kachina because the Abyss was disrupting the Night Kingdom, and Mavuika suspended the Pilgrimage. During the 5.1 invasion she extended the rite to every Natlanese warrior. In 5.3 Kachina led the Stadium in the Ode to strengthen the Traveler and Mavuika in the final battle, and afterwards it was discontinued.

## entry: Kachina's Failed Ode
@keys: Kachina's resurrection, failed Ode, Kachina's Ode
@version: 5.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Kachina's Ode was led by Mavuika with Ntotila as officiator. Mavuika returned from the Sacred Flame alone, saying neither Kachina nor her Name could be found. Some in the crowd, led by Tepexpan and Tamuin, doubted Kachina's worth; Mavuika declared her a hero. She suspended the Pilgrimage and began investigating. She later said the Abyss's unrest in the Night Kingdom had blocked her.
### LARGE +
Mavuika told the Traveler, Paimon, Mualani, Iansan, Chasca and Kinich that without working Odes Natlan would fall to the Abyss, and that suspending the Pilgrimage at least meant no more warriors went to a war they could not return from.

## entry: Ronova and the Borrowed Power

Quest text says the Ode's power comes from Ronova, the Ruler of Death, who let Xbalanque borrow it. In 5.3 Ronova required the Archon's life as the price for the power used in the war. The Lord of the Night held back the blow, and Capitano merged his life force with the Lord of the Night instead.
