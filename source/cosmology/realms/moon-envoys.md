## summary
### LITE
Moon Envoys were servants of the Three Moons who lived in the Lunar Palace during the era of the unified civilization. The name is a title, not a species: an angel or someone who was not one could hold it. Known examples are Zibai, Thoth and, by inference, Nabu Malikata. After the war of funerary flame killed the moon goddesses, the office lost its purpose.
### STANDARD +
The envoys carried out many divine duties. Some laboured across the moons to guide humanity and weave the threads of fate for living things, while others came down to the mortal realm to bless nations with good harvests. They could take animal forms and travelled by lunar chariot.
### LARGE +
Players mostly meet the concept through Zibai (6.3), Thoth (6.1) and the Nod-Krai moon story; most of what is known comes from a handful of quests, character stories and item texts, so the picture is partial and several memberships are inferred.

## established
### LITE
- After the Three Moons accepted the Heavenly Principles' divine plan, the new masters sent subordinates to share the sisters' work, and Moon Envoys were assigned to assist each goddess. In a remembered scene, Sonnet says the work can simply be left to the Moon Envoys.
- Envoys lived in the Lunar Palace above the clouds.
- Souls of the dead once followed the Moon Envoys' trail to the cities on the moons, where they rested.
### STANDARD +
- The title could be held by any individual, angel or not. Zibai, an angel who guided Lang-Gan, became an envoy when Istaroth, the Ruler of Time, gave her a crest bearing the sigil of the moons; her character story says she never fully accepted it. The crest is her Moon Wheel.
- Duties varied. The furnishing text for the Moon Envoy of Silver Radiance says some envoys toil across the three moons to guide ignorant mortals, others descend to bless a nation with bountiful harvests, and that the title does not name one specific person.
- Envoys could change shape into animal forms and used a lunar chariot to travel, for example between the Lunar Palace and Lang-Gan, according to the wiki's summary of Zibai's quests.
- The Lunar Palace is also called the Moon Capital; boatmen such as Diotima and Euthyphro ferried souls toward it, and Diotima says the souls followed the envoys' trail.
### LARGE +
- Thoth, later the Ibis King of King Deshret's Seven Pillars, is described by the Guiding Notes and by Zibai's memories as a former Moon Envoy and her colleague. Zibai recalls a colleague who liked to take ibis form.
- Zibai was killed after trying to save Lang-Gan from the Celestial Nail against the Heavenly Principles' will; Istaroth froze the moment of her death and held her soul in fragments, which the Traveler and Zhongli reunited during Lantern Rite (6.3 event).
- The end of the office: the Eternal and Iridescent Moons were destroyed and the Frost Moon was banished beyond the false sky in the war of funerary flame, killing their goddesses. The wiki states the envoys were then no longer needed and that souls were afterwards deposited into the ley lines instead.
### ULTRA +
- Nabu Malikata, an angel who later hid beneath the Great Red Sand, is listed by the wiki as a Moon Envoy residing in the Lunar Palace, on the basis that her legend calls her the Moon Maiden and her city Ay-Khanoum the City of the Moon Maiden, paradise of migrants from the moon. One weapon legend says she often danced with the three moon sisters. This is inference; see open_questions.
- The envoys sat beside a larger order. The angels in general served as envoys between Celestia and humanity, and the moon sisters had a good relationship with the angels. The sisters accepted the Heavenly Principles' offer of helpers rather than refusing it, which is how Sonnet and Canon speak of it in the remembered scene.
- In the Moonheart Gap scene Sonnet also admits to skipping work to play with humans; the exchange shows envoys were covering real duties of the moons, not decorative ones.

## accounts
### LARGE
- Official wiki synthesis: envoys were a staffing arrangement made after the Heavenly Principles arrived, with each goddess given assistants.
- Zibai's character story: she was handed a moon crest by the shade of Time without explanation, wondered whether it was part of the plan, and began travelling between the morning stars, the moons and her garden, without accepting the crest.
- Thoth, as quoted by Nefer: he boasted that he laboured across three moons to guide mortals and weave fate so that life could sprout anew in cycles. Nefer judged it a liar's pitch, though other sources support that he really was an envoy.
- Boatman hymn (Euthyphro): a boatman prays to the goddess Prusmene to be spared from the coming order of the Primordial One and speaks of ushering souls to the Lunar Palace above the clouds. This is a believer's account, not a statement about envoys.
### ULTRA +
- Diotima, a boatman of Meropis uplifted to the Temple of Space, says souls of the noble once went to sleep eternally in the cities upon the moons. She says she never visited them herself, so her account is second-hand.

## open_questions
### STANDARD
- Whether boatmen and Moon Envoys were one role or two.
- Which moon each named envoy served; only Zibai's link to Sonnet is argued, and from indirect evidence.
- Whether Nabu Malikata and the Mistress of Chenyu Vale were envoys; both are inferences.
### LARGE +
- How many envoys there were and what happened to most of them. Only Zibai (restored) and Thoth (freed in 6.6) are known to have survived; Nabu Malikata is dead.
- Why Istaroth chose Zibai, and whether her gift was part of Time's own intentions; Zibai herself asks this in her story.
- Do not resolve these. Treat anyone not named in canon as unknown.

## connections
### STANDARD
Three Moons and their goddesses Aria, Canon and Sonnet; the Heavenly Principles and angels; the unified civilization; the war of funerary flame; the shadowmoon that hid the moons' fate; Istaroth; Zibai, Thoth, Nabu Malikata; the Moon Wheel; Lang-Gan; the afterlife systems of the moons and, later, the ley lines and Irminsul.
### LARGE +
Moon Wheels were originally made by the Heavenly Principles and the moon sisters and given to human followers so the goddesses could communicate with them in rituals, per the wiki; Zibai's wheel differs in design because Istaroth gave it to her directly as the symbol of her envoy status. In Nod-Krai the wheels later underlie the Moonsign and Lunar reaction mechanics, which are game systems and not part of the envoy office.

## entry: Zibai as a Moon Envoy
@keys: Zibai, White Horse Adeptus
@keys2: Moon Envoy, Lunar Palace, moon crest, Moon Wheel
@version: 6.3
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Zibai was an angel who guided the nation of Lang-Gan and was later made a Moon Envoy when Istaroth gave her a crest of the moons. In the wiki's reading she was assigned to Sonnet of the Iridescent Moon, who left much of her work to her. She travelled between Lang-Gan and the Lunar Palace by lunar chariot. She died defying the Heavenly Principles over the Celestial Nail, and was restored in the Lantern Rite event story (6.3). Her own entry carries the detail.

## entry: Thoth the Moon Envoy
@keys: Thoth, Ibis King
@keys2: Moon Envoy, Lunar Palace, three moons
@version: 6.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Thoth, the Ibis King and Lord of Lies, is described as a former Moon Envoy of the Lunar Palace and a colleague of Zibai. He later served King Deshret as a Seven Pillar. His claim of laboring across the three moons to guide mortals comes from Nefer's recollection of him, and she doubted it, but other texts back the envoy role.

## entry: Souls and the moons
@keys: boatman, Diotima, Euthyphro, cities upon the moon
@keys2: Moon Envoy, Lunar Palace, soul, souls
@version: 6.0
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
In the unified-civilization era the dead were said to follow the Moon Envoys to cities on the moons and sleep there. Boatmen ferried souls toward the Lunar Palace above the clouds; Diotima of Meropis was one, and Euthyphro of Arcadia wrote the hymn found at the Golden Hall. After the moons fell the wiki says souls went into the ley lines. The hymn suggests this afterlife existed alongside the Irminsul.
