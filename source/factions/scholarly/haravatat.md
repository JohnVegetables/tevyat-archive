## summary
### LITE
Haravatat is the Sumeru Akademiya's school of semiotics, the study of signs, meaning and language. It covers linguistics, ancient runes and the translation of old texts. Its colour is black, its crest shows a bull, and it is led by Sage Rushd. It is the smallest Darshan by enrolment, but it has produced Alhaitham, the Akademiya's Scribe, and the veteran scholar Faruzan.
### STANDARD +
Haravatat sees language and writing as the core of the world. The Akademiya receptionist Sitt sums it up as deciphering ancient texts or creating new words. Because its focus includes ancient languages, the Darshan's work overlaps with Vahumana's study of history.

## structure
### STANDARD
Haravatat is one of the Six Darshans, with one Great Sage at its head. Its disciplines include linguistics, ancient runes and syntax, as in Gregor's remark that his Haravatat classes spend their time on syntax and structure rather than literature. Members range from students and Driyosh to researchers and mentors. Entrance is by exam: Alhaitham was admitted with top marks in the Haravatat entrance test, and his grandmother also arranged for him to attend classes in other Darshans.
### LARGE +
The Darshan studies old scripts, among them the King Deshret script. Since a century ago, its research has shifted from the analysis of ruin machinery with the aid of ancient texts to language itself. The older machinery work became a niche within Haravatat as Kshahrewar grew into the Darshan of technology and overtook it.

## leadership
### LITE
Rushd is the current Sage. He is the youngest of the sages and succeeded Khajeh after the Akademiya-Fatui plot collapsed.
### STANDARD +
Khajeh, the previous Sage, was one of the four sages who joined the plan to build an artificial god. In 3.0 he travelled to Gandharva Ville with several subordinates to invite Tighnari into the project, implying that Tighnari's refusal damaged Naphis' reputation. Khajeh was among the sages exiled to the Avidya Forest after the Sabzeruz Festival. Rushd was Khajeh's junior. In 6.6, among the six sages at Caravan Ribat, he warned that Irminsul is vital to all Teyvat and asked for backup plans. He also called the Temple of Silence plan too risky.
### ULTRA +
After the plot, Alhaitham was offered the Grand Sage position, refused it, and acted as Acting Grand Sage for a time. The wiki says he resigned, worried that "Acting" would turn into "Official", and went back to being Scribe. The Scribe belongs to the Mahamata, the Akademiya's civil administrators, not to Haravatat's own leadership. In 6.6, Naphis introduced Rushd and the other new sages as having taken office after Azar's circle fell.

## members
### STANDARD
Alhaitham is the Akademiya's Scribe, a Haravatat graduate whose brilliance baffles colleagues because he refuses higher office. His father had been a Haravatat mentor, and his mother a Vahumana scholar. Faruzan is a Haravatat professor who returned to the Akademiya after a century trapped in a King Deshret ruin. Her mechanics research became the foundation of mechanical study in Kshahrewar, but she remains Haravatat and has refused Kshahrewar's offers to move. Gregor, a student from Mondstadt, studies syntax here. Yavanani is a Haravatat scholar who debates Vahumana's Khaldun, and Rifaet, also Haravatat, is one of the top candidates for Grand Conservator of the House of Daena.
### LARGE +
Others the wiki lists as Haravatat include Amandeep, Azmi, Bahar, Murtada Radkani, Rezakhani, Rowshan, Tafazzoli, Viraf and Ziryab. Hawa of Rtawahist sometimes dabbles in ancient runes, though she may have learned them from her family rather than the Darshan.

## goals
### STANDARD
Haravatat tries to understand meaning through signs: translating ancient writing, studying structure and building theories of language. In debates with Vahumana, Haravatat members say Vahumana's habit of seeking universal rules for all events puts the cart before the horse. Faruzan, defending the Darshan, insists that someone has to keep studying niche topics to expand the frontiers of knowledge.

## methods
### LARGE
The Darshan works in libraries and texts rather than the field. Haravatat students are said to spend more time on grammar than on stories, and a posted comment in the Akademiya complains that Haravatat means only research and papers, with no chance to see the outside. A rule that students master twenty languages before graduating is cited by Alhaitham, though it appears to be a former policy kept alive as a joke.

## history
### STANDARD
Haravatat is one of the Six Great Schools. A century ago its scholars, Faruzan among them, studied ruin devices alongside ancient texts. As Kshahrewar matured, that work moved over to Kshahrewar, and Haravatat focused on language alone.
### LARGE +
**Faruzan's return.** When Faruzan came back, her profile says no Haravatat student could follow her essays, and she says the Darshan has declined her funding requests as too niche. Kshahrewar students who learned from her old manuscripts repeatedly tried to recruit her. She declined each time.
**The sages' plot (3.0-3.2).** Khajeh was one of the four sages who sided with Azar and Dottore. After Scaramouche's defeat he was removed. Alhaitham told the Traveler the Haravatat and Kshahrewar seats would need new sages.
**6.6.** The Irminsul crisis forced the sages to Caravan Ribat and then to the Temple of Silence. Alhaitham took part in the follow-up work with Kaveh at Aaru, translating obelisks (Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana).

## relations
### STANDARD
- **Kshahrewar**: tied through Faruzan and the old ruin-machinery work. Haravatat says Kshahrewar has taken over its mechanics; Kshahrewar's scholars rely on Haravatat's findings. Faruzan says Kshahrewar can use Haravatat's research freely.
- **Vahumana**: the two schools overlap on ancient history and argue about method. Vahumana's Khaldun says Haravatat tunnel-visions on linguistics, while Haravatat's Yavanani says Vahumana is "putting the cart before the horse" by seeking universal rules.
- **Rtawahist**: loose overlap through rune study, as with Hawa.
### LARGE +
- **Amurta**: a poster says Amurta members get to sightsee while Haravatat people write papers, and people from other schools call Haravatat snobs.
- **Khajeh and Naphis**: Khajeh hinted that Tighnari's refusal damaged his master Naphis' reputation, while Naphis was in fact imprisoned by the sages.

## reputation
### LARGE
Haravatat is known as a school of erudite, sometimes proud scholars. Some other Darshans' members call them snobs. Faruzan jokes that her Darshan has the lowest enrolment and might as well close if funding is the test. Alhaitham, its public face, is respected for his intellect but is also seen as aloof and egocentric.

## entry: Rushd
@keys: Rushd, Sage Rushd
@keys2: Haravatat, Akademiya, sage
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Rushd is the Haravatat Sage and the youngest of the current six. He is described as a tall, pale man in a purple sherwani, with round green glasses. In the 6.6 Archon Quest he greets the Traveler warmly, insists the sages bear responsibility for Irminsul and Teyvat, and cautions that a worst case needs backup plans. At the Temple of Silence council, he considered the plan too risky, while Firnas judged the risks manageable.

## entry: Khajeh
@keys: Khajeh, Sage Khajeh
@keys2: Haravatat, Tighnari, Akademiya
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Khajeh was the Haravatat Sage during the Akademiya-Fatui plot. He took part in the Sabzeruz Festival samsara and was among the four sages punished and exiled to the Avidya Forest. In Silent Seeker of Knowledge he visited Tighnari in Gandharva Ville to press him to join, hinting that Tighnari's refusal harmed Naphis. Tighnari answered that if the project mattered, Naphis would have come in person.

## entry: Twenty languages
@keys: twenty languages, 20 languages
@keys2: Haravatat, Akademiya, graduate
### STANDARD
Alhaitham says that every student must master at least twenty languages to graduate. Faruzan repeats it in a later world quest, then wonders whether it still applies. The wiki describes it as an out-of-date rule from about a century ago, kept as an in-joke, and cites Sethos for the in-joke. Treat it as a school legend rather than current policy.

## entry: Haravatat and Kshahrewar rivalry
@keys: Haravatat mechanics, ancient mechanisms
@keys2: Faruzan, Kshahrewar, Haravatat
### LARGE
Faruzan's old work on ruin mechanisms was long Haravatat's pride. As Kshahrewar's mechanical theory advanced, Haravatat's version became niche, and Kshahrewar built its basics on her manuscripts. Faruzan jokes sourly that the Kshahrewar scholars "were playing with rudimentary building blocks" when she was cracking puzzles across Sumeru, and also says knowledge cannot be restrained, citing how Kshahrewar can use Haravatat's research.

## entry: Interdarshan Championship
@keys: Interdarshan Championship
@keys2: Haravatat, Faruzan, Akademiya
@version: 3.6
@availability: limited
### LARGE
During the limited-time event A Parade of Providence (3.6), the Akademiya Extravaganza featured an Interdarshan Championship in which each Darshan sent a representative. Tighnari represented Amurta, Faruzan represented Haravatat and Kaveh represented Kshahrewar. Faruzan scored points in the event and spoke proudly of Haravatat, while also teaming up with a Kshahrewar junior.
