## summary
### LITE
House Solovyov was an old human noble family of Snezhnaya, loyal servants of the Belyi Tsar. Its emblem was the nightingale. It began as a family of spies, was raised to the gentry by the Tsar and sat among the fae at the Pale Star Court. Its best-known son is Solovei, the first Torchforger and founder of the Lightkeepers.
### STANDARD +
Only two members are named in canon: an unnamed elder son who guarded a sacred object and was killed for seeing a secret, and his younger brother Solovei. Everything known about the house comes from item text and one quest memory, so it is a small family story, not a fully mapped clan.

## structure
### STANDARD
Canon gives no hierarchy, estate or head of household. Two facts about its order are stated: the nightingale emblem passed to the elder son, and the two brothers belonged to the clan's younger generation. After the elder son died, the younger took over the family name.
### LARGE +
Because the house is never described holding land or titles after the Cataclysm, portray it as a court lineage serving the Tsar, not as a landed dynasty.

## members
### LITE
Solovei, the younger son, later the Torchforger. His elder brother, never named, who inherited the nightingale emblem and was killed in the Tsar's service.
### STANDARD +
The elder brother was given the honour of guarding the "sacred chariot" when the Tsar's court of wise humans and fae met in a labyrinthine palace by Snezhnograd. Solovei, who had always admired him, was away quelling unrest when the killing happened; he came home to a world already deep in snow and found his brother's spear stained with blood. The spear is the weapon Bloodsoaked Ruins.
### LARGE +
Years later Solovei, whom the weapon text calls the general who had inherited the family name, left his homeland for the Nod-Krai borderlands on a quest for vengeance. Whenever he touched the spear he relived that snowy night outside the palace gates, and the words his friend, the first Reed Miller Avrelian, spoke before the guillotine. He went on to found the Lightkeepers. His own file carries the rest of his life.

## history
### STANDARD
The family first served the Belyi Tsar as spies. Its loyalty won favour and the Tsar raised it to the gentry, so that a man of modest origin could stand in the royal palace among the fae dignitaries. When the Tsar opened his realm to humans and gathered the wisest humans and fae in a secret court, the Solovyovs were among the retainers who guarded it.
### LARGE +
One brother's death ended that service. While on guard, the elder son came upon Aksinya and Koshchei at Hvergelmir and saw something he was not meant to see. In a memory shown in the 7.0 Pale Crown Palace quest, Zver brings up the unauthorised execution of a human noble and starts to plead ("I beg you to..."). The voice answering it, credited as '???' and generally taken to be the Tsar, tells it to dispense with the terror and says Aksinya and Koshchei have reported, that the eldest son of House Solovyov had chanced upon something he should not have, and that the fault lies with the two of them.
### ULTRA +
The Long Night's Oath artifact text says the Ratniki of Nod-Krai wear heavy armor because it marks them as descendants of the warriors who first fought beside the Torchforger. It does not say these are Solovyov blood relatives; it speaks of an oath and a bloodline of warriors. Canon never shows the house at the Pale Star Court after the Cataclysm.

## relations
### STANDARD
The house's defining bond was with the Belyi Tsar, whom it served until its elder son died. Its place among the fae dignitaries was one of rank at court. Its link to the Lightkeepers runs through Solovei alone. Its only known clash was with Zver, the Tsar's artificial companion, which executed the elder son.
### LARGE +
Avrelian, the first Reed Miller, was Solovei's childhood friend, not a member of the house, but the weapon text ties his last words to Solovei's grief. Aila, who foresaw Solovei's arrival in Nod-Krai, dealt with him as a person and not as a house representative.

## reputation
### STANDARD
Item lore remembers the family as an illustrious lineage of northern warriors with a venerable nightingale emblem and a record of loyalty to the Tsar. In Nod-Krai the name survives mostly through Solovei, the Torchforger. Nothing in canon says the wider world still knows the Solovyov name.

## secrets
@spoiler: major
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
The house's one real secret is not its own: the elder son died because he saw what Aksinya and Koshchei were doing at Hvergelmir. Canon says only that it was the philosophers' secret research, and Zver's plea shows the killing was not sanctioned by the Tsar.

## status
### LITE
No living Solovyovs are shown in canon through 7.1. The two named members are long dead.
### STANDARD +
Solovei apparently died of old age; his brother was executed earlier, in the Tsar's reign, before Solovei left for Nod-Krai. The house has no known seat, and no present-day character claims the name.

### STANDARD
The elder son of House Solovyov inherited the nightingale emblem and guarded the sacred chariot at the Tsar's secret court. He is never named. He saw the philosophers' secret at Hvergelmir and was executed by Zver without the Tsar's leave. His polearm, Bloodsoaked Ruins, passed to his younger brother.

### STANDARD
The house's emblem is a nightingale. The Dyed Tassel artifact calls it a mark of illustrious heritage, and the elder son inherited it. The surnames Solovyov and Solovei both come from the Russian word for nightingale.

### STANDARD
The Dyed Tassel text says the family were first employed as spies and then elevated to the gentry by the Winter Tsar, which gave them a place in the royal palace among the fae dignitaries of the court. The palace is linked to the Zapolyarny Palace in the text.
