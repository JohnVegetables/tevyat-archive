## summary
### LITE
The Seven Pillars, also called the Seven Sages or seven monk-kings, were the chief retainers of King Deshret (Al-Ahmar), the desert god-king of ancient Sumeru. He gave them his power, and they served as his "pyramid of authority". Three were gods he had defeated: Heryshaf the Goat King, Sobek the Crocodile King and Thoth the Ibis King. Bennu and Shesepankh were intelligent constructs he built, and Hermanubis was a human sage raised to be his familiar. The seventh, the Dendro Dragon Apep, was named a Pillar by Deshret but never accepted it.
### STANDARD +
Almost all of them are gone. Hermanubis died long ago. In 6.6 the three Kings, who had been shut in Aaru for thousands of years, were freed, and Bennu and Shesepankh were woken. Together they voted to open Aaru to the people of Sumeru.

## structure
### STANDARD
Heryshaf stood at the head, as deputy-king and Minister of Ministers. Heryshaf, Sobek and Thoth were the highest-ranking and are called the Three Retainers. Each Pillar had a style of office. Heryshaf was Minister of Ministers, Sobek Captain of Captains, Thoth Scribe of Scribes, Hermanubis the Greatest of Sages, Bennu the Most-Radiant and Shesepankh the Most-Holy. Apep carried the title Dragon King, the Supreme Spirit, which Deshret and the other six devised and which Apep herself dismissed.
### LARGE +
- **Heryshaf, the Goat King**: embodied Deshret's rulership. He executed the king's authority and kept his own counsel.
- **Sobek, the Crocodile King**: embodied Deshret's strength, as foremost general. Statues of him stand at oases and water temples.
- **Thoth, the Ibis King**: embodied Deshret's wisdom as a counsellor. Texts also record him as a flatterer and a god of deceit.
- **Hermanubis, the Greatest of Sages**: the only Pillar who rose from humanity, born among the Tighnar people.
- **Bennu, the Most-Radiant**: Deshret's "soul". A construct who guarded the gates of Aaru.
- **Shesepankh, the Most-Holy**: Deshret's "will". A construct who oversaw all the Jinn of the world.
- **Apep, the Dragon King**: the Dendro Dragon Sovereign, counted by Deshret but not by herself.

Bennu is likened to a great bird winging toward the sun and Shesepankh to a lioness with a human head. In art, Heryshaf wears blue-violet, Thoth red, Sobek blue and Hermanubis purple with a jackal head. Sumeru's scholars say the three Kings were once on the level of the gods. Hermanubis was counted a priest, sage and warrior and was the guide of all priests.

## leadership
### STANDARD
Heryshaf was chief of the Pillars and the "right hand" of King Deshret. Above him stood only the king, who raised the Pillars to run his realm. Hermanubis led the priestly class. A Pillar's rank came from the king, and as Thoth puts it, a retainer's fate, including any chance to walk away, was the king's to decide.

## members
### LARGE
The seven Pillars and where they stand as of 7.1:
- **Heryshaf** (Goat King): alive, freed from Aaru in 6.6, and has asked to stay in the Temple of Silence.
- **Sobek** (Crocodile King): alive and freed. Sobek Oasis in the Hypostyle Desert bears his name.
- **Thoth** (Ibis King): alive and freed, and speaks with Nefer as his chosen disciple. He formerly served as a Moon Envoy.
- **Bennu** and **Shesepankh**: constructs that were dormant after Deshret's death. They were woken in 6.6 and can appear on the shield of Aaru Village's Guardians (Bennu) and Dehya's gauntlet (Shesepankh).
- **Hermanubis**: dead. His power lives on in the Temple of Silence, and Heryshaf calls Cyno his representative.
- **Apep**: alive. She helped heal the Dendro Archon Nahida and made her choice about Aaru ahead of time.
### ULTRA +
Bennu, Shesepankh and Hermanubis carry the formal style "Saint". Heryshaf, Sobek and Thoth call each other nicknames in private ("Goatie" and "Croco", Thoth says), while the constructs differ in temperament: Bennu is stern, earnest and cannot stand disrupted plans, whereas Shesepankh is leisurely and curious and loves to ask travellers questions. Both say Hermanubis was the colleague they admired or missed most.

## goals
### STANDARD
The Pillars existed to serve Deshret's rule and his dream of protecting his people. In the Lay of Al-Ahmar, the three Kings urged him to seek a way past the Heavenly Principles by taking control of the past and defeating death. In 6.6 Heryshaf says Deshret's greatest wish was to protect his people, which is the standard he asks the Pillars to apply when deciding what to do with Aaru.

## methods
### LARGE
Deshret divided his power among his sages across the desert, and the Pillars governed, fought and recorded on his behalf. Sobek led campaigns across many lands, and Thoth is credited with inventing the desert script and its arcane arts. Thoth keeps a chess set tied to Safhe Shatranj, the great chessboard laid out in the desert by Deshret and the Pillars. In 6.6 Thoth shows Nefer that the set can be connected to Safhe Shatranj, and by moving pieces on it allies can be transferred across the material plane in battle. Bennu and Shesepankh needed the three Kings to call them awake with their voices, and Aaru's gate required the unanimous consent of the Seven Pillars to open.
### ULTRA +
The Kings could grant gifts. Thoth gave Goudarz the power to deceive, Makhaira the power to be loved, and Nefer the eyes to see through hearts (and a chess set). Each of these followed a human's answer to one of Nabu Malikata's three questions. Heryshaf also built a defence for the Mausoleum of King Deshret in 6.6, one that hides everything said or done inside from sight and hearing, so that even Irminsul cannot perceive it.

## history
### STANDARD
**Origin.** Several thousand years ago Deshret split his power among sages across the desert. Among the Pillars, Sobek, Thoth and Heryshaf had been gods defeated in the Archon War. The Lay of Al-Ahmar says the three urged Deshret to defy the Heavenly Principles after he mourned his losses. Heryshaf and then Thoth were rebuked, but Sobek talked Deshret round to seeking forbidden knowledge and immortality. In that legend Deshret then built a huge labyrinth and shut himself inside, and his kingdom was buried by the sand in a single night.
### LARGE +
**Banishment.** Deshret imprisoned the three Kings in Aaru for what Sobek calls iniquity. Thoth was told he could be released if humans could answer three questions put to Deshret by Nabu Malikata. They are what causes our rise and fall, what obliterates love and hate, and what determines truth and falsehood.

**Hermanubis and the Temple of Silence.** After Deshret's death, Hermanubis led his followers to Tulaytullah and founded the Temple of Silence. When the king of Gurabad besieged the city, Hermanubis shattered himself so his followers could use his power.

**The three questions.** Goudarz answered the first with conspiracy and betrayal, and was granted the power to deceive. Makhaira answered the second with vengeance, and was granted the power to be loved. Both gifts later helped bring them down, though Thoth is unsure whether the gift or the answer was at fault. Around five hundred years ago, the researcher Zandik also visited Thoth and refused a gift. Thoth then gave Nefer the third question, and she gave an answer meant for herself, which did not free him. He rewarded her anyway.

**Apep.** Apep agreed that Deshret could build his civilization in her domain in exchange for his knowledge and power after death. When he died, she took his body and was contaminated by forbidden knowledge. Deshret counted her as a Pillar, but she rejected the idea and never met with the others.
### ULTRA +
**The 6.6 reunion.** In Song of the Welkin Moon, Act IX, the Traveler's party reached Aaru's Shut in the Mausoleum. Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf recognised Nefer and the answer she gave: "time". It broke their seal. The three Kings then woke Bennu and Shesepankh and held a council. Apep's vote and that of Hermanubis's representative were counted as already given, and the other five agreed to open Aaru to Sumeru's scholars for modification. Bennu warned that opening it would end the perfect afterlife of the Golden Slumber, because the souls inside would return to the Ley Lines.

**Aftermath.** In Act X, with Irminsul shut down, Heryshaf and Thoth helped in battle by moving units through Thoth's chess set, and Bennu and Shesepankh lent their power through Candace's shield and Dehya's gauntlet. Afterwards Thoth and Heryshaf asked Sethos if they could stay in the Temple of Silence.

## relations
### LARGE
- **King Deshret (Al-Ahmar)**: their master. The Kings were loyal and also called his most treacherous advisers. His death left Bennu and Shesepankh dormant, and Hermanubis led his own followers away.
- **Apep**: Deshret's pact partner. The Pillars regard her as absent from their number. Sobek calls her vicious and cruel, and Heryshaf says she holds contempt for gods.
- **Nefer**: Thoth's chosen disciple. She freed the three Kings and holds the chess set.
- **Temple of Silence**: founded by Hermanubis. Sethos, its present leader, hosts Heryshaf and Thoth, and Cyno is Hermanubis's representative.
- **Nahida (Dendro Archon)**: Heryshaf and Thoth support her, and Heryshaf warns that her softer style of rule has both advantages and dangers.
### ULTRA +
- **Liloupar**: Deshret sealed her soul in seven parts under the Seven Pillars as a sentence. Thoth and the others recognise her in 6.6 and are hostile.
- **Aaru Village**: its Guardians' shields carry Bennu's blessing.
- **Eremites and the Akademiya**: the Eremites descend from Deshret's subjects. Scholars of the Akademiya know of the Pillars from the ruins, and some believe Thoth is behind many upheavals in the desert.
- **Zibai**: Thoth was a Moon Envoy and once knew Zibai.
- **Layla and Djer**: Thoth helped them when they touched a device in his ruins that tests brain power, and kept it from overwhelming them.

## reputation
### LARGE
Scholars of the Akademiya know the Pillars as the sages of King Deshret and honour Hermanubis as a foundational figure of desert civilisation. The desert's legends are less kind to the three Kings: the Lay of Al-Ahmar curses them as the retainers who led the king astray. Sobek is secretly worshipped by some mercenaries, and the Temple of Silence praises Hermanubis. In 6.6 Heryshaf remarks that to today's people the Ibis King seems a kind and passionate god.

## secrets
### ULTRA
Bennu and Shesepankh were constructs with their own will, a fact that only became clear in the 6.6 story. Opening Aaru would end the Golden Slumber, the afterlife Deshret built for the souls of his people. In 6.6 Nefer's answer "time" is what freed the Kings, and Thoth speculates that Deshret may have built Aaru in an attempt to reverse time itself. That is Thoth's guess, not confirmed.

## status
@version: 6.6
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Pillars no longer act as an order. Heryshaf, Sobek and Thoth are free, and Bennu and Shesepankh are awake. Hermanubis is gone, and Apep keeps her distance.
### STANDARD +
Heryshaf and Thoth have settled, for the time being, in the Temple of Silence, subject to Sethos's agreement. They declined to move their statues there, as Thoth refuses to leave his statue in Hermanubis's home. Aaru is open and the people of Sumeru may modify it. No new content about the Pillars was found for 6.7 to 7.1.

## entry: The Three Retainers
@keys: Three Retainers, Goat King, Ibis King, Crocodile King, Minister of Ministers, Captain of Captains, Scribe of Scribes
### STANDARD
Heryshaf, Sobek and Thoth were the highest-ranking Pillars. The Lay of Al-Ahmar curses them as the three retainers who flattered Deshret into seeking forbidden knowledge. Heryshaf is enigmatic and neutral and gives few opinions, but he is dry and sharp in 6.6. Sobek was once hot-tempered and competitive but has grown easygoing after thousands of years of tabletop games. Thoth is clever, quick-witted and fond of chess. His nickname for the others, "Goatie" and "Croco", appears in 6.6. Their long confinement softened them, and Thoth says prison can teach a person to cherish what they have.
### LARGE +
Thoth says Heryshaf and Sobek could not sit still for chess and played badly, yet the three remained good friends. Heryshaf concedes that the three of them spent their past on misguided ventures that wasted time.

## entry: Bennu and Shesepankh
@keys: Bennu, Shesepankh, Saint Bennu, Saint Shesepankh, Most-Radiant, Most-Holy
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Bennu and Shesepankh were intelligent constructs made by Deshret, not gods. Bennu guarded the gates of Aaru and the Golden Slumber within, and is serious, earnest and stern with careless colleagues. Shesepankh oversaw all the Jinn, whose mother was Nabu Malikata, and is leisurely, curious and fond of questions. They stood still after Deshret's death. In 6.6, Heryshaf woke them by invoking the right of the Three Kings, and they responded "Saint Bennu" and "Saint Shesepankh" with their will awakening. Bennu has said he admires Hermanubis most among the Pillars, while Shesepankh misses Hermanubis's willingness to take responsibility.

## entry: Hermanubis and the Temple of Silence
@keys: Hermanubis, Greatest of Sages, Sage of Sages, Temple of Silence, Ba fragment, Ba fragments
@keys2: Pillars, King Deshret, Cyno, Sethos
### STANDARD
Hermanubis was a Tighnar sage and warrior picked by Deshret to be his familiar, the only Pillar to rise from humanity. He was sincere, steadfast and kind, and he guided the priests. After Deshret's fall he took his followers to Tulaytullah and founded the Temple of Silence to guard Deshret's knowledge and technology. When the king of Gurabad attacked, he shattered himself and left his power to his followers. The wiki says two Ba fragments of his power were later held by the Temple, and that they were fused within Cyno. Heryshaf called Cyno "Hermanubis's representative" in 6.6.

## entry: Apep and the seventh seat
@keys: Dragon King, supreme spirit, Dendro Dragon Sovereign
@keys2: Pillars, King Deshret
### STANDARD
Deshret named the Dendro Dragon Apep a Pillar in his pact with her, but she never accepted it, and the other six never met her in council. Thoth jokes that she is like a card one never draws. Her relations with Deshret were a bargain for forbidden knowledge. In 6.6 Heryshaf says Apep and Hermanubis's representative had both voted ahead of time to open Aaru.

## entry: Aaru and the three questions
@keys: three questions, Golden Slumber, Aaru's Shut, Nabu Malikata's questions
### LARGE
Deshret locked the three Kings in Aaru and said they could be freed if humans answered the three questions that Nabu Malikata had put to him. They were what causes our rise and fall, what obliterates love and hate, and what determines truth and falsehood. Goudarz, Makhaira and Nefer each gave an answer, but only one more answer counted. Thoth spoke through the cats near his ruins, including one called Ashru, which Nefer later took with her. In 6.6 Nefer gave "time", and the seal broke.

## entry: The Pillars at Aaru's Shut (6.6)
@keys: Aaru vote, Pillars' council, unanimous consent
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The council that opened Aaru took place after the Traveler's party reached Aaru's Shut in the Mausoleum of King Deshret. Heryshaf, Thoth and Sobek awakened Bennu and Shesepankh, and the five present voted. Shesepankh asked what a unanimous vote means when Apep never answers. Heryshaf made Thoth accountable for the claim that Apep was healing Nahida, as Thoth's disciples had told them. Both constructs approved. Bennu said the Golden Slumber would end. By Heryshaf's final words, the gates of Aaru were reopened.

## entry: The Pillars' sages in an alchemy tale
@keys: Principle of Equilibrium, Exalted Earth
@availability: limited
### ULTRA
The description of the 4-star polearm Dialogues of the Desert Sages, which was available in the Alchemical Ascension event (2024-03-14), retells a story of the Seven Sages. The Crocodile King and the Ibis King bet on which effect would win out: that of a man blessed by an Oceanid, whose touch turned liquids into fine wine, or that of a man cursed by an ancient god's tomb, whose touch slowly turned things to silver. They asked Hermanubis, who said the two powers clash and each must yield, which he named the principle of equilibrium. The cup turned to silver and the liquid became fine liquor with silver grains. The text also summarises the other Pillars.
