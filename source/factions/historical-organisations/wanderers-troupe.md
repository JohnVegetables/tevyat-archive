## summary
### LITE
The Wanderer's Troupe, also called the Swordsman's Troupe, was a group of travelling musicians who were also skilled fighters. A thousand years ago they wandered across Teyvat, then ended their journey in Mondstadt, where they stormed the city to overthrow its tyrannical aristocracy. They failed, and the troupe broke up, but its memory lives on in artifact and weapon lore and in the people it inspired.
### STANDARD +
Its four known members were the Conductor, the Sojourner (a harpist), the Dawnlight Swordswoman and Kreuzlied. They owned no sheet music, played swords for flutes and bows for harps, and performed for friends and for foes alike. The troupe's story is told only through item text and a few related books, so details are sparse and its members are known by titles rather than names.

## members
### STANDARD
- **The Conductor**: the leader. He kept a book of the troupe's journeys called The Widsith and died holding it.
- **The Sojourner (the harpist)**: a privileged man from Fontaine who left home to wander. A skilled archer, he wielded The Stringless, a harp that was also a bow.
- **The Dawnlight Swordswoman (the Sword Dancer)**: wielder of The Flute, a sword that sang as she swung it. Her origin is unknown.
- **Kreuzlied**: a disowned member of the Lawrence Clan and the last to join. He carried The Bell, a greatsword with a clock in it.
### LARGE +
The meeting order was: the Conductor and the Sojourner first, then the Dawnlight Swordswoman, then Kreuzlied. It is unknown whether the troupe had other members. Chronologically it grew by chance meetings, and a tavern scene in the Goblet of the Sojourner lore has the harpist introducing a young woman he had met to his companions.

## goals
### STANDARD
The troupe set out to perform and to see the world, and its members decided there was so much to sing about that they wrote their journey down in The Widsith. Once in Mondstadt, they learned how the aristocracy treated the people and decided to end its rule by force.

## methods
### STANDARD
Music and combat were one skill. The Flute made a flute-like sound depending on its swing angle, The Stringless fired arrows that followed its tune, and The Bell's clockwork gave the troupe a combat rhythm. The harpist reportedly lured birds with his harp and shot them down, and never looked at the sky while playing, which one companion read as the hunter's mercy and others read as arrogance. Artifact lore says the troupe's performances had two audiences, friends and foes, and that they once entered Mare Jivari to perform for their enemies.

## history
### STANDARD
**Wandering.** The troupe travelled all over Teyvat and through harsh lands such as the desert and the burning Mare Jivari. Members came from many places. **Arrival in Mondstadt.** Their journey ended in Mondstadt around the time of Ludi Harpastum. The harpist fell in love with a woman who had been chosen as the Harpastum maiden, the one who would be claimed by a member of the tyrannical Lawrence Clan. **The uprising.** The troupe stormed Mondstadt's city to overthrow the aristocracy. It failed: the Conductor and the Sojourner died in the fighting, the Sojourner covering his friends' retreat and playing until he ran out of songs and arrows. The Dawnlight Swordswoman was enslaved as a gladiator. Kreuzlied was left alive because of his privileged birth, and what punishment he received was never made public.
### LARGE +
**Aftermath.** The troupe broke up. In item lore, the Sojourner's harp was stripped of its strings, The Bell was soaked in acid, and The Flute was buried. The Dawnlight Swordswoman kept singing of freedom as a gladiator and eventually died in the arena. A young squire, later the Dawn Knight Ragnvindr, watched her final fight and was moved. He befriended her, buried her with her sword, and took the title Dawn Knight in homage to her name. Kreuzlied went on to found a secret society against the aristocracy and helped Vennessa when she rebelled. The Widsith's own text says the troupe is nearly forgotten but that the spirit of resistance still runs in Mondstadters.
### ULTRA +
**What came after.** According to one popular rumour recorded in The Bell's lore, Kreuzlied's society kept working in the background, helping the Knights of Favonius with deeds no knight should do, and the Wolf Pup Rostam supposedly secretly ran it. These are rumours, not established fact. In the manga Prologue, Venti glimpses three silhouettes while an apple vendor warns him of the aristocracy, which the wiki guesses are troupe members, though that is not confirmed.

## relations
### STANDARD
- **Mondstadt Aristocracy and Lawrence Clan**: the troupe's enemies. Kreuzlied was himself born a Lawrence.
- **Ragnvindr**: inspired by the Dawnlight Swordswoman, he became the Dawn Knight and turned against the corrupt nobles.
- **Vennessa**: aided by Kreuzlied's society when she rebelled.
### LARGE +
- **Fontaine**: the harpist's homeland. Item lore says the ladies of the Court of Fontaine mourned when he left without a farewell.

## reputation
### STANDARD
The troupe is largely forgotten. The Widsith's lore says the memory of the rebellion faded, yet the spirit of resistance remains. Item lore treats it with fondness: the troupe's end is mourned, and the dying notes of its instruments are described as the last sound it made.

## status
### LITE
The troupe is long gone. Nothing in canon through 7.1 shows its members returning, though the Wanderer's Troupe weapons and artifacts survive in the world.
### STANDARD +
No story content about the troupe was found for 6.0 to 7.1.

## entry: The Sojourner's love story
@keys: Sojourner, harpist, The Stringless, Resolution of Sojourner
@keys2: Wanderer's Troupe, Ludi Harpastum, Fontaine
### STANDARD
The harpist, who came from Fontaine and was well loved by the ladies of its Court, left home to follow a wandering life and seek his destiny. In Mondstadt, by a lake, he met a stern, unsmiling maiden who was to be offered up as the Ludi Harpastum maiden of the Lawrence nobles. Item lore records his promise to break her chains and see her smile once. When the uprising came, he fired his last arrow for his comrades, for freedom and against the nobles who had taken her joy. He died unnamed in a foreign land, regretting only that he never sang another love song. Artifact lore (Feather of Homecoming) notes that the Mondstadt sky reminded him of his homeland. The wiki notes that "Sojourner" is an unofficial name.

## entry: The Dawnlight Swordswoman
@keys: Dawnlight Swordswoman, Sword Dancer, The Flute
@keys2: Wanderer's Troupe, Ragnvindr, gladiator
### STANDARD
She wielded the sword The Flute and moved to music, "beautiful as the light on water, elegant as the chirping lark". After the failed uprising she was made a gladiator but still sang of freedom. Ballads of the Squire quotes her challenging a young Ragnvindr about the lives he placed on a pedestal, asking whether he had not once known freedom. Ragnvindr watched her last fight, buried her with her sword and named himself the Dawn Knight after her title.

## entry: The Widsith
@keys: Widsith, The Widsith
@keys2: Wanderer's Troupe, Conductor
### STANDARD
The Widsith is the troupe's travel journal. The members had no sheet music and turned the notes they played into words. The Conductor held it tightly even in death. The weapon of the same name is a catalyst. Its text says the troupe's song was an anthem of rebellion and that their last act was an attempt to storm the city and sentence its rulers.

## entry: The Bell and Kreuzlied
@keys: Kreuzlied, The Bell
@keys2: Wanderer's Troupe, Lawrence
### STANDARD
Kreuzlied, a Lawrence who became the troupe's outcast member, wielded a greatsword with a clock in it. Its ticking gave the troupe a combat rhythm, and its text calls its strike the toll of the bell of judgment. After the troupe's defeat the weapon was soaked in acid and rusted. Kreuzlied avoided harsh punishment because of his birth, and later founded a secret society to carry on his companions' work.
