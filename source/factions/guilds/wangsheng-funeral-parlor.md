## summary
### LITE
The Wangsheng Funeral Parlor is the funeral house of Liyue Harbor, on Feiyun Slope. It arranges burials, cremations and memorial rites for the dead of every rank, and it also performs the rites that send off adepti. It is run by the Hu family, which has held the Directorship for 77 generations. The current Director is Hu Tao, and Zhongli works there as consultant. The parlour also keeps a duty most people never see: watching the border between life and death, and burning away the fetor that leaks across it.
### STANDARD +
In Liyue, people tend not to talk to Wangsheng staff or to dwell on what they do. The parlour treats that as part of the job, and its stated client philosophy is that everyone who departs, whatever their wealth or standing, deserves a ceremony that honours them.
### LARGE +
The parlour began, by the wiki's account, as something closer to a doctor's office, and grew into a funeral house over time. Its Chinese name, 往生堂 (Wǎngshēng-táng), is rendered "Hall of Rebirth" in the wiki's translation.

## structure
### STANDARD
The head is the Director, a title passed down the Hu family; Hu Tao is the 77th. Under her are undertakers who carry out ceremonies and prepare burials and cremations. The wiki lists the Ferrylady and Meng among them. Hu Tao also invites consultants from different trades to lecture the undertakers once the parlour closes each day, so that funeral practice is taught as a body of knowledge, not as habit. Zhongli is the most revered of these lecturers. Commissions are taken at a board outside the door, where clients write requests on blank wooden tiles and slide them under the door.
### LARGE +
Hu Tao is said to know the parlour's rules by heart. Funerals follow strict steps (a wake, a burial, a memorial plaque), but she instructs her undertakers to follow clients' wishes: some want a quiet ceremony, some a lively one, and wealthy clients may want something grand. The Director personally leads the undertakers through lamp-lit alleys when a funeral procession goes out, and that is the one time her dignified side comes forward.

## leadership
### LITE
Hu Tao is the 77th Director. She is a cheerful, eccentric young woman who treats the work seriously beneath the jokes. Zhongli is the parlour's consultant; Hu Tao teases him for being old-fashioned while trusting him more than anyone else.
### STANDARD +
Before her came Old Hu, the 75th Director and her grandfather, and Hu Tao's father, the 76th. Her great-uncle, Baizhu's master, was Old Hu's younger brother, according to the wiki. Zhongli handles ceremonies that rely on exact tradition. His profile says Hu Tao asks him to help with the rites for sending adepti away, because the tradition now exists only in texts and almost nobody alive has seen it performed.
### LARGE +
Hu Tao took over the Directorship young, after the Wuwang Hill disaster killed her father and, soon afterwards, her grandfather. Her father was considered one of the family's finest ritualists. Zhongli's profile calls Hu Tao the current owner of the parlour and himself a friend in more or less the same business, and the wiki records that he became its consultant after the Rite of Descension.

## members
### STANDARD
- **Hu Tao**: 77th Director.
- **Zhongli**: consultant who lectures the undertakers on rites and etiquette. In the Archon Quest Tartaglia introduces him to the Traveler as consultant to "an organization known as Wangsheng" and a trusted associate of the Fatui.
- **The Ferrylady**: an undertaker who stands outside the parlour at night. She prepares ashes silently while families stay shut in their rooms, and she does not speak to the bereaved, following old custom.
- **Meng**: a newer undertaker who joined, according to the story quest, so he could ask the Director to search for his dead childhood friend Big G.
- **Past Directors**: Old Hu (75th) and Hu Tao's father (76th), both dead.
### LARGE +
The Ferrylady is unused to being addressed; if the Traveler hints they are "something else", she takes them for a lost soul and bids them go into the light. Her title evokes the ferryman who carries the dead across the water, a comparison the wiki notes. Meng is described in the story quest as a nervous newcomer who at first takes the Traveler and Paimon for ghosts; after Big G's farewell Hu Tao grants him unpaid time off to process his grief.
### ULTRA +
In the Springtime Charms event (limited-time), the Feiyun Commerce Guild's butler An Su discusses funeral arrangements for an ailing patriarch with Hu Tao and the Ferrylady follows them up, and Gaming delivers funeral goods to Yilong Wharf, showing the parlour supplies as well as conducts funerals. The Hu family also includes Baizhu's master, according to the wiki, but the parlour and Bubu Pharmacy are separate institutions.

## goals
### LARGE
Two commitments are named. First, to give everyone who dies a ceremony that honours them, regardless of wealth or rank; this is described as the parlour's client service philosophy. Second, to keep the balance between life and death in Liyue: monitoring its "border", making sure death does not spill out, and burning off any fetor growths that appear. Hu Tao also wants the business to grow, and invents marketing schemes with that in mind, though they tend to backfire.
### ULTRA +
Hu Tao's view of death is that it is a natural part of life and that people should enjoy their short lives. She dislikes attempts to prolong a natural lifespan, a stance that colours her dealings with Baizhu. She also says the living give Mora to see the dead on their way, so the parlour has a responsibility to both sides and must keep both satisfied.

## methods
### STANDARD
Funerals follow traditional steps: a wake, burial, a memorial plaque, cremation where requested. Hu Tao's own approach is flexible about style but strict about respect. The parlour's secret technique is the Art of Homa, which purifies the border between life and death and burns away fetor. Practitioners use Homa altars and the Staff of Homa. Hu Tao says doing it right takes great focus and no hesitation in the face of death; a faulty performance can shorten the caster's life or even kill them at once, depending on how much fetor they take in.
### LARGE +
Hu Tao's repertoire includes showmanship as well as ritual. In the story quest she stages an "exorcism" for a worried client, using a Ley Line Monolith as a prop; hilichurls drawn to it prove real, and she later tells him it was a prank and that he was never cursed. She explains that frightened people have to be convinced with something tangible, by making a show of destroying the bogeyman they believe in. For adepti farewells, the parlour follows ancient protocols on costume, timing, place, items, weather, length of ceremony and the size and make-up of the audience.
### ULTRA +
Entering the border itself is a special procedure. Hu Tao leads the party in from Wuwang Hill, warns visitors not to get worked up or she will have to erase their memories, and describes the border as an ordinary-looking place most people never know about, where the Ley Lines run haywire. There she holds farewell ceremonies for the dead who are ready to go. She also asks the dead questions; when she asked about the Traveler's missing sibling, none of the answers offered was worth following up.

## history
### STANDARD
**Origin.** By the parlour's own account, after the Archon War the Yaksha accumulated karma while fighting vengeful gods, and part of it became a plague that swept Liyue. Frightened people took it for a divine curse. A few realised the plague could be stopped by purifying the air and burning the infected dead; they became the parlour's founders, and after many years the plague ended. Since then their successors have preserved the balance between life and death.

**Recent generations.** Old Hu and Hu Tao's father were the 75th and 76th Directors. Ten years before the Traveler arrived, the two went to Wuwang Hill to burn away a runaway growth of fetor. Her father, unable to let go of his regrets, faltered at the last moment and died on the spot. Old Hu contained the damage, but the fetor he absorbed killed him soon afterwards, before he was sixty. The nearby village was abandoned and the hill changed permanently. Hu Tao succeeded them.
### LARGE +
**Hu Tao's early life.** Hu Tao's profile says she began living at the parlour at eight and learned funeral etiquette there. After Old Hu's funeral she searched for his spirit at Wuwang Hill and found no trace; she concluded he had already passed on because he died without regrets, and was inspired by that to live fully. When she returned home she found she had received a Pyro Vision. In the story quest she says that on her many visits to the border she has never seen any former Director there, so she knows they all died without regrets.

**Zhongli joins.** After staging his own death at the Rite of Descension, Zhongli became the parlour's consultant. He had no funds of his own and, per the wiki, often relies on the parlour or Tartaglia to pay his way.

**Papilio Charontis (1.3).** In the story quest, the Traveler helps Hu Tao and Meng deal with Meng's lost friend Big G. The party goes into the border, Big G says farewell and moves on, and Meng is given leave. The quest is also where Hu Tao gives the short history of the plague.
### ULTRA +
**Springtime Charms (5.3, limited-time event).** During the Lantern Rite event, fetor leaking from the border near Wuwang Hill made elderly Liyue residents ill. Hu Tao proposed to redo the damaged stretch of the border with the Art of Homa, spread across Liyue's Ley Lines using the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method, an ancient array used in the Sanctification of Tao Dou. Ningguang and Hu Tao arranged the overseers, among them Keqing, Qiqi, Baizhu, Ganyu, Xiao, Beidou and Yelan. Hu Tao herself took the Gate of Death, the one that is obfuscated to seal the fetor. Overcome, she reached the border, where Old Hu and her father told her they had come only because they missed her and that her time had not come. The wiki states that the Traveler, a Descender whom the Ley Lines do not record, took her place in the ritual. The event also tells how the Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame died in the original Sanctification and how Wuwang Kid passed her arts to the parlour.

## relations
### LARGE
- **Fatui**: Tartaglia says the Fatui are glad to do business with "friends who walk in the shadows", and that the parlour ostensibly does not take part in assassination. The relationship is never explained.
- **Liyue Qixing and the Ministry of Civil Affairs**: they work with Hu Tao when the border is threatened; in Springtime Charms Hu Tao informed the Ministry about the fetor leaks.
- **Adepti**: the parlour keeps the rites for sending adepti off, and the Art of Homa traces to the Eight Adepts.
- **Bubu Pharmacy**: Baizhu and Hu Tao disagree about prolonging life, and Hu Tao once tried repeatedly to cremate Qiqi, who is a zombie, until Baizhu stopped her.
### ULTRA +
- **Qiqi**: Hu Tao's attempts to give her "eternal peace" ended when Qiqi's will to live convinced Hu Tao to stop; she now pampers her, though Qiqi still fears her.
- **Xingqiu and Chongyun**: friends who trade verse with Hu Tao in the parlour's main hall.
- **Adventurers' Guild**: Hu Tao tried to strike a business deal with Lan, the guild's branch manager, which failed.
- **Feiyun Commerce Guild**: arranged funerals for a patriarch during Springtime Charms.

## reputation
### LARGE
Liyue's people are polite but wary of the parlour. It is generally not customary to speak to its staff, who are associated with death. Since Hu Tao took over, her tactful ceremonies have changed some superstitious people's attitudes toward funerals. She herself is better known in Liyue Harbor as an eccentric and a poet than as Director.
### ULTRA +
The wiki reports that when the Qixing turned their suspicions on the Fatui, the Millelith suspected Hu Tao of collaborating because of the parlour's ties; Hu Tao kept things under control and the case was dropped. This comes from the wiki's Hu Tao/Storyline page and is only medium confidence.

## secrets
@spoiler: major
### ULTRA
The parlour's consultant, Zhongli, is secretly Rex Lapis, the Geo Archon, who staged his death at the Rite of Descension. That is revealed in the Archon Quest; the parlour is where he lives out his retirement. The border and the Director's visits to it are known to few, and Hu Tao warns visitors there that she will erase their memories if they do not keep calm.

## entry: Art of Homa and fetor
@keys: Art of Homa, Homa altar, fetor, Staff of Homa
### STANDARD
Fetor is the energy that leaks out when the border between life and death is disturbed. It ages the living, causes weakness and coma, and can kill; adepti can see it and humans generally cannot. The parlour's secret Art of Homa burns it away with altars and the Staff of Homa, and demands total focus. Minor leaks are routine for the parlour. A major leak may require purifying the whole border, which is what happened in Springtime Charms.
### LARGE +
The first user of the technique was the Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame, one of the Eight Adepts, who sacrificed herself in the Sanctification of Tao Dou. Wuwang Kid passed her arts to the founders of the parlour. Qiqi, being a zombie, is somewhat resistant to fetor.

## entry: Border between life and death
@keys: border between life and death, Wuwang Hill border
@keys2: Hu Tao, Wangsheng, fetor
### STANDARD
The "border" is a place most people never know exists, reached from Wuwang Hill. Ley Lines run wild there, and the dead who are unwilling or unable to leave linger. Hu Tao holds farewell ceremonies there and asks the dead questions. The parlour monitors it so that death does not spill out. Visitors must keep calm and follow Hu Tao's lead.
### LARGE +
A disturbance at the border ten years before the Traveler's arrival led to the deaths of the 76th and 75th Directors. A larger one in Springtime Charms required a ritual across Liyue's Ley Lines.

@keys: Springtime Charms, Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method, Eight Gates, Sanctification of Tao Dou
@keys2: Hu Tao, Wangsheng, fetor
@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In the limited-time event Springtime Charms (5.3), Hu Tao proposed purifying a damaged stretch of the border with the Art of Homa, using the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method to carry the rite across Liyue's Ley Lines. Eight gates had overseers: Keqing (Life), Hu Tao (Death), Qiqi and Baizhu (Repose), Ganyu (Concealment), Ningguang and Lan Yan (Initiation), Xiao (Terrain), Beidou and the Crux (Fright), and Yelan (Injury). Lan Yan made rattan dolls of Hu Tao to share the burden of the fetor. Hu Tao took the Gate of Death and was overcome, and the Traveler took her place.

## entry: Wangsheng and the Fatui
@keys: Wangsheng and the Fatui, Fatui associate
@keys2: Wangsheng, Zhongli, Tartaglia
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
In Archon Quest Chapter I Act II, Tartaglia introduces Zhongli as consultant to Wangsheng and as a trusted Fatui associate, saying the parlour understands discretion and that the Fatui are glad to do business with friends who walk in the shadows. He adds that the parlour ostensibly does not do assassinations and does not finish the thought. Nothing more is established; the wiki reports that the Millelith later suspected Hu Tao of collaboration and dropped the matter.

## entry: Commission board and special offers
@keys: Wangsheng board, wooden tiles, commission tiles
@keys2: Wangsheng, Hu Tao
### STANDARD
Outside the parlour hangs a board of wooden tiles. Clients write their commission on a tile and slide it under the door. During Hu Tao's story quest, her advertising scheme appears on the back of each tile as a "buy one get one free" offer for whoever holds it.
