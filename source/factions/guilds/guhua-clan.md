## summary
### LITE
The Guhua Clan is a Liyue martial arts school that honours the wandering hero Guhua and teaches sword and spear. It was once famous across Liyue and beyond. Today only a few members remain, and its base, Wangshan Hall on Mt. Lingmeng, is falling apart. Xingqiu is its best-known member.
### STANDARD +
Its belief, as former member Chen the Sharp quotes it, is that the noblest martial virtue is to pursue what benefits other people. It was formerly called the Guhua Brotherhood and later the Guhua Sect.
### LARGE +
The clan is a small and often comic presence in Liyue, but it carries real history. Its members are proud and anxious in equal parts: they cling to the old arts while short on money, students and a clear leader. Guhua himself never founded anything. The clan was built afterwards by someone claiming to be his disciple.

## structure
### STANDARD
There is no visible hierarchy beyond seniority and epithets. Members use titles such as Cloudpiercing Arrow (Huang the Third), Snowstriding Sword (Wen the Fourth) and Guhua Guru of Feiyun Fame (Xingqiu). Wangshan Hall is the headquarters and training ground. Visitors who ask for the master at the gate are told he is off in the mountains.
### LARGE +
Wen the Fourth counts the clan's masters by generation. The 11th Master, Yingmin, known as the Piercing Blade, judged the old techniques too hard for general study and had a set of easier entry-level forms made. Zhangsun Jijing, the Rainbearer, is another named Guhua disciple, now preserved in the Temple of Space.

## leadership
### STANDARD
No master is named in the present day. Guhua is honoured as founder and namesake but was never a master and never took a disciple. The actual founder was Boxuan, called Master Baixuan, a man Guhua had once favoured as a boy. After Guhua's death Baixuan founded the brotherhood in his name and called himself Guhua's disciple. Xingqiu is the clan's most celebrated talent: his profile says that when the clan's head read his verse on the Martial Principle, he wept and declared that it was the clan that needed Xingqiu, not the other way round.

## members
### LITE
Members the Traveler can meet include Huang the Third and Wen the Fourth at Wangshan Hall, Sun Yu and Little Que'er at Huaguang Stone Forest (until Sun Yu leaves), and Xingqiu. Former members include Chen the Sharp, a grilled-fish vendor in Liyue Harbor, and Zhiruo, a waitress.
### STANDARD +
Huang the Third guards the gate and, hoping to raise Mora for repairs, lures visitors in Yilong Wharf into a paid tour of the hall with inflated claims. Wen the Fourth is a gloomy senior who worries about what happens to the clan's books when the last old members are gone. Wakamatsu is an Inazuman who slipped out during the Sakoku Decree, inspired by a martial tale, to seek training at the clan. The wiki lists him among current members, though his place is informal. After the Traveler opens Wangshan Hall's hidden chamber, he begs Wen the Fourth to take him as a disciple, and Wen tells him to start with the basics.
### LARGE +
Chen the Sharp spent ten years training in seclusion with the clan and now uses its bladework to scale and gut the fish he sells. He thinks this is a worthwhile use of the art. Zhiruo left the clan and works at the Third-Round Knockout restaurant; the wiki says she remembers Sun Yu and Xingqiu from her time in the clan. Zhangsun Jijing, the Rainbearer, is a historical disciple who trained in Raincutter.
### ULTRA +
After the secret chamber is opened, a Stonehide Lawachurl near Wangshan Hall is labelled an uninitiated Guhua disciple and can be defeated for an achievement. This is a joke, not membership.

## goals
### STANDARD
The stated aim of the remaining members is to restore the clan's former reputation, or at least to keep Wangshan Hall standing. Sun Yu had sought the adepti's advice on reviving the clan before concluding that this was going nowhere. Huang the Third and Wen the Fourth worry about leaving no successors.

## methods
### STANDARD
The clan teaches the Three Guhua Greats: Light Piercer for polearms, Rain Cutter (Raincutter) for swords, and Life Ender for those who master both. Guhua is said to have worked them out at Lingmeng Chamber, now Wangshan Hall. New members start with the fundamentals, such as practising against wooden stakes. In its prime the clan travelled the world, doing justice with sword and spear.
### LARGE +
Xingqiu's profile says Guhua weapon arts treat the weapon as an extension of the body. Xingqiu's own insight was that a Vision should be seen as an extension of oneself and the weapon as an extension of the Vision, so both weapon arts are arts of the eye. He wrote a verse on this after four years of study, and it is sealed at Wangshan Hall. The reason given for sealing it is his dreadful handwriting. Xingqiu also found that the clan taught stage tricks (quicklime, smoking sand, paper fish, sword-swallowing, fire-breathing) that he valued as part of a heroic repertoire.
### ULTRA +
Guhua himself wielded a claymore, Rainslasher, described as a dimly glowing greatsword with no sharp edge. Mountain bandits reportedly called it the Beacon of the Nether, and while Guhua lived, bandits dared not act.

## history
### STANDARD
Guhua was a peace-keeping martial nomad who trained at Lingmeng Chamber and never meant to start a school. After his death the boy he had once favoured, Boxuan or Master Baixuan, founded the Guhua Brotherhood. In its heyday it had a large following, and its members travelled Teyvat, known for sword and spear. Over the following centuries members deserted, and it went through several name changes, shrinking to the small Guhua Sect and then to today's Clan.
### LARGE +
Guhua's notes in the hall's library show a very human man. His training was derailed by falls from the Plumwatcher Piles, he took up fish farming and filled pages with fish recipes. When young Baixuan brought him a mutton and fish dish while he recovered from an injury, he traded his Life Ender technique for the recipe. In the hidden chamber the Traveler finds an engraving attributed to Guhua whose grim tone is unlike his notes, asking what readers will do when they reach a dead end.
### ULTRA +
During the 2021 Lantern Rite Tales event, Sun Yu and Zhiruo each recall how Guhua disciples once released Xiao Lanterns together from a spot known only to members. That tradition has lapsed. Sun Yu later concluded, in a 3.3 commission series, that staying near Mt. Aocang would not revive the clan, and he left Liyue with a Fontaine merchant to look for answers abroad. In 4.4 the Traveler solved Wangshan Hall's trials and found a secret chamber that Huang the Third apparently did not know existed.

## relations
### STANDARD
The clan has no formal alliances. Huang the Third drops his tour swindle when the Traveler threatens to report him to the Millelith. Xingqiu's family, the Feiyun Commerce Guild, is wealthy; his father let him learn martial arts from the clan to keep him out of the Wanwen Bookhouse for a day, knowing it was a declining art. The luxury dojo Xingqiu first trained in was not the clan's and had been rented to keep up appearances.

## reputation
### STANDARD
In its heyday the clan's name rang like steel through Liyue Harbor. Now it is a faded name that many treat as a joke. Its members know it: Little Que'er calls it an archonforsaken martial arts club.

## secrets
### LARGE
Wangshan Hall's hidden library and secret chamber were unknown to the modern members, who remembered only legends of a treasure. The chamber lies behind a rusted door and elemental-colour book puzzles and holds trials and Guhua's engraved message. Huang the Third gave the Traveler the hall's backup key after the Traveler humoured him. He admitted the Traveler was more honest than other visitors.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Guhua Clan is nearly extinct. A few members still keep Wangshan Hall, and no source shows a revival.
### STANDARD +
Sun Yu has left for Fontaine. Wen the Fourth and Huang the Third remain at the hall, the former with a possible new student in Wakamatsu. The clan's future is unknown.

## entry: The Three Guhua Greats

@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Three Guhua Greats are the clan's three secret arts: Light Piercer (spear), Rain Cutter (sword) and Life Ender (both together). Guhua is said to have worked them out at Lingmeng Chamber. Xingqiu's profile describes them as arts refined over the generations that waited for a worthy successor, and he was the one who grasped the Martial Principle.

## entry: Wangshan Hall and Its Secret Chamber
@keys: Wangshan Hall, Lingmeng Chamber
@version: 4.4
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Wangshan Hall on Mt. Lingmeng, in Chenyu Vale, is the clan's base. It is run down, and Huang the Third gives paid tours to raise money. Beneath the library the Traveler discovers a hidden chamber sealed by scrolls readable only with Elemental Sight, then trial areas and Guhua's inscriptions. Wen the Fourth says the hall was where Guhua was said to have reached enlightenment.

## entry: Sun Yu and Little Que'er
@keys: Sun Yu, Little Que'er
@version: 1.0

### STANDARD
Sun Yu and Little Que'er are two young Guhua disciples who grew up training together. Sun Yu wanted to revive the clan and sought the adepti's guidance near Huaguang Stone Forest. Que'er is hostile to strangers who approach him and has feelings for Sun Yu that he does not notice; he regards her as a sister. In the 3.3 commission series he gives up the search and decides to travel abroad. Que'er refuses to go and is left heartbroken, mistakenly believing he never cared for her.

### STANDARD
Huang the Third, the Cloudpiercing Arrow, is the cheerful swindler at Yilong Wharf who is fond of the hall and ashamed of what he must do to maintain it. Wen the Fourth, the Snowstriding Sword, is the gloomy senior who explains the clan's history. They talk like old comrades and tease each other about who was the better fighter a few years ago.
