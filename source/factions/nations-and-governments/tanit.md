## summary
### LITE
The Tanit were a nomadic Eremite tribe of Sumeru's Desert of Hadramaveth, matrilineal and led by a Matriarch and Nine Elders. They worshipped the Goddess of Flowers, traded in crocodile goods, spices and oils, and raised stolen or lost children as Falcons. Their last camp, the Tanit Camps, lay on the desert's southern edge. The tribe was destroyed in the events of Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters...
### STANDARD +
The Tanit were the oldest of the wandering desert tribes and had many branches. They valued words and knowledge but distrusted the Akasha. They had no unified law and no written rules, only what was "written in their blood and habits", and they hated traitors from within more than any outsider. The Traveler meets them through Jeht, who joined the tribe after her father Jebrael's death.

## structure
### STANDARD
The tribe was matrilineal. A Matriarch led it, and Nine Elders held authority beside her. Babel had most of the other elders removed before the Traveler arrived, so by then power sat with her and her chosen lieutenants, notably Azariq, her "Left Hand". Warriors, guards and Falcons served under them. Jeht joined as a "half-outsider", since her father Jebrael had been born to the tribe and left it.
### LARGE +
The tribe had many branches, so the Tanit Camps were the largest and most recent settlement of a wider people rather than the whole of it. Trade was a main source of income, and the tribe also hunted Wenut for spices. Falcons were a special class: children taken in or plundered and raised to be devoted to the elders, of whom only a handful were favoured.

## leadership
### STANDARD
Babel Tanit was the Matriarch. She presented the tribe as a family and used that word to justify punishing anyone she called a traitor. She tasked Jeht with removing the remaining elders by accusing them of betrayal. In truth she was consolidating power for herself, with Azariq's help and with Snezhnayan supplies and technology obtained from the Fatui. Once she had what she wanted she turned on the Fatui, claiming she had never meant to share the Eternal Oasis with them.

## members
### STANDARD
Named members at the time of the Traveler's visit include Babel; Azariq, her trusted "son" and Left Hand; Aderfi, who fled after trying to have the Traveler removed; the guards Hugeweh and Mendas; the farmer Yuften; and the Falcons Nayram and Rezki, two of Babel's best. Jeht was a member until the tribe fell. Her father Jebrael and his companion Samail had belonged to the tribe earlier and left it.
### LARGE +
Others named in the wiki's member list include Masseira, who fled after The Fallen Falcon; Tadhla, the Bent-Winged Falcon; and elders such as Bleda, Gwaffah, Izder, Meddur, Tafreh, Teyya and Uzad, whom Jeht assassinated on Babel's orders. Tarafa was a mole Babel sent to kill her opponents, and Jebrael killed him before the story begins. Babel herself had a daughter whom she had lost to the sands before the Traveler arrived.

## goals
### STANDARD
Publicly, the Tanit sought to prosper as a tribe and to find the Eternal Oasis, a legendary place in the desert. Babel's own goal was larger. She wanted the Eternal Oasis, and after the Traveler became the master of the Jinni Liloupar, she wanted the Orchard of Pairidaeza too, so she could be the sole prophetess of Nabu Malikata and rebuild the glory of the desert empire that existed before its destruction, overthrowing the Sumeru Akademiya.

## methods
### STANDARD
The tribe fought with ordinary warriors and with Falcons, and it hired mercenaries when needed. Babel ruled by suspicion: accusations of treachery, assassination of rivals and elders, and sending groups of followers into dangerous places. The groups she sent to the Eternal Oasis died, and she made it taboo to follow them. She also sent scouts to other tribes to warn them of Jeht's "treachery" in advance, so the Eremites would see her as dangerous.
### LARGE +
The tribe kept an uneasy business relationship with the Sumeru Akademiya. A contract in the camp shows Babel hiring warriors, among them Azariq, to escort a Kshahrewar Dastur across the desert for a fee, with part of the pay for secrecy. Several books in the camp bear Akademiya Library emblems and are described as smuggled. Their pages are well worn, and some are books the Akademiya had refused to enter into the Akasha.

## history
### LARGE
**Earlier years.** The Tanit lived in the desert for an unknown time. A weapon's text suggests the Falcon custom began when "ascetics" left the priests of King Deshret and bent his archery ceremonies into training lost children. Babel herself was a Vahumana scholars' orphan taken in by an Eremite mercenary and raised as a Falcon of the tribe.

**Jebrael's exit.** Babel sent expeditions to Gurabad and toward the Eternal Oasis. Jebrael and Samail were in one of them, and Babel sent Tarafa to kill adherents of King Deshret. Jebrael saw through the plan, feigned death and killed Tarafa before he could kill Samail. The two left the tribe, and Babel came to regard Jebrael as a traitor.

**The Fatui and the elders.** The Fatui came seeking the Eternal Oasis. With Azariq's help, Babel had the other elders removed in exchange for Snezhnayan supplies and technology, then backstabbed her partners.
### ULTRA +
**Jeht joins.** After Jebrael's sacrifice at the end of Golden Slumber, his daughter Jeht found notes pointing to the Tanit and went to them. Babel took her in as a half-outsider and gave her work killing the remaining elders as "traitors".

**The Dirge of Bilqis.** Babel let the Traveler and Paimon into the camp through Jeht and sent Azariq to help them look for the Eternal Oasis. Aderfi tried to have the Traveler removed with hired Eremites and fled. Azariq later betrayed the party, was found to be working with the Fatui, and was killed by Jeht. The Traveler became the master of the Jinni Liloupar, and the party found the oasis and the way to the Orchard. Babel claimed to have already suspected Azariq.

**Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters...** Babel resolved to kill the Traveler, take Liloupar for herself and use its power to reach the Orchard. Jeht refused to kill her friend. Babel offered Jeht to the Fatui scientist Froderock Fronkonsteen as a "live sample" and told Jeht and the Traveler separately that the other had betrayed the tribe. When the Traveler found the truth and told Jeht, the Falcons Nayram and Rezki were defeated. Jeht and the Traveler returned, Babel confessed and ordered the tribe to attack, and the two defeated her and her people. Jeht killed Babel, who said as she died that scouts had already told the other Eremites of Jeht's "treachery".

## relations
### STANDARD
- **Eremites**: the Tanit are one of several desert tribes. Babel deliberately cast Jeht as a traitor to them, so the other tribes would turn on her.
- **Fatui**: Babel traded with them for supplies and technology, then double-crossed them. Azariq was secretly allied with them, and she later sold Jeht to their scientist Froderock Fronkonsteen.
- **Sumeru Akademiya**: ties of convenience. The Tanit hire out as guides and escorts and read Akademiya books, but distrust the Akasha.
### LARGE +
- **Vahumana**: after the tribe's fall, the Vahumana Dastur Tanjiyy came to give educational assistance to the Tanit under a deal Setaria had made, and found the camps empty. He worked out that the tribe had been wiped out but did not know the details.
- **Nabu Malikata**: the Tanit believe in the Goddess of Flowers. In The Dirge of Bilqis, the Traveler found that the goddess Babel worshipped was nowhere to be found.

## reputation
### LARGE
Among Sumeru's markets the Tanit were known for crocodile products, spices and essential oils. The Akademiya looked on the tribes mostly as desert folk to hire. Inside the tribe, Babel's reputation was one of strictness and family loyalty. Her private plans were different, and by the end she had spread word to other Eremites that Jeht was a traitor.

## secrets
### ULTRA
Most of what makes the Tanit's story significant was hidden from members and outsiders: Babel's killing of rival elders through Jeht, her private deal and double-cross with the Fatui, her wish to be the only prophetess of Nabu Malikata, and her plan to have Jeht killed. The Traveler learns the full picture only by the end of the final quest.

Babel Tanit was the Matriarch of the Tanit tribe, who raised herself to power by having elders removed and cheating the Fatui. She spoke warmly of the tribe as a family, and showed particular favour to Jeht, while planning to use or kill her. She died at Jeht's hand in Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters... ## entry: Tanit Falcons
@keys: Falcons, Falcon of the Tanit, Tanit Falcon
@keys2: Tanit, Eremite, Babel
### STANDARD
Falcons were children taken in or plundered by some desert tribes and raised devoted to the elders. In the Tanit, only a few became favoured. Babel, Tadhla (the Bent-Winged Falcon), Nayram and Rezki were Falcons. A weapon's text suggests the custom grew from the training of King Deshret's priests, in a distorted form.

## entry: The fall of the Tanit
@keys: fall of the Tanit, destruction of the Tanit, Tanit tribe destroyed
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
After the Traveler told Jeht of Babel's plot, the two returned to the Tanit Camps and fought Babel and the tribespeople loyal to her. Jeht killed Babel. Babel's final claim was that scouts had already told the Eremites that Jeht was a danger. Afterwards the camps were empty, and Tanjiyy of the Vahumana, who came to teach the tribe under Setaria's deal, assumed that the tribe had been annihilated.
