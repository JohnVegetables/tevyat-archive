## summary
### LITE
The Tri-Commission (Sanbugyou, "Three Commissioners") is the collective name for the three organisations that run Inazuma's government under the Raiden Shogun. The Tenryou Commission, led by the Kujou Clan, handles security and the military. The Kanjou Commission, led by the Hiiragi Clan, handles finance, customs and the borders. The Yashiro Commission, led by the Kamisato Clan, handles shrines, festivals and ceremonies. An Inazuman in Liyue compares them to Liyue's Qixing and Eight Trades and to the Knights of Favonius.
### STANDARD +
The three commissions can check one another. The Hiiragi and Kujou are described as political rivals who keep each other in balance. The Yashiro is the only one of the three that stayed out of the Fatui's scheme.

## structure
### STANDARD
Each commission has its own clan, its own commissioner and its own staff. The wiki lists them like this:
- **Tenryou Commission**: Kujou Clan; head of public security, military and police affairs, enforcer of the Vision Hunt Decree and of the Sakoku Decree against illegal entry or exit.
- **Kanjou Commission**: Hiiragi Clan; the financial arm, supervising the borders, trade and bureaucracy, and enforcing the Sakoku Decree for legal entry and exit.
- **Yashiro Commission**: Kamisato Clan; the cultural arm, handling ceremonies, celebratory events and public festivals, and managing shrines and temples. It is considered the commission closest to the Raiden Shogun.

Soldiers of the Shogun's Army are assigned to each commission.
### LARGE +
The wiki says the Tri-Commission meets at Tenshukaku to decide government affairs. Kamisato Ayaka says that when the Yashiro Commission proposed repealing the Vision Hunt Decree, the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions vetoed it, which suggests that proposals pass through some council and that the Yashiro cannot approach the Shogun directly. Naoe Hisamasa, who guards the area around Tenshukaku and says he takes orders only from the Shogun, tells petitioners to see the officers of the Tri-Commission.
### ULTRA +
The name echoes the Edo-period San-bugyou, a trio of magistrates for shrines and temples, finance and towns. The wiki sees the Yashiro, Kanjou and Tenryou as counterparts of those offices, though each Genshin commission has broader duties; the Kanjou, for example, also covers borders and foreign trade.

## leadership
### LITE
The Raiden Shogun rules over the three. The Kujou, Hiiragi and Kamisato clans lead the Tenryou, Kanjou and Yashiro Commissions. Kamisato Ayato leads the Yashiro Commission, which was untouched by the Fatui scandal.
### STANDARD +
Kujou Kamaji (Tenryou) and Hiiragi Chisato (Kanjou) are acting heads of their clans, as their fathers Kujou Takayuki and Hiiragi Shinsuke fell after the Fatui plot came out. In the Cypressus Custos Chapter a wedding announcement refers to both as Proxy Clan Heads. Kamisato Ayato leads the Yashiro Commission as clan head, and the wiki lists his sister Kamisato Ayaka among its leaders and Thoma among its staff.
### LARGE +
Generals Kujou Sara and Kujou Masahito serve under the Tenryou Commission. The Kanjou Commission's military chief is Katsuie.

## goals
### STANDARD
The Tri-Commission carries out the Shogun's decrees and handles ordinary government. During the Vision Hunt and Sakoku Decrees this meant seizing Visions and sealing the borders in the name of the Shogun's eternity. Since those decrees ended, its work is again security, trade, ceremony and the relations among the clans.

## methods
### STANDARD
Each commission governs through its specialty. The Tenryou Commission uses soldiers, patrols and wanted lists. The Kanjou Commission uses financial control, permits and border assessments at Ritou. The Yashiro Commission works through festivals, shrines and the Kamisato Estate's retainers, and controls the Shuumatsuban, a clandestine ninja organisation used for gathering information.
### LARGE +
The Fatui exploited this arrangement. The wiki says the Vision Hunt Decree began as a Fatui plot to create a market for Delusions, and that the Fatui won over the heads of the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions with promises of power and wealth. The Yashiro Commission took no part, because Ayato turned down the Fatui's offer of cooperation, according to the wiki's citation of his character demo.

## history
### LARGE
**Long-standing houses.** The Hiiragi Clan has served the Shogun for at least 500 years, and the Kujou Clan, which takes its name from Kujou Shigeyori, is regarded as the most loyal of the Shogun's major families. The sources read do not say when the three commissions took their present form.

**The decrees (about a year before the Traveler arrived).** The Sakoku Decree and Vision Hunt Decree were imposed. The Tenryou Commission carried out the Vision Hunt and the Kanjou Commission ran the border approvals. Ayaka says proposals from the Yashiro Commission to repeal the Vision Hunt Decree were vetoed by the other two.
### ULTRA +
**The exposure (2.1).** In Omnipresence Over Mortals the Traveler uncovers the Fatui's local support: Kujou Takayuki and Hiiragi Shinsuke lose their positions, and Kamaji and Chisato take over as acting heads. The commissions lighten up towards foreigners.

**The duel (2.1).** In Reflections of Mortality, Ei says the Tenryou Commissioner's post goes to whoever can beat her in a duel, as the Kujou founder once did. Takatsukasa Susumu, who had tried to claim the post, refuses out of fear; Kujou Kamaji duels her instead, and she decides to give the Kujou more time.

**The forged wedding (2.6).** In the Cypressus Custos Chapter, Hiiragi retainers forge a love letter from Chisato to Kamaji and push a marriage that would absorb her into the Kujou Clan and strip her of influence in the Hiiragi. Ayato and the Traveler undo the plot, and the wedding is postponed indefinitely.

## relations
### LARGE
- **Raiden Shogun**: the sole authority above the Tri-Commission. Naoe Hisamasa says the Tenshukaku guards answer only to her.
- **Grand Narukami Shrine**: a separate institution under Guuji Yae Miko, with religious and informal political influence. It is not one of the three commissions.
- **Fatui**: manipulated two commissions and left the Yashiro alone.
- **Watatsumi Army**: the opponent of the Shogun's forces in the civil war the Vision Hunt Decree caused.
- **Liyue Qixing and Eight Trades; Knights of Favonius**: the comparison offered by Atsuko, an Inazuman who fled to Liyue Harbor.
### ULTRA +
- **Thoma**: a member of the Kamisato household who was the target of the Vision Hunt Ceremony and who guided the Traveler through Ritou in Chapter II.
- **Shuumatsuban**: controlled by the Yashiro Commission, and used by Ayato to watch the Kanjou retainers in 2.6.

## reputation
### ULTRA
Ordinary Inazumans regard the three differently. The wiki calls the Yashiro Commission the least controversial, because its soldiers are relaxed, care for the people and avoided involvement with the Fatui. The Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions were harsher during the decrees: the Kanjou treated Ritou's foreign residents abusively, and the Tenryou suffered desertions. After Chapter II the Yashiro gained a little more influence and sympathy.

## secrets
### ULTRA
The Fatui's hold over two of the three heads was hidden from the Shogun and the public until Chapter II. Takayuki sent the Shogun falsified reports that all was well. Ei, the Shogun's true self, knew the Fatui were involved in the decree but accepted it because it fit her view of eternity.

## status

### LITE
As of 7.1 the Tri-Commission stands, with Ayato leading the Yashiro Commission and acting heads Kamaji and Chisato leading the Tenryou and Kanjou.
### STANDARD +
The Vision Hunt and Sakoku Decrees are gone. Former commissioners Takayuki (under house arrest) and Shinsuke (imprisoned) are still awaiting the Shogun's punishment. The sources checked show no later story, up to 7.1, that changes this.

## entry: Yashiro Commission
@keys: Yashiro Commission, Yashiro Commissioner, Sha-bugyou
@keys2: Tri-Commission, Kamisato, Inazuma
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
The Yashiro Commission manages shrines, festivals and cultural events and is led by the Kamisato Clan; its headquarters are at the Kamisato Estate. Kamisato Ayato is its Commissioner and clan head. Because its work is mostly civilian it has less influence than the Kanjou and Tenryou. The soldiers assigned to it are relaxed and caring toward the people. It runs the Shuumatsuban. It was untouched by the Fatui because Ayato declined their offer, and it gained some sympathy after Chapter II. Its members include Ayato, Ayaka and Thoma, and the Komore Teahouse, a Kamisato-linked business, is run by Taroumaru.

## entry: Comparison with other nations' governments
@keys: Tri-Commission equivalent, Sanbugyou comparison
@keys2: Liyue Qixing, Eight Trades, Knights of Favonius
### LARGE
In A Path Through the Storm, Atsuko explains Inazuma's government by comparing it to Liyue's Qixing and Eight Trades and to the Knights of Favonius. The comparison is loose: the Tri-Commission serves a Shogun, whom neither of the other bodies has. The English version of her line says the Eight Trades work "under" the Qixing, while the Chinese and Japanese versions list them side by side.

## entry: Fatui collusion with the commissions
@keys: Fatui plot in Inazuma, Fatui collusion with the commissions
@keys2: Tenryou, Kanjou, Takayuki, Shinsuke
@version: 2.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In Chapter II the Fatui, working through the Harbinger Signora, set out to create a market for Delusions by stirring conflict in Inazuma. They gained the cooperation of two of the three commissions. Kujou Takayuki, Tenryou Commissioner, agreed to push the Vision Hunt Decree and sent false reports to the Shogun. Hiiragi Shinsuke, Kanjou Commissioner, stockpiled Crystal Marrow for the Fatui and planned with Signora to keep the Traveler stranded in Ritou. After the Traveler unravelled the plot, both men were removed and held awaiting the Shogun's judgment. See the Tenryou and Kanjou entries for detail.
