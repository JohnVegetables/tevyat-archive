## summary
### LITE
The Inazuma Shogunate is the government of Inazuma. The Electro Archon heads it as the Raiden Shogun, also called the Almighty Shogun or "Her Excellency", from the castle of Tenshukaku in Inazuma City. Day-to-day rule belongs to the Tri-Commission: the Tenryou Commission (military and security), the Kanjou Commission (finance and borders) and the Yashiro Commission (shrines, festivals and culture), each led by a great clan. The Shogunate also commands the Shogun's Army. Its creed is the Shogun's ideal of Eternity.
### STANDARD +
Most Inazumans do not know that the throne has changed hands. Makoto, the first Raiden Shogun, died 500 years ago, and her twin Ei rules in her name. For centuries a puppet, the Shogun, governed in Ei's place.

## leadership
### LITE
At the top is the Raiden Shogun. Under her, Kujou Kamaji is acting head of the Tenryou Commission's Kujou Clan and Kujou Sara is the general commanding its forces. Hiiragi Chisato is acting head of the Kanjou Commission's Hiiragi Clan, and Kamisato Ayato is the Yashiro Commissioner.
### STANDARD +
The previous Tenryou and Kanjou Commissioners, Kujou Takayuki and Hiiragi Shinsuke, were removed for colluding with the Fatui. As of 7.1 no permanent successors have been named.
### LARGE +
Historically, Makoto ruled as the first Shogun, with Ei as her kagemusha (body double). After Makoto's death, Ei took her place and her titles. Around 400 years ago Ei handed rule to the puppet Shogun. The puppet followed fixed directives that could not be altered, while Ei meditated inside the Plane of Euthymia and ignored everyday affairs. After the events of 2.1-2.5, the puppet acts as Ei's kagemusha and Ei herself is far more involved.

## structure
### STANDARD
The Tri-Commission (San-bugyou) meets at Tenshukaku to decide government affairs. Proposals apparently pass through a council in which the commissions can veto one another: when the Yashiro Commission proposed repealing the Vision Hunt Decree, the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions blocked it. Each commission is led by a hereditary clan, and lesser clans serve under them. The Takatsukasa, for example, are a branch family of the Kujou.
- **Tenryou Commission** (Kujou Clan): the Shogun's executive arm. It handles military affairs, policing and public security, and it enforced the Vision Hunt Decree and, with the Kanjou, the Sakoku Decree. Its headquarters is inside Tenshukaku.
- **Kanjou Commission** (Hiiragi Clan, based at Ritou): handles national finances, customs, borders, trade and bureaucracy. It vets everyone who enters or leaves.
- **Yashiro Commission** (Kamisato Clan, headquartered at the Kamisato Estate): handles shrines and temples, ceremonies and public festivals, and is considered the commission closest to the Shogun. It also controls the Shuumatsuban, a clandestine ninja corps.
### LARGE +
Civil officials such as tax secretaries and a head of the civil office work within Tenshukaku alongside the commissions. The Shogun's Army is divided among the three commissions: the Tenryou supplies the main fighting force, the Kanjou guards the borders, and the Yashiro protects shrines and festivals. Some units serve the Shogun directly. These are the Hatamoto and the Okuzumeshuu, the Shogun's bodyguards, who take orders only from her.

## goals
### STANDARD
The Shogunate exists to uphold the Raiden Shogun's rule and her ideal of Eternity: a stable, enduring Inazuma protected from loss. In practice it governs, keeps order, defends the archipelago, which lies close to the Dark Sea and has been drawn into many wars, and manages the nation's faith and festivals. Since Ei's change of heart, Eternity has been redefined to include her people's wishes, and the Shogunate has turned outward again.

## members
### LARGE
Key figures include the Raiden Shogun; Kujou Sara (tengu general, adopted daughter of Takayuki, allowed to keep her Vision during the Vision Hunt); Kujou Masahito (general, in charge of military affairs in Kannazuka); Kujou Kamaji (who ran the Tenryou Commission's government business in Inazuma City and is now proxy clan head); Hiiragi Chisato; Kamisato Ayato and Ayaka; and Thoma, the Kamisato retainer. Shikanoin Heizou works for the Tenryou Commission as a doushin detective. Yae Miko, Guuji of the Grand Narukami Shrine, holds no Tri-Commission office but is one of the Shogun's oldest friends and keeps the Electro Archon's confidence.
### ULTRA +
Other named staff include the civil officials Matsukawa Souzen (a long-serving senior official), Takatsukasa Tomohide (Tax Secretary) and Nagaoka Hidemitsu (Reserve Officer and head of the civil office), as well as the Okuzumeshuu Takeshi and Saizou and the Hatamoto Naoe Hisamasa and Shima Masaoki. Okawara Goemon is the Tenryou's Chief Samurai and head of Kujou Clan security. Among the Shogunate's historical figures are Kujou Shigeyori, who built the Kujou Encampment during the cataclysm; Takamine the Mistsplitter, a hatamoto; and Mikoshi Nagamasa, who joined the Shogunate as Inspector of Tatarasuna to restore his clan's name.

## methods
### LARGE
The Shogunate rules by decree, backed by the Shogun's absolute authority and the Shogun's Army. The Army's ranks are modelled on a feudal hierarchy, from ashigaru foot soldiers to doushin patrol officers, yoriki, hatamoto and generals. The Tenryou Commission has issued draft orders requiring each family that receives one to send at least one adult to the army. The Kanjou Commission runs strict entry and exit assessments at Ritou and issues travel permits.

Disputes can be settled by a Duel Before the Throne. Both parties present their case to the Shogun at Tenshukaku, and if she allows it they fight until one cannot continue. The loser dies, either in the fight or by execution with the Musou no Hitotachi.
### ULTRA +
The Yashiro Commission works more softly. Its soldiers are relaxed compared with those of the other two commissions, it distributed wartime aid, and it uses the Shuumatsuban for intelligence work and for dealing with corrupt officials. Nagaoka Hidemitsu, a civil officer, criticised all three commissions before the Archon Quest's resolution. In his view the Kanjou's strict closure was making things worse, the Tenryou relied only on force against the Sangonomiya rebels, and the Yashiro meant well but lacked military or financial power.

## history
### LARGE
**Founding.** In the Archon War, Ei conquered many youkai races. With their help, humans built a city by the sea, and more than 2,000 years ago she brought all of Inazuma's islands under one rule. Ei believed her twin Makoto was better suited to rule. She gave up her body so that Makoto could ascend, and an in-world novel says Makoto then "set up her Shogunate", rebuilt Ei's body and kept her at her side as kagemusha. Most of the world believed there was only one god, Baal. The Shogunate's forces under Ei crushed Orobashi's invasion of Yashiori Island, and Watatsumi came under the Electro Archon, though its people were allowed to keep worshipping their god.

**Cataclysm.** Five hundred years ago, monsters ravaged Inazuma. Makoto went alone to Khaenri'ah and died there. The Kitsune Saiguu fell defending Inazuma, and Mikoshi Chiyo, tainted by a beast of sin, turned her blade on Ei. Ei took over the throne under her sister's name. Around this time the Kanjou Commissioner Hiiragi Hiroshi built Ritou into a trade port.

**Eternity.** Fearing erosion and loss, Ei built puppets. One rejected prototype later became the Balladeer (Scaramouche). About 400 years ago she activated the Shogun, entered the Plane of Euthymia and gave the Electro Gnosis to Yae Miko.

**Sakoku and the Vision Hunt.** A year before the Traveler arrived, the Fatui won over Tenryou Commissioner Kujou Takayuki and Kanjou Commissioner Hiiragi Shinsuke with promises of power. The Shogun then imposed the Sakoku Decree, which closed the borders behind storms, and the Vision Hunt Decree, which seized Visions for the Statue of the Omnipresent God. Ei knew the Fatui were involved but did nothing, since she judged ambition a greater threat to Eternity. The decree provoked the Sangonomiya Resistance, and a civil war followed. The Fatui armed both sides with Delusions, made from Crystal Marrow that the Kanjou Commission had stockpiled. By Amidst Stormy Judgment, 99 Visions had been seized.

**The turning point (2.0-2.1).** The Traveler exposed the plot alongside Kujou Sara, defeated Signora in a Duel Before the Throne (the Shogun executed her), and then defeated Ei in the Plane of Euthymia with Yae Miko's help. Ei abolished the Vision Hunt Decree, the Visions were returned, and the two commissioners were arrested. Kujou Sara and Sangonomiya Kokomi then held peace talks, with the Traveler as witness.
### ULTRA +
**Aftermath (2.1-2.6).** With Takayuki disgraced, the Kujou lost control of their vassals. In Reflections of Mortality (2.1), Kamaji tried to hand the commissionership to the Takatsukasa branch, and Takatsukasa Susumu held him in a hidden base to force a confession. Ei then offered the post to anyone who would duel her, as the Kujou founder once had. Susumu refused, and Kamaji fought her until he collapsed. She judged that he kept the Kujou sense of honour and gave the clan more time. In Transient Dreams (2.5), the puppet Shogun rebelled against Ei's changes. Ei defeated it in a duel inside Makoto's consciousness, kept it as her kagemusha and abolished the Sakoku Decree, and trade and visitors returned. In The Firmiana Leaf Falls (2.6), Hiiragi retainers forged a marriage announcement between Chisato and Kamaji to push Chisato out of the Kanjou Commission. Ayato and the Traveler foiled the plot, and the wedding was declared false.

## relations
### LARGE
- **Grand Narukami Shrine**: the shrine and the Shogunate jointly hold old ceremonies such as the offering of swords. Its Guuji, Yae Miko, is Ei's confidante and held the Gnosis for centuries.
- **Watatsumi Island**: once an enemy god's land, then a subject island that kept its own faith. It became an open enemy in the civil war. The Shogun has said that, because Watatsumi destroyed its local Narukami shrine and rebelled, Kokomi as Divine Priestess must be held accountable. After the war, peace talks set terms such as fishing rights and taxes, while the question of Watatsumi's forces returning to Tenryou command was left open.

## reputation
### ULTRA
Inazumans revere the Shogun as an absolute, eternal god, and her name is part of the national identity. "Glory to the Shogun!" is a common cry. The decree year badly damaged trust in the Tenryou and Kanjou Commissions. A Tenryou soldier's last letter describes recruits who had dreamed of fighting monsters finding themselves killing ordinary Inazumans, and the Tenryou had a higher desertion rate than the other commissions. After the decrees ended, officials described a "new era" and a Shogun whose mood had changed. They were also buried in reopened accounts, since the Kanjou's fraud and nepotism had come to light. The Yashiro Commission came out with more influence and sympathy than before.

## entry: Tri-Commission
@keys: Tri-Commission, San-bugyou, Sanbugyou, Three Commissions
### STANDARD
The Tri-Commission is the Shogunate's administrative council of three commissions, each held by a great clan: the Tenryou (Kujou Clan; military, police and security), the Kanjou (Hiiragi Clan; finance, customs and borders, based at Ritou) and the Yashiro (Kamisato Clan; shrines, festivals and culture). They meet at Tenshukaku, and any one of them can veto another's proposal. People compare it to Liyue's Qixing and Eight Trades. After 2.1 the Tenryou and Kanjou heads were removed for colluding with the Fatui, and their clans are now led by acting heads Kujou Kamaji and Hiiragi Chisato. Kamisato Ayato still holds the Yashiro.

## entry: Ranks of the Shogun's Army
@keys: Ashigaru, Doushin, Yoriki, Hatamoto, Okuzumeshuu, Shogunate Samurai
### STANDARD
The Shogun's Army uses feudal ranks. Ashigaru are foot soldiers; those of the Kanjou Commission stop people leaving Ritou without permission. Doushin are patrol and police officers, often seen in Inazuma City. Yoriki are mid-ranking samurai who command troops, including at the Kujou Encampment. Hatamoto are high-ranking samurai serving the Shogun directly, and two guard Tenshukaku. Okuzumeshuu are the Shogun's personal bodyguards and answer only to her. Above them are generals, such as Kujou Sara and Kujou Masahito. Rank-and-file armour is eggplant-coloured and bears the Shogunate's crest.

## entry: The first Shogun and her kagemusha
@keys: kagemusha, Shadow Shogun, True Shogun, Treasured Tales of the Chouken Shinkageuchi, Shinkageuchi
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
An Inazuman novel, once a restricted text, tells how the Shogunate began. The Electro Archon was originally twin sisters. Ei ("shadow") was the unmatched warrior, but she thought herself unable to understand mortal hearts. She gave up her body so that her sister Makoto could ascend, and Makoto, the True Shogun, set up the Shogunate. Makoto then rebuilt Ei's body and kept her close as her kagemusha. Ei says the book is fairly accurate up to the war against Orobashi but idealises her afterwards. Makoto died in the cataclysm, and Ei has ruled under her name ever since. In 2.5 the puppet Shogun became Ei's own kagemusha.

## entry: Fatui collusion in the Tri-Commission
@keys: Kujou Takayuki, Hiiragi Shinsuke, Crystal Marrow
@version: 2.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
A year before the Traveler arrived, the Fatui offered Tenryou Commissioner Kujou Takayuki support and rewards. Wanting to protect his clan's influence and thinking the Fatui no threat to the Shogun, he pushed the Vision Hunt Decree and sent the Shogun misleading reports about the war. Kanjou Commissioner Hiiragi Shinsuke stockpiled Crystal Marrow for the Fatui's Delusion factories, and he plotted with Signora to keep the Traveler stuck in Ritou. Kujou Sara read the evidence and confronted her adoptive father, who admitted everything. After Ei learned how far the deception went, Shinsuke was imprisoned and Takayuki was placed under house arrest, both to await punishment. Takayuki is said to have "aged" overnight.

## entry: Kujou succession crisis
@keys: Kujou Kamaji, Takatsukasa Susumu, Takatsukasa Clan
@version: 2.1
### LARGE
With Takayuki disgraced and in poor health, the Kujou lost control of their vassals and the Tenryou Commissioner's post sat empty. Kamaji, the younger son, had already privately doubted the decrees. He opened talks to pass the post to the Takatsukasa branch family. Its head, Takatsukasa Susumu, held him in a hidden Tenryou base with hired ronin and tried to force a confession blaming the Kujou alone. Ei arrived with the Traveler and declared that the post belonged to whoever would duel her, as the Kujou founder once had. Susumu refused out of fear. Kamaji fought, knowing he would lose, and kept going until he collapsed. Ei said that the puppet would have killed him, but she saw the Kujou sense of honour in him and gave the clan more time. Kamaji became provisional clan head. His elder brother Masahito, who stays with the army in Kannazuka, supported the decision.

## entry: Hiiragi Clan after Shinsuke
@keys: Hiiragi Chisato, The Firmiana Leaf Falls
@version: 2.6
### LARGE
Hiiragi Chisato, who earlier helped the Traveler escape Ritou in exchange for carrying her letter to Kujou Kamaji, became the Kanjou Commission's figurehead after her father's arrest. Some Hiiragi retainers forged a marriage announcement between her and Kamaji, hoping she would marry into the Kujou and lose her place so that they could take the commissionership. Kamisato Ayato and the Traveler exposed the plot. The wedding was postponed indefinitely and the announcement treated as false, and Chisato began taking the commission's affairs seriously.

## entry: End of the Inazuma civil war
@keys: civil war, peace talks, peace treaty
@keys2: Shogunate, Watatsumi, Sangonomiya, Kujou Sara, Kokomi
@version: 2.1
### LARGE
The war between the Shogun's Army and the Sangonomiya Resistance reached a stalemate around Yashiori Island and Kannazuka, and both sides were secretly fed Delusions by the Fatui. After the Vision Hunt Decree was repealed and the war ended, Kujou Sara and Sangonomiya Kokomi held peace talks at Tatarasuna, with the Traveler as witness (New Beginning, Kokomi's story quest). Terms added to the peace treaty included lifting wartime fishing restrictions on Watatsumi vessels in return for taxes under Narukami law. When a Watatsumi soldier's outburst exposed two Tenryou soldiers' dealings with Signora's vengeful Fatui subordinates, Kokomi declined, for now, to return Watatsumi's forces to Tenryou command, and Sara promised an internal investigation. During the war, the official Matsukawa Souzen had warned that it was worsening Inazuma's finances.

## entry: Shogunate officials at Tenshukaku
@keys: Matsukawa Souzen, Takatsukasa Tomohide, Nagaoka Hidemitsu
### ULTRA
Civil officials work in the Tenshukaku courtyards. Matsukawa Souzen, a respected veteran, could not sleep or eat for worry over the war and feared for Inazuma's worsening finances. After the decrees he saw a "new era" and a change in the Shogun, though he was buried in reopened accounts. His aide Takatsukasa Tomohide, of the Kujou's Takatsukasa branch, reviews the Kanjou Commission's financial reports; after the Vision Hunt Decree was abolished he noted that it had exposed many instances of nepotism and fraud within the Kanjou Commission. Nagaoka Hidemitsu, head of the civil office, says he has no real power. Once the fog lifted from Tsurumi Island, he and his colleagues discussed sending a survey team there.

## entry: Hiiragi Hiroshi and the founding of Ritou
@keys: Hiiragi Hiroshi
### ULTRA
Around 500 years ago, Kanjou Commissioner Hiiragi Hiroshi turned a deserted island into Ritou so that foreign trade would be centralised under the government. He imported foreign inventions to prove its worth, including a Snezhnayan blast furnace that heated the Tenshukaku hot springs as a gift to the Shogun. The Kitsune Saiguu counted him a friend. Ritou has stayed the Kanjou Commission's base and Inazuma's gate for outlanders ever since.
