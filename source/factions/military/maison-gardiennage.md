## summary
### LITE
The Maison Gardiennage is Fontaine's law enforcement service. Its main force is the Gardes, the uniformed officers, together with the Gardemeks, the clockwork meka that patrol with them. Two special branches sit beside them: the Special Security and Surveillance Patrol, which arrests violent criminals, and the Marechaussee Phantom, a detective force that answers directly to Chief Justice Neuvillette. The service is based in the Court of Fontaine.
### STANDARD +
It keeps public order, guards the courts, and feeds the machinery of Fontaine's famously theatrical justice: its officers investigate, arrest and escort suspects, and carry out sentences once the court has ruled. Fontainians generally trust it, though the Special Patrol's fearsome reputation is a running joke.

## structure
### STANDARD
The wiki lists five groups under the Maison Gardiennage: the Gardes, the Gardemeks, the Guard Poodle, the Marechaussee Phantom and the Special Security and Surveillance Patrol.
- **Gardes**: the uniformed officers, whose captain is Grosrochard. They respond to disputes, patrol, and are the officers most citizens meet.
- **Gardemeks**: Clockwork Meka built for security work, maintained and operated by the Maison.
- **Special Security and Surveillance Patrol** (the Special Patrol): a department led by Chevreuse.
- **Marechaussee Phantom**: the investigative branch, mostly Melusines, led by Neuvillette.
### LARGE +
The branches differ in how they work. The Gardes and Gardemeks handle everyday security. The Special Patrol is for particularly difficult cases and violent offenders, and it does less investigation than arrest. The Phantom investigates. The service is based in the Court of Fontaine, and the Palais Mermonia, which also holds Neuvillette's office, houses both the Maison Gardiennage and the Marechaussee Phantom.

## leadership
### STANDARD
No single head of the whole service is named. Grosrochard is Captain of the Gardes and is known from Chevreuse's profile, where he is shown sending her pleading letters about the Special Patrol's recruiting standards. Chevreuse leads the Special Patrol. Neuvillette leads the Marechaussee Phantom and is its direct superior as Chief Justice.
### LARGE +
Grosrochard and Chevreuse get on well enough to share praise: Turenna said in the 4.7 limited event Mutual Security Enhancing Simulation that a rating from "Captain Chevreuse and Captain Grosrochard" was the highest acknowledgement of both the Special Patrol and the Gardes. Chevreuse is lax with her own people, hires former criminals as "special consultants" on her personal cases, and defends them when challenged, which her Maison colleagues dislike.

## members
### STANDARD
Named current members include Captain Grosrochard of the Gardes, Captain Chevreuse of the Special Patrol, Special Patrol officer Turenna, and Gardes such as Edler. Many Gardes appear as ordinary NPCs. Former members include the retired Garde Nolette; Thurel, who served in both the Gardes and the Special Patrol; Riqueti, who left the Special Patrol for Maison Gestion; and Enghiene, a Special Patrol veteran who retired in 2026 events. Vaughn, a Garde, was killed.
### LARGE +
Melusines are common in the service, especially in the Phantom. Enghiene, a Melusine Special Patrol member and Turenna's former drillmaster, retired after 150 years in the Maison because age had impaired her firearm skills; this was shown in the 6.7 limited-time events Final Long-Range Sightlines and Dance Dance Easy-Breezy Disco. Historically, Vautrin was the Special Patrol's captain about 400 years ago, with Grizzetti as vice captain; both are dead.

## goals
### STANDARD
The Maison's job is to keep Fontaine safe and orderly and to bring wrongdoers before the court in a form that the courts can try. It also keeps the records of various civil cases. The Special Patrol's own stated aim, in Chevreuse's words, is to leave criminals no hope of escape, so that no one need be dragged away for real.

## methods
### STANDARD
Officers patrol, answer calls, investigate and arrest. Fontaine has many odd ordinances, and Fontainians judge how serious a breach is by who arrives: a Maison Gardiennage officer means the parties will not get off scot-free and may be fined, while the Special Patrol's arrival means a violent case is under way. Gardemeks aid patrols: citizens say that shouting for help is enough to summon a nearby one, and every Gardemek carries a serial number for accountability. Privately owned Gardemeks exist but only the wealthiest can afford them. Maison-owned Gardemeks are pre-programmed for assigned tasks, so a human Garde cannot fully control one.
### LARGE +
The Special Patrol works differently. Its members need not wear uniform outside formal occasions or use standard weapons, though most carry muskets and are trained in unarmed combat to Chevreuse's standards. They are the least mechanised section, because Gardemeks block their line of fire and could be turned against them by criminals. They will recruit descendants of criminals if the recruit can prove the ability to enforce justice. Members start the day with a six-mile jog, and the Patrol keeps a Melusine cafeteria that serves buttered Tidalga.

The Maison also transports and punishes prisoners. Suspects sentenced by the court are sent to the Fortress of Meropide: a notice at the Opera Epiclese records that the Maison carried out the sentence that sent Tartaglia there. It polices the Opera Epiclese too, expelling rule-breakers and prosecuting serious offenders. The Steambird's editor-in-chief Euphrasie uses Maison intelligence to publish bounties on dangerous monsters, meka and bandits.
### ULTRA +
The Maison also checks the court's own spectacle. An announcement at a Fontaine exhibit says the script of a dramatised case passed through Maison Gestion and Gardiennage supervision, and that the Maison may carry out preventive questioning or controls on exhibits whose subject matter requires it. Its employees must use their vacation days before the end of the year, per a quest note.

## history
### LARGE
**The Siege of Poisson.** More than 500 years ago, before the cataclysm, Eduardo Baker, an outlaw leader of Fleuve Cendre, was arrested during the purge of Fleuve Cendre and was being taken to exile when his followers rescued him. They joined allies and occupied Poisson, seizing the Maison Gardiennage personnel who were transporting them and making demands. When talks failed, the Marechaussee Hunters entered through secret paths and ended the siege by force, and the town was burned. Emanuel Guillotin, a Hunter who directed the operation, left the Hunters afterwards. The Maison's role was that of the escorting officers taken hostage.

**Carole and the emblem.** About 400 years ago, after Neuvillette brought the Melusines into Fontaine's society, a Melusine of the new Marechaussee Phantom named Carole was framed for murder by hardliners from the old order. When the Gardes failed to hold back the angry mob, she sacrificed herself to end the fight. Vautrin, the Special Patrol's captain, killed her accusers. The service later redesigned the Garde hat emblem, which at first showed only the scales of law, to add Melusines in her memory.
### ULTRA +
In the Carole episode, Vautrin's inquiry, run by his officer Rastignac, concluded that supporters of the old regime had lured her to the crime scene and incited the crowd. When Neuvillette tried Vautrin for abusing his authority, the captain's apparent anger was staged to prove the Chief Justice's impartiality, according to the wiki's reading of the 4.1 story. Vautrin was sent to the Fortress of Meropide, where he founded the Mutual Aid Society and died roughly 400 years ago.

## relations
### STANDARD
- **Marechaussee Phantom**: the investigative branch, led by Neuvillette, who is also the Chief Justice to whom it answers.
- **Neuvillette and the courts**: the Maison investigates and arrests; the Trial Court and the Iudex try. The Iudex retains the right to prosecute and enforce the law in person, a legacy that is now mostly symbolic.
- **The Steambird**: Euphrasie uses Maison intelligence for her bounty listings.
- **Fortress of Meropide**: the Maison transports prisoners there and serves as the link between court and prison.
### LARGE +
- **Champion Duelists**: Clorinde and the other duelists act for the court, not the Maison. Clorinde is Marechaussee-Hunter trained but is not stated to belong to the Phantom.
- **Chevreuse and Chiori**: Chevreuse overlooks Chiori's rough treatment of people in her boutique in return for information, according to a voice-over.
- **The old regime**: hardliners opposed to Neuvillette and the Melusines appear in the Carole story as the Maison's main internal tension.

## reputation
### STANDARD
Fontainians generally rely on the Maison, and say that its public security is "top-notch". Parents threaten naughty children with the Special Patrol, and adults still straighten up when Chevreuse approaches. Special Patrol officers resent being a bogeyman, but Chevreuse wants the reputation to remain. In the Carole story, hardliners accused Neuvillette's "reformed" police force of being corrupt, and the Gardes were unable to protect a Melusine from a mob.

## entry: Special Security and Surveillance Patrol
@keys: Special Security and Surveillance Patrol, Special Patrol, Special Security Patrol, Special Surveillance Patrol
### STANDARD
The Special Patrol is a department of the Maison Gardiennage, currently led by Captain Chevreuse. Its members arrest violent criminals and handle cases that ordinary Gardes would struggle with. Most use muskets and are skilled in unarmed combat. Outside formal events they need not wear uniform or use standard weapons, and they are the least mechanised section of the Gardes. They may recruit descendants of criminals who can prove they will enforce justice. Chevreuse has a standing habit of hiring convicted criminals as "special consultants" for her own cases. Its fearsome reputation is the basis of the parental warning "behave, or the Special Patrol will get you".
### LARGE +
There is an unwritten rule that when a member falls in the line of duty, the others hold on to their caps, and that the cap is passed to the fallen member's child should the child join. Chevreuse's namecard refers to a standard-issue "Type II Standard Musket" for the Patrol. Its Melusine cafeteria is known for buttered Tidalga. Grosrochard has written to Chevreuse nine times asking her to tighten recruiting, after he learned that her ranks included petty thieves, street brawlers and a would-be bank robber.

## entry: Gardes
@keys: Gardes, Garde, Captain of the Gardes
@keys2: Fontaine, Maison Gardiennage, Court of Fontaine
### STANDARD
Gardes are the Maison Gardiennage's uniformed officers, in charge of public security alongside the Gardemeks. Their captain is Grosrochard. Their arrival at an ordinance breach usually means fines for those involved. In the Carole episode the Gardes were stopped by an enraged mob and could not save her, and the service later changed its hat emblem in her memory. Retired members include Nolette and Thurel; Vaughn was killed.

## entry: Gardemeks
@keys: Gardemek, Gardemeks
@keys2: Fontaine, Maison Gardiennage, serial number
### STANDARD
Gardemeks are specialist Clockwork Meka that serve the Court of Fontaine and help Gardes patrol. They are maintained and operated by the Maison Gardiennage and are pre-programmed for tasks, so a human Garde cannot fully control one. The public sees them as reliable helpers, and a shout for help will bring a nearby Gardemek. Each carries a serial number for accountability, and privately owned units exist but are expensive. The Special Patrol dislikes them because their size and rigid movement block musket fire, and criminals might turn them against their allies. Miss Gardemetiquette and Mademoiselle Belleville are known individual Gardemeks.

## entry: Carole, Vautrin and the Garde emblem
@keys: Carole, Vautrin, Medal of Peace
@keys2: Maison Gardiennage, Marechaussee Phantom, Special Patrol, Gardes
@version: 4.1
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
About 400 years ago Carole, one of the first Melusines in the Marechaussee Phantom, and Vautrin, the Special Patrol's captain, worked together to win the trust of humans. After five years Neuvillette gave both a Medal of Peace. Hardliners of the old regime then framed Carole for murder, and the Gardes could not stop the mob that formed. She sacrificed herself to prove her innocence. Vautrin killed the culprits, was tried by Neuvillette and sent to the Fortress of Meropide. The Garde emblem was redesigned to add Melusines in her honour. Her Medal of Peace was later destroyed in a fire, and Kiara, a Melusine who came to the Court with her, remembers her in a dream during the quest In a New Light.

## entry: Siege of Poisson
@keys: Siege of Poisson, Eduardo Baker, Fleuve Cendre purge
@keys2: Maison Gardiennage, Marechaussee Hunters, hostages
### LARGE
More than 500 years ago, before the cataclysm, Eduardo Baker, the outlaw leader of Fleuve Cendre, was arrested in the purge of that district and was being taken to exile in the desert. His followers rescued him, joined allies and occupied Poisson, taking the Maison Gardiennage escorts hostage and making unreasonable demands. After talks broke down, the Marechaussee Hunters entered through secret paths and resolved the matter by force. The town was burned and many of its people died. Emanuel Guillotin, the Hunter who directed the operation, quit the Hunters afterwards. Eduardo is presumed to have died there. The source is mainly artifact and weapon text, so details are sparse.

@keys2: Grosrochard, Gardes, Special Patrol, Traveler
@availability: limited

### ULTRA
In the limited-time event Roses and Muskets (4.3), the Traveler's actions led Chevreuse and Grosrochard to nickname them "Proto-Boss". Gardes such as Edler and Turenna later adopted it. She likes reading stories where justice defeats evil, and she eats junk food at work.
