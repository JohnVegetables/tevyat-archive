## summary
### LITE
The Gardes are the uniformed security officers of Fontaine's Maison Gardiennage, the nation's law enforcement service. They patrol the Court of Fontaine, handle everyday public order, and share the patrol work with Gardemek meka. Their captain is Grosrochard. They are the standard-issue face of the law, as opposed to the Special Security and Surveillance Patrol or the Marechaussee Phantom.
### STANDARD +
Gardes are ordinary civil servants. The open world shows them chatting in cafes, grumbling about assignments and retiring to a quiet life. In other Fontaine entries they are mostly background: someone who arrests, escorts or guards on behalf of the Maison Gardiennage.

## structure
### STANDARD
The Gardes are one department of the Maison Gardiennage, listed with the Gardemeks, the Guard Poodle, the Marechaussee Phantom and the Special Security and Surveillance Patrol. A Captain heads them, currently Grosrochard. The Gardes and their Gardemeks make up the service's main force. The Special Patrol and the Marechaussee Phantom are its two special branches. The Special Patrol, led by Chevreuse, handles violent criminals whom ordinary Gardes would struggle with. The Phantom does investigation and answers directly to the Chief Justice.
### LARGE +
The Maison, and with it the Gardes, is based in the Palais Mermonia in the Court of Fontaine. Officers can move between departments: Thurel began as a Garde, joined the Special Patrol because of skill with a gun, switched departments several times, and was briefly posted as a guard at the Fortress of Meropide. Riqueti, also a former Special Patrol member, is now with Maison Gestion, a separate service.

## leadership
### LITE
Grosrochard is the Captain of the Gardes. He is only ever mentioned: in Chevreuse's introduction he is the superior who wrote to her for the ninth time, asking her to be pickier about who joins the Special Patrol.
### STANDARD +
After the Traveler's part in the Roses and Muskets event, Grosrochard and Chevreuse both took to calling the Traveler "Proto-Boss". Turenna presents their joint assessment as the highest acknowledgment of both the Special Patrol and the Gardes. Garde Edler repeats the nickname and can quote his advice from memory.
### LARGE +
Edler quotes Grosrochard's advice about blending in with criminals by learning to "swear like a sailor", and says she has never heard him swear. She also quotes him comparing good fighters to wolves, whose teeth are sharp but who also know exactly where to bite. These are the only glimpses of his manner: a mentor figure who is respected and never seen.

## members
### STANDARD
Named Gardes are few and come mostly from open-world conversations.
- Santallier: a patrolling Garde at the Quartier Lyonnais, who says he asked his superiors not to patrol that block, since the safest area in town sits across from the Champion Duelists' office, and was told that only he could take the task.
- Nolette: a retired Garde near the Vasari Passage, still proud of being a former officer of the Palais Mermonia, and regretful about failing to catch a pickpocket.
- Thurel: a retired Garde near the Fountain of Lucine, who later served in the Special Patrol and briefly at the Fortress of Meropide, and says their memory is fading.
### LARGE +
- Edler: an energetic Garde who appears in limited-time events. She told Wislawa she was a "kind-hearted merchant", has tinkered with the Maison's standard-issue wargame, and once mistook Wislawa for a kidnapper in Fontaine before meeting her again in Nod-Krai.
- Vaughn: a Garde who was secretly an accomplice of Marcel in the Archon Quest. He is the only known corrupt Garde.

## goals
### STANDARD
Keep Fontaine's streets safe: patrol the Court of Fontaine, respond when citizens call for help, guard places and prisoners on behalf of the Maison Gardiennage, and hand serious investigation to the Phantom and violent cases to the Special Patrol. Gardemeks share this work, and Fontainians treat them as reliable helpers.

## methods
### STANDARD
Gardes patrol on foot and with Gardemeks, which are pre-programmed to complete the tasks assigned to them. A Garde works with them rather than controlling them. Each Gardemek has a serial number for accountability, and a shout for help is said to bring a nearby Gardemek at once. The Maison keeps records of civil cases and transports and punishes hostages, and Gardes carry out such duties. For training it uses a standard-issue wargame.
### LARGE +
The Special Patrol distrusts the mechanised approach. Chevreuse's Character Story 2 says Gardemeks' rigid movements and large size block musket shots, and criminals could turn them against their allies, which is why the Patrol is the least mechanised section. The Maison also passes its information on dangerous monsters, meka and bandits to the Steambird's Editor-in-Chief, who publishes it with bounties.

## history
### LARGE
The founder and founding date of the Maison Gardiennage, and so of the Gardes, are unknown. The Gardes appear in the archive from Version 4.0. In the Archon Quest "Lies Cast Shadows Under Gathered Lights", during Lyney's trial, the Garde Vaughn claimed that the Gardes had found Primordial Sea water in Lyney's belongings, to protect his superior's identity. When Neuvillette saw through the deception, he ordered Vaughn to confess and seek the Gardes' protection, but Vaughn dissolved into water before he could name his boss.
### ULTRA +
In the Roses and Muskets event (4.3) the Traveler's help led Grosrochard and Chevreuse to adopt the nickname "Proto-Boss". In the limited-time Tracing Vanishing Trails event (5.8) Edler pursues the head of a crime syndicate, and in the Moonlit Patrol Exercise (6.3) she carries her improved wargame all the way to Nod-Krai. All of this is limited-time-event material.

## relations
### STANDARD
The Gardes belong to the Maison Gardiennage. The Special Patrol and the Marechaussee Phantom sit alongside them in the same service. The Steambird newspaper uses the Maison's intelligence for its bounties.

## reputation
### STANDARD
Fontaine's citizens regard the public security as "top-notch", as an Aquabus passenger puts it, and treat Gardemeks as dependable helpers. Individual Gardes are shown as dutiful but human: tired, nostalgic or complaining about assignments.
