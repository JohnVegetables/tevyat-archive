## summary
### LITE
The Eremites are the desert mercenaries of Sumeru. They come from the Great Red Sand, whose ancient civilisations have collapsed, and they hire out their blades across Teyvat. Outsiders use "Eremites" as a blanket name for many separate brigades and tribes. Some are honourable, some are criminal, and some ignore the others entirely. They are the only authorised mercenary organisation in Sumeru, and many foreigners judge them as unscrupulous soldiers of fortune. The best-known members are Dehya of the Blazing Beasts and the Corps of Thirty who guard Sumeru City.
### STANDARD +
The Eremites hold a claim to part of the legacy of the desert's lost civilisations, which the official Sumeru teaser calls a "deterrent of last resort in a crisis". They have an open partnership with the Adventurers' Guild.

## structure
### STANDARD
There is no overall chain of command. The Eremites are split into brigades and tribes, each with its own beliefs and code of conduct, and the groups overlap or conflict. Anyone can join the mercenary brigades. Traditionally only people from the desert may, but Rukh Shah is an exception. Some people simply borrow the name for their own benefit. Consultants and advisors help the groups talk to each other.
### LARGE +
Major groups named in canon:
- **Corps of Thirty**: the Akademiya's permanently hired brigade in Sumeru City, based in the Citadel of Regzar, with teal-green scarves instead of the usual red.
- **Blazing Beasts**: the brigade of Dehya.
- **Deshret's Relics**: a large faction that held its members by recording their sins, with subgroups **Ayn Al-Ahmar** (Port Ormos) and **Dakan Al-Ahmar** (led by Kusayla).
- **Rahman's faction**, near the Valley of Dahri.
- **Caracal Battalion**, active in Port Ormos.
- **Thutmose**, whose secret base is on Lamb-Devourer Rock.
- **Farrokhzadan**, a sellsword clan led by Siman Farrokhzad.
- **Aybak's group** at Caravan Ribat.
- Tribes: **Tanit**, **Shimti** and **Uzza** (matriarchal and devoted to the Goddess of Flowers), **Afiyah** (nomads of the Desert of Hadramaveth), and **Iab** and **Unut** (descendants of the Thamudians).
- Others: unaffiliated brigades and tribes that take private commissions as bodyguards, with no clear structure.
### ULTRA +
The fire motif is shared across groups. A furnishing text records their creed that only valor's flame is never extinguished. Lighting a campfire lifts team morale, and putting out an enemy's fire demoralises them. In emergencies they have learned to bleed their sumpter beasts for fluid as a last resort when food and water run out.

## members
### STANDARD
Dehya is the best-known: a legend among Eremites, former Corps of Thirty and now Blazing Beasts. Rukh Shah, the Corps of Thirty's Parchamdar, is the youngest ever to hold the post. Aybak leads a group at Caravan Ribat that is under contract with the Akademiya to guard the town and maintain the Wall of Samiel. His group calls each other brother and sister. Rahman leads his own faction. Dehya's adoptive father Kusayla led Dakan Al-Ahmar. Mizri led Ayn Al-Ahmar in Port Ormos.
### LARGE +
Further named figures:
- Babel, Tanit matriarch (see her entry).
- Siman Farrokhzad, sage of the Akademiya and head of the Farrokhzadan.
- Lunja, who leads one Farrokhzadan group.
- Asfand, Corps of Thirty Consultant.
- Mishal, last leader of Deshret's Relics.
- Sinuhe, last chief of the Shimti, and Nefer, his adopted daughter.
- Arif, last known chief of the Iab, and Nizar, last chief of the Unut.
- Tariq and Tumart, Ayn Al-Ahmar members at Port Ormos.
- Jebrael and Samail, once called the twin blades of Thutmose.
- Kasim, a fraud who uses the name for his own benefit and is known as one by the Corps of Thirty.
- Younan, who travels to Inazuma to facilitate trade from Sumeru, and Irus, whose crew was hired by Jakob in Fontaine.

## abilities
### STANDARD
Eremite fighting is tied to their ancestry. The Sword-Dancers' skills are said to date from Deshret's civilisation and are now an art as well as a combat style. Eremite dance traditions descend from their ancestors' worship rituals. Elemental power among them is not innate but borrowed from Spirits of Omen, ancient desert spirits that dwell in their weapons and awaken in times of peril. 
### LARGE +
High-ranking Eremites can summon Spirits of Omen: Stone Drakes for Geo, Wind Gledes for Anemo, Dendro Spirit-Serpents for Dendro and Pyro Scorpions for Pyro. Equipment varies, including axes, halberds, crossbows, katars, curved swords, lances, bows and catalysts.
Tiers in the game: the weakest are physical fighters, mid-level Eremites channel one element (Electro axes, Cryo twin blades, Hydro katars), and high-level Eremites wield Geo, Anemo, Dendro or Pyro with spirit support. The Loremaster's text also says that the oral history of the ancient civilisations is slowly fading or being distorted, though those who remember keep it alive. The Eremites' history survives mainly in their performance culture and combat.

## goals
### STANDARD
Most Eremites seek coin. They take commissions as guards, escorts, caravan protectors and fighters. Some groups follow causes: devout followers of King Deshret seek his resurrection or oppose the Akademiya's version of history, and some brigades act for their own survival. Dehya hopes to bring hope to the desert she considers home.

## methods
### STANDARD
Contract work: guarding caravans and settlements, hunting monsters, escorting scholars and delivering goods. Many play the Genius Invokation TCG across Sumeru. Criminal factions blackmail, raid and extort. Deshret's Relics collected "records" of members' shameful deeds to bind them, the Tanit raised captured children as devoted "Falcons", and Dakan Al-Ahmar raided caravans for supplies during its revenge campaign.
### LARGE +
Alliances can be pragmatic or religious. Thutmose sought knowledge and partners from Sumeru researchers to the Fatui. The Farrokhzadan carried out a commission for Dottore and later tried to capture Aranara for him. Rahman's faction held Village Keepers hostage to cross the forest. Corps members follow the Akademiya's assignments and civilians' requests.

## history
### LARGE
The Eremites descend from the lost civilisations of the Great Red Sand, particularly the realm of King Deshret. Their stories pass down mainly by word of mouth. Their age is unknown, and the best-documented events come from the 3.x content.

**Sumeru Archon Quest (Chapter III).** Rahman's faction intercepted men of the Akademiya who had kidnapped the Village Keepers of Aaru Village and took everyone hostage. They believed rumours that King Deshret would return. After the Traveler's party showed them what the previous Dendro Archon had done, Rahman's group abandoned their prejudice and helped free Lesser Lord Kusanali.

**Deshret's Relics.** Dehya's father Kusayla burned the faction's records room with himself inside, freeing members from blackmail. After his death Dakan Al-Ahmar attacked the Relics. In Sincerity's Facade, the Traveler, Dehya and Dakan Al-Ahmar dismantled the Relics and their leader Mishal. The Corps of Thirty took the survivors.

**Port Ormos.** Ayn Al-Ahmar and the Caracal Battalion competed for a Divine Knowledge Capsule that was rumoured to hold King Deshret's power. Ayn Al-Ahmar won it at auction, but Mizri went mad when he used it. Alhaitham and the Traveler defeated him, and Alhaitham secretly took the capsule.

**The Tanit.** In the Dune-Entombed Fecundity quests, the Tanit tribe, under Matriarch Babel, were destroyed by the Traveler and Jeht after Babel's attempts to remove them failed.

**The Shimti, Iab and Unut.** About 20 years ago, a sandstorm blocked the Shimti's well, and Sinuhe drew water from the Iab's well without permission. The Iab destroyed the Shimti save for his adopted daughter Nefer, who later set the Iab and Unut against each other, so that both lost their warriors in the fighting.

**Later appearances (6.5-6.6).** In the limited-time 6.5 event Where Waves Meet the Reef, the former scholar Ashvapati recounts how the Eremite tribe of Khaemwaset, which had taken him in, was razed by enemies armed with technology that the Eremite Sennedjem had sold them; Ashvapati saved three children, Ati, Raya and Neb. In the 6.6 Archon Quest act As All Falls to Emptiness, Dehya meets the Traveler and Nefer in Sumeru and introduces herself as an Eremite mercenary whose work keeps her in the desert.
### ULTRA +
The name "Eremite" probably comes from the Greek word for a desert dweller, the root of "hermit". The Chinese name, which means "gilded", carries a mildly pejorative sense of putting on a show without the substance. Several named groups echo ancient Egyptian and Near Eastern deities and names, and canon in-world gives no separate origin for them.

## relations
### STANDARD
**Akademiya.** The Corps of Thirty serves the Akademiya as hired peacekeepers, and the Akademiya's liaison officer is Effendi. The Akademiya's wider reputation among Eremites is poor, especially among devoted followers of Deshret. Aybak's group is also contracted to the Akademiya. After the reforms that followed the Archon Quest, Rahman's faction now works with Setaria to promote education among desert peoples. The Matra are the Akademiya's disciplinary officers, and their General Mahamatra Cyno hails from the desert.
**Adventurers' Guild.** An open partnership.
**Rival Eremites.** The Corps of Thirty has a tenuous relationship with other groups, and Rukh Shah warns visitors to be careful of Eremites outside the city.
### LARGE +
**Fatui.** Thutmose and the Farrokhzadan cooperated with the Fatui, and the Doctor commissioned the Farrokhzadan.

## reputation
### STANDARD
Outsiders see them as mercenaries who will do anything for coin. The scholar Nazafarin calls them true soldiers of fortune who will do anything for money. Impostors who use the name and do poor work for unwary foreign clients also drag down the reputation of the whole group. Dehya is considered a legend among them.

## secrets
### ULTRA
Deshret's Relics bound members with records of their worst deeds, stored at its headquarters. After Kusayla burned the records, many left, though loyalists remained. Mizri's Divine Knowledge Capsule episode is known to few: Alhaitham took the capsule after defeating him, and the Matra arrested Mizri, who muttered the words the Traveler heard from the Irminsul on arriving in Sumeru.

## status
### LITE
Active in Sumeru as of 7.1, with the Corps of Thirty guarding the capital and brigades taking contracts across the desert.
### STANDARD +
Rahman's faction now supports the reformed Akademiya. Deshret's Relics and the Tanit are gone, and Dakan Al-Ahmar surrendered after avenging Kusayla. In 6.6, during the crisis that sealed Sumeru, Dehya was still working in the desert as an Eremite mercenary. No Eremite story in the Snezhnaya content (7.0-7.1) was found.

## entry: Corps of Thirty
@keys: Corps of Thirty, Parchamdar, Citadel of Regzar
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Akademiya's permanently hired Eremite brigade in Sumeru City. It started as thirty founders hired by the sages and is said to be among the oldest Eremite groups. It now has more than thirty members, but keeps the name in memory of the founders. Its members wear teal-green scarves with a petal-like emblem like the Akademiya's, and its barracks are in the Citadel of Regzar. The Parchamdar, Rukh Shah, commands it, and Asfand serves as its Consultant. It keeps the peace in the capital and manages and coordinates the other mercenary brigades. Dehya is a notable former member.

## entry: Blazing Beasts
@keys: Blazing Beasts
@version: 3.5
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The brigade of Dehya, who joined at a young age after her last argument with her adoptive father Kusayla. Kusayla had quietly arranged her place there to protect her from Deshret's Relics. During the Whirling Waltz event, the Traveler and Paimon became the Blazing Beasts' official partners.
### LARGE +
The Whirling Waltz material is limited-time event content and Paimon's remark is the only source for the partnership claim here.

## entry: Deshret's Relics and Ayn Al-Ahmar
@keys: Deshret's Relics, Ayn Al-Ahmar, Dakan Al-Ahmar, Kusayla
@version: 3.5
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Deshret's Relics was a large Eremite faction that held its members by blackmail, having them leave behind "records" of their sins as proof of loyalty. Subgroups were **Ayn Al-Ahmar** and **Dakan Al-Ahmar**. The names refer to King Deshret's alleged true name, Al-Ahmar. Kusayla, leader of Dakan Al-Ahmar, died burning the records, and his followers took revenge on the Relics and on innocent travellers. The Relics' last leader Mishal was taken down by Dakan Al-Ahmar, Dehya and the Traveler. Ayn Al-Ahmar survived in Port Ormos under Mizri, devout in their worship of Deshret and disdainful of Greater Lord Rukkhadevata.

## entry: Rahman's faction
@keys: Rahman, Rahman's faction
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
An Eremite faction near the Valley of Dahri led by Rahman, a longtime friend of Dehya. Maqrin and Tanina are other members. They believed the Akademiya's rumours that King Deshret would return, resented the forest and its Archon, and held the Village Keepers of Aaru Village as hostages. After learning the truth about Rukkhadevata, they helped the Traveler's party against the sages and, afterwards, work with Setaria to promote education among desert peoples.

## entry: Tanit and the matriarchal tribes
@keys: Tanit, Tanit tribe, Shimti, Uzza, Iab tribe, Unut, Falcons
@version: 3.1
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Tanit were a nomadic, matrilineal tribe in the Desert of Hadramaveth who venerated the Goddess of Flowers. They were led by a Matriarch and Nine Elders, traded in crocodile products, spices and essential oils, hunted wenut, and raised captured children as devoted "Falcons". They were eventually annihilated by the Traveler and Jeht after Matriarch Babel's schemes failed. The Shimti and Uzza were also matriarchal tribes. The Shimti, known for their beautiful women, were massacred by the Iab about 20 years ago after their chief Sinuhe took water from the Iab's well; only his adopted daughter Nefer survived. The Thamudian-descended Iab and Unut were later wiped out or scattered in the conflict set off by Nefer's revenge.

## entry: Farrokhzadan
@keys: Farrokhzadan, Siman Farrokhzad, Lunja
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
An Eremite clan likely descended from a man named Agha Farrokh, now led by his daughter Siman Farrokhzad, an Akademiya sage connected with Amurta and the Bimarstan. ## entry: Spirits of Omen
@keys: Spirit of Omen, Spirits of Omen, Stone Drake, Wind Glede, Dendro Spirit-Serpent, Pyro Scorpion
@version: 3.1
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Ancient desert spirits, or fragments of them, that can manipulate the elements and dwell in Eremite weapons. They awaken in times of peril and are summoned by high-ranking Eremites: Stone Drakes by Stone Enchanters, Wind Gledes by Galehunters, Dendro Spirit-Serpents by Floral Ring-Dancers and Pyro Scorpions by Scorching Loremasters. The lore suggests an awakened spirit is satisfied only once blood has been spilled.

## entry: Port Ormos brigades

@version: 3.0

### STANDARD
The **Caracal Battalion** competes in Port Ormos for the Divine Knowledge Capsule; Ayn Al-Ahmar's Tariq and Tumart call them money-grubbing opportunists. **Thutmose** is a Deshret-devoted group seeking the secret of Al-Ahmar from researchers and the Fatui, from a base on Lamb-Devourer Rock. **Aybak's group** guards Caravan Ribat and the Wall of Samiel for the Akademiya.
