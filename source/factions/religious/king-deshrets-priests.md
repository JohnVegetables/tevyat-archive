## summary
### LITE
King Deshret's Priests, also called the Desert Priests, were the priesthood who served the god-king of Sumeru's desert in his ancient kingdom. They wore the same style of head ornament as their head, Hermanubis, the Greatest of Sages and one of the king's Seven Pillars. They guarded the king's knowledge, and when forbidden knowledge ravaged the land they built the temples that held it back. Canon says little about them directly. No priest is shown alive in the present, and most of what is known comes from ruins, relics and one dying priest's recorded memory.
### STANDARD +
Three threads survive: the priest Kasala's recording (Archon Quest, 3.1), Hermanubis' followers who founded the Temple of Silence, and flavour text about descendants who keep the old ceremonies. The wiki page for the group is a stub, so treat anything beyond these as unknown.

## structure
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Canon gives no ranks or headcount. Hermanubis stood over all the other priests, and the priests wore a head ornament like his. The Gilded Dreams circlet, Shadow of the Sand King, is a gold-inlaid headband once used by desert priests in imitation of a legendary ruler's.
### LARGE +
Its lore says the priests ruled the oasis cities justly in the king's name and received divine oracles together with him. Murals in the Khemenu Temple and the Unknown Ruins show Deshret, his Seven Pillars, priests and subjects together, and slabs there record the deeds of Thoth, Heryshaf and Sobek. The Seven Pillars were the king's ministers, not strictly priests, and canon does not say how the two groups divided duties. Hermanubis is the only Pillar who rose from human stock (a Tighnarian, in Guiding Notes) to the rank of a god's familiar, and the Temple of Silence calls him "priest over all other priests". The slab records seen in the 6.1 event were left by priests, not by the Pillars, which suggests that record-keeping was a priestly task.

## leadership
### LITE
Hermanubis was the guide of all the priests. Chosen from among the Tighnarians by King Deshret, he was a renowned sage and warrior known for being sincere, steady and kind.
### STANDARD +
Even his fellow Pillars respected him. After Deshret's death he led his followers to Tulaytullah. Kasala is the only other named priest; canon does not say he answered to Hermanubis directly. His own biography belongs in his separate entry.

## members
### LITE
Only two members are named: Hermanubis as head, and Kasala as one of the last priests to serve under the king.
### STANDARD +
Kasala presided over a temple that later lay buried under the sands, and was entombed there. Other priests appear only anonymously in murals and slab records.

## goals
### STANDARD
The priests served the god-king and his ideals of justice and abundance. When forbidden knowledge spread, their task became containment. Kasala's last words ask the children of the desert to drop old grievances and remember the kindness that saved them.

## methods
### STANDARD
In the disaster the priests built temples at Greater Lord Rukkhadevata's summons, and she infused them with the divine power of life (Dendro). That tempered the damage. The wiki links these to the structures called the Towers of Betrayal. Kasala spent his life guarding one.
### LARGE +
Descendants' lore adds that the old priests were proud of three martial skills, the cudgel, the spear and above all archery. They also kept ceremonies, and few could still read the ancient texts. This comes from in-world flavour text, not from a quest, and it describes people who hold the priests' heritage, not the priesthood as it was.

## history
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
**The disaster.** In his grief over the Goddess of Flowers, Deshret exposed the land to knowledge that, in Kasala's words, did not belong to this world. It spread like a plague: crazed whispers, dark grey scales, a drained land. Rukkhadevata came from the forests and had the priests build her life-infused temples. The embers of the civilization survived in Aaru Village. Kasala says Deshret finally sacrificed himself, because the knowledge would blight the world as long as it existed, and that Rukkhadevata exhausted her strength and took the form of a small child.

**Kasala's end.** Kasala guarded a temple for the rest of his life and left a recorded memory there, found in "Secret of the Scorching Desert" (3.1) by Alhaitham, Cyno, Dehya, Candace, Rahman and the Traveler.
### LARGE +
**After the king.** According to Guiding Notes, Hermanubis led his people to Tulaytullah and founded the Temple of Silence there to preserve and steward knowledge. The wiki's Temple of Silence page adds that the city's rulers later betrayed the Temple to the king of Gurabad. Hermanubis shattered himself into Ba Fragments to protect his followers, who were shunned and eventually settled near Thamud Oasis about 400 years ago, according to the wiki. The Rite of Duels, said to have been created by Hermanubis, is still carried out by the Temple. In Sage of Sages (4.6) Cyno and Sethos fought one, with Hermanubis as witness.
### ULTRA +
**Present-day echoes.** Cyno carries one Ba Fragment and Sethos is the Temple of Silence's appointed leader. In the 6.1 limited-time event Headlong Into the Sands, the Traveler's party read slab records written by the priests. Cyno confirmed that Hermanubis can possess him but that he cannot call him up on demand. Thoth remarked that when the priests of that place left or died, no one remained to record what followed.

## relations
### STANDARD
- **King Deshret**: the priests' lord. Kasala calls him "my eternal lord" and "the proud king of the desert".
- **Hermanubis**: their head, and the founder of the group's lasting successor, the Temple of Silence.
- **Greater Lord Rukkhadevata**: the Dendro Archon who directed the priests' temple-building. In Kasala's account she and Deshret were allies, not enemies.
- **Eremites**: a number of them believed Rukkhadevata had betrayed the king. Kasala's testimony contradicts that, which shook Rahman.
- **Akademiya**: scholars of the Akademiya hold a hollow body named the Temple of Silence. The real Temple, descended from Hermanubis' followers, works separately but has been rebuilding ties since the Oathkeeper quest.
### LARGE +
- **Seven Pillars**: ministers of Deshret. The priests' slabs record Thoth, Heryshaf and Sobek.
- **Scholars and desert folk**: descendants claim the priesthood's legacy. The Eremites call them ascetics.

## reputation
### LARGE
In Sumeru today the priests are a half-remembered part of the desert's past. Within the Temple of Silence, Hermanubis is praised as an immortal and valorous figure. Many Eremites, who grew up on a story that Rukkhadevata destroyed their civilization, had never heard the priests' account. The Akademiya treats Hermanubis as a foundational figure of desert civilization.

## secrets
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### ULTRA
The priests' most important hidden truth is Kasala's testimony. King Deshret did not fall to a betrayal by Rukkhadevata. The two worked together to stop forbidden knowledge, and Deshret chose to give his life. This account was lost under the sand until the 3.1 Archon Quest. The testimony is a recording, and it is not clear how much of the priests' wider history it covers.

## status
@version: 7.1
### LITE
The priesthood no longer exists as such. Its heritage continues through the Temple of Silence, now led by Sethos, and through descendants who claim it.
### STANDARD +
No new material on the priests was found for 6.x or 7.0-7.1, which are set in Nod-Krai and Snezhnaya. The most recent mention is the limited-time Ruins Exploration event in 6.1.

## entry: Kasala
@keys: Kasala, Kasala the priest, Lost Legacies in the Sand
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Kasala was one of the last priests to serve King Deshret. After the king's sacrifice he continued to serve by guarding one of the temples built at Rukkhadevata's direction, in what is now called the Mysterious Ruins beneath the Mausoleum of King Deshret. He was entombed there. The party found his inscription and heard his last message in "Secret of the Scorching Desert". In it he warns that knowledge can destroy a civilization, describes the disaster, honours Rukkhadevata and Deshret, and urges the children of the desert to drop old grievances and hold on to the memory of her kindness. His voice narrates the cutscene known as Lost Legacies in the Sand.

## entry: Mysterious Ruins and the Towers of Betrayal
@keys: Mysterious Ruins, Towers of Betrayal
@keys2: priest, temple, Kasala, Deshret
@version: 3.1
### LARGE
The Mysterious Ruins is a quest-only domain buried beneath the sands near the Mausoleum of King Deshret, discovered in "Secret of the Scorching Desert". It is the tomb and temple of Kasala. The wiki connects it with the Towers of Betrayal, the temples that priests built and Rukkhadevata infused with life power. The name reflects the Eremite belief that she betrayed Deshret, which Kasala's message contradicts. A message left by someone who infiltrated a temple, and who took the priests' warnings to be tricks, shows that outsiders did not understand them either.

## entry: Heirs of the Burning Sun
@keys: Heirs of the Burning Sun, Scion of the Blazing Sun
@version: 4.0
### LARGE
The weapon Scion of the Blazing Sun carries the lore of people who say they descend from King Deshret's priests. They call themselves the Heirs of the Burning Sun, keep the ceremonies of the old kingdom, and preserve a martial tradition of cudgel, spear and archery. Few can read the ancient texts. The text claims that the Eremites call them ascetics, that some have turned to the rainforest and repay the old favour in silence, and that most vanished into the desert. This is flavour text of unknown reliability and no quest depicts them.

## entry: Temple of Silence
@keys: Temple of Silence, Ba Fragment, Ba Fragments
@keys2: Hermanubis, Cyno, Sethos, priest, Deshret
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Temple of Silence is the lasting institution of Hermanubis' followers. It keeps the knowledge of King Deshret's civilization. The Akademiya has an office with the same name that handles unpublished information, but the wiki calls it a vestige of the real Temple, which operates from near Thamud Oasis in the Land of Lower Setekh. After the Oathkeeper quest Sethos was appointed its leader, and Cyno, who holds a Ba Fragment, is its leader in name. See the entries on Cyno, Sethos and Hermanubis for details.
