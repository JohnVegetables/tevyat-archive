## summary
### LITE
Teleport Waypoints are devices found across Teyvat. Once the Traveler unlocks one by touching it, it can be chosen on the map to fast travel there. In-world, they are old, odd structures the locals have grown used to without knowing what they are for; to the Traveler, a foreigner, their use is obvious.
### STANDARD +
The map text puts it this way: Teyvat's people regard them as ancient gimmicks and are accustomed to them despite not knowing their purpose.

## in_universe
### LITE
Canon gives no explanation of who built Teleport Waypoints or how they work. Treat them as unexplained ancient machinery that happens to carry the Traveler between places.
### STANDARD +
The Portable Waypoint's item text offers a different note: it describes a pocket-sized item that makes a temporary Waypoint, able to connect with the ley lines at least enough to bring about teleportation, and adds that the real principles behind Waypoints are not yet fully understood and that the hope is that using them has no undesirable side effects. That text speaks from inside the world and treats the Waypoints as known devices, which does not match the "ancient gimmick nobody understands" framing.
### LARGE +
Other teleportation exists in canon but is not shown to be the same thing: Mona uses a teleport in the event Unreconciled Stars, the Fatui appear to teleport in Archon Quest Chapter I, Act III, and a Fatui knight remarks that the Grand Master crossed from Nod-Krai to Mondstadt along the ley lines. In the Archon Quest "In the Depths, an Unexpected Reunion" Paimon likens Dainsleif's description of the Abyss's portal network to Teleport Waypoints, which the story does not develop. The wiki marks the canonicity of Waypoints as unclear.

## gameplay
### LITE
Gameplay (not literal lore): Waypoints are map markers that are unlocked by interacting with them, then used to fast travel. Statues of The Seven, Statues of the New Moon, domains and High-Speed Transit Lanes also work as teleport points once unlocked.
### STANDARD +
They also act as respawn points when a whole party falls, clear accumulated Sheer Cold, Withering decay and Abyssal decay from harmful climates, recharge the Lumenstone Adjuvant in The Chasm: Underground Mines, and replenish Phlogiston in Natlan. Most are unlocked by touching them once the area is lit up on the map, which is done through a Statue of The Seven or Statue of the New Moon, or through quest progress.
### LARGE +
Some Waypoints are hidden until a requirement is met, usually quest progress. Examples include those inside the Dainichi Mikoshi in Enkanomiya, the underground ones in Sumeru's deserts, those in Annapausis and the Fortress of Meropide in Fontaine, and sites in Chenyu Vale. Certain map locations also act as teleport points after set quest steps, among them the Alcor, the Cat's Tail, the Fortress of Meropide, the Hidden Dwelling and the Meeting Points in Nod-Krai (after the opening Nod-Krai quest "Where the Moon Rises" and each Meeting Point's own world quest). Several Waypoints are unlocked automatically on completing the Archon Quest "Song of the Dragon and Freedom". Co-op: a fallen player respawns at the nearest Waypoint they have unlocked in their own world.
### ULTRA +
Variants: the Portable Waypoint is crafted by alchemy, with the instructions earned at Mondstadt Reputation Level 6, and creates a single temporary Waypoint that lasts seven days, which guests in co-op can use. Sub-Space Waypoints are furnishings placed in the Serenitea Pot. The Realm Waypoint "A Place to Return To" sits in the first area of each realm layout.

## limited_event_waypoints
@availability: limited
@version: 1.6
### STANDARD
Event-only areas have had their own Waypoints that can no longer be reached: 7 in the Golden Apple Archipelago during the 1.6 event Midsummer Island Adventure, and 10 when it returned in 2.8; 10 in the Darkness-Encroached Enkanomiya in 2.5; 9 in the Veluriyam Mirage in 3.8; and 17 in Simulanka in 4.8. Some were unlocked automatically during the event quests or once the event ended.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): inside a scene, the Traveler stepping onto a Waypoint and arriving elsewhere is fine as a shorthand for travel. Locals treat the devices as familiar background furniture, not as technology anyone can explain. Do not claim Waypoints run on ley lines, belong to a particular civilisation, or that ordinary NPCs can use them, as canon supports none of these.

## limits
### STANDARD
Canon gives no builder, no mechanism and no in-world rules. Katheryne's fast-travel remark in Inazuma is not a basis for treating other characters as able to teleport. Fast travel is a gameplay convenience; most Fatui, Knights and travellers are shown crossing Teyvat on foot, by ship or by other means.

## entry: Portable Waypoint
@keys: Portable Waypoint, Sub-Space Waypoint
### STANDARD
The Portable Waypoint is an alchemy-made consumable that creates one temporary Teleport Waypoint at the user's location. It lasts seven days and only one can exist per world. Its item text calls it a pocket-sized item capable of connecting with the ley lines enough to cause teleportation, and notes that its convenience for Vision bearers comes with the caveat that the real principles of Waypoints are not fully understood. Sub-Space Waypoints are furnishings for the Serenitea Pot that look different but work the same way.
