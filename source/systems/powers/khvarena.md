## summary
### LITE
Khvarena is a pure, divine energy of Sumeru. Nabu Malikata, the Goddess of Flowers, made it for one purpose: to oppose the cataclysm she foresaw. Its divine nature lets it purify Abyssal energy and Forbidden Knowledge. She called the first Khvarena her innocent, pure daughter and gave it to Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, who shaped it into the divine bird Simurgh. When the Abyss began to corrupt the Amrita, Simurgh shattered her form into countless motes of Khvarena. The only sentient ones became the Pari.
### STANDARD +
The name is a Zoroastrian term for divine glory or radiance, and game texts use the word loosely: for the original being (spoken of as feminine), for the countless entities she became when she divided, and for the abstract quality of light and favour ("do not let your Khvarena dim"). Related Sumeru items include the Vourukasha's Glow artifact set and the Great Songs of Khvarena.

## in_universe
### LITE
Khvarena is not presented as an element and is not tied to a Vision. It is an energy of divine life and purity, opposed to the darkness of the Abyss. It exists in many forms: wild motes in the Girdle of the Sands, the Pari, Plumes of Purifying Light and orbs of coalesced light that can raise barriers.
### STANDARD +
The origin is told in two ways. A legend relayed by Sethos says Rukkhadevata received the Khvarena as a gift at the first Sabzeruz Festival. The artifact texts of Vourukasha's Glow instead show Nabu Malikata handing over a shard of her own being, after a dream of a faceless nightmare (the cataclysm), asking Rukkhadevata to keep it safe until the day the Abyss rises to drown life. Both agree on the purpose: to blunt a disaster that Nabu foresaw.
### LARGE +
Khvarena has a built-in destiny of sacrifice. Nabu's words in the artifact text tell it that it will face "the trial of division and death", then become immortal, which she calls a harder road. Item text also treats withering and rebirth as the shape of Khvarena's meaning: things born from the Khvarena of flowers wither, and are then reborn, nourished by the Amrita, no longer troubled by death.
### ULTRA +
The texts carry some unusual wording. Khvarena is called a "point in the flower's heart, a single drop of light in a sea of sweet dew". Nabu says it comes from "the wisdom of the flowers and the veins of the skies" and holds the pure essence of life. A scholar's note found in the desert, partly redacted, argues that Khvarena resembles the Abyss in one structural way: it assimilates whatever energy and material it touches and has a kind of self-recognition, though it is opposed to the Abyss's energy. This is one writer's inference and canon does not confirm it.

## origins
### STANDARD
Nabu Malikata, one of the three companion-gods of the old desert kingdom, foresaw the cataclysm. She separated a piece of her own being, called it her innocent and pure daughter, and gave it to her friend Greater Lord Rukkhadevata to protect and to use against the coming darkness. After Nabu and King Deshret died, Rukkhadevata, per the artifact texts, turned the Khvarena into a divine bird.
### LARGE +
Rukkhadevata shaped the Khvarena into a beautiful form: the divine bird Simurgh. The texts say she charged her with the protection of two worlds and the guardianship of "the border between new birth and death". Simurgh lived among pines, cypresses and snow-lotuses and slept until disaster came. The artifact texts present Rukkhadevata's act as the keeping of a pact with her fallen friends.
### ULTRA +
Each piece of Vourukasha's Glow tells part of this story in verse from a different side, and the details sometimes differ, so it is best treated as in-world legend. The Flower of Life piece is Nabu speaking to the Khvarena; the Sands piece has Nabu asking Rukkhadevata to guard the Khvarena; the Goblet piece has the Khvarena speaking of itself as the remnant of its mistress. The core events agree.

## simurgh_and_the_pari
### STANDARD
When the cataclysm came, the Amrita (made from the remains of Egeria, the earlier Hydro Archon, slain in the upheaval) began to be corrupted by the Abyss. To save it, Simurgh flew into the pool and shattered her form, becoming countless motes of Khvarena. These formed the Vourukasha Oasis, a sea of flowers. The only motes with intelligence became the Pari, who consider their god to be Egeria, whose consciousness lies in the Harvisptokhm.
### LARGE +
Pari live in the Vourukasha Oasis. Each is said to have a fated responsibility to carry out alone. At the end of its life a Pari turns into a Plume of Purifying Light, which should be returned to the Amrita Pool. A Pari stained by Abyssal corruption is rejected by the Oasis and turns into a Fravashi Tree, so the humans who bond with Pari (called Yasnapati) are charged with bringing them home. Wiki listings also name Sunyata Flowers and Farrwicks as descendants of Simurgh.
### ULTRA +
In The Hymn of Tir Yazad, the Pari are said to have fought the cataclysm alongside Khaenri'ahn warriors (a masked swordsman and a one-armed sage) and Akademiya scholars who later became the Order of Skeptics. In the Khvarena of Good and Evil quests, Zurvan, an elder Pari, says that the Pari alone were given intelligence among all the motes, so they can speak with what remains of the mother-tree's consciousness and guide Khvarena's power.

## great_songs
@version: 3.6
### STANDARD
Zurvan says that, in the past, she used the Khvarena to repair the seal that held back the darkness and fused the strongest Khvarena into five Great Songs to purify the land. Item and tutorial text describe them as the physical form of Khvarena's might, which create barriers; only a Pari with the Twin-Horned Chaplet, the symbol of Khvarena's recognition, can touch them. Sorush, who is given the Chaplet, goes with the Traveler to collect them.
### LARGE +
In As the Khvarena's Light Shows, the Great Songs lie in hard-to-reach places in the Girdle of the Sands, including a ruined Dahrian factory underground. Nasejuna, who joined the search, turns on the party and says the Songs could reverse the Rite of Chinvat and release the Abyss seal. He and Klingsor of the Abyss Order demand the Songs, and the party refuses and takes them back to Zurvan.
### ULTRA +
The party notes that the Songs have little effect near a petrified Setekh Wenut, which suggests to Nasejuna that Khvarena acts only on things connected to the Abyss. The Songs belong to the wider effort, in the Hymn of Tir Yazad quests, to deal with the Sign of Apaosha, which Sorush had sworn to destroy.

## known_uses
### LITE
Purifying: cleansing Abyssal energy and Forbidden Knowledge, and repairing seals against the Abyss. Raising barriers. Giving life to the Vourukasha Oasis.
### STANDARD +
In Sorush's hands, orbs of coalesced Khvarena in the desert can be woken to expand a barrier that deflects incoming attacks. Pari can use their link to Khvarena to guide its power. Plumes of Purifying Light can be offered to the Amrita Pool.
### LARGE +
- Small Khvarena creatures, floating motes sometimes called mayflies, appear around tree stumps in the Girdle of the Sands as a catching challenge.
- Zurvan's account in the quests says Khvarena has been used to repair the seal over the darkness and to fuse the Great Songs.
- Item text for Plume of Purifying Light calls it a fragment left by Khvarena, a carrier of past memories.
### ULTRA +
An Ode to Khvarena is a text found in the House of Daena, said to be lore passed down within a lost religious group of the desert. It recalls the Khvarena as the glorious aid of the Lord of Wisdom scattered in endless forms, and invokes Nagarjuna with a vow to cleanse the sinless and let them be reborn in a pure land.

## limits
### LITE
Khvarena is not unlimited. Its sentient form, the Pari, lives out a fated duty and ends as a plume; the great form, Simurgh, gave up her shape. It cannot cleanse every taint: a Pari that is corrupted is rejected by the Oasis and becomes a tree.
### STANDARD +
Only a Pari with the Chaplet can handle the Great Songs. Their effect, in the quest, did not extend to things not tied to the Abyss. Canon shows no ordinary person wielding Khvarena as a power.
### LARGE +
Do not treat Khvarena as proof against all Abyssal threats; the cataclysm still devastated Sumeru and the Abyss Order remained active there.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not literal lore): Khvarena appears as exploration content in the Girdle of the Sands, including Khvarena Mayflies, Khvarena Barrier orbs, Plumes of Purifying Light and Great Songs, all tied to Sorush's gadget and the Khvarena of Good and Evil quests. The artifact set Vourukasha's Glow carries its lore in item texts.
### LARGE +
Vourukasha's Glow gives HP +20% for two pieces; at four, Elemental Skill and Elemental Burst DMG rises 10%, with an additional 80% after the wearer takes damage, for up to five stacks. Plumes of Purifying Light are offered to the Amrita Pool for rewards; 36 can be found in the Girdle of the Sands.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): portray Khvarena as soft, clean light, the feel of petals, dew and clear water, not as a weapon. Pari speak of it as a sacred inheritance and a duty.
### LARGE +
Keep it tied to sacrifice and renewal: a bright thing that gives itself up and is reborn. Do not let a Khvarena user become an unbeatable hero or simply overwrite the Abyss. Ordinary Sumeru residents are unlikely to know the full tale of Nabu Malikata and Simurgh.
### ULTRA +
Avoid calling Khvarena an element, a Gnosis or an Archon power. Do not give Khvarena to people outside the Pari lineage beyond what the quests show (Sorush's Chaplet and gadget).

## entry: Simurgh
@keys: Simurgh, divine bird
### STANDARD
Simurgh was the divine bird made when Greater Lord Rukkhadevata gave a shape to the Khvarena that Nabu Malikata had entrusted to her. She slept until the cataclysm, then shattered her form in the Amrita Pool to purify it, becoming countless motes of Khvarena. The Pari descend from her. See her own entry for details.

## entry: Pari
@keys: Pari, Plume of Purifying Light, Plumes of Purifying Light, Yasnapati
### STANDARD
The Pari are avian, flower-like descendants of Simurgh who live in the Vourukasha Oasis in the Girdle of the Sands. They are the only sentient form of Khvarena, each with a fated duty. When their life ends they become Plumes of Purifying Light, to be returned to the Amrita Pool. Tainted Pari turn into Fravashi Trees. Humans bonded with Pari are called Yasnapati.

## entry: Great Songs of Khvarena
@keys: Great Song of Khvarena, Great Songs of Khvarena, Twin-Horned Chaplet
@version: 3.6
### STANDARD
Five concentrated forms of Khvarena, fused by Zurvan from the strongest Khvarena collected in the flower sea to purify the land. Only a Pari carrying the Twin-Horned Chaplet can touch them. In the Khvarena of Good and Evil quests, Sorush and the Traveler gather them; Nasejuna, with the Abyss Order, tries to seize them.

## entry: Vourukasha's Glow
@keys: Vourukasha's Glow, Vourukasha Glow
### LARGE
Vourukasha's Glow is an artifact set whose five pieces tell the legend of Khvarena in verse: Nabu Malikata's farewell to her daughter, Rukkhadevata's pact, Simurgh's birth, the corruption of the Amrita and the founding of the flower sea. The pieces are Stamen of Khvarena's Origin, Vibrant Pinion, Ancient Abscission, Feast of Boundless Joy and Heart of Khvarena's Brilliance. Treat the texts as poetic legend; they do not always agree on small details.
