## summary
### LITE
A Vision is a jeweled amulet, worn on the body, that lets its bearer channel one of the seven elements directly. The bearers are called allogenes: people with unusually strong desires or ambitions who might one day ascend to Celestia and become gods. Most people never receive one. A Vision does not give mastery, only the channel; the power still has to be practised.
### STANDARD +
Common belief says the Archons hand Visions out, but canon states they do not pick recipients and do not know who receives them. Visions are given as a form of acknowledgement by Celestia, and they cannot be destroyed.

## in_universe
### LITE
A Vision links a person to an element (Pyro, Hydro, Anemo, Electro, Dendro, Cryo or Geo). Venti told the Traveler in the Prologue that Vision holders are allogenes who might ascend one day. A bearer has some elemental sensitivity and can sense their element more keenly than other people.
### STANDARD +
Bearers are not automatically skilled: ability varies from Shenhe freezing a tsunami to young Yaoyao channelling power through a device. Overuse causes exhaustion that only rest cures.
### LARGE +
Skill varies widely. Shenhe, who trained in the adeptus arts, can freeze a tsunami; the young Yaoyao has only been shown to channel her power through a device. Bearers can channel energy through ordinary objects, as Charlotte does with her camera. Everyday uses are plausible and canon shows them: Keqing cooks fish with Electro, Pyro lights fires, Hydro waters plants, Geo raises barriers. Bearers also resist things that harm others: they can stay in the Withering Zones of Sumeru far longer than a person without elemental ability, and they breathe underwater in Fontaine, where non-bearers need a diving suit.
Cutscenes and official media show more than gameplay does. Ningguang teleports and summons the Guizhong Ballistae of the Jade Chamber with Geo energy, Mona can teleport herself and those she touches using Hydro, and Tartaglia uses a Hydro shield and a huge whale in his boss fight. Bearers have a degree of elemental sensitivity, and a Vision glows when it resonates with a nearby source of elemental energy. When Fischl received hers she subconsciously manifested Oz, whose look is based on a character from her favourite novel series.
### ULTRA +
Nicole is among the very few people who understand how a Vision works, and Albedo senses there is a deeper secret behind them. A "helix-shaped divine ladder" is said to be visible within a Vision, and also to those who ascend; it has not been shown in the game.

## origins
@version: 4.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Canon's account, pieced together: the Vision system is a comparatively new rule of Teyvat, set up by the Heavenly Principles at an indeterminate time after the War of the Funerary Flame. Before that war Celestia ruled humanity directly through Angels; afterwards the link to the heavens was severed, the Angels (now Seelies) fell from grace, and the Heavenly Principles were left weakened. To compensate, they worked with an unidentified entity known only as "the one who came after" to build a new order of Archons. Seven Gnoses were made from the remains of the Third Descender, and the Archon War selected seven victors who each received a Gnosis and an elemental Authority taken from the Dragon Sovereigns. Once established, the Seven were bound by duty to give a shard of their mastery as a Vision to anyone whose desire grew strong enough to "step into the threshold of the sacred."
### ULTRA +
Neuvillette's Vision story implies that the gods collect a gift "more abundant still" when a Vision bearer fulfils their duty; the gift is never specified, and the Seven are not allowed to speak freely about it. Neuvillette also says constellations are merely the Heavenly Principles' way of playing with the living beings of Teyvat. The Narzissenkreuz Ordo held that taking a Vision is to sell oneself to the fate of this world.

## allogenes
### LITE
Allogene is the in-universe term for a person who is fit to hold a Vision. In the original Chinese, Japanese and Korean the word is the same as the game's title and means roughly "primordial god" or "original god". Allogenes may ascend to Celestia, though most do not.
### STANDARD +
Venti, in Ending Note, calls Vision holders allogenes who could one day ascend. The Archons are not the givers, so an allogene is not a favourite of their own nation's god, although many believe otherwise. Allogenes who achieve their goals are said to cause the gods to receive an abundant gift (Neuvillette's Vision story).
### LARGE +
Some people attribute their Vision to their nation's Archon even when the element does not match, others to the Archon of their element even when they come from another nation, and others to the gods in general. People who never receive a Vision sometimes feel ignored by the Archons despite their deeds. One character wonders how Liyue's old soldiers stayed loyal to Rex Lapis without Visions, and another compares living without one to living like an outsider in one's own world. Both attitudes rest on the mistaken belief that the Seven control distribution.

## receiving
### LITE
There is no sure way to get a Vision. They arrive during crises and fights, at moments of resolve or revelation, after major accomplishments, or on an otherwise ordinary day. Survival alone is not enough.
### STANDARD +
The wiki sorts the cases into three groups. Some bearers receive it directly before or on them while conscious, in a crisis, a resolution or after an accomplishment. Some notice it nearby, or find it later among their belongings, after making a resolution. Others receive it while asleep or unconscious, during or after a crisis, unrelated to one, or after reacting in a dream. A person worthy of a Vision either receives a new one or reactivates a nearby Masterless Vision.
### LARGE +
Most humans receive theirs between childhood and early adulthood, as children hope for one and adults may decide none will come. Confirmed ages at receipt include Shenhe (6), Diluc (10), Hu Tao (13) and Fischl (14). Kaveh received his after raising funds to rebuild the Palace of Alcazarzaray, Citlali got hers after turning 70, and Ganyu, a half-adeptus, got hers after the Archon War ended 2,000 years ago. Lauma received her Moon Wheel shortly after birth, which she finds troubling.
### ULTRA +
Non-human beings including Xiao, Yae Miko, Kirara, Xianyun and Sigewinne can receive Visions, though the narrative questions whether theirs are the same as humans'. Artificial humans such as Albedo, the Wanderer and Ineffa can too. The Traveler's Vision story says they cannot receive one since they are an outsider to Teyvat; Yae Miko suggests they simply lack a strong enough ambition, one that transcends the world below and the starry sky above. There are no known cases of a person with more than one permanently active Vision.

## wearing
### LARGE
Most bearers wear their Vision openly, usually at the waist or back, or attached to a belonging such as a diary or pouch. It does not have to be visible to work: Diluc uses his Pyro Vision in his Red Dead of Night disguise. Visions can be customised with tassels, ribbons and charms.
There seems to be a point beyond which a person can no longer connect to their Vision. Tartaglia left his with the Traveler so it would not run out of control again; the item text says it has lost its glow now that its owner left it. Visions taken from their owners under the Vision Hunt Decree and installed in the Statue of the Omnipresent God likewise looked dulled. Visions share a regional shape and differ in finish, size and personal decoration.

## losing
### STANDARD
Visions cannot be destroyed, according to Keqing's research, but they can be separated from their owners, willingly or by force. A person parted from their Vision may lose the ambition that drove them, and sometimes memories too; others show no ill effect.
### LARGE +
Under the Vision Hunt Decree, Tejima and Kurosawa Kyounosuke lost memories that defined their past actions, while Domon hallucinated the people he had beaten until he spoke with Anzai. Arataki Itto stayed as rowdy after losing his in a duel with Kujou Sara, and Kuki Shinobu gave hers up willingly and was not much troubled. Nobumori, who surrendered his to Kamisato Ayato, says his old obsessions all disappeared. Jiangxue, who had already won the justice he sought, calls himself free of desire. Those who regain a Vision regain their drive, sometimes muted: Domon felt he had changed too much and left his dream to his disciples. People who have given up a Vision still seem to keep some elemental sensitivity.
### ULTRA +
Diluc's aspirations, which burned bright in his Vision, were extinguished after Crepus's death; he left Mondstadt and his Vision, and used a Delusion with no apparent ill effect. He returned and resumed using his Vision once his ambition was restored. Many who lost Visions to the Decree did not despair and joined the Watatsumi Army.

## masterless
### LITE
When an allogene dies, their Vision goes dull, losing colour and power, and becomes a Masterless Vision. A Masterless Vision rarely resonates with someone new.
### STANDARD +
A Masterless Vision wakes when a person's desire matches the original owner's, and takes on the same element as before. Kaedehara Kazuha briefly woke his late friend's Electro Vision while deflecting the Raiden Shogun's Musou no Hitotachi, using it alongside his own Anemo Vision. It went dormant again afterwards. Most people think two Visions at once is impossible.
### LARGE +
Mona and Ningguang are among those who have reawakened Masterless Visions. Separately, counterfeit Visions exist: Venti's fake Vision, which can turn into his lyre, is the only fake known to have a use beyond deception. In a related oddity, in the play "The Little Oceanid", when Furina replaced the actress in the role of Clio, a genuine Hydro Vision appeared before her instead of the prop.

## distribution
### LARGE
Contrary to popular belief, the Archons do not control who receives a Vision. Giving away a shard of their Authority is part of their duty, and they do not know the recipient or the desire. Visions come from whoever holds the elemental Authority, who need not be one of the Seven: after regaining his full Hydro Authority, Neuvillette willingly set aside a portion of it for humans to claim.
After the Vision Hunt Decree began, Electro Visions stopped appearing in Inazuma; scholars at the Sumeru Akademiya who studied this concluded that the Archons' wills decide who gets one. Kazuha likewise guesses that each Archon has their own criteria. But Ei had been sealed in the Plane of Euthymia for about 400 years and was surprised to hear from the Traveler that new Electro Visions had stopped, so the scholars' conclusion is a belief, not settled fact; the true reason is unknown.

## appearance
### LARGE
Visions are orbs set in a regional frame, mostly gold or polished bronze with a few in silver or grey. Mondstadt: a pair of tri-feathered wings. Inazuma: a bow ring with three round studs. Sumeru: a teardrop or leaf frame. Fontaine: ribbon and clamshell ornament in two Arkhe-aligned variants. Natlan: a saltire with meander patterns. Snezhnaya: an orb with angled protrusions top, bottom and sides. For Nod-Krai and Fatui Visions a silver or grey finish is the norm.
Venti's "Vision" is an ordinary glass orb he uses to avoid suspicion, and Zhongli's is probably not a real Vision either. Furina's borrows from both Arkhe-aligned designs, matching her ability to switch alignment. Nicole, an angel who kept her form, has a unique flame-and-wing frame. Aloy's four-pointed star design is of unknown canonicity, since it may be a collaboration feature. The Snezhnayan shape resembles a military badge. The Fatui's Delusions look the same but carry the Fatui emblem in place of the element symbol.

## related_objects
### LITE
Delusions, Gnoses, Moon Wheels and Stellar Linchpins are separate things; do not call them Visions.
### STANDARD +
A Gnosis is a chess-piece-like object that embodies the Heavenly Principles' control of Teyvat, with only seven made. Archons use their powers without needing it, while humans usually depend on a Vision. A Delusion is a Fatui device that lets anyone wield elements, more powerfully than a Vision and at serious cost, such as lost vitality. Bearers of a Vision or elemental ability resist the effects better.
### LARGE +
Moon Wheels (Nod-Krai) look like a Nod-Krai Vision and Stellar Linchpins (Snezhnaya) are four-pointed star objects. The wiki notes that Nod-Krai Visions are indistinguishable from Moon Wheels, which serves as a lore explanation for who cannot enable Lunar reactions via a Moonsign Benediction passive. Vesna's element source in 7.1 is a Stellar Linchpin rather than a Vision.

## gameplay
### LARGE
Gameplay (not literal lore): each playable character's element corresponds to their Vision, shown in the element icon of their character card. The Traveler has no Vision but resonates with Statues of The Seven to use elements. Vision bearers may use Portable Waypoints.
Gameplay (not literal lore): Elemental Sight is the game's name for sensing elemental sources, and Visions glow near them.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): treat a Vision as a personal symbol of an unmet or fulfilled ambition. Characters may touch or glance at it when they speak of their goals. Do not make it a mere weapon permit: mention how its owner received it, and let characters differ in attitude, from pride to unease.
### LARGE +
A Vision glows faintly when its holder uses or senses the element. Someone who has lost theirs may seem dulled or forgetful about a goal, but canon shows great variation, so not everyone suffers. Never have an Archon say they gave someone a Vision, and do not let an ordinary person know the Celestia link in detail unless the character plausibly learned it.
### ULTRA +
Avoid showing a Vision as granting unlimited power, as freely transferable between living people, or as destructible. Keep Fatui Delusions distinct from Visions. Do not give the Traveler a Vision.

## entry: Allogene
@keys: allogene, allogenes
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
An allogene is a person with a desire strong enough to hold a Vision and, in principle, to ascend to Celestia. Venti defines Vision holders this way in Ending Note. The word is the same in Chinese, Japanese and Korean as the game's title.

## entry: Masterless Vision
@keys: Masterless Vision, Masterless Visions
### STANDARD
A Vision whose owner has died. It loses its colour and elemental power and is an empty husk until someone with the same desire resonates with it. Kazuha's temporary use of his friend's Electro Vision is the best-known case.

## entry: Vision Hunt Decree
@keys: Vision Hunt Decree
@version: 2.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Inazuma's decree under the Raiden Shogun collected Visions from their holders. Losing a Vision had varied effects on memory and ambition, and Electro Visions stopped appearing during it. Full details belong to the Decree's own entry.

## entry: Vision Shell
@keys: Vision Shell, Vision casing
### LARGE
Visions come in a decorative casing set by the nation the person was in when they received it, usually with the same basic shape for the whole region. The finish is usually gold or bronze, and personal decorations such as tassels and charms are added by the owner. Whether the original casing can be altered is not stated, though Yelan's outfit changes the finish.

## entry: Microcosmic Orbit
@keys: Microcosmic Orbit, Microcosmos
@availability: limited
### ULTRA
Because Vision bearers are matched to constellations, they are said to each hold the power of a tiny cosmos; Liyue's thaumaturges call this the "Microcosmic Orbit" or "Microcosmos". This comes from a challenge in the limited-time Hyakunin Ikki event (2022), so not everyone will know the term.
