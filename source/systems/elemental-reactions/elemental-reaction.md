## summary
### LITE
An Elemental Reaction happens when an element is applied to a target that already carries a different element. Attacks, Visions and elemental creatures leave an "aura" of their element on a target, and a second element meeting it triggers an effect such as Vaporize, Melt, Overloaded, Swirl, Bloom or Crystallize. It is chiefly a combat rule, so treat it as a gameplay abstraction that a scene may borrow from, not as a named in-world law.
### STANDARD +
Reactions fall into groups by how they work: Transformative reactions deal their own damage, Amplifying reactions multiply the hit that triggered them, and Additive reactions add to it. Two newer families sit on top of these: Lunar reactions (from the Hydro-related reactions) and Stellar Glimmer reactions (from the Cryo-related ones).

## gameplay
### LITE
Gameplay (not literal lore): when an element is applied to an enemy, player or object it leaves an aura that fades over time. Applying a second, different element triggers a reaction, which consumes some or all of the first aura. Many attack sources have an internal cooldown, so they cannot reapply their element, or trigger a reaction, on every hit.
### STANDARD +
The base reactions by element pair, as listed on the wiki's overview table:
- Pyro with Hydro: Vaporize. Pyro with Electro: Overloaded. Pyro with Cryo: Melt. Pyro with Dendro: Burning.
- Hydro with Electro: Electro-Charged. Hydro with Cryo: Frozen (a frozen target hit by Geo or a blunt Physical attack Shatters). Hydro with Dendro: Bloom.
- Electro with Cryo: Superconduct. Electro with Dendro: Quicken.
- Anemo with Pyro, Hydro, Electro or Cryo: Swirl. Geo with Pyro, Hydro, Electro or Cryo: Crystallize.
- Bloom can follow with Pyro as Burgeon or with Electro as Hyperbloom; Quicken can follow with Dendro as Spread or with Electro as Aggravate.
- Dendro with Cryo, and Dendro with Anemo or Geo, do not react. Geo cannot be the first element on a target, so it only ever reacts as the second.
### LARGE +
Transformative reactions are Overloaded, Shattered, Electro-Charged, Superconduct, Swirl, Burning, Bloom, Hyperbloom and Burgeon. Each deals its own damage, based on the reaction type and the level of the character who triggered it, scaled by that character's Elemental Mastery; it cannot CRIT and ignores the target's DEF but is affected by Resistance.

Amplifying reactions are Vaporize and Melt. They deal no damage themselves; they multiply the triggering attack by 2x or 1.5x depending on which element came second, and Elemental Mastery boosts the multiplier further. The wiki's table: Hydro onto Pyro and Pyro onto Cryo give 2x; Pyro onto Hydro and Cryo onto Pyro give 1.5x. The 1.5x versions consume less of the first element and often leave the aura in place.

Additive reactions (Spread and Aggravate; an unofficial grouping name, which the game calls "Catalyze Reactions" in Miliastra Wonderland) add a flat bonus to the base damage of the triggering hit rather than multiplying it, and Spread's bonus is slightly higher than Aggravate's.

A reaction's strength is taken from the character who triggers it; the stats of whoever left the first aura do not count. Some Elemental Resonances react to reactions: High Voltage (two Electro characters) can generate Electro particles on several reactions, and Sprawling Greenery (two Dendro characters) grants Elemental Mastery after Dendro reactions.
### ULTRA +
Persistent auras are a special case. Players cannot apply a lasting Anemo or Geo status, so Swirl against an Anemo aura is practically limited to the Anemo Hypostasis, and certain enemies' lasting Geo status cannot be reacted with (the wiki's note adds that players cannot apply a persistent Geo status without Planet Befall).

The wiki cites the Behind the Scenes video "To Snezhnaya and the Future" for the statement that the reaction system was designed long before combat strategy and numerical planning, as modular, intuitive rules to support interaction. Further depth, such as gauge units and decay, belongs to the wiki's "Elemental Gauge Theory" page, which I identified but did not summarise here.

## lunar_reactions
@version: 5.8
### STANDARD
Lunar reactions are a subset of reactions. To trigger one, the party must include a character with a Moonsign Benediction passive talent that converts specific Hydro-related reactions. There are three: Lunar-Charged (Electro with Hydro), Lunar-Bloom (Dendro with Hydro) and Lunar-Crystallize (Geo with Hydro). Unlike their base versions, Lunar reactions can CRIT.
### LARGE +
Lunar damage can be dealt directly by a character's talent, or (for all but Lunar-Bloom) as a separate instance that draws on the stats of every contributing character. It ignores DMG Bonus and the target's DEF but is affected by Resistance, and its base damage is boosted by Moonsign Benediction passives and by Elemental Mastery. The wiki records Lunar-Charged and the family itself as introduced in Version 5.8, Lunar-Bloom in Luna I and Lunar-Crystallize in Luna IV.
### ULTRA +
Each has its own signature effect on the wiki's description. Lunar-Charged summons a thundercloud that strikes wet and Electro-affected enemies every 2 seconds and lasts 6 seconds, only one existing at a time. Lunar-Bloom gives the player Verdant Dew, up to 3, which persists across teleports and sessions. Lunar-Crystallize creates three Moondrifts, which every third reaction resonate into a tracking Geo projectile (Moondrift Harmony). Moonsign itself has its own entry.

## stellar_glimmer_reactions
@version: 6.7
### STANDARD
Stellar Glimmer reactions are a subset of reactions, triggered when a party includes a character with a passive talent that converts specific Cryo-related reactions. There are two: Stellar-Conduct (Cryo with Electro) and Stellar Swirl (Anemo with Cryo). They also interrupt a specific attack of Snowland Fae and Aberrant Chimeric Monsters.
### LARGE +
Stellar Glimmer damage can be dealt directly by a talent or, for Stellar Swirl, as a separate instance using the stats of all contributing characters. It ignores DMG Bonus and DEF, is affected by Resistance, and is boosted by Stellar Jubilee passives and Elemental Mastery. Those passives belong to Snezhnaya characters with Stellar Linchpins and to the Cryo Traveler. The wiki records the family and Stellar-Conduct as introduced in Version Luna VIII (6.7, with Sandrone) and Stellar Swirl in 7.0.
### ULTRA +
Stellar-Conduct leaves a Starlight Prism for 7 seconds that sustains a Polestar Field boosting Cryo and Electro damage and lowering Physical RES, and which also affects the Kresnik Energy mechanic in the Snezhnayan open world. Stellar Swirl deals Anemo damage and creates a Stellar Vortex that bursts with Cryo area damage, grows stronger once Stellar Swirl has been triggered three times, and raises the player's next jump. The wiki's lore note says Saarelainen introduced Stellar Glimmer reactions to ancient Snezhnaya, citing the Version 7.0 Special Program.

## in_universe
### STANDARD
Canon discussion of reactions is thin: most of the system is delivered as tutorial text and combat feedback. The pages read offer one clear in-world statement, that Stellar Glimmer reactions were brought to ancient Snezhnaya by Saarelainen. Stellar Linchpin users are described in quest material as having power that appears to come from beyond Teyvat, and the Snezhnayan reactions are enabled by their Stellar Jubilee passives.
### LARGE +
The Stellar Linchpin page, citing the quests "Should We Meet Again" and "Mistilteinn", says the Fatui link Linchpins to the deceased Third Descender and plan to use their energy in Project Mistilteinn against the Heavenly Principles. I did not read those quests directly. The wiki identifies the Third Descender as Saarelainen, the same figure credited with bringing Stellar Glimmer reactions to ancient Snezhnaya; exactly how the reactions relate to the Linchpins' otherworldly power is not spelled out.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): describe reactions as visible, sensible results of two elements colliding. Fire on water becomes a burst of steam, fire on ice a fast melt, lightning in water a crackling spread, wind carrying flame, frost or sparks outward, stone crystallising around an element. Keep the effects local to the people involved.
### LARGE +
Characters can notice that certain elements go well together without a formal "aura" theory; do not have ordinary characters speak in gauge units, internal cooldowns or multipliers. Treat Lunar and Stellar Glimmer effects as phenomena tied to Moonsign or Snezhnayan Linchpin users, not something a Vision holder does unaided. Hot-and-cold combinations, like Melt, are a natural fit for dramatic scenes.

## limits
### STANDARD
Do not treat the tables above as cosmic law. Dendro does not react with Cryo, Anemo or Geo, and Physical damage reacts only as the Shatter follow-up to Frozen. Do not credit a reaction to the wrong trigger: a reaction counts for the character that triggers it, not the one who left the first aura.
### LARGE +
Do not claim the in-world cause of reactions, who discovered them, or that a god designed them; canon does not say, apart from the Saarelainen note. Do not move Lunar or Stellar Glimmer reactions earlier than the versions that introduced them (5.8 for Lunar-Charged, 6.7 for Stellar-Conduct and 7.0 for Stellar Swirl, as the wiki records them).

## entry: Transformative Reactions
@keys: Transformative Reactions, Transformative Reaction
### STANDARD
Transformative reactions deal their own instance of damage, separate from the hit that caused them: Overloaded, Shattered, Electro-Charged, Superconduct, Swirl, Burning, Bloom, Hyperbloom and Burgeon. Their damage depends on reaction type, the triggering character's level and Elemental Mastery; it cannot CRIT.

## entry: Amplifying Reactions
@keys: Amplifying Reactions, Amplifying Reaction
### STANDARD
Vaporize and Melt multiply the damage of the attack that triggers them. The stronger "forward" case is 2x (Hydro onto Pyro, Pyro onto Cryo), the weaker "reverse" 1.5x (Pyro onto Hydro, Cryo onto Pyro), and the weaker case tends to leave more of the first element in place.

## entry: Additive Reactions
@keys: Additive Reactions, Catalyze Reactions
### STANDARD
Spread and Aggravate, the follow-ups to Quicken, add a flat bonus to the base damage of the triggering hit. The name "Additive Reactions" is unofficial; the game's Miliastra Wonderland mode calls them "Catalyze Reactions".

## entry: Elemental Aura
@keys: elemental aura, element aura
### STANDARD
An aura is the lasting mark of an element on a target. It decays over time, and triggering a reaction shortens or removes it by an amount that depends on the reaction and the strength of the second element.
