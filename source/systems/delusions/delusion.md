## summary
### LITE
Delusions are Vision-like devices made by the Fatui. They let their wielders command an element, can be used by almost anyone, and are said to be far stronger than Visions. The price is the user's vitality: they can drain life force, age the wearer or backfire fatally. How they are made is secret; the evidence points to the remains of dead gods. The Chinese name means "Evil Eye", a counterpart to the "Eye of God".
### STANDARD +
Few Fatui members outside the Eleven Harbingers hold one, and the Tsaritsa grants them herself, according to the Fatui member Viktor. In practice, Delusions have also been handed to ordinary people in Inazuma and carried by defectors abroad. As of 7.1 the Fatui are working on a finished model that drains only the regenerable part of a user's life force.

## in_universe
### LITE
A Delusion looks like a Vision with a Fatui emblem instead of an element symbol. It is a glass orb in a jagged frame, and its colour follows the element it grants. Unlike a Vision, which the gods give to one person, a Delusion can be used by anyone who holds it.
### STANDARD +
Viktor calls Delusions the pride of the Fatui and says they come by the Tsaritsa's own hand. Pyro Agents, Cicin Mages, Mirror Maidens, Operatives and Oprichniki soldiers are all seen with Delusions in the colour of their element. The Fatui treat the subject as a closely guarded secret, even among themselves.
### LARGE +
- In hindsight, players first see one at work in the Prologue, when Signora's blizzard freezes Venti's legs: the power is later shown to come from a Cryo Delusion given to her to suppress her fire, not boost it.
- Tartaglia's Electro Delusion was pinned on him by Pierro, called the Jester, when he became a Harbinger. He keeps a Hydro Vision as well, so a Delusion is not bound to its wearer's Vision element.
- Higher-ranking Oprichniki soldiers in Nod-Krai carry Delusions with axe-head prongs on each side.
### ULTRA +
Delusions also show up outside the Fatui's ranks. Yelena, a renegade Fatuus, kept hers when she and her brother Kirya fled to Sumeru and then Fontaine; Kirya later took it up after her death. Diluc once carried the Delusion that backfired on his father Crepus. These cases are in the entries below.

## origins_and_production
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Canon does not describe a general method. The Delusions made in Inazuma used Crystal Marrow, which holds the Tatarigami curse of the slain god Orobashi. The wiki therefore reads Delusions as made from the remains of gods or other powerful beings, but that inference is not stated as a rule.
### LARGE +
At the Delusion Factory on Yashiori Island (Archon Quest Chapter II, 2.1), the Traveler found rooms full of Crystal Marrow supplied by the Kanjou Commission under the Sakoku Decree. The Fatui Balladeer said openly that the Vision Hunt Decree suited the Fatui because conflict created a market for Delusions; the factory was later raided and destroyed by the Resistance.
### ULTRA +
In the manga, Delusion research is linked to experiments on injecting "archon residue" (remains of ancient gods; a translation note on the wiki says the original term means pre-Seven demon gods) into people such as Collei, and to Dottore's subordinates. That material is manga-only and is treated as uncertain.

## side_effects
### LITE
Delusions drain the user's life force and can backfire. Resistance soldiers in Inazuma aged rapidly; Teppei died of it. Diluc's father Crepus was fatally hurt when one backfired.
### STANDARD +
Effects vary. In the manga, Diluc carried his father's Delusion for three years without obvious ill effect, and people who already hold a Vision or wield elemental power resist abnormal elemental energy better, according to Emilie. Yelena aged quickly as she used hers to grow the Auguste flowers, and her brother Kirya, using it for the same purpose, aged himself by decades. In the manga, Delusions give off a smell of Mist Grass when used.
### LARGE +
- Pierro's use of the Eye of the Fatui in 7.1 cost his mind and soul heavily, per Dainsleif afterwards.
- Signora's Delusion is described as different: item text frames it as the Tsaritsa's grace, offered to put out the flame that consumed her, and the wiki reports it suppressed her memories.
- Tartaglia shows a drawback only when his Delusion is combined with the Abyss technique Foul Legacy Transformation.
- Arlecchino says the drain of life force in exchange for power falls under the authority of Death (7.1).
### ULTRA +
The Jester says the Fatui's Delusions have always been an unfinished product that drains the user's life force (7.1). They are issued only on a voluntary basis: members apply for them for important missions or emergencies, and nobody is forced to take one.

## notable_users
### STANDARD
Harbingers with Delusions in canon: Tartaglia (Electro), Signora (Cryo), Arlecchino (Pyro, used when she draws on her Balemoon Bloodfire) and Pierro (Electro). Others: Fatui Agents, Cicin Mages, Mirror Maidens, Operatives and Oprichniki. Outside the Fatui: Crepus (dead), Diluc (formerly), Yelena (dead), Kirya, and Inazuma's Watatsumi Army soldiers (formerly), among them Teppei (dead).
### LARGE +
Arlecchino wears a Pyro Vision on her back and swaps it for a Pyro Delusion when she calls on her bloodfire. Pierro hides his Electro Delusion under his clothes rather than wearing it openly.

## delusion_project
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In the 7.1 Archon Quest (Chapter VII Act IV, "A Rekviem for the Underworld"), the Fatui build a new model of Delusion in the Armory Palace before facing Ronova, the Ruler of Death. The Jester (Pierro) directs the project and Arlecchino volunteers as test subject, because as a descendant of the Crimson Moon she holds a share of the authority of Death, which the drain of life force falls under. Her price for taking part is that the House of the Hearth becomes independent of the Fatui and stops training children as spies. Researchers promise the new model will only draw on the regenerable part of life force. In the Traveler-assisted tests, Arlecchino's baseline power came out about three times higher than expected, but at maximum output her readings went out of control; the researchers concluded the new model had drawn on another Crimson Moon authority besides Death, an unstable variable that can induce hallucinations and loss of control. The Traveler purified the Abyssal energy in time and the test was stopped with no lasting harm to her. She judged the output still too timid to stand against Ronova.
### ULTRA +
Pierro then completed the Delusion as the "Eye of the Fatui", a more jagged form resembling the symbol of Khaenri'ah. He used it in the battle with Ronova, summoning an elemental nail whose strike, with Dainsleif and the Traveler, decided the fight. The cutscene text says it cost him his own soul and life force. Dainsleif later says the battle took an exceptionally heavy toll on his mind and soul, and Paimon notes he had not been seen all day. Ronova's authority was reclaimed by Celestia, and her Curse of Death lifted with it.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay, not lore: Fatui enemies carry Delusions as part of their models. Operative's Constancy, an enhancement material, is dropped by Wind and Frost Operatives and is the item tied to Delusions. In the 7.1 quest, Arlecchino is a temporary trial character who gains Bond of Life quickly during tests, and the player must keep an Abyssal Influence Index from rising too high.
### LARGE +
Tartaglia's boss fight in the Golden House has phases where his Delusion is shown (Electro) and a final phase where Vision and Delusion swap places.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): treat a Delusion as a bargain, not a free power. Show the cost in the user: breathlessness, greying, a look of being older than their years. A Fatui who speaks of one should do so with pride or guarded caution, and never explain how it is made. Let someone who knows Visions sense that a Delusion feels wrong: cold, hungry and without the warm sense of being chosen.
### LARGE +
A Fatui character will not volunteer how few people hold a Delusion; Viktor is an example of a lower-ranked member who enjoys boasting about them. In Inazuma, once soldiers began to age, Kokomi ordered the whole army to stop using Delusions and had the affected soldiers treated.
### ULTRA +
Do not claim Delusions are made of a particular god in a general way, give every Fatui a Delusion, or make the drain painless for ordinary people. Do not treat the Eye of the Fatui as standard issue; it is Pierro's.

## entry: Crepus's Delusion
@keys: Crepus's Delusion, Ragnvindr Delusion, Crepus Delusion
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Diluc's father Crepus used a Delusion to defeat a monster, Ursa the Drake, and it backfired and left him fatally hurt. Diluc's and Kaeya's character stories tell the outcome differently: Diluc's says Crepus died in his arms, Kaeya's says Diluc ended his father's agony. Diluc left the Knights and traced the "counterfeit Visions" to the Fatui; in the manga he also takes up his father's Delusion. In the manga it is a red orb on a glove that raises barbed chains, sometimes wrapped in black fire; this version is manga-only.

## entry: Delusion Factory
@keys: Delusion Factory, Yashiori Delusion factory
@version: 2.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A hidden facility on Yashiori Island, found by the Traveler during the Inazuma Archon Quest (2.1). Fatui staff made Delusions there from Crystal Marrow, supplied via the Kanjou Commission. The Balladeer said the Fatui gained from the Vision Hunt Decree because it created demand. The Resistance destroyed the factory. Kokomi had already ordered the army to stop using Delusions after soldiers began to age.

## entry: Eye of the Fatui
@keys: Eye of the Fatui
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Pierro's upgraded Electro Delusion, completed through the 7.1 Delusion project in time for the battle against Ronova. Its casing is jagged, with extra protrusions, and resembles the symbol of Khaenri'ah. In that fight it let Pierro summon an enormous elemental nail, powered by a surge of Abyssal energy and his own life force, and it left him drained in mind and soul. The Chinese wording for "Vision" is "Eye of God", which gives the name its sense.

## entry: Watatsumi Army Delusions
@keys: Watatsumi Army Delusions, Teppei's Delusion
@version: 2.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Resistance soldiers, ambitious people opposed to the Vision Hunt Decree, secretly used Fatui Delusions and began to age fast. Gorou seized one, a black orb with a pale grey Fatui emblem, and showed it to the Traveler. Teppei, a soldier who never owned a Vision, had used one and was dying of its effects. Kokomi ordered the army to stop using them.

## entry: Auguste Delusion
@keys: Auguste perfume, Auguste flower
@version: 4.8
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Yelena, a defected Fatui member, used her Delusion to grow Auguste flowers in Fontaine for the perfume; it aged her fast. After her death, her brother Kirya took up the Delusion to continue and to take revenge on her associates. In the 4.8 story quest "Base Note: Auguste", Emilie and Collei work out that the flowers and seeds carry elemental energy from the Delusion, which may harm sensitive people, and that using one costs the time the user has left.
