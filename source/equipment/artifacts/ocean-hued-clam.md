## summary
### LITE
Ocean-Hued Clam is a 4 and 5 star artifact set from Watatsumi Island's songs. Its texts recall Omikami, the sea god the islanders worshipped; the first Divine Priestess and her twin sisters; shells and pearls given as tokens of family; and the sacrifice that ended that age. It is a healing set whose 4-piece turns healing into burst damage.
### STANDARD +
It drops from the Slumbering Court domain on Seirai Island, and came with Version 2.3. The texts are legend rather than record: they say what the islanders sing, and the Pearl Cage admits that the tale may be an ancient story that can no longer be verified.

## appearance
### LARGE
Five pieces: Sea-Dyed Blossom (flower, a soft sea flower with a pearl core that shimmers many colours in moonlight), Deep Palace's Plume (plume, a coral-coloured down feather said to come from a shrine maiden's ceremonial garment), Cowry of Parting (sands, a clean, flawless shell from the bottomless ocean), Pearl Cage (goblet, the ever-shining pearls shrine maidens offer), and Crown of Watatsumi (circlet, an ancient pearl-and-coral crown of a forgotten clergy, now ceremonially enshrined).

## lore
### LITE
Watatsumi's people say they came out of the dark depths, and took a single shell each as a keepsake of their clan; those who had none were welcomed into another family. Omikami chose a Divine Priestess and rulers from among them, and the age ended in sacrifice.
### LARGE +
Cowry of Parting: the ocean people left the silent glowing depths and escaped the gaze of the Dragonheirs by climbing a coral stair into daylight. Each took a seashell to remember their clan; the shells came to be called Parcels of Reunion in the old tongue, and two parties who embrace on one should not be parted by an outside force, though the bond is not eternal. The shells mark a farewell to life under the waves and the start of life under the sun.

Deep Palace's Plume and Pearl Cage: when the clans first saw daylight Omikami chose shrine maidens from the sea people. The first Divine Priestess was a pearl-gathering sea-daughter who comforted children lost in senseless fighting and old people who had lost sight of life's beauty; her songs gave the people hope for the first time. The plume comes from her ceremonial garb, pulled out by a child by mistake and kept by a worried person. Only she could sing the great song of the pearls, which the rainbow-hued clams produced in gratitude for Watatsumi's tenderness, and some said her bloodline began in those pearls. At the moment of defeat the Priestess and the twin sisters traded clothes and hid in the waves; one bright pearl was lost and sank back to the silent depths.
### ULTRA +
Crown of Watatsumi: Omikami picked its clergy from the people and gave them circlets of office; when the age of martyrdom ended, the old priesthood ended and the crowns were sealed. The people sing that pearl-and-coral crowns never stain and that whoever receives one is recognised as a ruler by Omikami. The valiant vassal lord Touzannou and the twins who crossed the waves freely (a note identifies them as the Yuna twins, Mouun and Ayame) had Omikami's tender favour and live on in the islanders' songs, and once served as the god's auxiliaries, guiding the sea people in farming, fishing and hunting. As the time of destined martyrdom came a god fell, and the crowns passed with their owners into forgotten rifts.

Sea-Dyed Blossom: songs say the flowers bloom in the pearl-lit depths, steeped in the lovesickness of diving sea-daughters and in moonlight. When conflict ceases, sea beasts will no longer weep for lonely companions, and when the moon rises over Touzan the lovely deity will rise and sing, with the lightning and storm unable to hide the moon's pearly glory.

## owners
### LARGE
No individual owns the set; its pieces are ceremonial and sacred items of the Watatsumi priesthood, the Divine Priestess and the Yuna twins' circle, as told in island songs.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Gameplay, not lore: the 2-piece bonus is Healing Bonus +15%. The 4-piece bonus makes each heal create a Sea-Dyed Foam that collects the healing for 3 seconds (up to 30,000 HP, overflow included) and bursts as Physical-type damage of 90% of the total, with one foam every 3.5 seconds and one at a time; it works off-field. The damage ignores Elemental Mastery, level and reaction bonuses, and counts healing absorbed by Bond of Life.

## entry: Omikami and Watatsumi's faith
@keys: Omikami, Watatsumi Omikami, Orobashi no Mikoto
@version: 2.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Ocean-Hued Clam texts speak of Omikami as a deity who chose priestesses and rulers from the sea people, rejoiced in their love and gave pearls. Wiki summaries identify Omikami with Orobashi no Mikoto, the coral serpent once worshipped in Enkanomiya and on Watatsumi Island, who met its end near the close of the Archon War; the artifact texts themselves allude only to sacrifice and martyrdom, not to the war.

## entry: Parcels of Reunion
@keys: Parcels of Reunion, Cowry of Parting
@version: 2.3
### STANDARD
The Cowry of Parting says that in the old tongue the keepsake seashells of the Watatsumi people were called Parcels of Reunion; each person took one for their clan, and those without one were welcomed into a new family. The shell symbolises leaving the depths for daylight.

## entry: Divine Priestess and the twin sisters
@keys: Divine Priestess, Divine Priestess of Watatsumi, Yuna twins
@version: 2.3
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Divine Priestess of Watatsumi is, in these texts, the first of the shrine maidens, a pearl-gathering sea-daughter whose songs gave her people hope. She and her twin sisters were favoured by Omikami and, at the moment of defeat, swapped clothes and hid in the waves. A note names the Yuna twins as Mouun and Ayame, but it is not stated whether the Priestess is one of them.
