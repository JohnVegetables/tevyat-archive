## summary
### LITE
A Day Carved From Rising Winds is a 4- and 5-star artifact set (added in 6.3) about the old uprising against the storm god Decarabian in ancient Mondstadt and the birth of the wind god who became Barbatos. Its pieces are an oath of the Gunnhildr clan, a guard's laid-down sword, a bard's last song, an archer's final arrow and a huntress's doomed devotion to her king. It is obtained in Moonchild's Treasures in Nod-Krai.
### STANDARD +
The set's titles borrow German medieval verse terms (Spruchdichtung, Leich, Heldenepos, Minnesang). The pieces are Windborne Flower's Spruchdichtung, Dawn's Brilliant Oath, A Note in Spring's Leich, Heldenepos's Unspoken Tale and Minnesang of Love and Lament.

## appearance
### LARGE
Azure-themed relics: a crystal flower that never withers, an azure feather like the dawn, an hourglass of azure sand whose grains refuse to move, a weathered azure goblet, and a luxurious hair accessory of jade and azure crystals (Minnesang of Love and Lament), said to have been a token from the Lord of Storm to a favoured recipient.

## lore
### LITE
The texts retell the fall of the tyrant of storms in Old Mondstadt and the rise of a wind god, in verse. The bard who gave his song and life, and the freed people who followed, are never named.
### STANDARD
Dawn's Brilliant Oath tells of an iron-clad guard who laid down his sword at the song of a young bard and joined the rebels. A Note in Spring's Leich tells of the bard's last song before the crumbling throne, the song that became Mondstadt, and of the wind spirit who kept his name and a strand of time. Heldenepos's Unspoken Tale tells of the archer's last arrow and of a final storm of the dying tyrant that the bard silenced. Minnesang of Love and Lament tells of the huntress who loved the tyrant and was never truly seen by him.
### LARGE +
The Windborne Flower's Spruchdichtung is an oath: a speaker for the Gunnhildr clan head pledges allegiance to the newborn god, promises to guard Mondstadt's song while the winds are gentle, and swears to turn against the divine throne again if a tyrant torments mortals. The clan contrasts its gift (blossoms and sincerity) with the gifts of the Gotini (golden songbirds), Hlaewringi (deeds of war), Bructeri (sacred halls) and Sitones (verse). The oath ends by crowning the new god with 'the thousand winds' in the name of the descendants of Great Arcadia.

Dawn's Brilliant Oath praises the 'God King's' most trusted confidant, a taciturn huntress or archer, who left her master's side and gathered the scattered winds for the bard. A Note in Spring's Leich says the bard played strings that once belonged to the Master of Time, that even the Mother of the Thousand Winds was stunned, and that his body could not bear a nation's song; only a tiny wind spirit claimed his name and a strand of time on the day the Mother of Time and Wind bestowed her authority. Heldenepos's Unspoken Tale says the archer died shooting the last arrow through the tyrant's wind wall, that the God King's dying malice raised one last storm over the weakened rebels, and that a frail boy with a lyre walked into it and silenced the wind, an event later devotees called a miracle. Minnesang of Love and Lament is the huntress's side: offered by her bloodline as a sacrifice to the king of the spire, raised by him to favoured counsel for her skill with the bow, she gave him victories, dungeons and ruined rebel villages in devotion, realised he never loved her as he loved to speak of, and wished only that his gaze would linger on her. The text ends with the line that, even as they tore each other apart, neither ever knew the other's heart.
### ULTRA +
These texts match the known tale from the Nameless Bard and Decarabian's fall: the wind spirit, the boy bard (the original Venti), Amos, Gunnhildr and the red-haired warrior against Decarabian; the bard's song, 'Mondstadt', flung the tower through time; the wind spirit then ascended as Barbatos. The texts add emotional detail but remain poetic and unreliable on specifics. The text does not say that the set's iron-clad guard is the red-haired warrior; the wiki infers it. Treat the identification of any figure as inference.

### LARGE
The tale concerns events about 2,600 years ago. Its framing matches the wiki-attested history of Old Mondstadt; its additions (an oath by the Gunnhildr clan head, the archer's last arrow) are only in this set.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Use it as a poetic retelling, not a plain chronicle. A bard or Mondstadt scholar may know pieces of it. Nobody should name the unnamed bard as 'Venti' unless they know the Archon's history.

## gameplay
### LARGE
Gameplay (game mechanics, not in-world fact). A 4- and 5-star set, dropped in Moonchild's Treasures (Nod-Krai). 2-piece: ATK +18%. 4-piece: after a Normal Attack, Charged Attack, Elemental Skill or Burst hits, gain Blessing of Pastoral Winds (ATK +25% for 6s); a wearer who has completed Witch's Homework instead gains Resolve of Pastoral Winds, with an extra 20% CRIT Rate. It can trigger off-field.
