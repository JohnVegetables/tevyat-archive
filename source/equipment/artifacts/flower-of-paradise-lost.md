## summary
### LITE
Flower of Paradise Lost is a Sumeru desert artifact set (Version 3.3, City of Gold domain) about the Lord of Flowers, also known as Nabu Malikata, goddess of the oasis and mother of the Jinn. Its texts tell how she was cast aside by the heavens, how the Jinn were born from her wanderings, and how she bent to her friend King Deshret's ambition at a heavy price.
### STANDARD +
The set's mood is elegy: gardens, wine, moonlight, and a friendship that ends in the loss of a god.

## lore
### LITE
Long ago the heavens cast out the Lord of Flowers. She wandered the barren desert for seventy-two nights; her wounds became springs, the springs became gardens, and night-blue water lilies sprouted. The Jinn were born from those lilies. The first Jinn begged her to stay and she remained, and purple moon-like flowers named Padisarah bloomed wherever she walked.
### STANDARD +
Later King Deshret, the red-crowned king, let two others share his throne, and the three became friends. Deshret rejected the gift the divine throne offered and chose rebellion, knowing it would cost him those closest to him. She saw only ruin ahead for him but admired the rebellion. One night, over pomegranate wine, he told her his deepest wishes; the words are lost. She warned him to remember how the heavens punished the fallen envoys, then used her body as a conduit and gave up the oasis so he could see his desires made real. Sandstorms swept the paradise, King Deshret returned from them, and she was never seen again.
### LARGE +
Wilting Feast frames Deshret's plan as a "noble lie" that shepherded his followers toward oblivion, gathering the wisdom of thousands and binding their dreams to power. The poem ends on a question put to the king: have the so-called gods not been superfluous to you from the start? A Moment Congealed says that after the Lord of the Desert built a cemetery for his beloved, an Eternal Oasis became a legend among wandering tribes: a place of ever-green gardens ruled by a sleeping goddess, guarded by Ferigees, the "last mother of the Jinn". The Amethyst Crown tells the origin of her warning: invaders from beyond the firmament came, and the master of the heavens sent divine nails to mend the land and punish her kin; she was exiled, cut off from heaven and its enlightenment.
### ULTRA +
The recurring theme is that eternity is a lie and flowers must wither to bloom again. The Secret-Keeper's Magic Bottle says the Lord of Flowers drew enlightenment from the conversation: she had long foreseen it, and the desert and oasis, though sovereigns, held the most rebellious thoughts. In the Sands piece she tells the king that an hourglass whose grains fuse into one lump has no meaning, so freezing time is not a paradise. The Amethyst Crown ends with King Deshret wiping away her tears under the moon and telling her his own desires. All of this is the Jinn's account, not an objective record.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): a 4- and 5-star set from the City of Gold domain and the Flower of Paradise Lost strongbox. 2-piece: Elemental Mastery +80. 4-piece: raises Bloom, Hyperbloom and Burgeon reaction DMG by 40%, and Lunar-Bloom DMG by 10%; triggering one grants up to four 10s stacks of a further 25% of that bonus. Lunar-Bloom was added to the effect in Version 6.0 (Luna I).

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): do not have a mortal claim to know what King Deshret said that night; the set says nobody knows.

## entry: Ay-Khanoum's Myriad
@keys: Ay-Khanoum's Myriad
### STANDARD
The flower piece of Flower of Paradise Lost: an amethyst bloom sculpted in the image of an extinct flower. Its poem is the origin of the Jinn: the Lord of Flowers, cast aside by the heavens, wanders seventy-two nights across the wastes; water lilies grow from her wound-fed springs; the first Jinn, born from dreams and loss, beg her to stay as their creator and mistress of the garden.
### LARGE +
The infant Jinn crown her with wild chrysanthemums and call her "dream-mother, mistress of forgetting and wine". Her husk had been left savaged and her kinsfolk stripped of their minds. The flowers that bloomed where she walked were named Padisarah.

## entry: Wilting Feast
@keys: Wilting Feast
### STANDARD
The plume piece: a feather of a long-extinct bird, inlaid by ancient adherents of the Goddess of Flowers. Its poem makes the Mistress of Oases choose to help King Deshret defy the "absurd shackles" binding the world, even though she saw only ruin for his path. It ends on the question of whether gods were ever needed.
### LARGE +
The king is called red-crowned, refusing the divine throne's gift. She says humanity will become "king of kings" over the tomb of divinity and sees the plot as a secret rebellion run on one stubborn will. The Lord of Flowers never knew a love as sweet as wine, nor the smallness of human feelings.

## entry: A Moment Congealed
@keys: A Moment Congealed
### STANDARD
The sands piece: an hourglass whose sand will not flow. Its poem tells of the Eternal Oasis built as a cemetery for the Lord of the Desert's beloved. The legend spreads among desert tribes of a green place ruled by a sleeping goddess, guarded by Ferigees. Tribal matriarchs call themselves Daughters of the Flower Goddess.
### LARGE +
The hourglass lines argue against stopping time: crystal sands that fuse into one lump lose their meaning, and those who bloom and die like flowers need not fear death. The poem names the Tanit, Uzza and Shimti tribes and ends on the search for the "immortal lie" of the Eternal Oasis.

## entry: Secret-Keeper's Magic Bottle
@keys: Secret-Keeper's Magic Bottle
### STANDARD
The goblet piece: a small purple crystal bottle with an emerald cap. It tells of the night King Deshret poured out his ambitions before the Lord of Flowers over pomegranate wine, and she gave in. Her words promise a bridge to higher knowledge, a warning to remember the punishment of the fallen envoys, and a hope that will be found in ordinary mortals. Sandstorms then swallowed the paradise; she never returned.
### LARGE +
The text insists the content of the night is unknown. She guided her dearest friend toward understanding "the skies and the abyss", using her body as a conduit and the oasis as payment.

## entry: Amethyst Crown
@keys: Amethyst Crown
### STANDARD
The circlet piece: a crown inlaid with amethyst and emerald, seemingly worn by ancient priests of the Goddess of Flowers. It records the three friends sharing a throne of emeralds and rubies, the Lord of Flowers' confession of exile and her warning not to seek the Master of the Four Shades or the mysteries of the sky and abyss, and Deshret's silent vow to transgress.
### LARGE +
She describes a time when divine envoys spoke openly with people, invaders came from beyond the firmament, and the master of the heavens sent divine nails that laid waste to mortals. The crown is worn in honour of the eternal oasis and the Padisarahs.
