## summary
@spoiler: major
### LITE
Night of the Sky's Unveiling is a 4- and 5-star artifact set added in version 6.0 that strengthens Lunar reactions. Its item text tells of Rauthvangi, a young stargazer of subterranean Khaenri'ah who glimpsed a "pitch-black Elysium" beyond the sky. For this he was exiled, survived the surface, wandered the nations, and as Rotwang, the Tsar's "mad monk", travelled to Nod-Krai to claim a hidden treasure and break out of the celestial shell.
### STANDARD +
The set links Khaenri'ah's astrology, the forbidden knowledge of the great sin, the court of the Snowland fae and the Cataclysm-era Nod-Krai. It never states his later fate; that comes from Nod-Krai quests.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Item text only names the pieces: a flower, a feather, a bell or clock-like piece, a vessel and a crown. It gives no physical description of the set.

## lore
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Rauthvangi, a novice monk in subterranean Khaenri'ah, glimpses a forbidden "pitch-black Elysium" in a star mirror, is exiled from the Universitas Magistrorum and survives the surface unharmed. He later wanders the nations, becomes the Tsar's favourite as Professor Rotwang, the "mad monk" of Zapolyarny Palace, and goes to Nod-Krai as envoy to seize a treasure and break through the celestial shell.
### LARGE +
Item text, summarised in story order:
- Bloom of the Mind's Desire: before the Five Sinners committed their offence, the people of subterranean Khaenri'ah had never seen the sea or the real sun, yet some astrologers recorded odd star-chart phenomena as "Signs of Truth" and decided that piercing the mirror's illusions leads to truth. Long after the earthly gods fell in the Archon War and the atrous sun replaced the crimson moon, the study of stars was banned because some had uncovered the secret of the great sin; only a few chosen servants of the one-eyed ruler were exempt. A novice monk named Rauthvangi cleaned the dust from the "mirror of Apaosha" in a stargazing chamber and saw the light ringing the horizon, a beautiful and profane "pitch-black Elysium", which consumed his every thought.
- Feather of Indelible Sin: Rauthvangi never defended himself, and the court expelled him from the Universitas Magistrorum and sent him into exile. For one of outland stock, going above ground was a curse that would catch him quickly, yet he stepped into the sunlight unharmed while those beside him screamed. He took this as the cosmos choosing him and said that flame illuminates the world but also reduces it to ash, and that he was like a moth leaping into it.
- Revelation's Toll: in the golden years before the Cataclysm, the scholar read every chronicle and library of the north without finding the Elysium. He had visited every corner of the known world except the barren borderland of Nod-Krai, which the Tsar of Snezhnaya had only recently ordered explored. He went there as a last attempt, saw the truth again, and uncovered a sinful path to the Elysium. Only the gate remained to be opened by his own hands at any price.
- Vessel of Plenty: at Zapolyarny Palace the banquets never stopped, and the fae nobles called Professor Rotwang their "mad monk". He was the Tsar's favourite for alchemy and astrology. Two rumours circulated: a grand duke who tried to poison him ended up kneeling as his follower, and a fae noble who called his research a hoax saw him summon the spirit of his late wife. He never confirmed the stories, and the nobles trusted an outlander with no local ties. The text ends hinting that none of them foresaw the tragic ending.
- Crown of the Befallen: as cities and factories rose in Nod-Krai, only the Tsar's favourite knew the Tsar's ruler of the fae was planning a revolt the sublunary order would not tolerate. The mad monk, as envoy, said the Tsar thought too small and had never looked at the stars. He travelled to Nod-Krai on a secret mission for a treasure of world-turning power, wanting it for his own ends, and called on "Reed Miller" to follow him out of the celestial shell.
### ULTRA +
Cross-references the wiki draws on: the "Rauthvangi" of the first pieces and "Professor Rotwang" of Vessel of Plenty are the same man, a Khaenri'ahn exile who joined the Belyi Tsar's court; the wiki names the treasure as the Sampo Mill and the Court of Eternal Bliss as the fae ruler's "bold scheme". The text's "sunless" Khaenri'ah, the banned astrology and the "veil of the celestial shell" tie it to the False Sky and to the rest of the Khaenri'ah setting without stating how.

Details worth noting: the text calls his exile worse than death for people of his stock, describes the expulsion as evidence of the kingdom's decline, and uses his own words in each piece to show a growing fanaticism that treats other mortals as fleeting. The narrator is sympathetic to his curiosity but never calls his aims good.

Time frame: the first two pieces are set in the Eclipse-era Khaenri'ah under the one-eyed ruler (the wiki identifies him as Irmin). The last three are set in the years before and at the start of the Cataclysm in Snezhnaya and Nod-Krai. The "Elysium beyond the sky's veil" is tied to the Abyss only by the wiki and by Nod-Krai quest text, not by the artifact text itself.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner is named. The text follows Rauthvangi (Rotwang); none of the pieces says who owned the objects.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### LITE
Game mechanics: a 2-piece Elemental Mastery bonus and a 4-piece bonus that rewards Lunar reactions with CRIT Rate and shared Lunar reaction damage.
### STANDARD
Game mechanics (not lore): 4- and 5-star; domain Frostladen Machinery in Nod-Krai (6.0) and an Artifact Strongbox. 2-piece: Elemental Mastery +80. 4-piece: when nearby party members trigger Lunar reactions, if the wearer is on the field they gain Gleaming Moon: Intent for 4s (CRIT Rate +15% with Nascent Gleam, +30% with Ascendant Gleam). Party Lunar reaction DMG is increased by 10% for each different Gleaming Moon effect among party members, and these effects do not stack. Per the wiki, Silken Moon's Serenade's 4-piece and this one together give a total of 20%, and extra copies of either do not add more.
