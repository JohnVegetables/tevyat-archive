## summary
### LITE
Thundering Fury is an Electro artifact set (4 and 5 star). Its five pieces tell, in fragments, of an ancient tribe that worshipped the Thunderbird, a violet-lightning bird. A young lad who sang to the bird became a blood sacrifice, and the enraged Thunderbird destroyed the tribe. The set is earned in the Midsummer Courtyard domain.
### STANDARD +
The set's pieces are Thunderbird's Mercy (flower), Survivor of Catastrophe (plume), Hourglass of Thunder (sands), Omen of Thunderstorm (goblet) and Thunder Summoner's Crown (circlet). The tribe is the one now called the Thunderbird Civilization, of Tsurumi Island in Inazuma.

## appearance
### LARGE
- Thunderbird's Mercy: a purple wild flower charged with lightning, said to have survived the ash.
- Survivor of Catastrophe: a lightning-charged feather the Thunderbird lost, still flickering.
- Hourglass of Thunder: a costly hourglass of amethyst and amber gold that belonged to a shaman.
- Omen of Thunderstorm: a golden ritual goblet that held the blood offered to the bird.
- Thunder Summoner's Crown: a crown worn by the tribe's elder shamans.

## lore
### LITE
The tribe feared the Thunderbird and thanked it for its blessings, and shamans offered blood to it for protection and forgiveness. The texts insist the bird was a monster that did not care for human worship, and the tribe took its unpredictable moves for divine revelations.
### STANDARD +
One stormy season a young lad sang fearlessly in the mountains, and the proud bird landed beside him. He said he had been told he could bring peace to thunder and storms, and the bird, moved by his song, said nothing. That was their only meeting. The next time the bird saw him was as blood in the golden goblet on the sacrificial altar. At the tribe's last festival, where the hourglass counted down to the bird's arrival, the frenzied Thunderbird blew away the altar and destroyed the tribe in vengeance for the singer. The text adds that the lad himself saw his death as an offering for the tribe's good.
### LARGE +
The Survivor of Catastrophe text records that the lad kept a feather the bird dropped and promised to sing another tune when it came again with thunder and rain. The Thunderbird, driven mad by the broken promise, left the mountain in cinders. Years later it was hunted down and killed as a monster, the land slowly recovered, and the feather lay buried in the growth; the story of the bird and the lad nearly perished with the tribe. The lone flower is described as the sole survivor of the day of disaster.
### ULTRA +
The quest 'Through the Mists' (Tsurumi Island, 2.2) retells the same events through the ghost Ruu, who is identified as the sacrificed boy; there the bird is named Kanna Kapatcir, the name Ruu gave her. The wiki attributes her killing to Ei and says she was sealed away on Seirai Island. Those details come from the quests and other pages, not from the set's texts, which never name the bird, the boy, the island or the killer.

## owners
### LARGE
No named owner. The texts attribute the hourglass and crown to the tribe's shamans, and the goblet to the sacrificial rites; they belonged to the Thunderbird Civilization, not to a living character.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not literal lore): a 2-piece bonus of +15% Electro DMG. The 4-piece bonus boosts Overloaded, Electro-Charged, Superconduct and Hyperbloom damage by 40% and shortens the Elemental Skill cooldown by 1s when the wearer triggers Quicken or those reactions (once per 0.8s); the set also boosts Aggravate, Lunar-Charged and Stellar-Conduct by 20%. The set drops in the Midsummer Courtyard domain.
### LARGE +
The wearer must apply their element second to trigger the cooldown reduction, and it does not work for an off-field wearer. The damage bonus still applies off-field.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): use the set as a grim, quiet relic. Show purple lightning clinging to the pieces, and treat it as a remembered tragedy rather than a trophy. Do not give it to a canon character or make it grant powers.

Thundering Fury texts describe a tribe on Tsurumi Island that worshipped the Thunderbird and offered blood to it. A singing lad was sacrificed, and the Thunderbird annihilated the tribe in anger. The bird was hunted down and killed years later.
