## summary
### LITE
Gilded Dreams is a Sumeru desert artifact set (Version 3.0, Spire of Solitary Enlightenment domain). Its five pieces remember a lost golden age: a desert paradise ruled by a "God King" who judged every heart, three inseparable friends, and a law that hardened into chains. The set never names the God King; other lore points to King Deshret.
### STANDARD +
Its images are gilded: dark gold flowers that never open, molten metal weighing hearts, sundials, wine cups and sand-king crowns. The tone is mournful rather than triumphant.

## lore
### LITE
Ancient legends tell of three inseparable friends: one withered like a rose in the mud, one made a great hidden oasis, one used all their intellect to build an eternal illusion in the sand. They meant for no one to suffer partings or bitter water, and promised a "golden slumber".
### STANDARD +
Long ago the heavens fell silent and the land lost its master. A God King then judged all life by law: a feather weighed hearts and molten metal measured wisdom. Tribes who lived by blood-law and the memory of famine imitated his thrones and walls. The priests ruled the oasis cities justly in his name, but later decrees were issued from "illusory madness", and the citizens' fates were chained to their God King's choice. A banquet of the three God Kings, with the moon, sun and green fields as their symbols, ended when time broke the oath between day and night.
### LARGE +
The poem moves between ideals and failure. The Dreaming Steelbloom says obsessive remembrance rises "like a smokeless flame" and that those who look back with one eye and forward into dreams with the other get lost. Feather of Judgment says the law, once twisted by sorrow, made nobles abettors and let palace foundations sink into quicksand. The Sunken Years says that when time swept across the land the God King rose casting a long shadow, and the tribes imitated him and recalled days when kings spoke with the heavens. Honeyed Final Feast says the three God Kings were oathbound friends, one holding the banquet, one authority, one life, but dreams are "prey for reason", thrown into dead machinery from which "a new intelligence shall molt". Shadow of the Sand King records the pillar from the heavens that buried trees and meadows under sand.
### ULTRA +
A reading, not stated outright: three images recur: the dream (golden slumber), the law (the scales of feather and metal) and the machine (calculation unified into one mind). Each is presented as a good idea that reversed itself: the dream paralysed, the law entrapped, the machine promised humanity godhood at the price of its individual minds ("meld all thoughts into one"). Treat the set as the desert's own memory of Deshret's age, not a neutral history. Several lines are presented as quotations or prophecy: the circlet's "ruler shall come with light that blazes like the sun" to take the thorn crown, and the sands piece's vow to "stop the sands of time with divine power".

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): a set from the Spire of Solitary Enlightenment domain (4 and 5 star) and its strongbox. 2-piece: Elemental Mastery +80. 4-piece: within 8s of triggering an Elemental Reaction, ATK rises 14% for each party member of the wearer's element and Elemental Mastery rises 50 for each of a different element (3 members counted each), once per 8s, even off-field; the triggering reaction itself is not buffed. Released in 3.0; no later change is listed.

## entry: Dreaming Steelbloom
@keys: Dreaming Steelbloom
### STANDARD
The flower piece of Gilded Dreams: a bud of hammered dark gold with a crimson core and petals that will never open. Its verses tell of three inseparable friends, one who withers in mud, one who builds a hidden oasis, one who builds an eternal illusion, and a promise that in the golden slumber no one need drink bitter water.
### LARGE +
It says the "kingdom of flowers" would be ground by sandstorms into a tale in a song. The closing lines turn to a listener who has turned his gaze to "the wisdom of the depths" and inclined his ear to "honeyed whispers".

## entry: Feather of Judgment
@keys: Feather of Judgment
### STANDARD
The plume piece: a feather once used to weigh the hearts of the guilty, its original function lost. The verses describe the past of "civilization and ease" abandoned, then the new law: a feather to weigh hearts, molten metal to measure divine wisdom, the God King's blood-rooted law carved into the desert paradise, and sorrow twisting governance into shackles.
### LARGE +
Dignitaries became abettors while palace foundations sank into quicksand, and "all betrayals must be mercilessly judged" with utter devastation. Citizens' fates were chained to the God King's choice.

## entry: The Sunken Years
@keys: The Sunken Years
### STANDARD
The sands piece: a dark-golden sundial that tells the desert's story. In the beginning tribes lived with the sands, bound by blood-law and the fear of famine, until a God King rose; they copied his walls and thrones into feudal kingdoms, and wise kings received oracles from the heavens.
### LARGE +
Its closing prophecy says the king will, in wisdom, revive the golden past and stop the sands of time with undiminished divine power, so the age of the "king of the ocean of sand" will return and the "golden slumber" will summon wandering sand.

## entry: Honeyed Final Feast
@keys: Honeyed Final Feast
### STANDARD
The goblet piece: a wine cup from a grand ancient feast that has lost its luster. It recalls the three God Kings as oathbound friends, one for the banquet, one for authority, one for life, whose happiness ended when time broke the contract between day and night. The final lines hint at dreams ground in lifeless machinery.
### LARGE +
The moonlight sank into quicksand and the sun covered everything in its fearsome gaze. The final dirge is for "the most lonesome king of kings", for whom the gilded sands long divined failure. The text quotes a plan to meld all thoughts into one and let humanity become the lord of lords and god of gods.

## entry: Shadow of the Sand King
@keys: Shadow of the Sand King
### STANDARD
The circlet piece: a gold-inlaid headband once worn by desert priests, modelled on one worn by a legendary desert ruler. Its verses describe a divine pillar from the sky burying forest and meadow, sacred cities on fertile oases, priests ruling justly under the God King, and later absurd decisions that led the people toward a bitter end.
### LARGE +
The text says kings and clergy once received divine oracles themselves and were masters of the land, while those who rule as proxies today are the shadows of the gods. It also promises that a ruler will come with blazing light and take from the people the "crown woven from rose thorns".
