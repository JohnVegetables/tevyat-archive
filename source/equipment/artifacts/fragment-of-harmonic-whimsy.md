## summary
### LITE
Fragment of Harmonic Whimsy is a 4- and 5-star artifact set from Fontaine, added in Version 4.6. Its five pieces are written as one story: Remus, a god who lost his homeland, becomes the God King of Remuria, is told that his glorious empire will drown, and chooses to fight fate with a "Grand Symphony." The set is a Remurian legend, not a family heirloom.
### STANDARD +
The bonuses reward the Bond of Life mechanic (see gameplay). The five pieces are Harmonious Symphony Prelude (flower), Ancient Sea's Nocturnal Musing (plume), The Grand Jape of the Turning of Fate (sands), Ichor Shower Rhapsody (goblet) and Whimsical Dance of the Withered (circlet).

## appearance
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
The flower is described as an honorary medal in the shape of a blooming flower, adorned with blue stone and gold, once bestowed upon immortals. The other pieces are known only by their names and lore text.

## lore
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The set tells a Remurian legend in order. A homeless god from the desert, later called Remus, meets a prophet, becomes king over the High Sea, hears that his realm will fall, and gambles on a symphony to break fate.
### STANDARD
The five pieces run in order: a homeless desert god meets the prophet Sybilla and is told he will rule the High Sea; he builds a glorious kingdom yet grieves at its foretold fall; he confronts a vishap prince and the imprisoned Mistress of Many Waters and finds no salvation; as Sebastos he resolves to compose his own symphony against fate; and after the realm sinks, a new nation of Many Waters rises.
### LARGE +
Piece by piece, the text runs as follows.
- Harmonious Symphony Prelude: in an age when the High Sea's people were still unenlightened, a god who had lost his homeland between the scarlet sands and the murky mountains was once an oasis singer. His home fell to the Lord of the Blazing Sun, and he refused to serve the Sand King's throne, so he wandered. At the sea he stumbled into a ruined city older than the land, found a silver tree guarded by golden bees, and was told by the prophet Sybilla, once an envoy protecting the tree, that he would rule the whole High Sea, bring it civilization and justice, and be undone by that same justice. He asked whether fate left room for choice. This was the first meeting of the future king and Sybilla.
- Ancient Sea's Nocturnal Musing: the king, now glorious, built a great tower in Meropis, united scattered peoples and brought peace, but grew mournful as the prophesied century of glory wore on. His singer-advisor answered that empires rise and fall by the law of Fortuna. The king could not accept this and was told that the Mistress of Many Waters lay imprisoned in the lightless depths and might have answers.
- The Grand Jape of the Turning of Fate: before the Grand Symphony was played, the king went to find the sourcewater, fought a vishap prince who guarded the Mistress's prison for thirty days and nights, and was taken to see her. He learned terrible secrets but no salvation, since her sin and the resulting curse could not be undone. He took a cup of the purest water, vowing to seal his subjects' souls in an Ichor insoluble in water and to forge bodies of bronze and stone against time.
- Ichor Shower Rhapsody: the king, now the Sebastos, ruled from a golden capital with the Legion Athanatoi. Told his empire would fall, he searched the Musica Mundana of Fortuna for a flaw and resolved to compose his own symphony, asking Sybilla to help. His wildest hope was to put fate's baton into humanity's hands. Sybilla, in whose eyes there was already no future, agreed without hesitation.
- Whimsical Dance of the Withered: after the Domus Aurea was rubble and the judgment day came, waves sank the realm and a gray steed carried away surviving life. A new nation of Many Waters rose, and the golden authority became legend. The piece closes on the idea that dreams born of the ruins will one day merge into one.
Cross-check with wider canon (not stated in this set): Remus was a god from Sumeru whose land was conquered by King Deshret. He founded Remuria above Fontaine's waters, sharing rule with four human Harmosts. Remuria ultimately fell because of the Grand Symphony, and its end led to Egeria's return, which is why the set's dream-like tone is at odds with the empire's real fate.
### ULTRA +
Reading the pieces together, the king is never shown as a simple tyrant. Each piece frames his drive as grief at a foretold end, as the pieces describe. The set also shows how the empire's ideas of "justice and salvation", Ichor and bronze bodies were present before the catastrophe; for the Harmosts' side of the story see the Golden Troupe set.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): 2-piece bonus is ATK +18%. The 4-piece bonus triggers whenever the wearer's Bond of Life value increases or decreases, giving 18% increased DMG for 6s, stacking up to 3 times. It is obtained from the Faded Theater domain (located in Fontaine's Nostoi Region at Petrichor) and from the matching artifact strongbox.
### LARGE +
Faded Theater's Ley Line Disorder also rewards Bond of Life changes with an All Elemental and Physical DMG Bonus. The domain's text describes an ancient troupe performing the elegies of an ancient civilization under an immortal musician, and monsters of unknown provenance now holding the stage.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): treat the set as a theatrical, mournful retelling that characters in Fontaine may know as an old opera or legend. Do not present its words as proven history; Remuria's fall is better documented in books and quests than in these verses.
