## summary
### LITE
Echoes of an Offering is a 4- and 5-star artifact set that rewards Normal Attacks. Its five pieces (Soulscent Bloom, Jade Leaf, Symbol of Felicitation, Chalice of the Font, Flowing Rings) carry item text made of Chenyu Vale legends: tea served when the spiritscent flowers bloom at Qiaoying Village, the first tea tree, a jade split into hidden pieces, and the friendship of the vale's adepti.
### STANDARD +
Added in version 2.6. The pieces are told as tales and rumours, and mostly name no one; the wiki ties the unnamed figures to the Herblord and the carp adeptus Fujin (see uncertainties).

## appearance
### STANDARD
Jade ornaments with a water-and-tea theme: a jade carved as a flower, a leaf-shaped jade pendant, a circular jade loop that sounds like running water, a small teacup that holds a spring and the reflected sun and moon, and a pair of jade earrings.

## lore
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
The pieces tell Chenyu Vale legends about its adepti, its tea and a sacred jade that was broken up and hidden.
### STANDARD
The pieces recount Qiaoying Village's tea ceremony, the first tea tree planted in Chenyu Vale, a rain-bringing jade split into hidden pieces, and a pair of adepti friends, one bound to water and one to the mountain.
### LARGE +
Soulscent Bloom: Qiaoying Village holds a tea-serving ceremony each year when the Spiritscent Flowers bloom, and remembers a vanished adeptus nicknamed the Herblord, of whom there are many foggy legends. Jade Leaf: long ago, before the ford existed, someone planted the first tea tree on the Herblord's mountain, promising friends tea once it grew; the tree's branches were later grafted across the river by mountain people, and tea spread from Chenyu Vale to Liyue Harbor. Symbol of Felicitation: a jade ornament said to come from the long-sealed Mt. Laixin; rumours say the mountain's master split a rain-bringing jade into pieces and hid them from demons, and the ornament sounds like flowing water. Chalice of the Font: a gift cup with a tiny spring-fed realm inside, which a water-bound adeptus took on a journey to Liyue Harbor, and whose shape later became the common teacup. Flowing Rings: variants of the legend of a precious gem lost to the waters, ending with koi leaving the water to fly on the wind.
The pieces share a frame: a pair of adepti who were close friends, one tied to the water, one tied to the mountain. Soulscent Bloom ends with a moment in which a young lady scrambles ashore, jokes angrily with the one who made her swim, and the glittering figure sinks never to return. Jade Leaf has the mountain's master returning later in a changed form and no longer able to untie the pendant, and says the vale's tea trees thrive only in Chenyu Vale because they remember a promise made beside the sapling. Symbol of Felicitation has a priest showing the jade to a friend every year before a departure, while the friend, busy grinding herbs, does not listen; artisans of Yilong Wharf still make such loops, and merchants hold them to their ears to hear rain on stone. Chalice of the Font describes a water adeptus who compared herself to the yaksha and judged herself luckier, whose inherited rites kept her from living long on land, and who imagined Liyue Harbor as a distant dream.
### ULTRA +
In the item text the figures are anonymous, and the wiki marks them as Fujin (the carp planter and cup-owner) and the Herblord (the mountain's master). The set's own treatment is selective: Flowing Rings explicitly offers several 'tributaries' of one legend and lets the reader choose. Jade Leaf's promise to invite Cloud Retainer and Mountain Shaper to the tea, and Chalice of the Font's reference to the Guili Assembly and the harbour city, place the story in the age before Liyue Harbor, which fits the text's own phrase that the sweet waters did not flow across Liyue in such abundance then. The Chalice names no cause for the fate of the yaksha it mentions; it only contrasts that with the speaker's own lot.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner is named. The pieces speak of a young lady master of a mountain, a priest, and a friend who carries a teacup; the wiki ties these to the Herblord and Fujin.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### LITE
Game mechanics: the 2-piece bonus raises ATK; the 4-piece bonus can add a bonus Normal Attack damage hit called Valley Rite.
### STANDARD
Game mechanics (not lore): obtained from The Lost Valley domain and the Artifact Strongbox. 2-piece: ATK +18%. 4-piece: Normal Attack hits have a 36% chance to trigger Valley Rite, increasing Normal Attack DMG by 70% of ATK until the next Normal Attack deals damage; each failure raises the next chance by 20%; triggers at most once per 0.2s.

## entry: The first tea tree of Chenyu Vale
@keys: first tea tree, Chenyu tea tree
@keys2: Chenyu Vale
@version: 2.6
### STANDARD
In Jade Leaf, the first tea tree was planted on the Herblord's hillside by someone who promised to brew tea for friends, including Cloud Retainer and Mountain Shaper, once it grew. A jade pendant was tied to its branches. Mortals later grafted its branches across the river, and tea spread from Chenyu Vale to Liyue Harbor. The text says the trees thrive only in Chenyu Vale because they remember an old promise.

## entry: The hidden jade of Mt. Laixin
@keys: Mt. Laixin jade, hidden jade, rain-bringing jade
@keys2: Laixin
@version: 2.6
### STANDARD
Symbol of Felicitation and Flowing Rings preserve rumours that Mt. Laixin held a jade able to bring sweet rain; the mountain's master split it into pieces and hid them underwater, in hills and in shrines so that demons could not use its power. Variants say it was carved by Rex Lapis, or was merely a lesser jade or a cup, or that the 'jade' is a figure of speech for a beautiful person. Treat all as legend.
