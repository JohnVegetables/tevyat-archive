## summary
### LITE
Desert Pavilion Chronicle is a Sumeru desert artifact set (Version 3.3, City of Gold domain). Its five item texts form one poem, recited by an old blind poet, about Gurabad: the shepherd Ormazd made vassal king with the Jinni Liloupar's help, his slide into tyranny, Liloupar's revenge, the city's fall, and the desert splintering into seven states.
### STANDARD +
Each piece addresses a different listener. The recurring warning is that love and power left unchecked destroy the city they build.

## lore
### LITE
Legend says the Lord of the Sands, after his lover's death, made a pact with humanity through the Jinn: only incorruptible mortals could serve as vassal-kings. The Jinni Liloupar chose the shepherd Ormazd, warned him that her blessing carried "sharp retribution", and watched him ignore it. He united the clans, was crowned, and made Gurabad the mortals' capital.
### STANDARD +
Ormazd then took slaves, tribute and sacrifice and treated her advice as pillow talk, so Liloupar took off the earrings he had given her and swore three generations of ruin on his line. Gurabad fell to a "great plague" of a mob under a brass-masked leader, and her soul was split. The survivors formed seven desert states, with the "city of sapphires", Tulaytullah, among the greatest. Two other pieces step outside Gurabad: Jinn fragments trapped in machines cry out against a "mother who never bore" them, and the Jinni Ferigees seals herself into a crystalline cup to restrain the desert's wrath and protect mortal cities.
### LARGE +
The poet tells the story to Xiphos, who is setting out for Tulaytullah. The Plume lists later tales without names: a general led by a slave, a king killed over a hunting falcon, a dancer sworn to a prince, a lost prince remembering his burning palace. The wiki ties the poet to Xiphos's blind teacher; the set never says so. The poem presents Gurabad as a human state under divine patronage and records its end in the language of epidemic and mob rule, not conquest. Other lore (Liloupar's own account and The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin) fills in the scheme the poem only hints at. The Goblet's Ferigees chooses her dead mistress and the mortals over King Deshret's order and accepts that bonds are temporary.
### ULTRA +
The "city of sapphires" and the lost prince place the end of the golden age shortly before Tulaytullah's era. The Plume's list of nobles enslaved, princes cast down by slave-soldiers and dancers seizing power summarises a long period of instability, not one event, and is best read as the poet's moral rather than a ledger. The closing lines have the poet tell Xiphos that hatred burns like wildfire and leaves ash, but uncontrolled love is wilder still: the poem's thesis.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): a 4- and 5-star set from the City of Gold domain and the Desert Pavilion Chronicle strongbox. 2-piece: Anemo DMG Bonus +15%. 4-piece: when Charged Attacks hit, Normal Attack SPD rises 10% and Normal, Charged and Plunging Attack DMG rise 40% for 15s. Released in 3.3; the wiki lists no later change.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): treat the set as a moral tale told by an elder. A character may quote its warnings but should not claim to know who the unnamed figures were. The Gurabad stories are specialist knowledge, not common in modern Sumeru.

## entry: The First Days of the City of Kings
@keys: The First Days of the City of Kings
### STANDARD
The flower piece of Desert Pavilion Chronicle: an artificial bloom that shimmers and seems to laugh when held to the ear. Its poem tells of the Lord of the Sands' pact with mortals through the Jinn, of Ormazd and the lily-born Liloupar, and of Gurabad blooming "like a man-made flower in a cliff wall". It ends with Xiphos setting out for the city of sapphires.
### LARGE +
Liloupar warns of a "hundred generations of blessings" paid for with sharp retribution and spilled red wine, because the Jinn's mad love is chained to greed and want. Ormazd, under the moon, did not take heed. With her guidance he led his nomadic clan, beat his divided rivals and was crowned vassal king, proxy of the Lord of the Sands.

## entry: End of the Golden Realm
@keys: End of the Golden Realm
### STANDARD
The plume piece: a clear artificial feather from an ancient human realm, with eagle cries sealed inside. Its poem records Gurabad's fall to a "great plague" and a brass-masked leader, Liloupar's split soul, the seven desert states with Tulaytullah among the greatest, then unnamed tales of a general, a falcon-killed king, a dancer and a lost prince.
### LARGE +
A feather-shaped body of mortal craft held a broken Jinni and flew from Gurabad's cliffs past the desert states until it reached "gilded descendants", its lost memories flaking off like sand. The poet closes by saying one figure lost sight and another lost a throne.

## entry: Timepiece of the Lost Path
@keys: Timepiece of the Lost Path
### STANDARD
The sands piece: an old mechanical clock with Jinni fragments glowing and trembling at its core. Its voices curse a "mother who never bore us" and swear to return to their birth mother, Shirin. The text does not explain who the speakers are.
### LARGE +
The voices describe themselves as born old, with power resting on shattered consciousness, voices replaced by copper tubes, and hatred as fuel. They name no individual speaker.

## entry: Defender of the Enchanting Dream
@keys: Defender of the Enchanting Dream
### STANDARD
The goblet piece: an ancient gold cup that murmurs from its empty inside. Its poem covers the Lord of Flowers' death, her Jinn passing to King Deshret, the Eternal Oasis, and Ferigees binding herself in frost-like chains as a crystalline cup to hold back the desert's wrath and guard the mortal cities.
### LARGE +
Ferigees was given governance of the oasis by King Deshret, spread greenery over the desert to shelter people who had lost their homes, and later disobeyed his charge to make her sacrifice. In her speech she says she is waiting for the King of Sand to fulfil an ancient promise. The wiki places her spirit in a machine on Deshret's Glass Goblet.

## entry: Legacy of the Desert High-Born
@keys: Legacy of the Desert High-Born
### STANDARD
The circlet piece: amber-gold earrings. Its poem shows Ormazd turning a young state into a slave-fed tyranny and his Jinni consort laying aside the earrings he gave her as the sign of her intent to curse his line with three generations of ruin. The wiki notes the earrings are implied to be the ones Ormazd gave Liloupar.
### LARGE +
Ormazd took the Lord of the Desert as patron, built palaces and temples, took slaves from tribes, demanded service from states and sacrifices from cities. The Jinni, once an attendant of the Goddess of Flowers, advised him gently in private and was dismissed as a lover's whispering.
