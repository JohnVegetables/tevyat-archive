## summary
@spoiler: major
### LITE
Silken Moon's Serenade is a 4- and 5-star artifact set tied to Lunar reactions and the Moonsign. Its five pieces carry one narrator's account of the far north: the night Hyperborea's golden realm was destroyed by dark blue crystal nails, the pity of the Frost Moon's mistress, and the later suffering of the Frostmoon Scions, down to the Moonchanter Aila and her sister. It was released in version 6.0.
### STANDARD +
Four pieces open in a time before the new moon (Columbina) rose; only the Flower is set earlier, at the disaster itself. The stories are the Scions' own memory and myth.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Immaculate northern relics: an everlasting flower, a feathered accessory said to have been forged by the first Moonchanter, a long-stopped timepiece, a silver cup from old Frostmoon Scion rituals, and a circlet imitating the antlers of the grove's envoy.

## lore
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The set text has five stories, one per piece.
- Crystal Tear of the Wanderer: in a single night the golden tower fell, and dark blue crystal nails wiped out every city with any sign of life; the apostle and the gods' first messenger (wiki-linked to Anacharsis and Koitar) vanished. The gods stayed silent. Only the mistress of the Frost Moon wept for mortals; legend says she spun silver threads to guide survivors, and her tears became lilies. Hence the descendants of Hyperborea call themselves the Frostmoon Scions.
- The other four pieces follow the Scions' later decline, from the twin sisters Aila and Alia to the Archpriestess Lovia's long project of a "pure" bloodline and a manufactured faith.
### LARGE +
- Pristine Plume of the Blessed: two sisters were born "defective products" and never expected to be blessed. They knew only their grandmother (Lovia, per the wiki link) and a faded storybook showing a white bird under a perfect moon, and promised each other they would one day fly together. Years later one of them (Alia, wiki-linked) broke the promise and left alone, into a darker night; Aila, later the first Moonchanter, endured twice the torment from then on. The last quoted lines have Aila saying she will not resent her older sister.
- Frost Devotee's Delirium: a plan carried through generations, with a bloodline purified for over a thousand years, aimed to give birth to a chosen one who would reign over Elysium. The girl chosen to bear the heir met a young officer from the northern realm, fell in love, cast down her bloodstained antler-crown and betrayed her people. A spy sent by a mad monk captured her to return her to Hiisi Island, and she killed herself with a dagger beside the wounded officer. The Archpriestess Lovia turned to a lesser branch of the bloodline, the twin sisters, a pale shadow of the lost girl. Maddened, she devised a scheme so profane that even the Frost Moon's mistress would recoil. The details are blacked out in the text.
- Joyous Glory of the Pure: after the Scions could no longer hear the heavens' teaching, they could not remake the apostle's marvels (a reliquary that tilled the sea of ice, the arrow that could pierce the false sky, creation's authority from the highest of angels). They turned to ploughshares-into-swords and, finally, to law to govern the generations until blood was "purified into holy seed". The grandmother urges the children to drink "pure moonlight" and embrace joyous pain as the apostle once drank bitter wine; they can no longer tell what fills the silver cup.
- Holy Crown of the Believer: when the heavenly moon had gone, miracles faded to prayer and the line between faith and lies grew thin. Moonchanters today say their antler crown was a gift from the Frost Moon at the moment of its obliteration, woven from moonlight. The young Lovia, then a priestess who had never witnessed a moonlit blessing, forged her circlet from cold iron, hoping that such shared fictions could still bind believers together.
Taken together the set presents the Scions' history as faith sustained through disaster, then through engineered breeding, and finally through a deliberate myth.
### ULTRA +
Details worth keeping apart in a scene: the Flower is the only piece set before the silver mirror shattered, and its tone is mournful rather than accusatory. The other four are set in the era "before the new moon rose" and read as an insider's indictment of Lovia's long project. The text says the apostle's marvels were lost, but not how. The sister who broke the promise is described only as leaving "for a faraway place alone", with no destination given. The text does not say which girl is addressed as "my most cherished child" in the Goblet. The set's tie to Columbina is limited to the phrase "the new moon"; the item text does not itself say she is any of these figures.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner is named. The pieces are relics of the Frostmoon Scions' heritage and are collected by the player as artifacts.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### LITE
Game mechanics: 2-piece gives +20% Energy Recharge; the 4-piece gives party Elemental Mastery when a Moonsign is active and boosts Lunar Reaction DMG.
### STANDARD
Game mechanics (not lore): 4- and 5-star set from the Frostladen Machinery domain (Paha Isle, Nod-Krai). 4-piece: when the wearer deals Elemental DMG they gain Gleaming Moon: Devotion for 8s, giving all party members +60 Elemental Mastery under a Nascent Gleam Moonsign or +120 under an Ascendant Gleam Moonsign (off-field works). All party members' Lunar Reaction DMG rises 10% for each different Gleaming Moon effect present; they do not stack.
### LARGE +
Per the wiki, this 4-piece together with Night of the Sky's Unveiling's 4-piece gives a combined 20% Lunar Reaction DMG increase, and more wearers do not raise it further.

## entry: The mistress of the Frost Moon's mercy
@keys: lilies of the far north, threads of silver glow, mistress of the Frost Moon
@version: 6.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the Crystal Tear of the Wanderer text, when the golden realm fell and the gods stayed silent, one celestial ruler alone shed tears for mortals. She was the mistress of the Frost Moon, queen of the celestial chariot and of light. According to legend she wove threads from the silver glow to lead survivors across the frozen plains, and her tears became everlasting lilies. The text adds that she acted from pity and a deeper, unspoken wish, "whatever her original intent".
