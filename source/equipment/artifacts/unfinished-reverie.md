## summary
### LITE
Unfinished Reverie is a 4- and 5-star artifact set, added in Version 4.6, whose lore belongs to Natlan. Its pieces tell of the scarlet-eyed youth who gathered heroes against an insane king, and of a young girl, twin heroes, a taciturn warrior and a scarred craftsman who helped him. The final piece shows the girl grown old and erasing the craftsman's name. Its bonuses reward fighting Burning enemies with off-field damage.
### STANDARD +
The pieces are Dark Fruit of Bright Flowers (flower), Faded Emerald Tail (plume), Moment of Attainment (sands), The Wine-Flask Over Which the Plan Was Hatched (goblet) and Crownless Crown (circlet). The set is a Natlan story that was released before Natlan itself, in Version 4.6.

## appearance
### LARGE
The flower is grey stone covered in gold foil, said to have marked friend from foe in a war. The plume is a tail-feather ornament whose lustre has faded, its patterns said to be the work of a skilled artisan. The sands is an ancient sundial with tiny marks visible only on close inspection. The goblet is a three-legged cup. The circlet is a golden crown decorated with turquoise and emerald feathers that has never been used in a coronation.

## lore
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The pieces recount an uprising against a mad king of a city of ash, carried out by a few heroes and an artisan who left no name.
### STANDARD

- Dark Fruit of Bright Flowers: as the tribes' banners faded, a half-man holding a cracked ring played on the throne and ordered that none heed the instructions of the ancestors and first ascended.
- Faded Emerald Tail: the girl, bonded to the youth, searched the city for the renowned craftsman among the nobles and found nothing, until a hooded drunkard at a dilapidated tavern told her the story of the feather ornament.
- Moment of Attainment: researchers puzzle over identical burin marks in the same spot on the sundials in the city's ash.
- The Wine-Flask Over Which the Plan Was Hatched: the craftsman told the youth and the heroes how the king of the ineffable city had gathered tribal artisans to dig up huge beast-shaped constructs.
- Crownless Crown: the girl, grown old as the mistress of the mines, had promised a turquoise crown to the craftsman once the ancient kingdom fell.
Each piece has its own entry with its full text.
### LARGE +
Wiki cross-references that the text leaves implicit: the red-eyed youth is the scarlet-eyed youth, later the second Pyro Archon, said by the wiki to have asked that his own name be erased; the girl and later mistress of the mines is Sakkuk, chief of the Children of Echoes; the taciturn warrior is Yupanqui; and the twins are Atawallpa and Waskar. The insane king is Och-Kan, the Sacred Lord and Python King, whose city is Ochkanatlan. The blind old woman is not named in the item text.
### ULTRA +
The set is framed as a puzzle: researchers find the same burin marks on many sundials, and Moment of Attainment says unofficial histories credit an unnamed artisan with the plan. Crownless Crown shows the tribes swearing to erase his name, and the wiki's Scarlet-Eyed Youth page adds that the youth himself asked Sakkuk to remove his own name from history. The pieces are best treated as legend about a rebellion, not a verified chronicle.

## owners
### LARGE
No wearer. The only named people in the text are Sakkuk, Yupanqui and Atawallpa; the set contains no keepsake attributed to a living owner.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): the 2-piece bonus is ATK +18%. The 4-piece bonus gives +50% DMG after leaving combat for 3s; in combat the bonus drops by 10% per second after 6s without a nearby Burning opponent, and climbs 10% per second to 50% while a Burning opponent is nearby. It still works if the wearer is off-field.
### LARGE +
Obtained from the Faded Theater domain (listed on the wiki in the Nostoi Region of Fontaine) and the artifact strongbox. Added in Version 4.6.
The Faded Theater's Ley Line Disorder gives a stacking All Elemental and Physical DMG Bonus whenever a Bond of Life gains or loses value. The wiki's trivia notes that the set's lore relates to Natlan, citing the Saurians and names such as Sakkuk, Yupanqui, Atawallpa and Waskar, which echo Maya and Inca figures.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): present the pieces as bittersweet relics of a rebellion told by those who loved its unsung planner.
### LARGE +
Characters in the present would know little of the craftsman, because the tribes deliberately erased his name; do not give him a name or face beyond what the text allows.

## entry: The Wine-Flask Over Which the Plan Was Hatched
@keys: The Wine-Flask Over Which the Plan Was Hatched
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
the craftsman told the youth and the heroes how the king of the ineffable city had gathered tribal artisans to dig up huge beast-shaped constructs, and burned everything and everyone to bury the secret behind a stone gate. A golden tear from a stone head fell into the craftsman's eye as he lay dying, and he saw the machines, shadows climbing to the moon, golden patterns and an alternate escape route, and the iron laws that built the empire. The twins laughed it off as a tale. He hoped that, once the dust settled, he could design a brilliant imperial wall for the new king.

## entry: Crownless Crown
@keys: Crownless Crown
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
the girl, grown old as the mistress of the mines, had promised a turquoise crown to the craftsman once the ancient kingdom fell. After she saw his corpse hunched beneath a black beast's butcher's blade, the crown waited alone for a master who would never attend a coronation. She bought his genuine works and forgeries alike, at any price in gems, so the fakes would not stain his name, and she dealt harshly with cheats. Outliving the youth, who returned to the sacred flames, the warrior who fell into the ruler's flames, and the twins, she took the last of his true works into the dark night and never returned. Next day, the tribes swore beneath the tree where the crown lay to follow her last order, to blot the craftsman's name from history.
