## summary
### LITE
Long Night's Oath is a 4- and 5-star artifact set that rewards Plunging Attacks. Its five pieces (Lightkeeper's Pledge, Nightingale's Tail Feather, Undying One's Mourning Bell, A Horn Unwinded, Dyed Tassel) carry item text that reads as a folk history of Nod-Krai: how the Lightkeepers swore their oath, how the Wild Hunt and the Abyss threatened the borderlands, and how the oath was renewed again and again at great cost.
### STANDARD +
The pieces were added in version 5.5. The text is told in a storyteller's voice and names almost no one directly; the wiki links the unnamed figures to Solovei, Avrelian, Aila, Sigurd, Rerir and others (see uncertainties).

## appearance
### STANDARD
Northern military and ceremonial gear: a metal flower worn by Ratniki ("warriors"), a hat ornament of nightingale feathers, a bronze bell, a horn once used to summon the oath-sworn, and an ancient tasselled helmet.

## lore
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The five pieces tell Nod-Krai's story from its founding oath to the cost of keeping it, centring on the Lightkeepers who guard the region.
### STANDARD +
Across the pieces: after the Cataclysm the borderlands were left without divine protection, a Torchforger from the far north rallied a moonlit defence, and the survivors raised a lighthouse at Piramida and swore an oath of vigilance to the new ruler of Snezhnaya, the Tsaritsa. Centuries later the Wild Hunt returns, the Fatui arrive at Piramida with ulterior motives, an oath-bound legion is lost, and a border officer chooses to save civilians against orders. Each piece frames the oath as a burden that descendants take up knowing what it costs.
### LARGE +
The five pieces each tell one episode; see the per-piece entries (founding oath, Fatui at Piramida, the Wild Hunt and the bell, the Warrant Officer, the dyed helmet and the first Torchforger).
### ULTRA +
The pieces move among several eras. Founding era: the Cataclysm, the Torchforger's light and the first oath (Lightkeeper's Pledge, Dyed Tassel). A later age of the Wild Hunt: the Fatui at Piramida's gates and the oath-bound legion lost in the forest (Nightingale's Tail Feather, Undying One's Mourning Bell). A Horn Unwinded gives no names or dates for the Warrant Officer's desertion, so its place in the sequence is not stated. The text keeps the Fatui ambiguous: they are presented as the Tsaritsa's own force who undermine Nod-Krai's autonomy, yet one of their own officers breaks ranks to protect civilians. The Starshyna and the Lightkeepers are portrayed as proud of their autonomy; the oath is the source of both their pride and their casualties. Dyed Tassel's reference to the lord of all fae links the Torchforger's heritage to the court that Lightkeeper's Pledge shows ignoring the frontier.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner is named; the text speaks of Ratniki and Lightkeepers, and the wiki ties several pieces to Solovei and Sigurd.

## gameplay
@availability: permanent
### LITE
Game mechanics: the 2-piece bonus raises Plunging Attack DMG; the 4-piece bonus stacks a Plunging Attack damage buff.
### STANDARD
Game mechanics (not lore): obtained from the Derelict Masonry Dock domain (Natlan) and the Artifact Strongbox. 2-piece: Plunging Attack DMG +25%. 4-piece: a Plunging Attack, Charged Attack or Elemental Skill hit gives 1, 2 or 2 stacks of Radiance Everlasting (each source once per 1s); each stack gives Plunging Attacks +15% DMG for 6s, max 5 stacks, independent timers.

## entry: The Nightingale emblem and the first Torchforger
@keys: Nightingale emblem, first Torchforger, original Torchforger
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
According to Dyed Tassel and Nightingale's Tail Feather, the first Torchforger came from a family of spies raised to the gentry by the Winter Tsar, with a nightingale as their emblem. After the Cataclysm he rode south from the far north in dark silver armour; with the new-moon maiden's beacon he repelled the black tide and later bound himself to an oath of vigilance. The wiki identifies him as Solovei; the text itself does not name him.

## entry: The Mourning Bell legend
@keys: Mourning Bell, bell of the Undying One, warrior of the dark blade
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Undying One's Mourning Bell tells of a warrior who fought toward the Wild Hunt's source, died without noticing, and continues to walk the abyss to fulfil his oath. His bell's toll became a folk omen that the hunter of the Wild Hunt is near. The wiki links him to Sigurd.

## entry: Piece text: Lightkeeper's Pledge
@keys: Lightkeeper's Pledge, Hunting Winds, Moonchildren oath
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
describes a time when the northern Hunting Winds levelled the frontier at the bidding of the tyrannical Knyazes of the fae. A mad monk who spoke strange tongues sought the power left by the heavenly moons and pitched a treacherous plot to the Tsar of all fae; the Moonchildren hiding in their mountain forests were scattered. The monk himself could not escape the celestial shell and was consumed by the sky. Before "paradise" existed it was merely a shared daydream of two children under the moon; years later one was executed as a notorious bandit and the other understood the words left behind. When the long night fell, the royal court ignored the borderlands and a horde of black beasts overran them until the original Torchforger, borrowing the power of the Moonchildren's priestess, raised the first beam of light. The survivors built a lighthouse atop the mountains and the Torchforger defended his friend's childlike dream before the Tsaritsa's court; he won a royal edict but bound himself to an oath of vigilance and sacrifice, and so began the history of "Nod-Krai".

## entry: Piece text: Nightingale's Tail Feather
@keys: Nightingale's Tail Feather, Fatui at Piramida
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
legend of a nightingale carrying an ember on moonless nights. Piramida's high tower stands on the ruins of an age of pioneers, and the Lightkeepers' ancestors had sworn fidelity to the Tsaritsa centuries before. The people of "paradise" believed that so long as the lighthouse burned they could live free of the black beasts. One night the leader of the Wild Hunt rode across the sky and the sun failed to rise; as warriors gathered to march south, the current Starshyna sat silent in the mead hall because a host of the Fatui, the Warriors of the Pale Star, had reached the city gates, claiming to be reinforcements but with their own motives. The Starshyna knew that yielding would end centuries of autonomy. A masked officer's cold voice declared the dark calamity was beginning, that Snezhnaya could not bear the weight of their defeat, and that if none of the Nightingale warriors returned, the Fatui would handle the aftermath.

## entry: Piece text: Undying One's Mourning Bell
@keys: Undying One's Mourning Bell, Wild Hunt tempest
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
blood-scented air heralds the return of the great war as an army of spirits flies over the night. Country folk called it a demonic host from another world harvesting souls; others believed it the lingering phantoms of an ancient nightmare. The borderlanders named it the Wild Hunt, and a tempest unseen for a century fell like black-blood rain, proving the legends were born of real terror. A flock of black-feathered crows moved between light and shadow, led by one who stood against the light; the oath-bound legion marched on as moths to a flame. At the path's end only the warrior with the dark blade still stood, aware that the force that summoned the Hunt lay deep in the forest. His body had already been torn apart without his noticing, and his warning bell hissed through the blood and black mire, freezing the carrion-eaters. He was left to walk alone in the abyss to fulfil an unfinished oath. Folklore later held that tolling bells signal the approach of the one sworn to hunt down the Wild Hunt.

## entry: Piece text: A Horn Unwinded
@keys: A Horn Unwinded, Warrant Officer
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
when horns proclaimed the northern realm's might, a Fatui contingent sworn to the Tsaritsa and to no single Harbinger vowed to forge a pure new world beneath the stars. The borderlanders, granted autonomy, remained children of the motherland. The Warrant Officer leading a border squad held that conviction until he hovered before the gates of hell and saw souls no one loved. Before any strike was sanctioned he resolved to act, rejecting an order that would let black beasts devour mothers and children. The text skips ahead: the foul miasma is swept away by northern winds at dawn, the war ends, and no triumphant hero returns to the high tower, only a criminal and the children he saved. A renegade doctor ignores the deviation because the goal was met. Desertion and treason against the Tsaritsa are absolved by a newly sworn oath, and new warriors keep watch on a staircase built of bones until the abyssal tides dry up.

## entry: Piece text: Dyed Tassel
@keys: Dyed Tassel, Ratniki helmet
@version: 5.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
the first Torchforger came from the furthest northern city, his Nightingale emblem marking a family who began as spies and were raised to the gentry by the Winter Tsar. When the Cataclysm left the frontier without the gods' protection, a single rider in dark silver armour came from the north and the maiden of the new moon lit a beacon. Before departing for the Lempo Isles, warriors feasted in the mead hall, among them a young Ratnik in an archaic dark-tasselled helmet and holding a polearm; such armour marked descent from those who first fought beside the Torchforger against the black tide. Although they held no fief or title, they kept their ancestors' oaths in the belief that the lord of the fae and former protector of Snezhnaya still watched. Later the young warrior kneels among fallen comrades on a blood-soaked tundra and prays to an old god and to the Knyazes his people once ruled. Somewhere, perhaps woken by the horn, a long-dormant phantom stirs, and the ghostly blue wildfire of legend is said to burn again on new battlefields.
