## summary
### LITE
The Padisarah is a holy, noble flower of Sumeru. It is very demanding about where it grows, and its buds are processed into valuable spices. Its archive text says it is a cultivar restored to the land by the Anahitian Blessing, after the original flower vanished.
### STANDARD +
It is a Sumeru local specialty and a cooking ingredient, seen around Sumeru City, Pardis Dhyai, the Palace of Alcazarzaray and the real Vanarana.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Item text gives no description of the blossom beyond calling it holy and noble. The wiki's wild-growth image is the only visual reference; do not invent colours or scent.

## lore
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
The item text calls it a holy and noble plant with demanding growing conditions, whose flower buds become valuable spices. Its archive text adds that the name is said to come from an ancient deity. It tells of a time when three gods were on excellent terms and a great plain of lovely flowers lay in the land; people gathered the blossoms as incense to offer to their gods.
### LARGE +
Those original flowers are no longer extant. The Padisarah is a cultivar brought back to the land through the Anahitian Blessing, a fertilizer said to be a blessing of the Dendro Archon. The wiki links the deity of the name to Nabu Malikata, though the text itself does not name her.
### ULTRA +
The Anahitian Blessing is distributed free by the Akademiya to farmers on a certain list in Sumeru City, and guarantees a good harvest if applied correctly; used wrongly it yields mushrooms instead, per the wiki's account of NPC dialogue. Nothing says the Padisarah itself grows from it by ordinary farming.

## gameplay
### LARGE
Gameplay note, not in-world lore: a Sumeru local specialty used in cooking. Dana at Pardis Dhyai and Jut at the Grand Bazaar in Sumeru City each give four on a first dialogue branch. It grows near Pardis Dhyai, the Palace of Alcazarzaray, Sumeru City, the real version of Vanarana, and a few spots near Vissudha Field.

## scene_use
### LARGE
Use it as a quiet marker of Sumeru's restored past: a spice merchant praising its price, a gardener complaining how fussy it is, or a scholar reciting the story of the three gods. Keep to the archive text and do not give it magical properties.
