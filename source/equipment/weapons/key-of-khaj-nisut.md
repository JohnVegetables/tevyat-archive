## summary
### LITE
The Key of Khaj-Nisut is a 5-star sword of the Deshret series. The in-game description calls it one of a paired set of scepters fashioned from obsidian, said to work as a key to the gate of a paradise at the end of the ocean of sand. Its story follows a hidden prince, later called Xiphos, and the aged blind poet who served and then freed him.
### STANDARD +
The name Khaj-Nisut comes from ancient Egyptian words for "the appearance of the king", the coronation of a pharaoh (wiki etymology note). The sword is the partner of the Staff of the Scarlet Sands.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Described as a black sword (the poet strokes "the dull blade of the black sword") and as a scepter of obsidian. Canon text gives no further detail.

## lore
### LITE
The story is set after the Lord of Sand's dreams fell apart and the Grandmaster of Verdure hid away the whispers of the demonic skies. A queen of one of the many kingdoms that arose from the broken realm burned her little prince's gilded robes and crown and sent him away in a servant's sackcloth. He grew up as the son of one who ruled the slave markets and then became a wanderer who had lost everything. His last master was a blind poet.
### STANDARD +
The poet, who once advised an overlord and blessed the former king's son at birth, is now blind, and takes the prince as an apprentice to be his eyes. The prince recalls his mother's words that they would meet again in the "eternal oasis"; he should use the sword as the key to the paradise's gate. The poet names him Xiphos, the Jinni's favourite, and sends him to wander the failing kingdoms.
### LARGE +
The last paragraph is explicitly legend. A key is said to have made the sands flow like a river in mid-air and sealed the homeland after the Lord of Sand parted from old friends, and to have hidden the desert tower and paradise of dreams under illusions when the bubble burst and the nations scattered. It passed through the hands of mortal tyrants and kings and finally returned to the sand. The old poet then follows stories and blood-tainted footsteps into the forest. The text of Beacon of the Reed Sea adds that a blind wandering poet later gathered ballads including the story of the homeless prince's death, the fate of his swords and the killer who travelled into the forest.
### ULTRA +
The paired Staff of the Scarlet Sands text is spoken by King Deshret and ties the scepters to his making of an "eternal oasis" for Nabu Malikata, which the wiki identifies with the Orchard of Pairidaeza. Two layers therefore coexist: an old function as a key to a hidden paradise, and a later mortal history of the sword as a princely heirloom. Keep them apart; the Key's own text does not state that Deshret made it.

## owners
### LITE
Named in the text: the prince Xiphos, who wields it as his key, and before him his master the blind poet. Legend adds mortal tyrants and kings in between.
### LARGE +
Xiphos is linked elsewhere with the sword Xiphos' Moonlight and with Tulaytullah, where he became a famed hero before being killed in a duel by the exiled prince Faramarz (wiki's Xiphos page; the Beacon text only alludes to it). Nobody is established as holding the Key now.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): 5-star sword released in Version 3.1 (2022-10-14), level 1 base ATK 44, secondary stat HP%. Passive Sunken Song of the Sands raises HP; hitting with an Elemental Skill grants Grand Hymn stacks (up to 3) that give the wielder Elemental Mastery based on Max HP, and at 3 stacks nearby party members gain Elemental Mastery based on the wielder's Max HP. It works only while the wielder is on the field.
### LARGE +
At refinement 1 HP rises 20%, each stack gives 0.12% of Max HP as Elemental Mastery and the party bonus is 0.2%; refinement 5 gives 40%, 0.24% and 0.4%. A stack can be gained at most once every 0.3 s, and the party bonus also applies to the wielder. Ascension uses Damaged, Turbid and Radiant Prism with Faded Red Satin, Trimmed Red Silk and Rich Red Brocade.

## roleplay
### LARGE
In scenes the Key suits desert-legend storytelling: a scholar, poet or desert traveller may speak of it as the fabled key to a lost paradise. Keep the framing of legend; canon never shows anyone opening that gate in the present.
