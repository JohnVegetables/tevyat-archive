## summary
### LITE
Jadefall's Splendor is a five-star catalyst and a ritual vessel carved from jade, reflecting the luster of the skies and clear waters. Its item story says jadeite fell from the sky, was washed clean and was shaped by mortal hands into a splendid instrument awaiting its sacred purpose. The rest of the text is a tea-table debate between a scholar from Sumeru and a consultant in dark robes about whether history bends toward decay or rebirth, framed by the story of Chenyu Vale's ancestors.
### STANDARD +
It belongs to the Primordial Jade series, though it is the later entry with a different focus. The text ends with the Bishui River flowing eternal and the jade's luster as constant as its former master.

## appearance
### LARGE
The in-game description calls it a ritual vessel carved from jade that reflects the luster of the skies and clear waters. The story adds a "splendid jade instrument" of primordial stone; nothing of its shape is given.

## lore
### STANDARD
The story opens with jadeite that fell from the sky, was cleansed by water, freed of dark earth and rotten wood, and shaped by a mortal youth into a ritual instrument. A researcher from the rainforest realm of Sumeru then argues over tea that history bends toward decay: the fall of humanity can only be stopped by the wisdom and mercy of gods, and he points to deserted ruins and hilichurls without speech or spirit as proof that earlier peoples vanished without a ripple.
### LARGE +
The text digresses to Chenyu Vale's people, who once lived among the crystal-veined mountains of the Chasm in mining settlements stretching from peak to peak. A buried ancient sin beneath their shafts brought catastrophe described as divine punishment, and the forebears had to move north. Qiaoying Village's elders say the ancestors came from the chasm to the south, brought a great jade altar and left enigmatic ruins. When the war of the gods brought tragedy they became wandering refugees; their descendants once believed in a now-forgotten god and are now lost to history.
### ULTRA +
The consultant answers that the history of mortals is one of rebirth: glory and ruin come from mortals themselves, and without agency there is no rise or fall. The ruins lie forsaken, but their former inhabitants live on, absorbed into the people of a new god; the fading of the ancestors gave rise to today's prosperity. He adds that the wisdom and laws of gods rest on mortal consensus, not personal whim. The tea-table debate ends inconclusively and the Bishui River flows on. The wiki identifies the debaters as Soraya and Zhongli and links the forgotten god to Zibai, the altar to the Chiwang Terrace and the ruins to Lang-Gan; these are the wiki's links, not named in the text. The English name's Chinese origin, "Biluo", is a Taoist term for blue sky, unlike the other jade-named weapons in the series.

## owners
### STANDARD
Item text names no owner beyond a "former master" (read by the wiki as Morax) and a mortal youth who shaped the jade. No source consulted ties it to a particular playable character.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): a five-star catalyst from weapon event wishes. Base ATK 46, secondary stat HP 10.8%. Passive "Primordial Jade Regalia": for 3 seconds after an Elemental Burst or creating a shield, the wielder restores 4.5-6.5 Energy every 2.5 seconds and gains an Elemental DMG Bonus of 0.3-1.1% per 1,000 Max HP, up to 12-44%, even off the field (refinements 1 to 5).

## roleplay
### LARGE
Roleplay use (this project's suggestion, not canon text): the story suits themes of memory and the long view of mortal history. A character may cite one side of the debate, but do not make either side the settled lore.
