## summary
### LITE
The Flute of Ezpitzal is a 4-star sword from Natlan: a peculiarly shaped jade longsword that the Masters of the Night-Wind have passed down as a "priestly flute". Its story explains how the tribe called Mictlan began.
### STANDARD +
It is forgeable and belongs to the Salutary series. The story spans two pages, about a Holy Sovereign, her loyal attendant Dinga (later the dragon slayer Maghan) and the dragons' vanished pact.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon: a jade longsword of strange shape, handed down as a flute. Do not invent holes, engravings or sounds.

## lore
### LITE
Long ago, when dragons still roamed a deep valley, a girl with mutilated ankles inherited the title of Holy Sovereign. Dragons, frightened into withdrawing into dreams, had made a pact with the mortals: they would shield the village with a labyrinth of mirrors and a fortress of mist if the people chose a Sovereign to guide them into their dreams.
### STANDARD +
The Sovereign's only companions were a flute and her silent attendant. She had foreseen how the dream would end. When the hero passed through the mist and mirrors guided by her flute song, she whispered her last command: when they met again he must pierce her heart, let her name be swallowed by the beacons, break the old pact and make a new one bringing true peace. She names him her most loyal servant Dinga, dragon slayer and fated Sovereign Maghan.
### LARGE +
Page #2 skips ahead. The village that worshipped the dragons was denounced by the ruler of Cinder City as traitors. The mirror labyrinth and fortress of mist fell to the Python King's armies, led by the hero Maghan, who had once fought alongside the One Entombed With the Primal Fire. Offered a reward, he asked only to retire, saying he had failed to catch the beasts, who had escaped into the smoke-shrouded valleys. That was the origin of Mictlan, one of the six great tribes of the next thousand years. The tale deliberately skips how Maghan's successor renewed the dragon pact after Cinder City fell and how he was buried among unmarked graves rather than returned to the Sacred Flame, calling that another story.
### ULTRA +
The closing lines record the Sovereign's final vision: a katun without fear, a city of jade founded where the sun rises and a new lord born twice, with the golden emissary, the green bird and the 'tongue of fire' coming, as the night's promise; she dreams of descendants free from smoke and phantasms, protected by the master of flames and blazing infernos until the stars cease to turn, and asks her hero to make that future real. The wiki notes katun (a unit of about 20 years) and Aq're Q'aq (a K'iche term for 'tongue of fire') as real-world references. Compare the text of Rainbow Serpent's Rain Bow, which tells the later covenant renewed by Maghan's successor.

## owners
### LITE
Handed down among the Masters of the Night-Wind. Its story is tied to the Holy Sovereign and to Maghan.
### LARGE +
The wiki lists no modern bearer. Any scene giving it to a character is that scene's invention.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): a forgeable 4-star sword of the Salutary series, base ATK 41 at level 1, secondary stat DEF%. Forging needs a diagram that the Traveler receives from the Hidden Exploration Objective Stoking the Ancient Flames in Ochkanatlan (activate five furnaces, then draw the ancient sword).
### LARGE +
Passive "Smoke-and-Mirror Mystery": using an Elemental Skill increases the wielder's DEF for 15s. The recipe lists a Borderland Sword Billet, 50 Condessence Crystal, 50 White Iron Chunk and 500 Mora.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Masters of the Night-Wind characters may know the story as tribe origin lore. Treat the Holy Sovereign's sacrifice and the broken pact as solemn; page 2 is deeper, possibly less commonly known knowledge.

Smoke-and-Mirror Mystery is the passive of the sword Flute of Ezpitzal (gameplay): casting an Elemental Skill raises the wielder's DEF for a while. The name recalls the labyrinth of mirrors and fortress of mist that shielded the valley.
