## summary
### LITE
Heretic's Molten Blade is a 4-star sword from the Battle Pass, belonging to Och-Kan, the Sacred Lord of Ochkanatlan. A gift from the red-haired Pyro Archon, it was later turned against him.
### STANDARD +
The item text is a short tragedy: a half-dragon tyrant who was never a swordsman, a gift given at a bonfire, and a death at the hands of the crimson-eyed youth.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon says only that the blade was made from smelted works of the ancient dragons and kept in a leather scabbard, with an edge that dulled from long disuse yet kept clean of dust. Keep descriptions to a well-kept, unused sword.

## lore
### LITE
Och-Kan stood alone in his empty royal hall and drew a sword that had gone unused for so long its edge had dulled. He kept it spotless for decades, though he was no swordsman.
### STANDARD +
The sword had been a gift. At a bonfire after a battle, the heroes asked the red-haired Pyro Archon to judge the best weapon, and he asked Och-Kan what he wanted; the blade was made from smelted dragon works and tuned to his proportions.
### LARGE +
Och-Kan's dragon blood gave him strength far beyond ordinary people, so he did not need a sword: he was known for commanding the golden language and its rainbow light, but none knew he could fight a dragon bare-handed. After the heroes swept a stronghold of draconic descendants, they lit a bonfire among the bones and broken mechanisms and bragged about their weapons. One argued for a great two-handed sword; Lianca replied that her venom-tipped arrows made such blows possible; Ixquieh was credited for luring the dragons into the trap with Phlogiston; her brother Ahpub, called elder brother, was praised for dodging claws and crashing a dragon's head into a cliff; Maghan's flute-playing and Uenuku's teasing round out the scene.

When asked to name a weapon, Och-Kan realized he saw himself as the other man's most perfect weapon. His answer is not given, but it pleased only Ahpub and the red-haired man, who then kept his word and had fire smelt the works of the dragons into a blade. The weapon was standard practice, made from materials gathered on the hunt. Och-Kan swore to use it to hunt every inhuman heretic and build a realm for humans alone for the giver.
### ULTRA +
The text ends in his fall. It had been too long since he last wielded the sword in battle, and he was never good at it. The crimson-eyed young man, immature but wrathful, appeared before him with a might like a god's, wrenched the sword from his hand, and drove it into his chest as he staggered toward the mud. As he sank into the black mire, Och-Kan murmured that it could only have been so, and in the youth's flames took a last glimpse of the shadow of a cunning crimson fox, face filled with sorrow, flashing across the corner of the collapsing palace. Read alongside the wiki's profile of Och-Kan, the weapon records the hinge between his time as a hero's comrade and his tyranny and fall; the text itself does not narrate the years in between.

## owners
### LITE
Och-Kan, the Sacred Lord, is the owner. He received the blade from the red-haired Pyro Archon and died by it.
### LARGE +
The blade passed from the red-haired man's gift into Och-Kan's keeping and, in the end, into the hand of the crimson-eyed youth who killed him with it. No source shows who holds it after that.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): a 4-star sword, base ATK 42 at level 1, secondary stat CRIT Rate; Battle Pass (Gnostic Hymn, BP Bounty), Version 7.0; part of the Gnostic Hymn 3 series.
### LARGE +
Passive "Lone Light's Blessing": after using an Elemental Skill, the wielder gains "Gleam of First Light" for 14s, usable once every 14s and removed when the wielder leaves the field. Each second it grants an ATK bonus based on the distance travelled in the previous second, from 18% to 36% at refinement 1 up to 36% to 72% at refinement 5.

## roleplay
### LARGE
For scenes: speakers can refer to the blade as the Sacred Lord's sword, a relic of the age when he fought beside the heroes. Keep the story's moral weight: it was a gift of trust that he later betrayed.
