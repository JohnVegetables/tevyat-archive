## summary
### LITE
Astral Vulture's Crimson Plumage is a 5-star bow: an ancient longbow crested with red feathers, a sacred relic of Natlan's Flower-Feather Clan handed down through generations.
### STANDARD +
Its story is not one tale but several rival songs about a red-eyed young man, banished from the clan, whose pilgrimage ended in divinity.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon gives only the description: an ancient longbow crested with red feathers. The legend ties the feathers to a red vulture; do not add carvings or colours beyond that.

## lore
### LITE
The Flower-Feather elder, who only pretended to obey the usurper king, banished a young man to help him escape capture, because he wished to protect the Saurians. His red eyes reflected only the cold aqua moon ring above him.
### STANDARD +
Poets later sang many versions of the pilgrimage and its hero, and none became the single accepted account. All agree that the red-eyed young man ascended to divinity.
### LARGE +
The story is set when the oaths between humans and Saurians had faded like dying bonfires, Cinder City's shadow had buried the Night under fearful whispers, and the Python King's mad dreams left only a chilling night wind. Och-Kan, called the Sacred Lord, had turned his back on the alliance for the only god he loved. On the high cliffs, in turmoil after the loss of their master, the Flower-Feather Clan's elder banished the young man.

In one version, sung to an eight-stringed nyatiti, his companion was a red vulture, a noble scion who once served the dragonlord and whose eyes reflected the deep sky and stars. Moved by the hero's ideals, the bird gave its flesh and blood to make the keenest of bows, and its feathers guided him to Sakkuk, a revered ancestor of the Children of Echoes.
### ULTRA +
A rival version, which the text says differs from that of the Children of Echoes, denies that the hero was an ordinary mortal. It holds that he was a thread of thought of the One Entombed With the Primal Fire, returned from the Night Realm to save the oppressed; the longbow is offered as proof, since a mere mortal could not have razed Cinder City, and the hero is expected to emerge from the Flame again when Natlan is in crisis. The text notes that poets who doubted the pilgrimage was made alone invented a guide who never existed, and that those who doubted he was mortal tied him back to the first human god.

The consensus is only that he ascended to divinity, which is why the saying "humans can become gods" is held dear across the plains. The poem ends with two voices: one asks that Sakkuk's name be erased so that all know any human can become the god who guides them, and a closing exchange, with part of a name redacted, promises that a feather given in parting will be passed down for a thousand years in the homeland the hero could never return to.

## owners
### LITE
The Flower-Feather Clan keeps it as a sacred relic; no individual wielder is named.
### LARGE +
In legend the bow came from a red vulture who followed the red-eyed hero. Any character shown holding a copy is an invention of that scene; canon does not say who carries the relic today.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): a 5-star bow, base ATK 46 at level 1, secondary stat CRIT DMG; Weapon Event Wishes; introduced in Version 5.2.
### LARGE +
Passive "The Moonring Sighted": for 12s after triggering a Swirl reaction, ATK is increased (24% at refinement 1 up to 48% at refinement 5). If half or more of the party are of a different Elemental Type from the wielder, Charged Attack DMG and Elemental Burst DMG are also increased. Ascension uses Night-Wind's Mystic materials, the Refractive Bud boss line and whistle common materials.

## roleplay
### LARGE
For scenes: speakers in the Flower-Feather Clan can treat the bow as a sacred relic whose legend has several versions. Have them name the version they favour rather than the "true" one, since canon says none is definitive.
