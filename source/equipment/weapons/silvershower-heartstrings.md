## summary
### LITE
Silvershower Heartstrings is a 5-star bow, the legendary bow of the fairies, said to touch the hearts of listeners whether on stage or on the battlefield.
### STANDARD +
Its story is not about a person but about a very old Fontainian play, Dryas, and the many versions of its ending.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon calls it a bow, fairy-made and later modified as a theatrical prop. It carries a Pneuma or Ousia mark for Arkhe-aligned wielders. Add no other detail.

## lore
### LITE
Fontainian playwrights liken strings to hearts, and the word heartstring appears in one of the oldest plays, Dryas, though the string there was a bowstring.
### STANDARD +
The hero is Aurelius, glorious as gold, who conquered countless traitorous nations for Fontaine. He is the forerunner of the Aurelius in Coppelius's The Saga of Aurelius, yet no such figure exists in recorded history, so he is often regarded as fiction, like Ajax. In the story he falls into a foe's trap and is lost with his army in a pine forest.
### LARGE +
As an assassin's blade is about to kill him, silver arrows fall like rain. He looks for the quivering bowstring and sees a maiden, Dryastis, who takes his hand and leads him from the bloodstained pines. She asks only that no more die in vain, that he take the conflict away from the land and stop tainting the pure waters with death.

The play has been edited for millennia. In some versions the hero accepts her bow and sounds the withdrawal, only to be killed by traitors and have his body sunk in the sea. In others he is driven by maddening love, leads his army back into the forest to find her, and she flees to the riverbank and begs her pure-water mother to save her. The ruler of the Many Waters (a title that Splendor of Tranquil Waters uses for Egeria) takes pity and turns her into a pine tree; the bowstring snaps and falls among blooming Pluie Lotuses, and the hero stares at the flowers until he falls into a deep pool.
### ULTRA +
Later natural philosophers say a barbarian tribe called Dryas once settled the area, which is why the forest has that name; the text calls such unromantic explanations irrelevant to theatre. It offers further endings: he sinks to have his will sanded down by time, or resurfaces after many years only to be killed by the new White Armada or the Marechaussee Hunters. The final paragraph says how the bow, changing hands many times, came to belong to someone who dwelled deep beneath the waters and was adapted into a prop for children's performances in that person's homeland, and calls this a different tale altogether.

## owners
### LITE
No owner is named. The bow is attributed to the fairies in legend.
### LARGE +
The bow's most recent holder is only described as a person deep beneath the waters (see uncertainties). Do not state who without a source.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): a 5-star bow, base ATK 44 at level 1, secondary stat HP; Weapon Event Wishes; introduced in Version 4.7. Passive "Dryas's Nocturne": the wielder can gain Remedy stacks, one for using an Elemental Skill (25s), one when a Bond of Life value increases (25s), and one for performing healing (20s); each stack count adds Max HP, and with 3 stacks Elemental Burst CRIT Rate rises, ending 4s after falling below 3. Works off-field. Values scale with refinement.

## roleplay
### LARGE
For scenes: Fontainians know this as a theatre tale, likely from school or playbills. Let characters disagree on the ending, as the text itself keeps several.

## entry: Dryas and Dryastis
@keys: Dryastis, Silvershower Heartstrings
@keys2: Aurelius, Dryas, bow, heartstring
### STANDARD
Dryas is an ancient Fontainian play in which the fairy maiden Dryastis saves the hero Aurelius with silver arrows and begs him to end the war on her land. Its many endings are the story of the weapon Silvershower Heartstrings. No historical Aurelius is recorded.
