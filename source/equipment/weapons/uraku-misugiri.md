## summary
### LITE
Uraku Misugiri is a five-star sword made by the renowned literato Urakusai, a Kitsune. Legend says that in hundreds of years it has never cut any living creature. The name comes from an episode where a drunken Urakusai sliced the gold-lacquered stage curtains of the bake-danuki in a dance, and the sword was later given as a reward to the man known as the Otogi King, "he of the Front Gate".
### STANDARD +
The item story adds a second, oral account from the oni: the Otogi King traded the blade to a young tailor for her scissors, and she later wove a scroll of him with two swords.

## appearance
### STANDARD
The text calls it a great golden blade, "Misugiri", of famous make. Its owner is pictured in golden brocade threaded with autumn grass and clouds, with painted features; in some scrolls he holds one large and one small sword. The sword's own look is not detailed beyond its name and size.

## lore
### STANDARD
Urakusai once earned the bake-danuki's undying enmity by cutting the gold-lacquered stage curtains in the forest during a drunken reverie. That gave his sword its name, and his blade-wild dance under the moon became a tale told by everyone but the bake-danuki. He later apologised to the performers and gave them his tea set and other treasures. The man "of the Front Gate" had met him only once before, and received the blade as a reward for mediating between the parties.
### LARGE +
The recipient was unlike the rumours: a strange character who fought a vicious battle against Lavender Melon trees, yet had a sense of elegance and loved plays, toys and clothes. In battle he wore golden brocade and painted his face. Ancient texts and folk tales never show him wielding Misugiri in his final battle; his weapon in anecdotes is always the prodigiously large blade with an equally long name, which the Redhorn Stonethresher text names. The "Anthology" of the time records that Misugiri was lost by him one summer before the disaster. The oni tell a different story, preserved only in song and chant. After a festival wrestling match he gave the blade to a tailor from a non-martial family: a young girl who had patched a golden flower loose from his haori, and who refused a reward.
### ULTRA +
He insisted on a swap of the sword for her scissors so it would not count as a reward, claimed a long blade would cut cloth better, and said he was giving it away because its owner had been long-winded and he did not want Urakusai visiting every few days to ask after it. He then drew it, cut off one of his own sleeves cleanly, and handed over sleeve and sword, frightening the girl by his sudden seriousness before laughing and leaving. The villagers called him "The Great Idiot" and he did not mind. Later, he was said to have sighed while cleaning the sword, blaming Urakusai for leaving such a treasure to a man like him and saying it should enjoy the beauty of the world rather than be tainted with slaughter. The tailor, wracked by guilt for never being able to return the blade to help him fell his foes, later designed the scroll showing him with two swords cutting through the tide of black monsters. The text ends before that scroll, with the oni Chiyo dancing with a sword in her resplendent juunihitoe, and says Urakusai first had years of grieving the scattered, wandering people.

## owners
### STANDARD
Maker: Urakusai. Recipient: the Otogi King, "he of the Front Gate". According to the oni account, he gave it to a young tailor of common birth, who is unnamed in the text. The item is an ordinary player weapon in-game.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): a five-star sword from the Otogi King series, base ATK 44, secondary stat CRIT DMG 19.2%. Passive "Brocade Bloom, Shrine Sword": Normal Attack DMG +16-32% and Elemental Skill DMG +24-48%; after a nearby active character deals Geo DMG, those bonuses double for 15s; the wielder's DEF rises by 20-40% (refinement 1-5). It was one of two event-exclusive weapons in the Version 4.5 Epitome Invocation (2024-03-13), alongside Redhorn Stonethresher.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Roleplay use (this project's suggestion, not canon text): the sword suits a theme of a treasure refusing to be only a trophy. Characters who tell its story should keep both accounts, the Anthology's and the oni's, and not pick one as the truth.

## entry: The Great Idiot of the Front Gate

### LARGE
The man "of the Front Gate" is the oni warrior of the oral account, the same figure as the Redhorn Stonethresher text's Otogi King by the weapon connection the Uraku Misugiri text draws. Villagers called him The Great Idiot; he was quick to laugh and anger, made many friends and protected many lives. The text states he was neither exceptionally long-lived nor short-lived.
