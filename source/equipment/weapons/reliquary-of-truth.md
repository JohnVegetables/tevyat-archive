## summary
### LITE
Reliquary of Truth is a 5-star catalyst added in Version 6.1. The in-game description calls it an angular box carved from gold and jade that legend says was once enshrined in a temple deep within Tulaytullah, the Sapphire City. Its story is about a young noble named Goudarz, who is given a power by a holy sage in exchange for an honest answer, and who rises as the king Saleb Dowleh, a king whose reign is built on lies.
### STANDARD +
The weapon connects to the Sapphire City and its Tighnarian people, a part of the Great Red Sand's history after King Deshret. It also links to the Lunar-Bloom reaction in its gameplay passive.

## appearance
### STANDARD
An angular box of gold and jade. Canon text gives no more detail than that.

## lore
### LITE
After the great tower of Shah Ramshahr fell, the royal power passed among states as fleeting as dew. The nobles of Tulaytullah, free of the tyrant, followed false sages into new carnage. By the time the brutal Khagans arrived on horseback, no warriors guarded the Garden of Endless Pillars, and foreign soldiers and tax collectors preyed on the weary people. People then looked back to the brief reign of King Humayun.
### STANDARD +
The young noble Goudarz enters a sacred hall beneath the sands, the palace of the Ibis King, wisest of the seven sage monks and keeper of records. The wise men of Tighnar had long fled and the sanctuary lay buried. From a dried-up spring a voice answers him and says it can grant a power never seen by prophet or proselyte, but only if he answers the riddles that the lord of flowers and oases left for the sage's master, King Deshret, who fell into a poisonous dream before answering. Goudarz asks to hear them and promises honest, mortal answers.
### LARGE +
The sage poses a first riddle: what rises and falls endlessly, perishing at its peak and swelling again like the moon and tides, desired by wise and foolish alike. Goudarz answers that it is conspiracy and treachery, and that this weakness brought down the sapphire domes. The sage laughs without confirming the answer, and hands him the Staff of Falsehoods. Goudarz's name is lost and replaced by the title King Saleb Dowleh, a warrior who would not tell lies who gains kingly power through cunning and casts the illusion of a golden age.
### ULTRA +
The close is a reversal. Eventually even the perfect lie cannot satisfy his thirst for power and King Deshret's creations can no longer fake divine manifestation. When the Ibis King's voice repeats the question amid the laments of Gurabad, the aged king answers that all he speaks are lies and that he will fight to the death at least so as not to betray again the trust of the brothers who have come to help; whether the holy one seeks truth or falsehood, he must taste it in his own blood. The Deys of other tribes seize his power, and the kingdoms are ground to dust. The sage takes the king's last words as his final answer. These names appear in no other source gathered here, so tell the tale as the legend the item itself calls it.

## owners
### LITE
No owner is named. Goudarz, later King Saleb Dowleh, receives the Staff of Falsehoods in the story; the reliquary itself is only said to have been enshrined in a temple of Tulaytullah.
### LARGE +
The story does not explain how the weapon came to be a catalyst or who carries it now.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): 5-star catalyst released in Version 6.1 (2025-10-22), level 1 base ATK 44, secondary stat CRIT DMG. Passive Essence of Falsity raises CRIT Rate; casting an Elemental Skill grants Secret of Lies (Elemental Mastery for 12 s), dealing Lunar-Bloom DMG grants Moon of Truth (CRIT DMG for 4 s), and when both are active their results are increased by 50%.
### LARGE +
At refinement 1 the CRIT Rate bonus is 8%, Secret of Lies gives 80 Elemental Mastery and Moon of Truth 24% CRIT DMG; refinement 5 gives 16%, 160 and 48%.

Good for legends of rulers and sages in the Great Red Sand. The box suits scenes about truth and falsehood. As canon does not say more, keep any claims about the object itself modest.
