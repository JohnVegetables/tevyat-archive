## summary
### LITE
Beacon of the Reed Sea is a 5-star claymore of the Deshret series. The in-game description calls it a large, flat-tipped sword designed as the weapon of an ancient king or some ceremonial instrument. Its story is told through a blind wandering poet who gathers the desert's ballads after the Lord of Sand sank into the desert with dreams in his embrace.
### STANDARD +
The name echoes the biblical "Reed Sea" (Yam Suph) per the wiki's etymology note; in the text the Reed Sea is the name of a dream that never existed.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A large claymore with a flat tip, built like a king's ceremonial weapon. Canon text gives no more detail.

## lore
### LITE
In an age when kingdoms rose and fell like mushrooms and sandcastles, the blind poet, once of privileged birth, crosses the sea of desert and collects ballads from its scattered people. He hears of the fall of his homeland, of the prince who blinded him finally crushing the overwhelmed throne, of a dancer who helped one man after another become king and then buried them in sandstorms, and of springs replaced by sand and nations shrunk to villages and tribes.
### STANDARD +
He also hears the tale of the homeless prince's death, the fate of his swords and the killer who went deep into the forest. From the shards of ballads comes a vision, in quotation, of King Deshret's abode on the other side of the dunes: streets like paths of gold converging on one throne, a king who was the destination of all roads until the dreams of gold broke. The kingdom of flowing sands was crushed to golden dust.
### LARGE +
The last verses return to the present of the ballads. A paranoid prince built forts outside the forest line to uphold the honour of the sand, but his soldiers and people scattered and his name was lost. Over the ruins of the pool where regalia from past executions were dug up, people who had lost the dream of the desert and refused to give up their sea of sand gathered "in the name of the Reed Sea that never existed", connecting with a dream promised to them. The wiki annotates the homeland as Tulaytullah, the prince who blinded the poet as Faramarz, the throne as Badanah's, the dancer as Makhaira, the homeless prince as Xiphos and the pool as Aaru Village.
### ULTRA +
The Key of Khaj-Nisut's text ends with an old blind poet following stories into the forest, and this text also features a blind wandering poet gathering ballads; the wiki treats them as the same poet. Keep in mind it is a second-hand ballad collection, so details such as who killed whom belong to the ballads' tellers.

## owners
### LITE
No owner is named. Dehya is shown wielding a claymore of this kind in her Genius Invokation TCG character card and in the Sumeru promotional video (wiki trivia).
### LARGE +
Because Dehya's use is shown only in promotional material, do not state that she owns the specific sword in the story.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): 5-star claymore released in Version 3.5 (2023-03-01), level 1 base ATK 46, secondary stat CRIT Rate. Passive Desert Watch raises ATK for 8 s after the wielder's Elemental Skill hits and again after the wielder takes damage, both effects working even off-field, and raises Max HP when the wielder has no shield.
### LARGE +
At refinement 1 each ATK bonus is 20% and the Max HP bonus 32%; refinement 5 gives 40% and 64%. Ascension uses Desiccated, Sturdy and Marked Shell with Faded Red Satin, Trimmed Red Silk and Rich Red Brocade. A Genius Invokation TCG equipment card of the same name exists.
