## summary
### LITE
The First Great Magic is a 5-star bow: a stage prop once used by a past Great Magician, whose final secret was that it was also a weapon beyond the pale.
### STANDARD +
Its story is the rise of the magician Parsifal the Great and her assistant Josephine, two sisters from the Fleuve Cendre undercity, and what became of the name.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon only mentions a gem shaped like a cat above the grip, which glows with Pneuma or Ousia when an Arkhe-aligned character holds it. The magician's patter mentions a hat leading to a legendary eastern paradise and a mirror showing peril, as stage props, not as parts of the bow.

## lore
### LITE
Parsifal the Great is the stage name of a Fontainian woman from the Fleuve Cendre, who performed with her sister Josephine as assistant. The name came from a thief in Master Coppelius's play, which the text calls an elusive thief who appears and disappears at will.
### STANDARD +
The pair, two young thieves who cheated and stole at the opera house, were dazzled by the knight-errant comedies onstage and forgot why they had come. They began performing in taverns, beside the sewers and at food stalls, then in the spotlight of the grand theatre. Their acts include Feathers into Butterflies, Paradise, Fire in the Mirror and The Golden Fish. The name Parsifal became the talk of both the city above and the city below.
### LARGE +
The text says the light of the shows fell equally on VIP boxes and standing audience, while outside the theatre anger and indignation spread like roots through a stone wall. During the Fleuve Cendre operation that wall collapsed. The girl who always played Josephine rushed back to find only the smell of iron, rot, anger, grief and undried blood. The assistant, who had never been the famous one, lost her place in life and designed a grand magic trick of her own.
### ULTRA +
The story closes with the patter again: a fantastic world never imagined, with no aristocrats and no kings, no swords pointed, no one born into wealth or into inescapable poverty. The wiki adds that Parsifal joined Eduardo Baker's takeover of Poisson, appears in Karl Ingold's photograph, saved Karl at the Siege, and was brought before the court, where she requested trial by combat against the Champion Duelist Marfisa and presumably died. The wiki is also careful to say this may refer to Josephine using her sister's alias. The Lumidouce Elegy story separately shows a girl from the Fleuve Cendre who is dazzled by this act and later becomes the Champion Duelist Marfisa.

## owners
### LITE
A past Great Magician, Parsifal, per the description.
### LARGE +
Lyney is paired with it by convention only (see uncertainties). Do not narrate him inheriting it without a source.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not in-world): a 5-star bow, base ATK 46 at level 1, secondary stat CRIT DMG; Weapon Event Wishes; introduced in Version 4.0. Passive "Parsifal the Great": raises Charged Attack DMG. Each party member sharing the wielder's Elemental Type (the wielder included) gives 1 Gimmick stack, raising ATK at 1, 2 and 3 or more stacks; each member of another type gives a Theatrics stack, raising Movement SPD. Values scale with refinement. Because the wielder always counts, 1 Gimmick stack is the minimum.

## roleplay
### LARGE
For scenes: Parsifal is a theatre legend of five hundred years ago; most Fontainians know the stage name, not the sisters' origin. Characters who enjoy magic or theatre may recite the patter; do not give the sisters invented birth names.

## entry: Parsifal and Josephine
@keys: Parsifal the Great, Josephine, The First Great Magic
@keys2: Great Magician, magician, assistant
### STANDARD
Parsifal the Great and her assistant Josephine were sisters from the Fleuve Cendre who became the most famous magic duo in Fontaine, per the weapon story of The First Great Magic. Their birth names are unknown. After the Siege of Poisson, a Parsifal asked for trial by combat.
