## summary
### LITE
Whitelake Frostfeather is a five-star sword added in version 7.0. The in-game description calls it a longsword as light as a snow swan's feathers that stays pure and untainted. Its story is an academic note about two older scripts, a "white" and a "black" one, on which the ballet Black Snow Swan draws, in which a tempter hands a young woman a sword.

## appearance
### STANDARD
The only description is "a longsword light as the feathers of a snow swan, and which stays pure and untainted at all times". The "pristine longsword" in the black script is called a fine blade without a scabbard.

## lore
### STANDARD
The text is written in the voice of a scholar who says academia recognises that the script of Black Snow Swan draws on folk songs, manuscripts and scripts that predate the Korolevskiy Troupe. It quotes two passages from the Polestar Songbook, about 300 years older than the present. They are nearly identical in lines, tone and rhythm, and nobody can tell which is older. The final dance of the white snow swan Tatyana in act two of the ballet was adapted from one of them, with an old fae-theatre sword dance removed.
### LARGE +
In the "white" script, the Elfking tempts Tatyana, who has resolved to elope, to kill her lover. She and her father exchange lines (he says she hears only the wind) and the Elfking offers her a sword "purer than his lies", fine but with no scabbard, then claims he meant only to present a gift. In the "black" script, an envoy from the south tries to persuade the Rusalka Vaselisa, about to leave her homeland, to murder her new liege, who proclaims himself of love. He suggests the liege's retainers plotted against her people, and presents a pristine longsword forged by his own liege, again without scabbard.
### ULTRA +
The two scripts share the same dialogue structure: a daughter answers her father's warnings, a tempter replies that human hearts are cold, the girl refuses the tempter's lies, and the tempter offers a blade while denying any wish to bring harm. The Elfking says "I am you", while the envoy says "We are both fae". The black script has the envoy say trust is as sharp as the blade. The wiki trivia notes that the opening exchange echoes Goethe's poem Erlkonig and that the "stage" line may echo Shakespeare; these are the wiki's observations, not statements in the item.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner is named. The sword appears only as the blade offered by a tempter in the scripts, and the item text does not say this weapon is that blade.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay (not lore): five-star sword from weapon event wishes. Base ATK 48, secondary stat CRIT Rate 4.8%. Passive "Snow Swan's Finale": hitting with an Elemental Skill grants "Lake-Hued Lament" stacks of ATK (up to three, each lasting 8 seconds). At three stacks, the wielder's Stellar Glimmer reaction DMG gains extra CRIT DMG, and triggering the reaction or its DMG restores Elemental Energy (once per 3.5 seconds). It works off-field.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay use (this project's suggestion, not canon text): a ballet scholar in Snezhnaya might know the scripts as the origins of Black Snow Swan; the weapon can serve as a prop for scenes about temptation offered as a gift.

### STANDARD
The item's note says the script of Black Snow Swan is based on an original story but also on earlier folk songs, manuscripts and scripts from before the Korolevskiy Troupe. Two of them come from the Polestar Songbook, about 300 years older than the present day, and Tatyana's final dance in act two of the ballet is adapted from one of them.

### LARGE
In the white script, Tatyana plans to elope. The Elfking, who says "I am you", warns that love may fail and that the oath, once broken, will shut the skies, then offers a pure sword and denies meaning murder.
