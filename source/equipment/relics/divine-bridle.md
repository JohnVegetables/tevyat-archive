## summary
### LITE
The Divine Bridle is a bridle-shaped quest item from Enkanomiya (Byakuyakoku). It was the badge of the Sunchild's office, the sign that a child was the nation's puppet ruler. It has no power of its own. The spirit Aberaku hid it and gave it to the Traveler; the Traveler then used it to help the official Clymene release the souls of the seven Sunchildren.
### STANDARD +
Its journey spans two linked world quests of Enkanomiya: Hyperion's Dirge, where Aberaku hands it over, and The Phaethons' Syrtos, where it becomes the medium that lets Clymene reach the dead children's souls. It is a quiet, sorrowful object, not a weapon or relic of power.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Shaped like a bridle; the in-game text gives no further description of materials or markings.

## lore
### LITE
Byakuyakoku was ruled by a Sunchild, an infant chosen to be the nation's figurehead and said to be a child of the sun. Per the quest accounts, the real power lay with people behind the scenes. The bridle marked the office. Aberaku, who built the Dainichi Mikoshi, calls it proof that a child is a child of the sun, and says he hid it intending to alert the people. Clymene, the official who raised the Sunchildren, believed it lost.
### LARGE +
Aberaku's account (Hyperion's Dirge): the Sunchildren were puppets of schemers, and none ever grew up, because a grown ruler would see through the manipulation. He quotes a verse about the Sunchild riding Pegasus to the feast of the sun, which he ties to the children being led into the inner sanctum of the Dainichi Mikoshi on their birthdays. He gives the bridle to the Traveler as a testament to the nation's "laughable" history and tells them to keep it or do what they like.

Clymene's account (The Phaethons' Syrtos): she had the Traveler pay respects at the seven royal tombs, then at the Soul Guide's Locus explained the Rite of Solar Return, after which only shreds of each child's soul returned to the Locus. Her own memories could not call them back, but she believed the Divine Bridle, the badge of the Sunchild's office, could serve as a medium. Seeing the real one she was overcome. The Traveler placed it in the waters of the Locus. She said it had passed through the hands of every Sunchild, so its memories could fill the gaps in their souls, memories of the children themselves and of how the nation saw them as children of sin. She then spoke to Piramumon and Orupeusu, whom she had raised, and let them go.

### ULTRA +
The seven Sunchildren named on their tombs are Orupeusu, Surepio, Risutaiosu, Isumenasu, Rikoru, Ion and Piramumon (each with "no Mikoto"). The tomb inscriptions describe them as young people with interests in music, medicine, sculpture, boating, building, fortune-telling and books; the wiki says Rikoru was the first Sunchild. Per the wiki summary, Orobashi later took control of Enkanomiya after a child asked him to become their god, ending the Sunchild's rule.

The people of Byakuyakoku, in Clymene's telling, hated the Sunchildren and held their ignorance to be sin; she nonetheless could not hate children she had watched grow up, but was forbidden to speak to them while under surveillance. She says the official who serves the Sunchildren is meant to stay pure and never marry. These details color the bridle as an object the nation remembered with bitterness and she with grief.

## owners
### STANDARD
Made as the Sunchild's badge, so carried by each Sunchild in turn, per Clymene. Aberaku hid it before the events of Hyperion's Dirge and gave it to the Traveler. The Traveler offered it to Clymene, who had it placed in the waters of the Soul Guide's Locus. Its fate after that is not stated.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Quest item received as a reward for Hyperion's Dirge (2.4) and needed for The Phaethons' Syrtos; the hidden exploration objective Sunchildren Hide and Seek opens after that quest. It has no use outside these quests.

## entry: The Rite of Solar Return
@keys: Rite of Solar Return, Solar Return
@keys2: Sunchild, Sunchildren, Clymene, Enkanomiya
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
Per Clymene, a Sunchild of a certain age was taken in a ceremony into the Dainichi Mikoshi on their birth date, to return to the "seat of the sun" before the mortal world could taint them. Only shreds of their souls came back to the Soul Guide's Locus. Aberaku ties it to a verse about riding Pegasus to the feast of the sun. The wiki's summary adds that the nobles used it so that no Sunchild would live long enough to see through their manipulation; the quests themselves are less explicit.

## entry: Clymene and the Seven Sunchildren

### LARGE
Clymene was a female official who tended the Sunchildren's daily lives within the Dainichi Mikoshi. Her request, in The Phaethons' Syrtos, was for the Traveler to lay a Sango Pearl at each of the seven royal tombs, and later to find the Divine Bridle. Her own character has its own entry; this file covers only the bridle's role in her release of the children's souls.
