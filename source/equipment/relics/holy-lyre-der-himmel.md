## summary
### LITE
The Holy Lyre der Himmel is Mondstadt's sacred lyre, said to be the instrument Barbatos, the Anemo Archon, once played. The Church of Favonius keeps it. In the Prologue the Fatui hide it, the Traveler retrieves it, and Dvalin's purified tears restore enough of its power for Venti to use it against the Stormterror's madness.
### STANDARD +
Its frame is rosewood carved with a pattern of flowing wind. By the time of the Prologue its Anemo power had run dry; restoring it takes Dvalin's own crystal tears, and it is damaged again in the final confrontation.
### LARGE +
Only the Prologue (1.0) features it. Its in-game description calls it a treasure of Barbatos whose Anemo power has dried up, and Jean later calls it a blessing from Lord Barbatos with the power to purify evil.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A lyre with a rosewood frame carved with flowing wind patterns. Venti notes the strings still feel cool to the touch. Its repaired and damaged states are separate quest items; the damaged lyre's description says its once-clear sound has become dull, ear-piercing noise.

## lore
### STANDARD
Mondstadt treats the Holy Lyre as Barbatos's relic. It is used in the Ludi Harpastum ceremony, and otherwise borrowing it requires signed documents from the Grand Master, the Seneschal and a Community Representative. In the Prologue the Fatui hide it in a ruin; the Traveler fights through to retrieve it. Back at Angel's Share, Venti greets it with obvious nostalgia and says that although it is genuine, a thousand years have drained its Anemo power. The Traveler uses a purified teardrop crystal of Dvalin on it, partly restoring it.
### LARGE +
Details of the repair. In State of the Holy Lyre der Himmel, Venti explains that the lyre itself is fine and the weakness is in the strings, whose Anemo intensity has faded. One purified tear stops it losing more power but does not refill it, so Jean mobilises the Knights to find more tears. Crystal Tears completes the repair after three teardrop crystals are purified. Venti then plays it to bring Dvalin back to himself; the lyre is broken during the final confrontation.
### ULTRA +
Ending Note (end of the Prologue). Venti, Jean and the Traveler take the wrecked lyre to the Cathedral to hand it back. Barbara, the deaconess, is horrified at its state. Venti casts an Anemo illusion so it appears repaired, then hints that it will not last and they should leave quickly. Barbara storms off with it, saying the Traveler and Venti will never touch it again. Outside, Signora ambushes Venti (see item.anemo-gnosis). Earlier, Venti told Diluc and the Traveler that the Fatui might be using Dvalin to draw out Barbatos, which prompted Diluc's wary question about why he thought so.

## owners
### STANDARD
Originally Barbatos's own instrument; held by the Church of Favonius in modern times. The Fatui held it briefly in the Prologue. It ends the Prologue in Barbara's custody.

## status
@version: 1.0
### LARGE
As of 7.1 the lyre has no further role in the main story. The last canon image of it is the illusion-repaired but actually broken lyre taken back by the Church. Treat its present condition and location as unspecified.

## roleplay
### LARGE
Roleplay interpretation (this project's recommendation, not canon text): the lyre is a symbol of Mondstadt's faith in an absent god. Most townsfolk revere it without knowing Venti is Barbatos. If Venti is in the scene he is fond of it and reluctant to explain why. Do not have it play or summon anything now, since its power is gone, and do not hand it out as ordinary loot.

## gameplay
### ULTRA
Gameplay (not literal lore): it appears only as a quest item in the Prologue Archon Quest chain, in four states (Holy Lyre, Partially Repaired, Completely Repaired, Damaged), and cannot be kept or used afterwards.

### STANDARD

It is badly damaged in the fight and returned to the Church of Favonius, where Venti covers the damage with an illusion.

### LARGE
Venti recognises the lyre by its carved rosewood and cool strings. Paimon scolds him for having once borrowed it to play for tavern drinkers; he answers with a laugh. He treats it as a friend's keepsake rather than a weapon, and says Dvalin used to listen to the wind's songs with him.
