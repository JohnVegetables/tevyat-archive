## summary
### LITE
Customs of Liyue is a three-volume book about Liyue's traditions, originally compiled by Fadhlan, a scholar from Sumeru living in Liyue, then edited by local scholars and published. It is described as one of Liyue's bestsellers. The volumes cover the wedding flower ball, the Rite of Descension in which the Geo Archon receives his people, and the cultural place of the Silk Flower and its perfumes.
### STANDARD +
Much of the commentary is the outsider's viewpoint of a Sumeru scholar, who contrasts Liyue's practical, prosperity-minded devotion with Sumeru's ecstatic scholar-sages and who declines in the last lines to guess at the Geo Archon's inner feelings.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Three short essays headed Flower Ball, Receiving God and Silk Flowers. The tone is that of a gently wry travel-writer's survey, mixing legend with practical observation, such as Millelith injury logs.

## lore
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
- Vol. 1, Flower Ball: at Liyue weddings the bride tosses a flower ball to the guests; catching it is said to bring a year of good luck. Wealthier families use silk balls, poorer families paper or cloth.
- Vol. 2, Receiving God: the Rite of Descension is the most iconic Liyue ceremony, at which the Geo Archon descends to deliver his proclamations. In the Qixing era a member of the Qixing hosts it.
- Vol. 3, Silk Flowers: the Silk Flower is used for silk and perfume. Rural legend credits an adeptus on Mt. Aocang with teaching mortals perfume making, and the flower is a symbol of Rex Lapis.
### STANDARD +
- Vol. 1 notes that the Millelith's security logs attribute roughly as many injuries per year to flower ball tossing as to monster attacks. It gives two possible origins: Mondstadt's Ludi Harpastum, or the Salt God Havria, a gentle god ousted in the Archon War and murdered by one of her own followers, said to have handed flowers to her people as comfort. Her remains are thought to lie in the ruins of Sal Terrae, and her followers may have spread the custom across Liyue.
- Vol. 2 says early farmers chose representatives to greet and see off the Geo Archon, who made predictions that set the year's work. After the Archon War, Liyue Harbor's merchants prized his predictions above ore, and travelled home to hear them. Only a Qixing member may host the rite. The author contrasts Liyue's worldly gains from divine guidance with the raving sages of Sumeru's rainforests, and notes that the Seven's paths can diverge.
- Vol. 3 says the Silk Flower is cultivated in a special strain for luxury perfume, offered first to Rex Lapis. An unwritten rule makes it impolite to ask a woman about her perfume, but an admirer who correctly identifies it tactfully may win her favour. Wild Silk Flowers have nearly vanished from rural areas because of mining and geographic change; the remaining few are tended by reclusive adepti.
### LARGE +
- Vol. 3 tells of the adeptus who appeared as a graceful illuminated bird to a young woman bathing in a spring and taught her to prepare fragrant oils, and says countless versions of the story exist. It also says the author once saw a Statue of the Seven accept a distinctly feminine offering, while declining to comment on the Archon's feelings.
- Merchants attribute their Silk Flower varieties to chance meetings with Rex Lapis or gifts from adepti, a sales habit the author calls tasteless.
### ULTRA +
- The wiki notes the flower ball custom echoes a real-world Zhuang tradition of throwing embroidered silk balls; this is a real-world parallel and not in-universe canon.
- Vol. 2's description of the Qixing as intermediaries is a pre-7.1 snapshot of the rite as written; compare current Liyue entries on how the rite is run now.

## owners
### STANDARD
No individual owner. Vol. 1 is on a shelf at Wanwen Bookhouse and is a reward from Soraya. Vol. 2 is on Ningguang's desk in the Jade Chamber and appears during Zhongli's story quest Sal Flore. Vol. 3 is sold by Jifang at Wanwen Bookhouse in Liyue Harbor.

## entry: Flower Ball Custom
@keys: flower ball, Liyue wedding flower ball
@keys2: Customs of Liyue, wedding
### STANDARD
At Liyue weddings the bride throws a flower ball; the guest who catches it is said to enjoy good luck for a year, whatever their situation. Wealthy families use silk-flower balls and poor families paper or cloth. Origin is disputed between Mondstadt's Ludi Harpastum and the followers of the ancient Salt God Havria. Millelith logs count many injuries from it.

## entry: Silk Flower Perfume
@keys: Silk Flower perfume, Silk Flower
@keys2: Customs of Liyue, perfume
### STANDARD
In Customs of Liyue Vol. 3 the Silk Flower is a Liyue luxury, used for silk and perfume. Perfume is a delicate social topic; guessing a woman's scent tactfully is a courting skill. Legend says an adeptus on Mt. Aocang taught the art, and the flower is one of Rex Lapis' symbols.
