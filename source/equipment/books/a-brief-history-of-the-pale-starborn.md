## summary
### LITE
A single-volume fae chronicle sold at Mimisbrunnr Books in Nasha Town, Nod-Krai (6.3). It records Emperor Monomakh's war that broke the Volkodlak king Miscislav, and Tsaritsa Anastasya's later takeover of the fae princes' power.
### STANDARD
Dated by Cycles, a fae calendar, and believed from its details to be written by fae elders, with scholarly footnotes. It covers two stretches: the 428th Cycle and the 799th Cycle.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 3-star book. The text is formal and archaic in cadence ("the Emperor dispatched envoys unto the fiefs of the Vila"), written as year-by-year entries, with bracketed numbers pointing to long explanatory footnotes. Some years are listed but left empty.

## lore
### LITE
In the 428th Cycle Miscislav of the Volkodlak demanded huge tribute from Emperor Monomakh, lost three battles and died; the Emperor became the Belyi Tsar. In the 799th Cycle Anastasya was crowned and the fae princes lost their rule.
### STANDARD
Monomakh's envoys told the Vila to stop paying Miscislav tribute. Miscislav demanded ten years of tribute from the Emperor, who refused. He attacked Kitezh in year seven and lost three battles; the Emperor killed him and two Grand Dukes, and the remaining clans accepted his authority. In the 799th Cycle the Tsaritsa used the Fatui to take the lands of rebellious princes and the Snezhnograd Veche to govern, and many fae gave up their titles and left.
### LARGE +
**The war (428th Cycle).** The Emperor's envoys put a pointed question to the Vila: to whom do you pay tribute? When they named Miscislav, the envoys said his tribute was "unlovely", a word a footnote explains can mean beneficial or endearing, so some scholars read it as a veiled threat. The Vila stopped paying. In year six Miscislav demanded ten years of tribute from the Emperor (the grain of 600 granaries, gold for 30 storehouses and enough slaves to carry it). The Emperor refused, declaring that giving in would only bring his people's children a greater war. In year seven Miscislav marched on Kitezh with the Miscislavli clans, the Nyholmgarthir, the Dalegrad, the Cirnareka and their retainers. Three battles were fought, at Kitezh, in the foothills, at the frozen lake and on the plains. The Emperor killed Miscislav and the Grand Dukes Skoldyryt and Vodyanoy; Grand Duke Seriviiki escaped. The Emperor had the fallen returned to their clans for burial, said they were not kings nor of sacred blood, and, as heir to the Pale Star and protector of all fae, was given authority by the remaining clans. From then on he called himself the Belyi Tsar.

**The Tsaritsa (799th Cycle).** In the fourth month of the first year Anastasya Feodorovna was crowned Tsaritsa of all Snezhnaya; many fae princes did not come to Snezhnograd to pay respects, and the Dalegrad clan broke ties because the Emperor had died. In year two the humans of Port Belovodye declared self-government and Grand Duke Drevogorye failed to put them down. In year three the Tsaritsa sent troops, took the lands of the Drevogorye, Volkhovan and Cirnareka clans, revoked their hereditary princely titles and replaced them with non-hereditary officials. She did this with a new force, the Fatui, not the Druzhna that Monomakh had raised. Drevogorye accused her of treason and died fighting the Fatui. She then calmed Port Belovodye by diplomacy, asking only for the instigators, and demanded all authority from the remaining princes in exchange for honorary titles; most acceded. In year four she had the Fatui build a fortress near Snezhnograd named Glupov, "the city of fools". In year six she founded the Snezhnograd Veche to run administration, with the human Ioanna Ivanovna as first chair. In year seven the Imperial Order Reformation Edict was published, and many fae, grateful for her mercy, gave up their titles and left Snezhnaya.
### ULTRA +
**The footnotes.** They are where most of the book's depth lies.
- Kitezh (note 127) was the first city in Snezhnaya, founded in the fourth year of the 417th Cycle and the seat of power until the Emperor, "pardoning humanity", moved his court to Snezhnograd.
- Miscislav (128) is called the Blood-Devourer in The Volkhov Chronicles and was known to fae chroniclers as Gorynishche, "Son of the Mountain"; Volkodlak accounts describe a hulking figure that stood out on any battlefield. The footnote on the war (130) says his accusations were preposterous but not unreasoned: he feared the Pale Star court's rapid rise in economy and government since the previous conflict.
- The clans (131-134): Nyholmgarthir were bovine-aspected Volkodlak, Cirnareka wolf-like Volkodlak, Dalegrad a clan of water imps.
- Miscislav's death (135): legend says that in despair he tried to use the power of the Shadow Realm to delay the Pale Star advance. It failed but influenced the Emperor's later decisions. The Schpekin Legion, ordered to stop the imperial forces uniting, was misled by an unexplained icy fog and missed its chance. His bear-shaped skull, large as a chariot and marked by the spear, is said to be in the Zapolyarny Palace Treasury.
- Seriviiki (136), the Gray Wolf, ruled the Cirnareka, reportedly dealt secretly with the Pale Star finance minister Porfiry early in the war and was the only fae Grand Duke to slip straight into the court's ministers afterwards. Some scholars suspect the Schpekin Legion's failure was no accident, but the note says the evidence is insufficient. The Emperor honored him with the ceremonial sword tap.
- Skoldyryt (137), the Deepwood Bull, proposed a vanguard flanking raid. The fae infiltrator Filipov, a master of transformation loyal to the Emperor, seized the plan; the Emperor adapted and encircled him, and relief columns were destroyed. This decided the second battle.
- Vodyanoy (138), "the Lackland", ruler of Dalegrad and forebear of the waterborn of the frozen lake, was lured beyond the lake by the Domovoy Grand Duke Alvis, then killed.
- The spear (139): readers doubt a commander could duel in such a melee. The note says he did not duel; the spear was thrown like an arrow with unnatural force across the field.
- Sacred blood (140): the note says its meaning is unknown, observes that the Emperor, claiming to be lord of all fae, should have shared their blood, and infers the phrase was legitimacy-building.

**Voice and reliability.** The book is a fae and Pale Star account. It praises the Emperor's mercy at length, treats the Tsaritsa's takeover as lawful, and attributes the princes' departure to gratitude. That the Fatui conquered the princes' lands, that Port Belovodye was spared only after the Tsaritsa's envoys made demands, and that "many would pass on before they could make their replies" are recorded in the same breath, so a reader can see the harder facts under the polish. It pairs with General History of Snezhnaya, sold in Snezhnograd, which covers the Emperor's youth and companions; this book picks up at his wars and the later end of fae rule.

**Wiki dating note.** The wiki calculates from the dates that a Cycle is seven years, and, from the Belyi Tsar's death occurring around the Cataclysm in other texts, estimates the calendar began about 6,100 years before the present and the Belyi Tsar's coronation about 3,100 years before the game, early in the Archon War. This is the wiki's deduction, not an in-text statement; treat as inferred.

## entry: Miscislav's war with the Pale Star court
@keys: Miscislav, Gorynishche, Blood-Devourer, Miscislavli
@version: 6.3
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In the book's account, King Miscislav, ruler of the Volkodlak alliance (the Miscislavli), provoked war in the 428th Cycle by demanding ten years of tribute from Emperor Monomakh, then lost three battles around Kitezh and was killed by the Emperor's thrown spear. Fae chroniclers called him Gorynishche, "Son of the Mountain"; The Volkhov Chronicles call him the Blood-Devourer. Footnotes say he feared the Pale Star court's rise and, in the last battle, tried to use the Shadow Realm to delay it, without success. Grand Dukes Skoldyryt (Nyholmgarthir) and Vodyanoy (Dalegrad) died with him; Seriviiki (Cirnareka) escaped and later joined the court's ministers.
### LARGE +
The three battles were at Kitezh, in the foothills, at the frozen lake, and on the plains; the book refers readers to "A History of the Snezhnyi Expedition" for detail. Filipov's seized battle plan and Alvis's lure of Vodyanoy across the lake decided the second campaign. The Schpekin Legion's misdirection by icy fog is called a mystery.

## entry: Anastasya's consolidation of the north (Pale Starborn account)
@keys: Imperial Order Reformation Edict, Drevogorye, Glupov
@version: 6.3
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In the 799th Cycle's first seven years, the book says Tsaritsa Anastasya took the north from the fae princes: she crushed the Drevogorye, Volkhovan and Cirnareka holdings with the Fatui, accepted Port Belovodye's surrender on terms, had Glupov built, set up the Snezhnograd Veche under the human Ioanna Ivanovna, and published the Imperial Order Reformation Edict, after which many fae gave up their titles and left Snezhnaya.
### LARGE +
She required each surviving prince to surrender all authority while keeping honorary aristocratic titles. Grand Duke Drevogorye accused her of treason and died when he met the Fatui in battle. The text presents the exodus of the fae as voluntary and grateful.
