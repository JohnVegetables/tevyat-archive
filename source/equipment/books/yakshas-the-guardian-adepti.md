## summary
### LITE
Yakshas: The Guardian Adepti is a single book from the folklore series Hidden Wonders and Folklore of Liyue, Liyue's answer to the Sumeru scholar Masudi's encyclopedia Tour of the Land of Liyue. It was largely dismissed by publishers and readers for its archaic style. It tells how Rex Lapis summoned yakshas to purge daemons, how the five strongest were afflicted, and how only Alatus survived. It is given to the player in the story quest Glimmers of the Vigilant Yaksha (Version 1.3).
### STANDARD +
The book mixes an old-fashioned classical retelling with a plain-language gloss after each passage, a format that lets a reader see both the mythic voice and the plain meaning.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Paragraphs in an archaic, solemn style alternate with plain-language paraphrases in quotation marks. This makes the book a short folklore treatise rather than a story.

## lore
@spoiler: major
@version: 1.3
### LITE
In ancient Liyue plagues and monsters arose from the bitterness of defeated gods, called daemons. Rex Lapis summoned yakshas, ferocious illuminated beasts, to purge them. Five were strongest: Bosacius, Indarias, Bonanus, Menogias and Alatus. Over about a thousand years the yakshas were poisoned by the lingering divine bitterness; three of the five died, one vanished, and only Alatus survived, remembered as the Golden-Winged King and Conqueror of Demons.
### STANDARD +
- The author's account: warring gods left the defeated crushed under rock, where they returned to the cycle of elements, but some held their hatred and re-emerged as daemons whose rage produced plague, miasma and monsters. The line "the daemonic is the shadow of the divine" is the book's key phrase.
- The yakshas are described as fierce, violent illuminated beasts who followed Rex Lapis in battle and spared no brutality in defending his rule, until the evil was wiped out at its source.
- Their downfall: some sank into blind rage or fear-driven madness, some turned on and killed one another, and others were lost to delusions. After a thousand years, three of the five were slain, one disappeared, and many lesser yakshas died or fled.
- Of Alatus, nobody knows where he came from or went. During the Lantern Rite people watch for a glow above Guyun Stone Forest and say the Vigilant Yaksha ends the dominion of daemons, and some hear a flute from Dihua Marsh and say the yaksha calls his old friends home.
- The closing paragraph says the price of supernatural power is hardship and eventual destruction: the yaksha lost all companions and incurred a karmic debt, leaving a heart full of darkness. It compares his torment to that of a preta, never sated across countless lifetimes.
### LARGE +
- The book never names which of the five disappeared or fell, but it fits with other quests and with the entries for Bosacius, Indarias, Bonanus and Menogias. Use those entries and the Alatus chapter for specifics.
- The text uses both Alatus' titles, the Golden-Winged King and the Conqueror of Demons, and the folk custom at Lantern Rite that connects him to Guyun Stone Forest.
### ULTRA +
- The book's final passage speaks of the yaksha in the singular, and a reader who knows Xiao's story will read it as a description of Alatus. The text itself does not say so; the identity is established in Xiao's story quest and character material.
- The book's own framing of the cause is divine bitterness and a karmic debt built up by violence. It is one folklorist's account and not a complete theory of Liyue's monsters.
