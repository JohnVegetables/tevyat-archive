## summary
### LITE
The Fall of the Faded Castle is a three-volume poem of Fontaine by an unknown author. It describes a nightmare black ship, a gilded castle by a lake where a fearful master held endless revels, and the night a dark-robed spirit destroyed it. It reads as a ghost story about a debt coming due.
### STANDARD
The poem is anonymous and names no one. Its images (a castle by the purest lake, a master haunted by midnight, a dark ancient world, a blood moon) match the story of the Faded Castle in the Nostoi Region, but the book itself never says so. Treat it as an in-world ominous poem whose literal truth is not confirmed.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 3-star book collection of three volumes. Vol. 1 lies inside The Rusty Rudder next to the entrance. Vols. 2 and 3 are sold by Hubel. The cover title is written in the Fontaine Script.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1: in a dream the narrator sees a black ship sailing through night and fog, its hull rotten and mast shattered but its pieces bound together with algae and seaweed into a shape no one can look at without panic. A tall, slim figure in dark robes stands on deck with cold, piercing eyes. The narrator asks whether it is a sea monster, a lord returned from an ancient world, or the nightmare itself.
### STANDARD
Vol. 2: by the shore of the world's purest lake stood a glittering castle, with gold leaf and gems on its walls, banners, towers, colourful windows and odd lifelike statues. A golden stage held musicians who played day and night for the castle's master, and guests of every class and homeland danced and drank. Nothing eased the master's fear. Each time the tower clock struck midnight he was gripped by panic, sure that the dark-robed figure would come to collect a debt and turn everything, including his guests' souls and his own, to dust as tribute to a dark ancient world.

Vol. 3: the castle falls quiet, with sounds of animals chewing and a rasping laugh from an empty room. A glowing red fog and storm wrap the castle, a tall thin figure in black robes appears on the central stage, and guests fall one by one without time to scream. The master's soul is taken on his throne. The castle collapses into the waves and the blood moon its towers had hidden reappears. Today the water reflects the ruin, whose statues look like monsters, and only darkness, death and loss remain.
### LARGE +
The three volumes form an arc: a dream image (Vol. 1), the castle at its height under dread (Vol. 2), and its destruction and aftermath (Vol. 3). The debt is never explained and the poem does not say who incurred it or why. Guests of any class or homeland were welcome, and their deaths come as part of the master's reckoning. The wiki's links tie the master to Boethius, the "swarming bees" to Phobos and the ancient world to Remuria, but the text itself is only imagery.

### ULTRA +
Placement notes: the three volumes are split between The Rusty Rudder and Hubel's stall, so a reader must visit both to hold the full poem. The description warns of a strange, ominous aura, so characters should treat it as unsettling verse of unknown origin rather than a documented account, and should not state the Faded Castle connection as established fact.

### STANDARD
In the poem The Fall of the Faded Castle a tall, slim figure in dark robes appears twice: on a rotten black ship in a dream, and on the castle's stage at midnight to collect a debt and take every soul in the hall. Its nature is left open by the text. Identifying it with the Faded Castle's real fall is an inference.
