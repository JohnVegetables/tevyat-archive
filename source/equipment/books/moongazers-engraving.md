## summary
### LITE
A two-volume set of stone tablets on the Frost Moon (6.7) recording the Stellar Blackbody's message: a warning that pitch-black annihilation crosses the universe, and a plea to keep a light burning.
### STANDARD
Vol. 1 is the dragon researcher Lusalim's digest; Vol. 2 is Nin-Kiggia's annotated translation of the first message, the lament of the immortal Ysna'qurel. The script is unreadable, yet readers understand it in their own tongue.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Both volumes are described as an ancient stone tablet found on the moon, with writing that is completely indecipherable but understood by anyone who reads it. Vol. 1 reads as a plain, hopeful letter; Vol. 2 as a long epic poem in the third person, with repeated formulae and untranslatable words in brackets.

## lore
### LITE
The message warns, gently, of pitch-black annihilation crossing the universe and says the listener is not alone.
### STANDARD
Vol. 1 is addressed to a friend from a distant world and urges them to beware. Vol. 2 tells of Ysna'qurel, ruler of Throgg, who learns of a Dark Destroyer and sends a last messenger to warn other worlds, despite the cost.
### LARGE +
Vol. 1 is addressed to a "friend from a distant world". The senders do not know if they will be heard, and say that if someone does hear, they were caught by some exceptional gravitational force. They warn that pitch-black annihilation, the mortal foe of all life, crosses the universe and may have reached the reader's era already, and offer their message as a small light: one ray is feeble, but while another shines out before it fades, a ray will one day pierce the night like dawn. Lusalim adds a private note to his "daughter" Ninnin (Nin-Kiggia) that the key help the moon goddesses gave their plans lies at the lunar core, reachable through fissures.

**Volume 1.** Lusalim's note says the text records the message conveyed by the Stellar Blackbody. The message is gentle in tone: it says the cold silence of a lonely universe may be eased by hearing others, that the receivers "are not alone in this vast star-filled cosmos", and it urges them to be on their guard against the darkness without knowing where they are. The final line is a hope that its light will "embolden" the reader. Lusalim's addendum to Nin-Kiggia points her to Moonheart Gap, which he calls the lunar core.

**Volume 2.** Nin-Kiggia notes that 3,655 earlier lines tell how an immortal, Ysna'qurel, defeated seven monsters (or perhaps compatriots) and brought the world under the empire's rule. The passage translated then turns: Ysna'qurel hears from another land of a "Dark Destroyer" and an impending decline, and dispatches a last messenger to warn other worlds. An assembly of rulers or kin ("the [...]", which may mean brothers, friends, rulers and servants at once) answer that sending a messenger will not save them and will draw the Destroyer's notice. Ysna'qurel, "child of Ghel'vur, Ruler of Throgg", replies that all that dies shall die and even gods must submit to that law, and that they will send the messenger, who is "nimble as Fnost" and swift as T'rinqu'oss. Ysna'qurel admits to greater fear than all of them together, and weeps for what will never be again (a partner's embrace, tales sung to the young). The text sets out the dilemma: sending the warning will make the Destroyer notice the world, and withholding it would also bring death. The decision: to warn other worlds so they may be ready. The closing lines, spoken by the dead warriors, ask the listener to "know that you are not alone" and speak of a dim light which, once heard, can be kindled anew. The final line is "We are here, looking up at the starry sky."
### ULTRA +
**Translator's notes (Vol. 2).** Nin-Kiggia's notes are the interesting layer. She explains that the species seems to have framed whatever it wished to say as long third-person narrative poems, which is why a short message is so verbose. Unknown words are glossed from context. Pairs like "merciful and good" and "zealous for war and destruction" appear to be one word. The proverb about Olmegratheen racing through flame and the third mud-candle is quoted as-is and its meaning is described as wholly obscure. A stretch of 137 lines is only a roster of names and titles, and 32 lines are skipped because they are close to a line already translated but harder to parse. She notes that the word used for light is a verb of hearing ("listen... this is our light"). Her final note refuses to claim that the Blackbody's civilization was Ysna'qurel's empire.

**The Blackbody (wiki context).** The Stellar Blackbody wiki page says it is an extraterrestrial lifeform found in Project Moongazer and an information-bearing organism that communicates by vibration, from which black filamentous "blackbody matter" was extracted and used in Project Lunar Veil. It also says the dragons took the warning as the model for their own transmissions into space, one of which reached the Traveler and their sibling, who replied that they wished to visit Teyvat. These are wiki-level syntheses of quest and interactable text; use with care.

**Reading the two volumes together.** Vol. 1 is a digest in which Lusalim has swapped unintelligible terms for ones he could understand, so it should be read as his rendering. Vol. 2 is closer to the original and reveals a more tragic frame: a civilization whose ruler chose to warn others although doing so would hasten its own end. Both converge on one message: you are not alone, be watchful, and keep a light burning. The father-daughter note at the end of Vol. 1 ties the Moongazer project to Nin-Kiggia, who later annotates Vol. 2, so the two volumes are also a handover between researchers.

**Boundaries.** Canon does not say who wrote the tablets, how old they are, or whether the senders survived. "Pitch-black annihilation" is linked by the wiki to the Abyss in its links, but the text itself uses only the words "pitch-black annihilation" and "Dark Destroyer"; do not state more than that.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner. Vol. 1 is credited to Lusalim and Vol. 2 to Nin-Kiggia; both are long dead as of the cutoff (the wiki lists both as deceased).

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Game mechanics: a 2-volume Book Collection on the Frost Moon. Vol. 1 is obtained during the quest Project Moongazer (Part 1); Vol. 2 is picked up from an Edict Module in Lusalim's Laboratory.

## entry: The Stellar Blackbody's message

### STANDARD
A warning carried across space and recorded on stone tablets found on the Frost Moon: pitch-black annihilation is crossing the universe, other worlds should be watchful, and a light kindled by one civilization can embolden another. Lusalim's digest and Nin-Kiggia's verbatim translation survive. Whether the original senders survived is unknown.
### LARGE +
The Stellar Blackbody is, per the wiki, an information-bearing lifeform discovered during the Moongazer expedition that speaks by vibrations. Its relation to the empire in Vol. 2 is not confirmed by Nin-Kiggia.

### STANDARD
In Vol. 2 of Moongazer's Engraving, Ysna'qurel is an immortal, "child of Ghel'vur" and ruler of Throgg, who united the world under an empire after defeating seven monsters or compatriots. When they learn of the coming "Dark Destroyer", they choose to send a final messenger to warn other worlds, against the assembly's objection, and weep for what they will lose. Their sex, if any, is unknown.

### STANDARD
Nin-Kiggia ("Ninnin") annotates Vol. 2 as an artificial, draconic Moongazer raised by Lusalim as his daughter. Her notes stay careful: she marks unreadable words, summarizes omitted line ranges, and declines to say whether the message's authors were Ysna'qurel's own empire.
