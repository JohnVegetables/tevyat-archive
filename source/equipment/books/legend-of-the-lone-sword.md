## summary
### LITE
Legend of the Lone Sword is a four-volume martial arts novel that sells well in Liyue Harbor. Its blurb stresses that the story takes place in a world without elemental energy or alchemy, so swordsmanship is purely physical skill and nerve.
### STANDARD +
It follows Jin the Seventy-Second, last survivor of a 72-member sword school, as he hunts the swordsman who destroyed it. It is fiction, not Liyue history.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Each volume is a short book with a one-line in-game blurb. Vol. 1 is at Wanwen Bookhouse (and in front of Verr Goldet at Wangshu Inn), Vol. 2 on a table behind a vegetable stand in Qingce Village, Vol. 3 on Su Er'niang's stall in Liyue Harbor, and Vol. 4 is sold by Jifang at Wanwen Bookhouse.

## lore
### STANDARD
- Vol. 1: Three days after his master and junior were killed in the snow, a starving, wounded man walks home in the rain. He calls himself Jin the Seventy-Second because he is the last of 72. He kills a cart driver who answers him with riddles, and rides the cart to Tupi Village.
- Vol. 2: At Tupi Village, a feared chief with red eyes awaits him. Jin, bound by no school or sect, slaughters the chief's followers, counting exactly 362 men and one gate dog, then enters the mansion.
- Vol. 3: Jin duels the chief. The text says the world has no elements, so the dead leave no lingering memories and swordsmanship cannot be elemental. Jin wins through "heart" and an incense burner held in his empty hand, and learns his enemy is in the barren mountains beyond.
- Vol. 4: Wounded, Jin crosses the barren mountains, which legend says were carved by the Celestial Emperor's blade and made salty by the Earth Mother's tears. Two watchers, an old crone and a stout old man, debate attacking him. He collapses and glimpses azure-blue skirts.
### LARGE +
Details. "Tupi" is said to mean burning away vanity's dross to find the gem of truth. The mines in the mountains were ruined by an earthquake, and bandits and beasts now haunt the caverns. The chief's last words point Jin onward.
### ULTRA +
The tale's recurring contrasts: Jin hates needlessly talkative people; the chief's blade is faster but his "heart" slower; the watchers in Vol. 4 weigh Jin's 362 kills against his wounds before leaving him to the mountain. The wiki lists the Chinese title as Huangshan gu jian lu, roughly "Lone Sword of the Barren Mountain", which fits the setting of Vol. 4.
