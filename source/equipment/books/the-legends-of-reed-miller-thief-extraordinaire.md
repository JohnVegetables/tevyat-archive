## summary
### LITE
The Legends of Reed Miller, Thief Extraordinaire is a three-volume anthology of pulp stories about the master thief Reed Miller, sold by Dhabit at Mimisbrunnr Books in Nasha Town and described as wildly popular across Nod-Krai. The description warns that the stories are collated from various works and authors, and that their truth is as dubious as the paper.
### STANDARD
Each volume is a different kind of tale: a serial caper in an inexpensive weekly magazine, a romance set around the governor's palace, and a cautionary adventure about a sailor on Miller's ship. All depict Miller as a gifted thief and master of disguise who steals from the corrupt.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 3-star book collection of three volumes, all sold by Dhabit at Mimisbrunnr Books in Nasha Town. Vol. 1 reproduces a cheap magazine serial and has its middle section torn out.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1: An issue of a ten-Mora magazine serial in which Miller plans to steal the Frostmoon relic, the Sampo Mill, from the governor's palace in Piramida. Chapter IV has the rival thief Wagtail confront him; in Chapter XVII a disguised Miller has the governor arrested and returns the treasure to the Frostmoon Scions.
### STANDARD
Vol. 1 details: the editor's note says the serial dwells on the young women pursuing Miller. In Chapter IV, Wagtail, known as "A" to a few, fails to steal his heart. In Chapter XVII Miller says he keeps nothing, and Wagtail remarks that he has stolen the heart of the Frost Moon's daughter.

Vol. 2: A romance about the governor's adopted daughter, Leonita Prokhorovna Trubetskoy, who is nearly of age to be betrothed. Miller announces in the paper that her father Lord Trubetskoy's treasure will be his. Disguised as a footman, he and fellow Treasure Hoarders prepare, after working out that the Sampo Mill, a relic said to produce endless riches, is in the palace. At her birthday ball Leonita learns that she, not the governor, is the true source of the wealth, and that her "treatments" were rituals. Miller reveals himself and says that she, not the Mill, is the treasure he came for. The narrator ends by saying she left to travel the world with him.

Vol. 3: Zima, a young man from a village near Snezhnograd, leaves Snezhnaya to avoid a predictable fate after seeing a friend turned into a statue by a Snegurochka. He joins Miller's ship, expecting adventure, and finds Miller careful and cautious about monsters. A siren demands a sacrifice. When tossed Mora fails to pay, Miller puts a scimitar to Zima's throat. Zima realises that his flight from patterns was only another pattern.
### LARGE +
Vol. 1 frames Miller as a defender of the poor, saying the treasures of the governor were taken from the people. The editor's note says the serial has the popular appeal that sells newspapers. In Vol. 2, the newspaper carrying Miller's notice sells out, and the whole region awaits the clash between thief and governor. Guests whisper that Leonita is human and the lord is not, which is the clue that leads her to the truth. Vol. 3 also recounts a rumour that Miller was captured and taken to Snezhnograd to be hanged, then escaped by emptying the vault and leaving a message in gold dust, a trick that made him a tavern legend.
### ULTRA +
The wiki trivia notes that Vol. 2 is a gender-swapped version of a real historical episode involving Brahe and Alia, with the names changed. Zima's name is a Slavic word for winter, and the text states that nothing about him survives in any log or epic. The silvery light and the diamond-producing rituals in Vol. 2 are as far as the book goes in describing the Mill's mechanism.

### STANDARD
In The Legends of Reed Miller the Sampo Mill is a relic said to generate endless riches, stolen from the Frostmoon Scions and hidden in the governor's palace. Vol. 1 has Miller return it, and Vol. 2 claims it works through the silvery-white power of the governor's adopted daughter. These details come from pulp fiction and are not confirmed canon.
