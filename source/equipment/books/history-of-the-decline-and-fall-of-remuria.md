## summary
### LITE
The History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria is a three-volume book of Fontaine, said to be the work of the great scholar Pulteney, written in the early years of Fontaine's founding. It tells how King Remus raised the empire of Remuria, how it was destroyed, and how Egeria afterwards brought order to the tribes. Each volume ends on the saying that oceans will rise, empires will fall, and the only constant is change.
### STANDARD
The author is a Fontainian historian under the first Hydro Archon, Egeria. He claims to reconstruct Remuria's true shape with "the universal rationality of history" and distrusts the folklore around it, including the claims of the Golden Troupe, who say they are Remuria's descendants. The tone is pro-Egeria and moralising: Remuria fell because its people abandoned their duties for hedonism and because Remus tried to hand divine authority to humans.
### LARGE +
It is the most direct in-world overview of Remuria that a Fontainian can read, and it contrasts with the satirical Anecdota Septentrionalis and the knightly La Chanson d'Erinnyes, which show Remuria from its own people and from its enemies.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 4-star book collection of three volumes. The cover title reads FALL OF REMURIA in the Fontaine script. In the game, Vol. 1 and Vol. 3 are sold by the NPC Hubel in the Quartier Narbonnais of the Court of Fontaine, and Vol. 2 is found in the Palais Mermonia.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1 opens with the legend that the ocean gave birth to everything and also devoured it. Heaven drowned the early peoples for turning from their fate, and the survivors lived like animals until the great king Remus descended on Meropis in his golden ship Fortuna, taught farming, built cities and temples, and spread music and art.
### STANDARD +
Heaven's first-era envoys had guided people to peace until people tired of it; Heaven then sent huge waves and a hundred days of rain. With an immortal fleet Remus conquered all the islands on the high waters, and even the great dragon beneath the abyssal depths submitted. Seers prophesied that the greatest empire would meet utter destruction, and that this is Fortuna. In answer Remus composed a symphony of prosperity from the seven-day rotation and the flowing winds, believing that if every city echoed it, they would escape fate's judgment.

### LARGE +
Vol. 2 describes the Eternal City as it was. Ships passed the Ocean Pillars along the royal fairway to a giant port, where the imperial ship Fortuna lay and a lofty tower guided ships by bell. The fairway led to Machimos, home of Remuria's warriors, and on to Capitolium, the centre of the empire, a paradise for artists where only top intellectuals and musicians were admitted and a golden palace with copper pillars stood. Remus listened to every note of the empire and corrected any discord with a pluck of his strings. He shared power with four capable humans, the Harmosts, to eliminate discord, and built fairways to carry his melody outward. The author counts Remus's worst sin as passing power reserved for the divine to humans, which brought corruption, decadence, violence and revolt.

Vol. 3 describes the end: the earth collapsed at once, the tower was uprooted and drowned in titanic waves, and the city with its people and its golden palace fell into a chasm of eternal darkness. When the royal city collapsed and the tribes were on the edge of barbarism, Egeria, the Noble Navigator, settled the conflicts among the tribes, and new cities were founded around the flowing springs under law.
In Vol. 3 the author says that the catastrophe was so swift that the researchers and records able to pass the knowledge on sank with the city, and that the Golden Troupe's "mysterious researchers" deliberately distorted what remained. He accepts that Remurians really made terrifying golems, which are still found ruined in the wild, but blames this on their moral decline. He objects to folklore that paints the greatest scholars of the age as soul-stealers who bound mortal spirits into golems.

Two readings matter for roleplay. The author states that the tragedy of Remuria occurred "merely a century ago", which is not consistent with other Fontaine material. And he calls the fall just punishment, a view that reflects Fontaine's official, Egeria-centred historiography.
### ULTRA +
Details worth keeping straight: the book names Gurabad in the far south and a noble kindred of the east (linked by the wiki to Chenyu Vale) as other powers that rose while Fontaine's ancestors were in ignorance. The golden ship Fortuna is linked by the wiki to the Domus Aurea. The Harmosts are four humans, and the book never names them individually. Its refrain about oceans rising and empires falling closes every volume.

## entry: Pulteney

### STANDARD
Pulteney is the great scholar credited in-game as the author of The History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria, written in the early years of Fontaine's founding. Nothing else about him is established. He frames his work as an effort to extract Remuria's true shape with the universal rationality of history, in contrast to the legends and the Golden Troupe's distortions.

## entry: Remus in Pulteney's history
@keys: Remus, Sebastos, God King, King Remus
@keys2: Remuria, Pulteney, Fortuna
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In Pulteney's account, Remus is the great king who descended on Meropis in his golden Fortuna, taught farming, building, music and art, conquered the islands of the high waters, and built a symphony of prosperity to escape the prophesied doom. He is called universal Harmost, immortal ruler and God King. The author judges his gravest sin to be the delegating of divine power to humans.
### LARGE +
The Anecdota Septentrionalis calls him Sebastos, and La Chanson d'Erinnyes shows his emissary demanding water and earth from Erinnyes.

## entry: The Harmosts of Remuria
@keys: Harmost, Harmosts
@keys2: Remuria, Remus, Capitolium
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Remus promoted four humans of great capability to be Harmosts, partners in governing every city and removing discordant sounds. Pulteney blames this delegation for the corruption and revolts that ended Remuria. The book does not name them; other sources identify Boethius and Cassiodor among them.

## entry: The Eternal City's layout
@keys: Capitolium, Machimos, Portus Anticus, Ocean Pillars

### STANDARD
Vol. 2 describes the approach to the capital: the Ocean Pillars, the royal fairway, the tower with its bell that guided ships between islands, the port where Fortuna lay, the warrior city of Machimos with temples and arenas of carved rock, and finally Capitolium with its theaters and the golden palace of copper pillars. Only the best intellectuals and musicians could live in Capitolium.

## entry: The Remurian golems in Pulteney's history
@keys: Praetorian Golem, Remurian golems

### STANDARD
Pulteney admits that the Remurians did build terrifying golems, whose ruins can still be found in the wild. He rejects the folk story that the greatest ancient scholars trapped mortal spirits in them as magisters, and attributes the golems to the moral decay of Remuria's last days.
