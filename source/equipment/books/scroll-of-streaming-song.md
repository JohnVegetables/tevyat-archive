## summary
### LITE
Scroll of Streaming Song is a three-volume set of torn pages from desert ruins, kept in the House of Daena. It tells how the Mistress of Pushpavatika visits a wise princess and tests her with three riddles. Scholars have added notes on its translation and gaps.
### STANDARD +
The pages were retrieved by Vahumana researchers, and Haravatat and Vahumana herbads argue in the margins about what the text means. In the end the two rulers ally. The in-game description says the author is unconfirmed.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Torn pages in verse-like lines, interrupted by the scholars' bracketed remarks. Missing words appear as (...) and some guesses are marked with question marks.

## lore
### STANDARD
Vol. 1: the Mistress of Pushpavatika, who calls herself a spirit created at the beginning, goes to the princess's land to test her wisdom. The princess is surrounded by attendants in a cloth no one has seen before, like stars around one moon. The Mistress offers spices, gold and gems if the princess can answer three riddles; the princess replies that knowledge is worth more.

Vol. 2: the first riddle (cold as the dead while alive, warm in death) is answered with the rose; the second (rises from earth, descends from the sky, unseen yet sees all, runs only top to bottom) with the universal law made in heaven, which only worship and not imitation may meet. Her warning is that forbidden arts bring calamity at the edge of divine knowledge.
### LARGE +
Vol. 3: the third riddle asks what cannot stop arrows but stops destruction, cannot break armour but destroys cities, and cannot be overcome by gods or demons. The princess names endless wisdom, but most of her answer is lost to gaps. The Mistress, utterly impressed, praises her kin, and the two princesses form an alliance together with a king whose epithet is damaged (Blood? Resentment? Red Crown?). Together they lead the people of a city whose name is lost in the damage (the wiki links the gap to Ay-Khanoum) to eternal prosperity.
### ULTRA +
Margin notes: Tafazzoli says the word translated as silk should read as a textile no one had seen, so as not to suggest Liyue's silk, that the "grand palace" is a land where the divine is present, and that a place-word could mean farmland or cemetery; Yarshater dryly says they are now more confused about whom the author describes. On the lost words in the third answer, Tafazzoli offers "Mine (Ours)," "Eternity" or "All", a possible person's name or "preserved remains", and "Return", "Death" or "Rebirth".

## identity_reading
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The text names no one. The wiki's reading is that the Mistress of Pushpavatika is the Goddess of Flowers (Nabu Malikata), the princess the Greater Lord Rukkhadevata and the red king King Deshret, and that the princess's phrase "owner of yesterday and master of tomorrow morning" alludes to her link with Irminsul. This is inference. In A Divergence Leading to Oneself (6.6), lines from the Goddess of Flowers' song, including "a spirit created at the beginning," are shown to calm Jinn when Collei recites them; Baladhuri notes few records of the song survive. Treat the scroll as folklore about a meeting of rulers, with the identifications offered, not proved, by later material.

## owners
### LARGE
No owner is named. The pages were found in desert ruins and are kept by researchers of the Akademiya's Vahumana, with notes from Haravatat.

## entry: Mistress of Pushpavatika
@keys: Mistress of Pushpavatika, Pushpavatika
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Mistress of Pushpavatika is the speaker of the Scroll of Streaming Song: a spirit who says she was created at the beginning and is a flickering illusion and the shimmering light from the creator's eyes. She tests a wise princess with three riddles. Pushpavatika is a Sanskrit-derived word for a flower garden. The wiki connects her to the Goddess of Flowers, but the scroll does not name her.
