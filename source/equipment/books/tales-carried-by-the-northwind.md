## summary
### LITE
Tales Carried by the Northwind is a single-volume book left on a box near Kofitse in Piramida. Its in-game description calls it a poetry collection chronicling the deeds of successive Starshynas, mostly lost in past calamities, with only a small fraction of the original fragments recovered. The surviving poems are attributed to Silvestr Petrovich Solovei and Sergei Semyonovich Mayakov.
### STANDARD
It reads as a memorial anthology. Its fragments are elegies and heroic verse about leaders who held the title of Starshyna, and about the cost of leadership to those who followed them.

## lore
### LITE
Silvestr Petrovich Solovei: two excerpted poems. In the first, a dying speaker thanks a listener, admits he trusted the nobles' deceptive words, and mourns comrades, homeland and loved ones resting in the night, then resolves to burn briefly as a beacon on the snowy borderlands. In the second, a Nightingale forges the first light on a summit against a dark tide of monsters, and the poem closes on generations, thunder, pine, tide and wind, who inherit the Starshyna name.
### STANDARD
Sergei Semyonovich Mayakov: one poem. An old man with a weapon stands against towering black waves while a voice in the mist reproaches him for the deaths of young men who trusted him, the orphans he adopted, the unsent distress signal and the fallen Lighthouse. He turns to the ocean and polishes his axe once more.
### LARGE +
The anthology is incomplete by design: the excerpts are numbered III, VI and VIII, with ellipses where material is missing. They belong to different voices. Solovei's work mixes personal regret with a call to keep a beacon alight, and Mayakov's poem presents a leader's self-reproach after a disaster.
### ULTRA +
The Solovei excerpts are numbered VI and VIII and the Mayakov poem III, so the numbering does not run in order of the surviving text. Poem VIII ends by naming the generations that inherited the Starshyna name, and the Mayakov poem is the only fragment that mentions adopted orphans. The book gives no dates for any of the poems.
