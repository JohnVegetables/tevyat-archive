## summary
### LITE
A one-volume Natlan folk tale, once told orally, obtained in the quest To Wish Upon a Star. Star-child Qoyllor falls to earth and Ukuku, a silent hunter, helps her search for her lost power.
### STANDARD
The in-game description calls it a long-standing tale with no knowable original author. It is the first part of a pair; its sequel is The Tale of Kuntur, about their son. The narrator addresses the listener directly in the manner of a storyteller.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A one-volume Book Collection. The wiki records no quality rating for it. It is obtained by talking to Orqo during To Wish Upon a Star.

## lore
### LITE
Curious Qoyllor fell from her star and her power, given by her father the Sun, shattered across the land. The hunter Ukuku promised to help find the shards. When she learned he had hidden some to keep her, she left; the Sun took her back and blinded him.
### STANDARD
Qoyllor lived on the star nearest the tribes and was curious about them. After wandering alone she met Ukuku, and they later formed a family, though he grew ever more gloomy. When she learned he had found shards and hidden them because he did not want to lose her, she left in anger and sadness. The Sun brought her back to the sky and blinded Ukuku with golden arrows; the sun now hides behind clouds wherever he goes. More details: Qoyllor lived on the star nearest the tribes and was far from her brothers and sisters. Over years of travel with Ukuku she learned the life of the ground: she hunted and rested with Saurians, told stories to the tribe's children, ate Tatacos and chased Monetoo. Her cheerful nature let her accept what could not be helped. Doubt came through their neighbour Pochica, who was jealous and who had never got along with Ukuku. He spoke ill of Ukuku to her for years, claiming he clipped the wings of birds he liked and caged them. Pressed by her questions, Ukuku confessed. She demanded the shards, he refused, hoping to keep her, and she left the tribe.
### LARGE +
Before leaving, Qoyllor made an enormous piece of graffiti on the land, said to be bigger than a volcano and visible from the Sun itself, in the way of a true person of Natlan. She decided that the lost power no longer mattered and that she would accept any punishment from her father. The Sun saw her plea and took her back; her star lost its power and is now almost invisible unless one looks closely. The narrator does not say whether she still watches the land. Their son Kuntur, who was very young, was affected by his father's punishment and could form no friendship with a Saurian until he was a young man. The narrator ends by saying his later adventures are another story.
### ULTRA +
Qoyllor's departure is framed as the result of deceit and betrayal, which she had never known before. The narrator comments that trust takes long to build and is destroyed in an instant, and that people differ in how far forgiveness reaches. The tale offers two guesses for Ukuku's gloom, innate temperament or guilt over a broken promise, and then reveals the real cause as his secret. The text also states that the star shards might have been claimed by birds, beasts, insects and snakes, a possibility Qoyllor herself raises while searching.

Gameplay note (not in-world): a Book Collection obtained after talking to Orqo in To Wish Upon a Star. The wiki's trivia notes that "quyllur" means "star" in Quechua and that ukukus in Quechua mythology are half-man, half-bear creatures; these are real-world notes, not canon.

### STANDARD
In the folk tale The Tale of Qoyllor and Ukuku, Qoyllor is a star-child, daughter of the Sun, who fell to the ground and lost the power he gave her. She was cheerful and curious, built a life among a Natlan tribe, and left when she learned Ukuku had hidden shards of her star. Her Sun-father brought her back to the sky. She is a figure of the story and not a known historical person.

### STANDARD
In the tale, Ukuku is a strong, taciturn hunter of the tribe, skilled at the chase and at tracking. He promised to help Qoyllor and then hid the shards he found because he had fallen in love with her. As punishment the Sun blinded him with golden arrows, and the sun withdraws behind clouds wherever he goes. He raises their son Kuntur alone in The Tale of Kuntur, where he still hunts despite his blindness.
