## summary
### LITE
The Legend of Vennessa is a two-volume ballad collection popular since Mondstadt's reconstruction. It tells how Vennessa, a fire-haired woman enslaved under the aristocrats, prayed for freedom, was answered by the Anemo Archon Barbatos, and went on to defeat the drake and break the rulers' tower. She is remembered as the founder of the Knights of Favonius.
### STANDARD +
The song is addressed to a drinking crowd and ends by urging listeners never to give up the pursuit of freedom, even in dark times.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Two short verse books written as a bard's performance, opening with a toast to freedom, the Anemo Archon and Vennessa, called the first Knight. The tone is festive and rhymed.

## lore
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
Vol. 1 sets the scene of old Mondstadt as a dungeon under royal chains, where festivities were the rich's vanity games. It introduces Vennessa as a fiery-headed woman from the southern plains, born free but imprisoned, who still prayed for her people and Mondstadt. Vol. 2 tells that Barbatos descended to her, said every being deserves a name and offered friendship; she marched to battle to his singing, the demonic dragon fled, and the aristocrats cowered.
### STANDARD +
- The Vol. 1 description says it covers her early years as a slave in Mondstadt. The bard says the Archon's gift was not freedom itself but defiance.
- In Vol. 2 Barbatos says he seeks only her friendship. When she wants to thank him, he answers that she acted first and his songs came to praise her freedom.
- Forest breezes converge and the tyrant's tower is overthrown from the sky; the song calls her a young lioness standing free in the winds.
- The bard closes by telling listeners to remember the dame with the flaming hair when days turn dark.
### LARGE +
- The collection presents Barbatos as a friend who credits her own action, not a god handing down a victory.
- The text names the drake only as a demonic dragon; it does not name Ursa in its lines.
### ULTRA +
- This song is one of several in-universe sources on Vennessa. Compare the Breeze Amidst the Forest ballads, which also mention her, and the history entries on the Aristocratic Period. Keep points where the accounts differ open.
