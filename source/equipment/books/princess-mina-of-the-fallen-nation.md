## summary
### LITE
Princess Mina of the Fallen Nation is a six-volume Inazuman adventure novel series, sold by Kuroda at the Yae Publishing House in Inazuma City. A wandering samurai in luxurious robes, accompanied by a small girl in an oversized haori, takes a princess prophesied to bring destruction on a journey to the Sky Tower at the centre of the world. The blurbs sell it as a hugely popular romantic adventure.
### STANDARD +
It is an in-world work of fiction, so nothing in it is evidence about Teyvat's real history. It is notable in Inazuma for its ironic, genre-aware voice: the narrator jokes about plot conventions.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Six paperback-style instalments. Each volume has a short blurb of the kind used to advertise serialised fiction. The narration is chatty and self-aware. Vol. 6 includes an appendix on the setting, with the wicked overlords and monsters who did not appear in the story.

## lore
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
Vol. 1: after thirteen years of peace, a century-scale age of warring states begins; a wandering samurai and a girl stop at a checkpoint where the samurai quickly disposes of three ashigaru. Vol. 2: the princess asks what the samurai thinks about her prophesied destruction; he calls the prophecy an excuse for war. Vol. 3: they see the devastation the war has caused. Vol. 4: a flashback. Vol. 5: at the Sky Tower the onmyouji reveal their plan. Vol. 6: the samurai walks the desert and leaves for another world.
### LARGE +
- Vol. 2 sets the principal tension: the princess wants to be rescued and to save the world, while the samurai says he did not rescue her since she gave him an order, and states that destroying the world is a stupid concept.
- Vol. 3 reveals that the war is fed by Nakura's Hex, a sorcery that drains life from living things and the land and gives it to powerful samurai, who are the Nakura Daimyo. The Hex was made by the Onmyouji of the Sky Tower at the world's centre. The samurai asks whether the world has not already been destroyed.
- Vol. 4: in Kogami, a wasteland, the samurai (called 'green') meets a former comrade ('pale'). Thirteen samurai once banded together to defeat the Nakura Daimyo, but Kogami did not recover and only two escaped.
- Vol. 5: the samurai tells the princess that the world cannot be saved; the Onmyouji say Nakura's Hex was made to preserve a declining world's vitality and that it can be used by whatever draws on the stored life. The princess insists that princesses are born to save the world.
- Vol. 6: it ends with the samurai deciding to destroy another world, if one exists.
### ULTRA +
- The book jokes about itself: an NPC speaks 'to push the plot forward'; a character says people would throw such beliefs into the nation of Tokoyo. These are narrator gags, and Tokoyo here is a fictional place.
- The recurring contrast is between the cynical samurai and the idealistic princess, with the samurai's past (the thirteen) as the engine behind his refusal.
- Only the premise and a handful of scenes are paraphrased from the wiki; details of the later chapters are not reproduced there, so avoid inventing the ending beyond what Vol. 6 says.
- Do not use the series as Teyvat history; Kogami, Tokoyo and the Sky Tower are story settings.

## owners
### STANDARD
No individual owner. Kuroda at the Yae Publishing House in Inazuma City sells Vols. 1 to 4 and 6; Vol. 5 is in the left-hand room of the Yashiro Commission Headquarters.

## entry: Nakura's Hex

@keys2: Princess Mina, Sky Tower
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In the novel, Nakura's Hex is a sorcery created by the Onmyouji of the Sky Tower that drains life from living things and the land, passing it to powerful samurai and feeding a war that has lasted over a century. The samurai who exploit it are the Nakura Daimyo.

### STANDARD
The titular princess is prophesied to destroy the world. The samurai says the prophecy is an excuse for war; the princess wants to save the world anyway. By Vol. 6 the story ends with the samurai travelling on to another world.
