## summary
### LITE
Rex Incognito is a four-volume Liyue fantasy novel telling of Rex Lapis' incognito excursions among mortals. A framing story set in the antique shop Xigu Antiques, run by Min'gui, surrounds tales of a jade craftsman and a harsh fishing-boat owner. Its running theme is that rules matter less than a person's own judgement, and that a relic is worth its story.
### STANDARD +
The in-game blurbs say fact merges with fiction. Treat it as popular Liyue literature about the Geo Archon, not as history; its closing twist deliberately undermines its own claims.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Four unillustrated chapters in alternating voices: a shop-set frame, then nested stories-within-stories. The mood is wry and playful, with an unnamed visitor who is a pedantic young expert with amber or golden eyes and dark long robes.

## lore
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
- Vol. 1: At night, Xigu Antiques of Feiyun Slope is visited by a black-robed young man who calls a damaged jade plaque in her shop a modern counterfeit. He argues that ancient tribes of Rex Lapis' followers carved such plaques as stand-in currency before Mora existed. The owner, Min'gui, says she can disprove his verdict with a story.
- Vol. 2: A prankish jade carver in Mingyun Village is asked by an amber-eyed woman to carve the true likeness of the Lord of Geo from what he has seen, or go unpaid. He delivers a plaque showing a woman and explains his three-day process of learning, observing and carving by heart.
- Vol. 3: A harsh fishing-boat owner in Liyue Harbor docks the pay of a new hand, a bashful youth from Qingce Village, who tells him a story about a jade plaque while someone takes the docked wages from the owner's pocket and returns them to the labourers.
- Vol. 4: Back in the shop, Min'gui argues that antiques are valued for their stories. The young noble buys the plaque, and admits his jade-currency theory was invented.
### STANDARD +
- Vol. 1 lists the shop's curios: a Fontaine timepiece, Sumeru incense, a Mondstadt aristocrat's goblet, a stool an adeptus once sat on, a teacup the Lord of Geo sipped from and a vase the Anemo Archon accidentally broke. The plaque, bought from an adventurer who brought it out of an abandoned palace deep in the abyss, cost Min'gui much of her fortune.
- The young man notes that this plaque carries a woman's image, unusual for the period, and that there is no historical record or physical evidence of Rex Lapis ever taking female form.
- Vol. 2's carver was known for contrary commissions, such as a fleeing boar for a hunter statue and an empty throne for a ruler. The Lord of Geo was rumoured to be cold and unfeeling, but trusted because his laws made trade fair.
- Vol. 3's youth asks why a person should do the work they hate most; the owner says that is how the world works, and the youth replies that perhaps that was not what Rex Lapis meant by his rules.
- Vol. 4 draws the moral that Rex Lapis never forced mortals to live by contracts as a perfect template, since the rules are a means rather than the end, and that a balance lies in people's awareness and choices.
### LARGE +
- The Vol. 1 appraisal names Orobashi (pearls and shells), the Mistress of Chenyu Vale (jade), Zhongli (rocks) and Havria (salt) as sources of idol materials for forgotten gods. The visitor says idols were made from these materials in the likeness of each god.
- Vol. 4 finishes with its twist: after hearing Min'gui's story, the noble son declares he will take the fake, because the story is what makes it worth buying.
### ULTRA +
- Each of Vols. 2 and 3 ends as the visitor-figure begins another story (the woman "reminds me of another story"; the Qingce youth offers "a story about a certain jade plaque"), so the volumes read as stories nested inside the antique-shop argument.
- The in-game blurbs say the novel tells of Rex Lapis' incognito excursions, but the text never states that any visitor is Rex Lapis. Keep that distinction when characters discuss it.

## owners
### STANDARD
No individual owner. Vol. 1 is on a table in Qingce Village's main plaza, Vol. 2 is at the bottom layer of the north boat docks in Liyue Harbor near the flower shop, Vol. 3 is at Wangshu Inn, and Vol. 4 is sold by Jifang at Wanwen Bookhouse.

## entry: Min'gui and Xigu Antiques
@keys: Min'gui, Xigu Antiques
@keys2: Rex Incognito
### STANDARD
In Rex Incognito, Min'gui is the first owner of Xigu Antiques on Feiyun Slope, an antique shop open after moonrise for wealthy customers. She is a skilled storyteller who sells relics by their legends and spars with a pedantic young visitor over a jade plaque.

## entry: The Jade Plaque
@keys: jade plaque, Geo Lord's likeness
@keys2: Rex Incognito, Min'gui
### STANDARD
The central object of Rex Incognito: a damaged jade plaque showing a woman's likeness. The visitor calls it a recent counterfeit and says ancient plaques were a form of tribal currency. In the finale he admits that currency theory was his own invention.

## entry: The Mingyun Jade Carver
@keys: jade craftsman, Mingyun carver
@keys2: Rex Incognito, Mingyun Village
### STANDARD
In Rex Incognito Vol. 2, a prankish jade carver in Mingyun Village makes a plaque of the Lord of Geo as a woman after a three-day process of learning, observing and carving blind. He cannot explain why it came out that way.
