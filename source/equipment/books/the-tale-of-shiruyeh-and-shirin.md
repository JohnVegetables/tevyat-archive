## summary
### LITE
The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin is a two-volume story told by a Jinni in a magic bottle to the shepherd she calls Raven, found in the House of Daena. It tells how the hero Kisra Parvezravan became king of Gurabad, how his father-in-law's line died in one night, and how his son Shiruyeh killed him.
### STANDARD +
It is a sequel to the quest item The Shepherd and the Magic Bottle, with the same two travellers crossing the desert. The Jinni says it is the spirits' version, and later quests tell the fall of Gurabad differently.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Two chapters of dialogue and long speeches by the Jinni, interrupted by camp and desert scenes: sunset over dunes, the sumpter beast, the youth watching the moon. The in-game description calls it a story of unknown origin, full of absurdities.

## lore
### STANDARD
Vol. 1: the Jinni explains that in the lost language "Shiruyeh" means "unweaned lion cub", though in the shepherd's tribe it is a branded disgrace. The orphan Kisra, raised in the nest of the giant bird Ghoghnus, becomes the champion of the vassal king Ormazd Shah, destroying ninety-nine cities, tribes and star-sages, and is rewarded with a new name, Parvez, and Ormazd's daughter Shirin. One night Ormazd and all 300 of his offspring die in Gurabad, and Kisra, now Parvezravan, takes the throne.

Vol. 2: Shirin is the daughter of Ormazd and the fairy Liloufar, whose prophecies tell of a hero husband, a sweet end to many relatives and Shirin alone inheriting the kingdom. Slaves, mages of the Mazandaran Oasis or Fellahin servants poison the banquet; only Shirin, shut out by her father, and her husband Kisra keep their lives. Parvezravan banishes their son Shiruyeh in a mask, and Shirin disguises herself, urging him to confront the coward on the throne. Shiruyeh stabs his father in bed, is crowned and later falls into a chasm from which Shiruyeh's Plague emerges. Shirin is freed to roam as a haunting spirit, or is said to have been bottled by Al-Ahmar.
### LARGE +
Further background in the tale. Parvezravan raids for Ormazd; his achievements make the crown too heavy for its owner, so it hangs from golden chains in the hall. The age of vassal kings ends after a century of chaos when Al-Ahmar and the Sage-King of the Greenwood reconstruct the oasis paradise, and one of the three living gods had fallen. Liloufar also gives three admonitions to her mortal husband: that Shirin's joy will bring tears to her father's eyes, that they must not feast at the same table as their daughter after she is wed, and that the daughter's heir will be an ill omen for the king's land. The king only smiles. Some say the tragedy was Kisra's own doing: a clawless bird wailed all night, rats with human faces gnawed the chains and the crown crashed down, and an adventurer later found crypts with withered bodies bearing inscriptions.
### ULTRA +
The tale's fine detail: Shirin is blessed by the white ibis, given a cerulean pearl by the cobra, and worshipped by a giant crocodile; the poisoners are mages of the Mazandaran Oasis or illiterate Fellahin servants who drip scorpion venom into musk honey, and are drowned in the honey pot by the new king; Shiruyeh weeps before Shirin after the murder and she removes his brass exile mask and blesses him; his nightmares end when he falls into a chasm, and the plague that follows takes half of Gurabad's souls. The wiki reads this as a story-within-a-story structure like One Thousand and One Nights.

## contradictions
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Per the wiki trivia, later quest material (The Dirge of Bilqis, 3.4, and the Desert Pavilion Chronicle set, 3.3) tells the fall of Gurabad differently. There Liloupar is the real architect of the city's fall, as revenge on Ormazd for failing to abolish slavery. Kisra Parvezravan was Liloupar's own son, left in the wilderness to be adopted by Ormazd. Shirin did not intend to poison the banquet and went mad when she realised what had happened. Shiruyeh's role as Khorramdin and the Brass Mask Rebellion are absent from the tale, and in the quests it is Liloupar who is imprisoned in a bottle, not Shirin. The wiki also notes that Liloufar's prophecies in the tale are self-fulfilling.

## owners
### LARGE
No owner is named. The books are found in the House of Daena, and their narrator is a Jinni, a servant of King Deshret (Al-Ahmar) who has been bottled.

## entry: Shiruyeh
@keys: Shiruyeh, Shiruyeh's Plague
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin, Shiruyeh is the son of Kisra Parvezravan and Shirin, banished masked from Gurabad, who kills his father in his bed, takes the throne, is haunted by nightmares and falls into a chasm. The plague that emerges from it is called Shiruyeh's Plague. In the lost language the name means "unweaned lion cub", while in the shepherd's tribe it is a disgraceful name branded on exiled chiefs.

## entry: Kisra Parvezravan
@keys: Kisra, Parvezravan, Ormazd Shah
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In this tale Kisra is an orphan raised in the nest of the bird Ghoghnus, adopted by the vassal king Ormazd Shah, and rewarded with the name Parvez and Shirin's hand after conquering ninety-nine cities, tribes and star-sages. After Ormazd and his 300 heirs die in one night, he becomes the king Parvezravan, and is later killed by his son Shiruyeh. The tale hints that some say he was behind the tragedy at Gurabad.

## entry: Shirin and Liloufar
@keys: Shirin, Liloufar
@keys2: Shiruyeh, Gurabad, Jinni
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In this tale Shirin is the daughter of the vassal king Ormazd and the fairy Liloufar, foremother of the spirits. Liloufar prophesies a hero husband, sweet ends for relatives and Shirin's sole inheritance, which become a curse of waiting. Shirin's marriage to Kisra is unhappy, and hatred grows in her; the tale then has her disguise herself to urge her son Shiruyeh to kill his father and finally freed as a haunting spirit or bottled by Al-Ahmar.
