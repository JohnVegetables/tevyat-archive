## summary
### LITE
Fables de Fontaine is a three-volume storybook of animal fables found in Fontaine, credited to the author Marie Hericart de La Fontaine. Each volume holds one short tale ending in a stated moral. Its in-game description says most of the tales were not originally written by her but gathered from ancient poems now lost.
### STANDARD
The three tales are "The Maiden and the Sun", "The Crow and the Fox" and "The Tale of the Donkey". All are moral fables with talking animals. Vol. 3 follows directly from Vol. 2, since the donkey tries to copy the fox's trick.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 4-star book collection of three volumes. Vol. 1 is sold by Hubel in the Court of Fontaine (Quartier Narbonnais). Vol. 2 lies at Romaritime Harbor next to Hermosa. Vol. 3 lies in front of the Beaumont Workshop. The cover title is written in the Fontaine Script.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1, "The Maiden and the Sun": a fox's adopted daughter, grown to marriageable age, asks to wed the most powerful being in the world. The fox offers her to the sun, but the sun points to the clouds that block it. The clouds point to the wind, the wind to the mountain that stops it, the mountain to a mouse that can tunnel through it, and so on through the cat, dog and wolf. The chain returns to the sun, and the maiden marries the sun. The moral is that one should accept one's destiny and cannot escape a duty by excuses.
### STANDARD
Vol. 2, "The Crow and the Fox": a crow on a tree holds stolen cheese. The fox below first warns that the cheese is dangerous, then that it is no delicacy, then claims the crow does not know how to eat it properly and that she has collected recipes from around the world. Curious, the crow opens its beak to ask, drops the cheese, and the fox takes it and runs to her nest. The moral is that those who seek mysteries they should not know will lose even what they had.

Vol. 3, "The Tale of the Donkey": after hearing how the fox won the cheese, a donkey resents that others praise the fox and ignore its own hard work. It copies her, ambushes a cheesemaker's daughter and tells her to throw her cheese away to travel faster. She takes it for mockery, calls for a stick and threatens to drive it back to the mill, and the donkey falls silent. The moral is that imitating someone else and hiding your own identity brings only more suffering.
### LARGE +
Vol. 3 opens with its own framing remarks before the story: that a plain person dressed up is rarely mistaken for a socialite, and that everyone has their own strengths and weaknesses, so the way forward is to know oneself and face one's flaws. In Vol. 2 the fox says the cheesemakers of the city let the crow keep the cheese, and in Vol. 3 the donkey's victim is a cheesemaker's daughter, which links the last two volumes. The fox's argument in Vol. 2 is a string of misleading claims, yet the stated moral blames the crow's curiosity rather than the fox's deceit.

### ULTRA +
Placement notes: the three fables are physically separated, with one volume at each of three Fontaine locations, so a reader needs to visit the Court of Fontaine, Romaritime Harbor and the Beaumont Workshop to hold the whole set. The in-game description frames the collection as folklore compiled under one author's name, so a character may cite it as a popular storybook but should not claim the tales are historical.

## owners
### STANDARD
No owner. It is a commercial book collection scattered across Fontaine.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay note (not in-world): a 4-star Book Collection added in 4.0. The wiki notes that the real-world models are fables by Jean de La Fontaine (the first tale resembles "The Mouse Turned into a Maid", the second "The Fox and the Crow"). The Fontaine-Script cover reads "les fables".
