## summary
### LITE
A one-volume Natlan folk tale, sequel to The Tale of Qoyllor and Ukuku. Kuntur, their shunned son with no Saurian companion, defeats the sorcerer Rumi and wins an Iktomisaurus partner.
### STANDARD
In the tale every tribe child is bound to a Saurian companion. Kuntur's stubbornness and honesty, not any special gift, carry the story. The narrator is a storyteller who comments on events and says "we do not know" about some details.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A one-volume Book Collection, listed with no quality rating. It is a reward of the Hidden Exploration Objective Kuntur's Pursuit (the wiki marks that name as unofficial) in Malinalco Grotto, Quahuacan Cliff. The wiki notes that its cover matches its prequel.

## lore
### LITE
Blind Ukuku raised young Kuntur alone; the tribe shunned them because clouds followed them. Kuntur found no Iktomisaurus willing to join him. When the sorcerer Rumi stole the tribe's Saurians, Kuntur volunteered, defeated him and won a companion.
### STANDARD
The tribe neither banished nor welcomed father and son, fearing the sun's anger. Kuntur grew up small, sickly and pale, with starry eyes like his mother's, and children who mocked him were answered with his fists. He reached Rumi, freed the Saurians, and let a captive Iktomisaurus go rather than force it to help him. Rumi had visited as a ragged old guest, and the tribespeople fed him until they ran out of patience and sent him away. Only Kuntur refused him, saying he had nothing to give. Rumi then bewitched the hosts and led away their Saurians. The tribe's three best warriors were turned into talking Pakpakas, who tried to trick Kuntur with the same lies that had defeated them. His father's lesson against deceit let him see through them. Rumi's fog failed because Kuntur navigated by sound and smell, learned from his blind father. After Rumi turned to smoke and fled, the bravest freed Iktomisaurus returned on its own, showed him the path, and the pair chased Rumi through animal forms.
### LARGE +
Rumi became a Brown Deer, a capybara in the water, a bird and finally a giant rock. The Iktomisaurus raised a cyclone that let Kuntur keep pace. At the end Kuntur gripped the rock and urged the Saurian higher until they passed the highest clouds, where he saw the sun for the first time, but the sun gathered storm clouds around them. The cold froze Rumi's rock solid, ending his magic. The stone was placed somewhere suitable, the three warriors were restored and treated Kuntur with shame instead of disgust, and Kuntur gained his companion.
### ULTRA +
The narrator suggests that Kuntur inherited his mother Qoyllor's starry eyes and her persistence, comparing his pursuit of Rumi to her wandering search for star shards. The story says the tribespeople who failed to satisfy Rumi justified sending him away by calling themselves kind. It also states that Kuntur held an unwilling Iktomisaurus fast at first but decided that forcing it would make him no different from Rumi, and so he freed it. The narrator admits not knowing why the Saurian came back, but argues that Iktomisaurs are intelligent and choose their partners, so it must have recognised him.

## gameplay
### STANDARD
Gameplay note (not in-world): a Book Collection added in 5.2, obtained from Kuntur's Pursuit in Malinalco Grotto, Quahuacan Cliff. The wiki notes that kuntur means "condor" and rumi means "stone" in Quechua, and that "Pakpaka" may derive from a Philippine word for "wing"; these are real-world etymologies, not canon.

### STANDARD
In the folk tale The Tale of Kuntur, Kuntur is the son of the star-child Qoyllor and the hunter Ukuku. Disliked because the sun withdrew from his father, he was nicknamed "Kuntur, Unloved of the Sun". He defeated the sorcerer Rumi using his father's hunting skills, his honesty and his persistence, and won an Iktomisaurus companion. He is a story figure with no known counterpart in other canon.

### STANDARD
In The Tale of Kuntur, Rumi is an infamous sorcerer who disguises himself as a hungry beggar, takes the tribe's Saurians by enchantment and turns three warriors into Pakpakas. He relies on fog, illusions, lies and shapeshifting, and ends frozen solid as a rock above the clouds. He appears only in this folk tale.
