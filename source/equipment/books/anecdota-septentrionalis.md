## summary
### LITE
Anecdota Septentrionalis is a three-volume ancient Remurian scroll found in the ruins of the Faded Castle. Its narrator states from the start that everything he writes is invented, then recounts a voyage to find the edge of the ocean. The in-game description says there is no way to verify its words.
### STANDARD
It reads as a deliberate parody of the tall-tale travelogues that Remurian poets, playwrights and historians wrote and swore were true. It is a rare piece of Remurian humour and shows that Remurians thought of their own writers as liars. It ends abruptly, promising the rest in a next chapter.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 3-star book collection of three scroll volumes found in the Faded Castle: Vol. 1 floating near books on the Lower Level, Vol. 2 floating near books on the Middle Level, and Vol. 3 at the top of the staircase mechanism, also on the Middle Level.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1: The narrator mocks writers who insist their stories are true, using the example of Xanthus Talassii filius, who wrote about northern barbarians and a king he calls a knight, though the narrator says he never left Capitolium. He then declares his own tale a total fabrication. His crew sails north to see the edge of the ocean, sails 79 days without land, and on the 80th day is attacked by bandits on giant birds who ask for potatoes. Warriors on armoured Blubberbeasts rescue them. The knights look like bipedal vishaps.
### STANDARD
Vol. 2: The leader of the knights says Remuria does not exist and is a fake history of southern barbarians, and he welcomes the crew into the Empire of Solaris, taking them for traders from Hyperborea. The empire is in civil war: decades earlier a philosopher named Lucilius invented a way to give some people extraordinary powers at the cost of an altered appearance, and one side wants them enslaved or destroyed. The narrator suggests they alter Blubberbeasts instead of humans, then leads imperial legions in a battle in which rebels' one-eyed giants hurl Bulle Fruit that sends people floating into the sun. He skips the war's end.

Vol. 3: After a further 300 days at sea the crew reaches a huge wall in the ocean on which liars are said to be hung after death for judgement, and they flee. They reach an island city called Amoria, meaning love, with walls of twelve gems and a moat of milk, inhabited by welcoming women. The narrator finds human bones in one woman's house, aims a Bulle Fruit at her, and she turns to water. When they flee to the ship the island is gone. It ends with a promise that the next chapter will explain.
### LARGE +
The narrator invokes Remus under the name Sebastos when praying, and mentions the golems made by Sebastos's own hand and the Ocean Pillars to give scale. The name Serenum, which the knights ask about, is never explained. The Solarians' division between the empowered and ordinary humans is the one piece of the plot that carries a message: the narrator says such conflicts have been written about in plays since ancient times.
### ULTRA +
Vol. 1 cites other authors by name as foils: Quinctilius, who held that the High Sea ends in impassable waterfalls, and Iuvenalis, who wrote of a faraway kingdom in the north. Vol. 2 cites the Pisculentum playwright Terentius and the giants of Pacuvius; Vol. 3 cites Ennius for tales of over-hospitable strangers. None of these authors is otherwise described.

### STANDARD
The Empire of Solaris appears only in the Anecdota Septentrionalis, an openly fabricated Remurian tale. In it the empire is a technologically advanced realm ruled by knights who ride armoured Blubberbeasts, in a civil war over Lucilius's method of granting extraordinary powers at the cost of one's appearance. It is not a real nation of Teyvat and must not be treated as history.

### STANDARD
Amoria, whose name the inhabitants say means love, is the vanishing island in Vol. 3 of the Anecdota Septentrionalis: a golden city with twelve gemstone walls and a moat of milk, populated by welcoming women, with bones in the houses. When confronted with a Bulle Fruit one woman turns to water, and the island disappears. It is part of the narrator's declared fabrication.
