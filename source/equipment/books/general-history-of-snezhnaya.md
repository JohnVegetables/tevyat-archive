## summary
### LITE
General History of Snezhnaya is a two-volume book sold by Moes at Torchlight Books in Snezhnograd (added in 7.0). Written by Y. S. Klyucheva and dedicated in fawning terms to Tsaritsa Anastasya, it is Snezhnaya's flattering "official" history: legends of golden Hyperborea, then the youth and campaigns of Tsar Monomakh (the Belyi Tsar) and his fae companions.

## lore
### LITE
Volume 1 sets out rival legends of Hyperborea, a warm golden realm where Snezhnaya now lies. Volume 2 tells how young Monomakh learned the spear from Yalyane Morevna, beat the shapeshifter Filipov and made him his "Synaimos" (sworn comrade), and spared the human warrior Nepla Korolevitsna's clan.
### STANDARD +
The in-world work has many volumes: Polar Night legends first, Monomakh's campaigns in the middle, and the Tsaritsa's deeds at the end. The game shows only the preface and excerpts of Volumes I, III and VII. The preface states the thesis: Monomakh conquered by force, but virtue renews the world, and the Tsaritsa's virtue is greater still.
### LARGE +
Volume VII deals with the war against King Miscislav's Volkodlak coalition. It records the ambiguous role of Vaselisa Strivozha of the Vila. It also tells how Vaselisa Vodyanovna, the "Zmey Maiden" and daughter of Vodyanoy the Lackland, defected to Monomakh, after which Miscislav massacred his own Water Imp allies. The book says Filipov later fell for the "mad monk" Rotwang's tricks out of grief for Nepla, and vanished after the Tsar's death in the Cataclysm.

## reliability
### STANDARD
This is the court-approved version of history. It passes on a genealogy that makes Monomakh Hyperborea's rightful heir, which other canon contradicts (he was an incomplete Primordial Human born of the Cryo Dragon's remains). Still, the author names competing sources and marks legends as legends.
### LARGE +
The sources named in the book are all in-world: *The Volkhov Chronicles* (the oldest, still thousands of years after the golden age it describes), Grand Duke P. N. Kostomarov, the *Songbook of Saint Strivozha*, *The Davograd Chronicles* (hostile to Yalyane), *The Belovodye Annals* and *A History of the Snezhnyi Expedition*.
### ULTRA +
Nothing is known of Klyucheva beyond the preface, where the author, calling themself a mortal, thanks the Tsaritsa for release from "the afflictions of my former days"; this is never explained. In the text shown in game, the author deals with fae and human matters even-handedly. The author records that fae of Monomakh's age saw humans as fragile and unimportant, yet praises human valour (Nepla) and Monomakh's mercy toward human clans. For a scene, a Snezhnayan scholar or a reader at Torchlight Books might quote the book as the respectable mainstream account. A fae noble of another house, or anyone who knows the truth of the Tsar's origin, could reasonably dispute its genealogy and its tidy moral framing.

## entry: Klyucheva's preface to the Tsaritsa
@keys: Y. S. Klyucheva, Klyucheva
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
In the preface of General History of Snezhnaya, Y. S. Klyucheva addresses Tsaritsa Anastasya Feodorovna Snezhnaya by a long chain of titles. They include Eternal Sovereign Under the Pale Star, Protectress of the Fae Kindreds and of the Lineages of the Peoples of the North, Holy One of All Winters, and Grand Suzerain of the Princes of Snezhnograd, Glupov, Kitezh and Belovodye. The author thanks her for release from "former afflictions" and for being allowed to stand among the immortals, then signs as her loyal subject and child. The preface contrasts Monomakh's conquests, fierce as a winter gale, with the Tsaritsa's greater virtue.

## entry: Legends of Hyperborea (General History)
@keys: Legends of Hyperborea, Golden City
@keys2: General History, Klyucheva, Kostomarov, Volkhov Chronicles
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Volume I of General History of Snezhnaya says many sources agree Snezhnaya was once a warm, fertile plain. Fae poems name the realm there Hyperborea. The author warns that even *The Volkhov Chronicles* was written thousands of years after the age it describes. Grand Duke P. N. Kostomarov pictures virtuous people in golden cities in eternal spring, living on heavenly honey and dew. When their descendants grew idle and lost their nobility, spring left and the long winter came. The author then turns to "true history": before founding Kitezhgrad, Monomakh wandered the blizzard lands for centuries as a young hero, gathering companions and imagining a realm of virtue and mercy.

## entry: Songbook of Saint Strivozha
@keys: Songbook of Saint Strivozha, Mother of Night, Preexistent Gods, Preexistent Men
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In-world source quoted in General History of Snezhnaya and attributed to Vaselisa Strivozha. It says the King of Light imprisoned his daughter in darkness. The King of Hyperborea freed her, and from then on night existed; she became the Mother of Night. They had seven Preexistent Gods and twelve Preexistent Men, whose descendants founded Hyperborea of gold and white stone. The King of Light then destroyed it and killed the seven divine bloodlines, except Tsar Monomakh, the last monarch's young son. The book calls this a "strange fable". Its claim about Monomakh's descent conflicts with other canon about his origin.

## entry: Yalyane Morevna in the General History
@keys: Yalyane Morevna, Knyazhna of Blood
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
General History of Snezhnaya (Volume III) says the Snegurochka of Hesperides were closest to Monomakh. He openly called Yalyane Morevna his teacher; she was the grandmother of Hesperides' first Grand Duchess. Real history barely mentions her, and she vanished before Kitezhgrad was founded. Folk accounts make her a Hyperborean survivor called the "Knyazhna of Blood" for savagely raiding other fae kindreds. Yet she never preyed on the destitute human clans of the snowfields; *The Davograd Chronicles* even says she sheltered humans from monsters. She took in young Monomakh and taught him the spear. The author reflects that before courage became a virtue it was a crime, cradled by plunder and nursed by greed.

## entry: Synaimos
@keys: Synaimos
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
"Synaimos" is a word from the fae tongue that General History of Snezhnaya glosses as comrade-in-arms, brother or kinsman. Monomakh offered Filipov Vseslavyevich crystals of the purest kind to become his Synaimos. Filipov first refused and challenged him to a contest.

## entry: Filipov's contest with Monomakh
@keys: Filipov Vseslavyevich, Filipov
@keys2: Monomakh, contest, General History, mountains, Synaimos
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
In General History of Snezhnaya, the young Filipov Vseslavyevich, progenitor of the shapeshifters, scorns Monomakh and demands his submission. He becomes a weasel and destroys Monomakh's weapons, then becomes a falcon, a white wolf, a great bear, a hill-sized bull, a giant the size of a mountain range, and finally Monomakh's own likeness. Each time Monomakh seizes him by the scruff and throws him like a spear over one more mountain, from one up to seven. Moved by Monomakh's purity of heart, Filipov becomes his Synaimos and dearest friend, and later the first Commander of the Druzhna. Legends of the pair sailing into a whale's belly and facing a dragon for seven days are noted as unverifiable. Chronicles always link Filipov with cunning and victory. He lost only three times: to Monomakh, to his wife, and to the pitch-black calamity from beneath the earth.

## entry: Nepla Korolevitsna in the General History
@keys: Nepla Korolevitsna, Nepla
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In General History of Snezhnaya, Nepla Korolevitsna is a human warrior guarding her clan with a huge war hammer and a fire-breathing horse; her shout alone drives Filipov's horse to its knees. Filipov unhorses her at the cost of one eye. Under the law of the age the victors could kill or enslave her clan, but Monomakh spares it, citing the mercy his teacher Yalyane taught him, and settles it at the future Port Belovodye. Filipov marries Nepla, saying her arrow took one eye and her valour and beauty the other. She soon dies of sickness, and only friendship and oaths keep him alive.
### LARGE +
The book says this grief is why, thousands of years later, Filipov trusted the "mad monk" (Rotwang, "The Professor") who summoned her phantom, and stopped warning the Tsar. During the Cataclysm the Abyss took her shape to lure him away. He claimed she had a mole behind her ear, and when the impostor showed him one, he laughed that she never had one and that all the world longs to be deceived. He fought on, deserted only when news came of the Tsar's death, and has not been seen since.

## entry: Strivozha and Filipov's sword
@keys: Vaselisa Strivozha, Saint Strivozha, The Pure One
@keys2: General History, Filipov, sword, Miscislav, Volkodlak
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
Volume VII of General History of Snezhnaya admits nobody knows what Vaselisa Strivozha told King Miscislav's envoy. Without wishing to defame her, the author notes that the Volkodlak coalition never attacked the Vila, even after the Vila denounced it and paid tribute to the Pale Star court. By contrast, Miscislav slaughtered his loyal Water Imp allies after Vaselisa Vodyanovna defected. Citing *A History of the Snezhnyi Expedition*, the book says Filipov laid a long sword before Strivozha at the war's end, warning that winter freezes even the wind. She answered that colder winters only make people wrap themselves tighter. Laughing, he revealed the sword was a trophy from Monomakh, sent to keep her an ally. She agreed and held a banquet lasting seven days and nights.
