## summary
### LITE
The Mondstadt Tower is a one-volume historical romance novel set in the dark age of Mondstadt's aristocrats. Its blurb promises a fateful meeting between an outlander girl and an ominous orphan. The text that survives in-game follows the singer Ines, who throws the Harpastum at Ludi Harpastum, and the Seneschal and his adopted son Octave.
### STANDARD +
It is fiction, so use it for the mood and imagination of old Mondstadt and not as a source of confirmed history.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A short novel whose cover and spine read Mond Tower in the Teyvat script. Its blurb reads like a serial teaser.

## lore
@spoiler: none
### LITE
In the time of the aristocrats a high tower stood in Mondstadt's plaza. It was nominally built to honour Barbatos but was really a show of wealth. The one comfort of the commoners was Ludi Harpastum. At one festival a wandering singer from a distant land, Ines, was admired for her beauty and her songs, and she gave her festival earnings to the poor and orphans. The frail Seneschal fell for her at first sight, but his piety and the Church's claim to aid the poor made him angry, and he plotted to confine her in the Cathedral.
### STANDARD +
- The novel's setting: the Anemo Archon sleeps, the nobles tyrannise, commoners are poor and the dragon ravages the city. The self-proclaimed orthodox church is a figurehead controlled by the ruling class, preaches abstinence, and even restricts the wind harp to approved holy songs.
- The text says that the idea of divine punishment was an excuse made up by the nobles to hide their greed and restrict the commoners.
- By tradition the maiden chosen to throw the Harpastum serves three days at the royal court under the aristocrats' protection. The Seneschal sends his adopted son Octave to sneak into the court and bring Ines to him quietly, without using the Seneschal's name.
- Octave was abandoned at birth by superstitious parents and was persecuted by townsfolk as a bad omen of draconic disaster. The Seneschal alone protected him, so Octave trusts him fully.
- At nightfall Octave climbs to the court balcony and sees the girl weeping in the moonlight. He forgets his task, until servants interrupt them, where the text ends.
### LARGE +
- Notice the novel's argument: it contrasts a church that forbids pleasure with the belief that the Anemo Archon encourages love and the pursuit of freedom.
- The novel mentions the Cathedral as the place where the Seneschal means to hold Ines.
### ULTRA +
- The wiki trivia says the novel is based on the French novel The Hunchback of Notre-Dame, and the name Ines is linked to Agnes, the real name of Esmeralda. Use that only as a real-world note.

## entry: Octave and the Seneschal
@keys: Octave, Seneschal
@keys2: Mondstadt Tower, Ines, novel
### STANDARD
In the novel The Mondstadt Tower, Octave is the Seneschal's adopted son, an abandoned child persecuted as a bad omen, who trusts the one man who protected him. The Seneschal sends him to fetch the singer Ines. Both are fictional characters of the novel.
