## summary
### LITE
Conch Echoes is a two-volume book of Nod-Krai, sold by Dhabit at Mimisbrunnr Books in Nasha Town. Its in-game description calls it a very old tale of children who spoke while holding conch shells in the empty frontier wastelands, by an author long forgotten. The story is about who deserves to lead and what happens when power is taken by force.
### STANDARD
It is a fable, not a record. No character is named beyond nicknames such as "Shorty", and no real place is identified. Its theme is that a leader's authority fails when he fears being seen as flawed, and that a shared symbol of speech should belong to everyone.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 4-star book collection of two volumes. Both are sold by Dhabit at Mimisbrunnr Books in Nasha Town.

## lore
### LITE
Vol. 1: Exiled children, who secretly call their place of banishment the "Outlands", have outlived the adults who made them work as slaves. They agreed a pact that whoever holds a conch, taken in secret from the beach, has the right to speak and that a clever leader would be chosen to hold it. On the thirtieth night a handsome young man with a blade demands the conch from the smallest boy, "Shorty". He argues that he earned the most and holds the blade, so he will command everyone. Shorty hands it over, walks into the forest, and seems to smirk as he goes.
### STANDARD
Vol. 2: The young man has become pale, green-tinged and stiff, slumped in a heap-laden chair, with the blade fallen where nobody will pick it up. A girl with glasses finds Shorty. He says he saw this coming from the moment the conch was demanded, and that the tyrant, afraid of showing flaws as the adults had, grew suspicious, sleepless and angry until he undid himself. Shorty picks up the nicked, chipped blade, throws it into the sea, and holds the conch to his ear to hear the waves. He proposes a town where the conch belongs to all, and he accepts that today's children will become tomorrow's adults and others may overthrow them.
### LARGE +
The text states that the ideal order the children hoped for did not arrive: without anyone to restrain them, the cruelty still in their hearts grew, and some reasoned that if violence could overthrow everything it could take everything. The narration comments that most stories' protagonists need only more patience. Shorty calls the conch a mere toy found on the beach, which suggests the symbol matters less than the agreement behind it.
### ULTRA +
The handsome young man is never named and is described only by his looks, his blade and his claim to have worked hardest. The blade is dented from years of chopping wood, and the text says he hid its need for care, which is how its decline became a metaphor for his own. The red marks on Shorty's palms from the conch's rope are noted when the girl hands him the shell.

In the fable Conch Echoes, "Shorty" is the smallest of the exiled children and keeps the conch that gives its holder the right to speak. He gives it up to a rival with a blade, lets that rival wreck himself, then throws the blade into the sea and makes the conch common property of a new town.
