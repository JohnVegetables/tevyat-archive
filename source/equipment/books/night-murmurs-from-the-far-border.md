## summary
### LITE
A one-volume book of Snezhnayan fae folktales, sold in Nasha Town, Nod-Krai (6.3). Its story follows a hunter who tracks a wounded wolf-like monster to a remote mansion and learns that its lord is a Varcolac, a fae with two hearts.
### STANDARD
The item description calls it a collection of folktales circulating in Snezhnaya that records lesser-known fae tales; this volume covers the dangerous fae called the Varcolac. The tale is told as a legend and ends on a deliberately open note.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A 3-star Book Collection with one volume, credited to an author named Viliana. The prose is a short horror-tinged narrative in the style of a winter tale, closing with a brief narrator's coda that steps outside the story.

## lore
### LITE
The hunter Czcibor shoots a huge black-furred beast in the shoulder, follows its blood trail to a mansion and finds a young lord with long black hair. He discovers the lord is the beast, kills him, and learns that a Varcolac has two hearts and does not die from one.
### STANDARD
Czcibor, a seasoned hunter, keeps watch in a hunting lodge after a village girl is found with her throat torn open. A monster of coarse black fur passes; he wounds its right shoulder and follows the blood to a gloomy estate deep in the forest. A pale elderly butler denies seeing it, and the master, a strikingly handsome man in his early twenties with waist-length black hair, invites the hunter to dine and stay out of the blizzard. Czcibor, raised in the Frostmoon Scions' encampment, recalls an old priestess's warning that Varcolac are the trickiest of the fae: they have two hearts, take a human and a fae form, and keep the same fur colour in both. He tests his host with pepper, garlic and his amulet, and nothing bothers the man.
### LARGE +
At night Czcibor finds the servants asleep with odd, non-fatal scars on their limbs and sees that the organ in the house is made of bone, not of any ordinary animal. He follows the master into the snow, where the man undresses, shows the fresh arrow wound and has it begin to close as the snow hangs in the air and the glow of Frostlamp Flowers streams to him. Czcibor attacks with a dagger. The man says he is not a monster, hesitates, and is stabbed through the heart. He then turns into the wolf-like beast, laughs, and explains that Varcolac are born with two hearts; he thanks the hunter for destroying the extra one, saying it left him complete. The beast lunges for the hunter's throat and the story stops there.
### ULTRA +
**The coda.** The narrator says it is impossible to tell whether this is a legend or an undiscovered truth, notes that the Varcolac are said to be nearly extinct and often dismissed as myth, and adds a rumor that Duchess Lady Evlampiya Orlova of Snezhnaya is a Varcolac, one not covered in wolf fur. The text says that verifying this is not possible.

**Reading the tale.** The hunter is shown as competent but acting on prejudice and fear; the host protects his household and warns the guest to stay indoors, and his one protest ("I'm not a monster") is the moment the hunter presses his advantage. The book does not decide who is in the right, and does not say what became of the servants beyond the scars. Treat the vampiric hints (blood tasting, pallid servants) as the story's atmosphere, not as established facts about the Varcolac as a species.

**Names.** The wiki notes that the protagonist shares his name with a present-day NPC, Czcibor, who once vanished for many days near Barrowmoss Barrens and returned without memory. Whether the book's hunter is connected is not stated. "Varcolac" echoes a real Slavic werewolf term, and the Volkodlak variant of the word appears in A Brief History of the Pale Starborn.

## entry: The Varcolac
@keys: Varcolac, Varcolacs
@version: 6.3
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In this folktale a Varcolac is a fae with two hearts that can take a human or a wolf-like form, with the same hair or fur colour in both. A hunter's priestess warns that they are the trickiest fae. A Varcolac heals wounds by drawing on the glow of Frostlamp Flowers, and the story claims that piercing one heart does not kill it. The book says they are now nearly extinct and often written off as myth.
### LARGE +
Only this tale describes them; no other source named in this entry confirms the two-hearts claim. Fae in general are covered by the snowland fae entry.
