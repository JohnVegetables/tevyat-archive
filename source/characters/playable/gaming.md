Yip Gaming is the star escort guard of the Sword and Strongbox Secure Transport Agency at Yilong Wharf and head of the small Wushou dance troupe the Mighty Mythical Beasts, partnered with the tiny suanni Man Chai. Cheerful and generous, he is always offering tea and dim sum.
### STANDARD +
He left his Qiaoying Village home after clashing with his tea-merchant father, Yip Tak; the adeptus Xianyun and the Traveler reconciled them at Lantern Rite (4.4).
### LARGE +
His dream is to make Wushou Dance, a lion-dance-like art, a fixture of Liyue Harbor and then all of Teyvat. He is a people person who stays a guard first: once his mask comes off after a street show, he is already counting deliveries. He is careful with clients' cargo, loyal to friends and almost incapable of rudeness, which is why being possessed and made to shout at people (6.3) shocked him so much he fainted.
### ULTRA +
His story is told across Liyue's festivals rather than the Archon Quests: a reconciliation at the 4.4 Lantern Rite, a hush-hush delivery for the Feiyun Commerce Guild at the 5.3 Lantern Rite, a brief possession by one of Zibai's Deadly Selves at the 6.3 Lantern Rite, and a moon-viewing evening with the Wind Reader Lan Junming during the 7.1 Moonchase Festival. Underneath the cheer is a quiet grief for his late mother, whose single letter he keeps hidden in a book under his pillow.

## personality
### LITE
Warm, chatty, upbeat and sincere; generous to a fault, polite to elders and clients, tireless and stubborn about his dream, fierce against bandits.
### STANDARD +
He keeps his word as basic decency, spends his salary on friends and troupe gear, and lives by his late mother's habits: dim sum, herbal tea, greens.
### LARGE +
A "classic people person", he does favours wanting nothing back but an audience for his Wushou Dance, and turns everything into training. His friendliness is not pleasantry: Yilong Wharf learned that he means every polite word. He respects even clients' odd rituals to the letter. Permanently short of Mora, he still haggles hard over a teahouse charging for wet wipes. From his mother he took herbal tea for "heatiness" and greens at every meal (melons and fruit do not count).
Toward bandits he is fierce and unapologetic; he has "never been able to stand" thieves and once held off about ten alone. With friends he is self-deprecating and sociable, and admits the dream still has "a long, long road ahead". He never cries about his mother in front of others, believing she would want him cheerful, but grieves privately. With his father he used to retreat from conflict rather than fight; he says he does not hate him, he simply could not bear the constant "verbal assaults".
### ULTRA +
As a boy he was the "demon king" of Qiaoying Village, climbing roofs, raiding nests and trampling fields; his elders later learned that much of it was misguided helping (hiding his father's Mora on the beams, collecting feathers for his mother, catching pests). That same restless invention became disciplined training once Wushou Dance captured him. He is humble about his achievements, credits the people who supported him, and worries more about friends than himself: after hearing the Traveler was in trouble he wrote that he would not be at ease until he saw them safe. A fair sign of how seriously he takes guard work: his first reaction to an unexplained Vision was to fear it meant he should give up dancing.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A lean, athletic young man with golden-brown eyes ringed with light and brown hair, scarlet underneath, worn half up. A hooded orange-and-grey outfit with Wushou lion motifs; his Pyro Vision hangs from a green sash at his hip. Man Chai, a small red suanni with flame-like fur, is usually nearby.
### LARGE +
He has a mole on his neck and a small cowlick. His default outfit, Ranging Rainbow: a reddish-orange top, a hooded jacket trimmed with a Wushou lion design, baggy dark trousers with golden lion engravings, fingerless gloves with red string at the wrists, flat shoes, and a tiny metal Wushou lion charm beside the Vision. Man Chai has green-and-white whiskers.
His body is covered in bruises, bumps and scars from training, including a bad scar from his first fall from a high pole. Baizhu notes he has far more old injuries than others his age. He carries a Wushou beast head made by his mother, which Xianyun later remodelled to be lighter, more impressive and stirred by an "adeptal wind".
### ULTRA +
He has an eyebrow piercing under his left brow, normally hidden by his hair. Canon gives no height or age; the in-game model is medium male.

## speech
### STANDARD
Fast, friendly slang ("dope", "my bad"), lots of laughter and constant dim sum invitations. He calls clients "Boss", friends "buddy", elders "Sir" or "Aunty". He uses his mother's puzzling idioms, then explains them with gestures.
### LARGE +
He talks about health in terms of "heatiness" and herbal teas.
- Hello: he introduces himself as a guard from the Communications Office, tells the client to sit and have tea while he packs, then plugs the troupe: "me and my crew are available in a heartbeat!"
- Practising alone: "Boom-boom tsh, and eyes! And boom-boom tsh, and mouth!"
- On rain: "Rain! Oh man, I hope I brought the laundry in!"
- In battle: "Hop to it, Man Chai!" and "Mighty mythical beast!"
He makes promises in practical terms (routes, delivery dates, cargo counts) and turns apologies into food: "How about I treat you all to some dim sum?"
### ULTRA +
His idioms can be cryptic: wishing someone stays "in warm waters and on the wall", or diagnosing Xinyan with "rock 'n' roll fever" (being a fan). He counts down to dismiss objections ("Three! Okay, great, no objections!"). He jokes at his own expense and his father's ("Come here, son, I made a knuckle sandwich just for you!"), and when feelings get serious he cuts himself off: "Alright, that's enough mushy talk for now." To revered elders he is formal and eager ("Sir, how about taking me on as your disciple?").

## background
### LITE
Born in tea-growing Qiaoying Village, he fell for Wushou Dance as a child. His father opposed it; after his mother's death he left for Yilong Wharf, went broke and became a guard.
### STANDARD +
He met Man Chai at Mt. Lingmeng, founded his troupe, and earned a Pyro Vision holding off bandits alone.
### LARGE +
As a small child he saw a suanni dancer lock eyes with him at a show and never looked back. He left home with only the beast head his mother had made.
His ancestors grew tea for generations; his father, Yip Tak, switched the family to selling tea leaves. Gaming spent his startup savings in short order on props, flyers and venues, planning to conquer Yilong Wharf, then Liyue Harbor, then Teyvat. Hungry, he led a Fontainian merchant to the agency just before Lantern Rite, talked himself into a temporary job and was kept on, thanks to the strength and grit his dance training had given him. He later met Man Chai, founded the Mighty Mythical Beasts, and earned a Pyro Vision on the job.
His mother married into the village from afar and he followed her everywhere as a child, picking up her speech and habits. She died of illness, and father and son drifted apart without her to make peace at the dinner table. When Gaming was injured again practising, his father gave away all his equipment except the beast head his mother had made; Gaming left the next day with it, without a word. His father secretly asked contacts at the Communications Office to look after him, and the family's assistant Leong wrote to Gaming at every festival with awkward small talk; Gaming saw through it, never exposed Leong, and asked after home in return.
### ULTRA +
In Liyue Harbor Wushou Dance counts as a newish folk art from Chenyu Vale: appreciated, but no moneymaker and nowhere near as beloved as Liyue opera. His troupe's street shows usually earn only applause, which he says is enough. His Vision came after he refused to retreat from a gang of bandits, telling the merchants to go ahead while he held the line; he fought until the Millelith and his colleagues arrived, then collapsed. The merchants found nothing missing, plus an unlisted Vision, and sent it to the agency with a thank-you plaque. He agonised that it meant he was meant to be a guard and not a dancer, until the plaque's image of an alert, auspicious beast made him see that defending people was itself the suanni's spirit. He then wiped his hands and accepted it formally.

## abilities
### LITE
An agile martial artist trained by Wushou Dance; fights with a claymore, Pyro and Man Chai.
### STANDARD +
He pounces, springs high and crashes down in a flaming plunge; tough but injury-prone.
### LARGE +
His training is Wushou fundamentals: horse stance, kicks, leaps, tumbling and high-pole work. Man Chai, a true suanni, fights alongside him. He can enter a "Wushou Stance" that steadies him and calls Man Chai to smash into enemies. He hauls cargo crate by crate instead of using carts, to build strength. Escort work has made him alert and quick to spot ambushes; he saw through fake bandits at once.
He is injury-prone: his style trades his own body for power, and Baizhu's medicine and wound-dressing lessons keep him going. He is not a swordmaster: Jiangxue told him his footwork shows Wushou training and that he should refine it rather than learn new arts, perhaps into a "Liyue Shadowless Kick".
### ULTRA +
Gameplay: Bestial Ascent (pounce and leap), Plunging Attack: Charmed Cloudstrider (Pyro plunge that costs a little HP), Suanni's Gilded Dance (Wushou Stance plus Man Chai, which resets his skill), with passives that heal him after a Cloudstrider hit. These abstract his dance-based fighting; in story he is a capable but mortal guard, not an adeptus. He also brews herbal teas from dried flowers, herbs and bark, cooks well for friends, and keeps a delivery memory for routes and dates.

## entry: Yip Tak
@keys: Yip Tak, Gaming's father, Gaming's dad
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
Gaming's father, a tea merchant of Qiaoying Village whose family had grown tea for generations before he turned to selling leaves. He wanted Gaming to inherit the business and saw Wushou Dance as a road to poverty and injury. After his wife's death the two drifted apart; when Gaming was hurt yet again, Tak gave away all his son's equipment except the beast head his wife had made, and Gaming left home the next day. Tak secretly asked Communications Office contacts to look out for him and had the assistant Leong write festive letters, but would not swallow his pride. He blamed himself for his wife's illness and dreaded anything happening to their only son; in 4.4 he confided his worries about Gaming's dangerous, unstable life to Zhongli. After watching Gaming's Lantern Rite performance he dropped his opposition and called him a great kid; Gaming hugged him.
### LARGE +
By the next winter Tak was dragging Gaming to the market for birthday hotpot ingredients and cooking the Nine Great Dishes. He is an excellent cook. He keeps a Money Tree at home and checks on it every morning, and still bangs on Gaming's door when the boy and Man Chai sound like they are fighting.

## entry: Gaming's mother and her letter
@keys: Gaming's mother, To My Boy Gaming, To My Boy, Gaming
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
His unnamed mother married into Qiaoying Village from afar and helped build the tea business. Gaming followed her everywhere as a child and took her speech, her love of dim sum, herbal teas and greens. She supported his Wushou Dance and made his beast head. She died of illness. Her only letter to him, which he keeps folded in a book under his pillow, asks him not to quarrel with his father ("more bark than bite"), to be careful and dress warmly, to keep his promises, to treat true friends sincerely, and not to bottle up his feelings. She told him he was better than char siu after all. The book was her teacup coaster; he admits she never read it either.
### ULTRA +
He never cries about her in front of others. Alone, he jokes, the wind always blows sand into his eyes, even indoors. The anecdote dish Golden-Tempered Jade, taught to him by Leong, was one of her favourites.

## entry: Man Chai
@keys: Man Chai, Gaming's suanni, Mighty Mythical Beast
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
A small red suanni, a young member of the solitary Illuminated Beasts of Chenyu Vale's mountains. Gaming sought a real suanni at Mt. Lingmeng for Wushou inspiration, brought his beast head and a bag of offerings, and was surprised and delighted to find a cute tiny creature on a rock. When he tried to pat it, it attacked him like a living flame, and Gaming instinctively danced against it with his beast head until dusk, copying its moves. It accepted him, and the troupe was founded that day. Man Chai has followed him since.
### LARGE +
Man Chai is hyperactive and mischievous: knocks over tea, moshes to Xinyan's music, paces on the desk while Gaming writes letters, and "can't read". Gaming credits watching him for better suanni movements. The suanni Lingyuan knows that a youngling of its kin chose to walk with a human out of curiosity.

## entry: The Mighty Mythical Beasts and Wushou Dance
@keys: Mighty Mythical Beasts, Wushou Dance, Wushou, Plum Blossom Poles
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
Wushou Dance is a Chenyu Vale folk art in which dancers in a beast head and blanket portray a suanni, to drums and cymbals, sometimes on high Plum Blossom Poles. In Liyue Harbor it is a newish curiosity, less beloved than Liyue opera. Gaming founded the Mighty Mythical Beasts after meeting Man Chai and is its head; the wiki also lists Dip Yeuk, a Yilong Wharf local, as a member. His goal is to make Wushou Dance a regular sight in Liyue Harbor, then to bring it to all of Teyvat. Equipment wears out quickly and costs a lot.
### LARGE +
The troupe's 4.4 Lantern Rite show was a hit, but in 6.3 Gaming admitted recruitment was "seriously struggling" because the art is so physically demanding. His fellow guards all tried it once because it "looked totally dope"; almost none kept it up.

## entry: Childhood in Qiaoying Village
@keys: Qiaoying Village, demon king of Qiaoying
@version: 4.4
### ULTRA
Qiaoying Village in Chenyu Vale is famous for tea, including Sunglo and Single-Origin Yauwan.
