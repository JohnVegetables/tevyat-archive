## summary
### LITE
Liyue Harbor knows him as a model young gentleman. In private he is a wuxia-novel addict who revived the dying Guhua Clan's sword arts and does good deeds in secret, and a cheerful prankster.
### STANDARD +
He also secretly writes martial arts novels as "Zhenyu". His first book flopped in Liyue but became a bestseller in Inazuma.
### LARGE +
His life is a balancing act between duty and desire. He slips away from lessons and banquets for "a moment of solitude", meaning bookshops, bandit roads and good deeds, and he uses the Guild's money and influence when a sword is not the right tool. As of his 7.1 birthday letter, he has decided to learn the family business properly so he can support his overworked elder brother.
### ULTRA +
He is a constant presence in Liyue's side stories rather than its Archon Quests. He debuts in his own Story Quest (1.0), reveals his pen name (2.0), visits Inazuma for the Irodori Festival (2.6), loses his great-grandfather nearly to the fetor (5.3), and in the 7.1 Moonchase Festival helps guard a convoy beside Hu Tao, Chongyun, Gaming and Lan Yan.

## personality
### LITE
Reserved and impeccably formal with strangers; chatty, playful and teasing with friends. Clever and nearly impossible to out-argue. His creed: "be good, and do good." He hates cynics who sneer at heroes, and carrots.
### STANDARD +
Chivalry is a mindset to him: true heroes shrug off setbacks, skip the self-congratulation and look for the next good deed. He keeps it discreet because his family expects a merchant's son. His humour has a mean streak, mostly aimed at Chongyun, but it is affectionate. He puts a book down only when someone needs help.
### LARGE +
He resents being seen only as "the second son of the Feiyun Commerce Guild" and has to "put on an act" around most people; with the Traveler he can be himself. He dreads stiff receptions, stale tea and staler talk, and will invent elaborate pretexts (practising calligraphy through swordplay, popularising theatre abroad) to escape them. Yet he takes his family's reputation seriously: he will not speak ill of the Tianquan in case it causes problems for his father and brother. He is proud of his writing and also mortified by it: hearing a friend read his prose aloud is worse to him than half a dozen banquets.
### ULTRA +
He is generous without fuss (refusing Keqing's reward for catching a bandit, then volunteering to guard Qingce Village) and his sense of justice is sharp; when a bully threatened Chang the Ninth, he could not stand by. He can be sincere when it matters: during his great-grandfather's illness he dropped the jokes, admitted the family was struggling, and comforted himself with Hu Tao's "Live in life, die in death". He also carries a quieter worry about what words are worth: in the Anecdotes he is troubled that a signature, not the words, sets a book's price, and that people value a letter for who sent it. His 7.1 birthday letter shows him maturing. On seeing grey at his brother's temples, he decided a true hero must stand for family as well as principle, and set his own wishes aside.

## appearance
### STANDARD
A slight young man with dark navy hair, asymmetrical bangs and golden-amber eyes. Default outfit (Azure Silk): a long silk coat fading from black to navy, lined in yellow, with gold trim, a jewelled brooch and frilled white sleeves; a tasselled blue earring on the right ear; white high boots. His Hydro Vision hangs low on the left of his coat. Sword at his side, book in hand.
### LARGE +
His alternate outfit, Bamboo Rain (4.4), is a Fontainian-style suit with bamboo patterns. When he first received it he complained it restricted movement and made hiding difficult. Canon gives no height or age (in-game: medium male model).
### ULTRA +
In Bamboo Rain's outfit story he wore it to a banquet for Fontainian guests mainly to hide the chimney ash on his other clothes after secretly rescuing a stage cat.

In public he is formal, flowery and archaic ("my liege", "shan't", "'twas"), quoting proverbs and making verses, and admits he enjoys it. With friends: quick teasing banter, "Hehe", and a lowered voice when his secret life comes up ("Shh! Not so loud..."). In battle, wuxia flourishes: "Rain outlines your fate!", "Let me weave you a verse."
### LARGE +
- On reading: "I never embark on a journey without a good book to accompany me."
- On a cliffhanger: a string of gasps, then dismay at "All will be revealed in the next volume".
- Wry about himself: he calls himself "a mere bookworm" and freely concedes his handwriting is dreadful.
He addresses elders with titles (Lady Beidou, Miss Shenhe, Ms. Yelan, Mr. Zhongli, Dr. Baizhu) and Chongyun as "dear Chongyun". When flustered he stammers ("Y-You...").
### ULTRA +
He thinks in stories: he explains situations through wuxia tropes, offers to put embarrassing scenes into his next novel, and reels off folklore with several variants and no definitive answer. His prose leans on balanced four-beat phrases and poetic headers; Chongyun quoted one of his chapter couplets aloud, to his horror. With people grieving or worried, he becomes plain and gentle ("It's alright, these are dear friends. The truth needn't be hidden from them").

- Friends: chatty, mischievous, quick to rope them into schemes, "haunted house" outings or a detour to Wanwen Bookhouse.

- His handwriting: concedes it is dreadful, then gets defensive.
### LARGE +

- Cynics who claim heroes act for rank or reward: smile unchanged, but they go on his private blacklist.
- Danger: calm and strategic ("Come. I have devised a plan."); he plans ambushes and economic traps rather than charging in.
- Embarrassment: blushes, begs for mercy, threatens melodramatically to crush his sword hilt.
### ULTRA +
- Superiors and officials: proper titles and respect, no personal gossip.
- Grief or fear: drops the theatre, speaks plainly, thinks of others (suggesting a meal once things calm down).
- Unfamiliar places: curious and studious; he researches folklore, ancient artifacts and local legends.
- He is a capable swordsman, but not invincible or omniscient; he relies on wits, preparation and friends.

## background
### LITE
A model student who hid martial arts novels in hollowed-out textbooks. His father sent him to the Guhua Clan to keep him out of Wanwen Bookhouse; he revived its dead art, and a Vision let him live out his heroic dreams.
### STANDARD +
As second son he was spared running the Guild; his elder brother expected him to become its great asset. Instead he vanished for hours ("a moment of solitude") to read, explore and make mischief, and out-talked anyone who complained. The Guhua Clan was a hollow shell with a rented dojo, meant by his father as a pastime, but Xingqiu grasped its "Martial Principle" in four years and sparked a Guhua renaissance.
### LARGE +
He wrote a martial arts novel, "A Legend of Sword", drawn from his own experiences. Liyue publishers rejected it as "wildly unrealistic" and "banal", so he printed a few copies himself and slipped them onto Wanwen Bookhouse's shelves. Few read it. A passing Inazuman merchant took one home, where it became a lasting bestseller that later also found readers in Fontaine. He publishes as "Zhenyu" through Yae Publishing House, with Albedo as illustrator.
### ULTRA +
The Guhua verse: after his epiphany that sword and polearm are both arts of the "eye", because a fighter's Vision is an extension of themselves and the weapon an extension of the Vision, he wrote a verse on the Martial Principle. The clan head wept and declared that the clan needed Xingqiu, not the other way round. The verse is sealed at Wangshan Hall, officially as a secret, but in truth because the handwriting is so bad. Guhua's three great arts are Light Piercer (polearm), Rain Cutter (sword) and Life Ender (both). He was also fascinated by the clan's showman's tricks (quicklime, smoking sand, paper fish, sword-swallowing, fire-breathing) and resolved to learn them all. His deeds include rescuing the pilgrim Musheng from bandits on the road to Liyue Harbor, which Musheng credited to Rex Lapis. His family is a multigenerational household: father, grandfather and a great-grandfather over a hundred years old, whose calligraphy is treasured by business partners. His mother is mentioned only once, mashing carrots for him as a child.

## entry: Guhua Clan and the Martial Principle
@keys: Guhua Clan, Guhua Sect, Guhua Brotherhood, Rain Cutter, Raincutter, Light Piercer, Life Ender, Wangshan Hall, Martial Principle
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
The Guhua Clan, once the Guhua Brotherhood, is an old Liyue martial school whose name once "rang like steel throughout the harbor", but which has declined for centuries. Its three secret arts are Light Piercer (polearm), Rain Cutter (sword) and Life Ender (for those who master both). Xingqiu's father enrolled him as a diversion, knowing the clan was nearly dead. Xingqiu revived it: he grasped the ancient "Martial Principle" after four years and wrote a verse on it. In his view, sword and polearm are both arts of the "eye", because a Vision is an extension of the fighter and the weapon an extension of the Vision. The clan head wept and said the clan needed him. The verse is sealed at Wangshan Hall, ostensibly from outsiders, actually because of his handwriting.
### LARGE +
His fame started a minor "Guhua renaissance": devotees come looking for the master, and the clan's disciple Sun Yu tells travellers that Xingqiu "wrote a mantra and utterly changed the legacy" of the clan. Bullies still mock the clan's decline. Xingqiu treats his Guhua training as a secret ("our little secret") from his family and from strangers. The clan reveres the wandering hero Guhua as its founder, although it was actually established after his death by Boxuan, who proclaimed himself Guhua's disciple.

## entry: A Legend of Sword and the pen name Zhenyu

Xingqiu wrote the martial arts novel "A Legend of Sword", based on his own adventures. Liyue publishers rejected it for a setting "as wildly unrealistic as the plot is banal". The novelist Gaiman, visiting Liyue, says Zhenyu is huge in Inazuma and Fontaine but unknown at home. Some early text named the pen name "Chenqiu"; since 2.2 it is consistently Zhenyu. Venti is an enthusiastic reader of Zhenyu.
