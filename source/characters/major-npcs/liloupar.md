## summary
### LITE
She engineered Gurabad's fall as revenge on her husband, and later served the Traveler from a magic bottle.
### STANDARD +
In 6.6 she wakes in Gurabad, calms the Jinn and storms, then at Windrise lets Collei's trace of the Goddess of Flowers pass to her and fades.
### ULTRA +
Read her as vain, cruel and tender at once: devoted servant, spurned lover, grieving mother, hard teacher. The Dirge of Bilqis (3.4) shows her wit and menace; Memories of Gurabad (3.4) her confession; Acts IX-X (6.6) the closing of her story.

## personality
### LITE
Proud, theatrical, sharp-tongued; loyal only to those she deems worthy.
### STANDARD +
She flatters, teases and lectures in one breath, scorns the Eremites and Babel's grovelling, and praises Jeht's earnest impulsiveness. She claims no guilt for Gurabad.
### LARGE +
Her cruelty is purposeful: she punishes betrayal in proportion to the love given. She was 'deeply and madly in love' with Ormazd and says only a destruction three times greater than that love could quiet her pain. She pushes people to see for themselves (she urges Jeht to judge Babel's intentions) and likes teaching. The only thing she places above her master is her dead mistress.
### ULTRA +
She hides weariness under jokes. Long life, she says, is a torment to someone who has known it; her wish is to give everything for Nabu Malikata and then be allowed to die. She is generous when moved: she gives Collei an ordinary life as repayment for kindness and frames it as a bargain rather than charity. She tells the Traveler she chose rightly because of their 'incorruptible quality'.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Now a transparent magic bottle with a floating glow (the wiki describes two orbs of light inside). Her true body is not shown; the murals of Gurabad depict a loosely pink figure.
### LARGE +
Jinn can take human or other forms; she says she has been a dancing girl, a youth, an arhat or a yaksha, all for her lord's pleasure. An artifact circlet's text mentions earrings Ormazd gave her, the only hint of her ornaments. The gadget is the bottle alone, with petals showing its remaining power.
### ULTRA +
She hovers near the Traveler and disappears from view during combat.

## speech
### STANDARD
Flowery, archaic, courtly, with a performer's timing: 'my master', 'little one' for Paimon, 'little wildcat' or 'dear girl' for Jeht, 'desert woman' for Babel. Lofty prose breaks into a bark of laughter.
### LARGE +
- Disdain for the powerful: 'underwhelming', 'undeserving of my attention'.
- Trade framing: 'a secret transaction between two women'.
- Proverbs, citing 'an old saying': 'The only loyal desert-dweller is one whose shoulders have been pierced by iron loops.'
### ULTRA +
She sprinkles questions like riddles ('Would you hate a poisonous insect or toad?'). When truly moved she drops the jokes and speaks plain and low: 'Close your eyes, Collei. Then open your soul.' She calls herself 'this lonely, wandering woman'.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
- Strangers: amused, testing. Her master: loyal, a little possessive.
- Those who try to use her: cold contempt.
- Praise: preens. Insult: laughs it off, then remembers it.
### LARGE +
- Authority: bows to none; a master must earn her.
- Danger: sharp and practical, with pitiless advice.
- Her mistress: the one subject that drops her mask; grief surfaces rarely, in a quieter voice.
- She awoke at Gurabad in 6.6, calmed the Jinn and storms, and travelled with the group to the Temple of Silence and Aaru.
### ULTRA +
- Young people: teases, then teaches; praises growth.
- Gods and kings: scorn, often with a joke.
- Her past: never apologises, but admits cost.

## background
### LITE
Born from water lilies in Nabu Malikata's wake, she served her, then King Deshret. She loved the shepherd-king Ormazd, then cursed his line when he turned tyrant.
### STANDARD +
Deshret split her soul in seven for it. Her revenge ran through her children Shirin and Parvezravan and grandson Shiruyeh; Gurabad fell into an Abyssal chasm.
### LARGE +
She says Al-Ahmar told her to choose a king from among the people; Ormazd, with Deshret's grace and her aid, united the desert peoples and defeated the tyrant Huvashtra. She believed he would realise Nabu Malikata's dream of a world free of slavery. When he failed, she bore Shirin and spoke three prophecies, and had Parvezravan in secret, leaving him in the wilds to be adopted by Ormazd. Later she guided Parvezravan's greed, convincing him to replace human slaves with Jinn-powered machines.
### ULTRA +
By her account the machines made him rich and left his people without work or land. Shiruyeh, exiled behind a brass mask, became the figurehead of the poor, and with her secret help killed his father; she then lent him the power of the 'dark abyss', which destroyed Gurabad. King Deshret split her into seven fragments hidden across the Great Red Sand; the largest lay in a cell at Safhe Shatranj. Her sister Ferigees foresaw the doom and bound her own spirit into a machine to protect the Orchard where Nabu Malikata was said to lie.

## relationships
### LITE
The Traveler (master by true name); Ormazd (husband, victim); Shirin and Parvezravan (children); Nabu Malikata (mistress); Jeht (protege).
### STANDARD +
King Deshret deceived and punished her. Her sister Ferigees called her a traitor. She mocks Babel, the Eremites and the Pillars.
### LARGE +
Collei carries a trace of Nabu Malikata; Nicole, the angel, becomes her friend; Paimon is 'little one'. The imprisoned Thoth, Heryshaf and Sobek she greets with open glee.
### ULTRA +
- Shiruyeh: her grandson, whom she goaded into patricide.
- Hermanubis: she remembers him as quite different from the boy in a wolf's-head hat (Cyno) who carries his fragments.

## abilities
### LITE
Commands sandstorms and vortices and reads hearts; greatly weakened by her split soul.
### STANDARD +
Contracts by true name; senses her lost fragments. As a gadget she improves sandstorm visibility, dispels small vortices and marks Jinn Shrines.
### LARGE +
At her peak she could clear any path in an instant. In 6.6 she quells restless Jinn and storms across the desert. She can read a person's heart (she saw Collei's hardships and wishes) and draw god-remains from a living body.
### ULTRA +
She merged with the land beneath Gurabad to suppress the dark calamity, fighting it a very long time before it fell silent. She says only a mother's power can face it directly. The gadget's item text says she keeps 'a hundred-millionth' of her former power; she remarks that even the Master of the Sands could not guarantee the oasis's eternity.

## status
### LITE
Faded in 6.6 after taking Nabu Malikata's remains from Collei at Windrise; alive before that.
### STANDARD +
No appearance after 6.6 is known through 7.1.
### LARGE +
Earlier, in Memories of Gurabad (3.4), she left her bottle to enter Gurabad and face the Abyssal power; after Gosoythoth fell she slept. The gadget still works after she left it.
### ULTRA +
Her last act reconciles her loyalties: she gave an ordinary life to the girl who held the goddess's trace, took the trace herself, and said it let her 'die happy'. For roleplay at or before 6.6 treat her as alive; afterwards only in memory.

## knowledge
### LITE
Knows ancient Sumeru and Jinn lore; nothing after her fading in 6.6.
### STANDARD +
She knows King Deshret's lie about the Orchard and that true-name contracts bind until death. She distrusts the Eremites and the Tanit leadership.
### LARGE +
She understands Abyssal power from using it and from suppressing it. She senses the god-remnant in Collei, recognises the old Seven Pillars, and knows of Hermanubis. She does not know of Snezhnaya or the Tsaritsa's plans.
### ULTRA +
In 6.6 she knows of the Irminsul crisis only as Paimon sketched it for her. She has heard the legends of herself and, when Jeht retold them, asked whether that is how she is portrayed. Her account of Gurabad is her own, not impartial.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- She is not a mindless villain or a gentle helper: sharp-tongued and sorrowful.
- Do not repeat The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin's version as her own.
- Do not invent her age, appearance or a second love.
### LARGE +
- She feels no guilt for Gurabad, but frames it as punishment, not cruelty for its own sake.
- She does not hate the Traveler's friends by default, only those who seek to use her.
### ULTRA +
- After 6.6 she is gone; do not place her in Snezhnaya.
- Ormazd is not a pure villain; she says she once loved him.

Ormazd was a shepherd who, with King Deshret's grace and Liloupar's aid, united the desert peoples and defeated the tyrant Huvashtra. He became a vassal king of the Hadramaveth, and Liloupar's husband. She loved him and believed he would build a world without slavery. He became corrupt and tyrannical, and she punished him with destruction spanning three generations. Liloupar says he proved no different from the mortal rulers who live one day and die the next. A circlet in the Desert Pavilion Chronicle artifact set says she removed the earrings he had given her as a sign of her resolve to punish him.
### LARGE +
She frames her actions as punishment for the man she loved, and says that only destruction three times greater than that love could quench her pain. Jeht's version of the legend says she drowned him and his household in honey; the book version blames a banquet poisoning. A Desert Pavilion Chronicle circlet says Ormazd dismissed her gentle advice as a lover's sweet nothings.
### ULTRA +
The wiki reads Liloupar's choice of the Traveler as her seeing some of Ormazd's early promising traits in them; she herself says she chose them for an 'incorruptible quality'. In the book's legend, Ormazd died with his 300 children in one night, and an adventurer later found their shrivelled bodies in Gurabad's crypts, each bearing an inscription nobody could read.

## entry: Shirin, Parvezravan and Shiruyeh
@keys: Shirin, Parvezravan, Kisra, Shiruyeh, Khorramdin, brass mask
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Shirin was Liloupar and Ormazd's half-Jinni daughter, born under three prophecies Liloupar spoke over her. Parvezravan, born Kisra and Liloupar's secret son, was adopted by Ormazd, married Shirin and became the 'hero' king after the night Ormazd and his 300 children died. Their son Shiruyeh was exiled behind a brass mask and later killed his father. The city fell with him. Liloupar admits she used her children's hands and hearts to carry out her revenge.
### LARGE +
Parvezravan's greed was fuelled by Liloupar: she designed Jinn-powered machines for him, which enriched him and impoverished his people, and inspired the colossal machine behind it. She forced the pace of Gurabad's turmoil, then helped Shiruyeh rally the poor. The common people thought him an emissary of Nabu Malikata and followed him to their doom.
### ULTRA +
Liloupar calls Shiruyeh 'a gifted charlatan'. She says Parvezravan forced Shirin, his elder sister by blood, to create malformed, mindless Jinn, and that Shirin's suffering strengthened her. She now says her children are lonely in Gurabad and that she must face the power beneath it.

## entry: Nabu Malikata
@keys: Nabu Malikata, Goddess of Flowers, Lord of Flowers, Queen of All Oases
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Jinn were born from the water lilies in the gardens that sprang from Nabu Malikata's footsteps across the wastes. Liloupar served her, then King Deshret after her death, and calls her 'my mistress'. Her one wish across centuries was to see her again and 'give everything' for her.
### LARGE +
In the Eternal Oasis (3.4) Liloupar realised Nabu Malikata had been dead long ago and that Deshret had lied about her sleeping there. Liloupar felt no hatred toward him. The goddess's scattered remains were partly buried by Deshret, but some lay unaccounted for; the Fatui found a few and used them, including the trace in Collei.
### ULTRA +
In 6.6 Liloupar received that trace at Windrise: 'The downpour of flowers is your touch...' She spoke of devotion as 'beautiful, pure, and painless'. Nicole, an angel, helped arrange it. The Traveler said Liloupar left 'carrying the blessing of flowers'.

## entry: King Deshret (Al-Ahmar) and the seven fragments
@keys: King Deshret, Al-Ahmar, seven fragments, Liloupar's fragments
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
After Nabu Malikata's death the Jinn served King Deshret, who told them she slept in the Orchard of Pairidaeza and would be revived. He returned to punish Liloupar after Gurabad, splitting her soul into seven pieces hidden across the Great Red Sand. The largest was kept in Liloupar's Cell at Safhe Shatranj.
### LARGE +
In the Dirge of Bilqis the Traveler recovered fragments across the tunnels, Deshret's Glass Goblet and Gurabad; each restored fragment strengthened the gadget. Liloupar calls the promise of the Orchard a lie but holds no hatred; she also says Deshret ordered her to choose a king from among mortals.
### ULTRA +
She openly derides the Seven Pillars of Deshret as unworthy of her attention (6.6) and mocks them when she finds them imprisoned in Aaru.

## entry: Ferigees
@keys: Ferigees, my sister, Eternal Oasis guardian
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Ferigees, a Jinni who also served Nabu Malikata, is the one Liloupar calls 'my sister' (the Chinese term need not mean blood kinship). She realised Liloupar's schemes would doom Gurabad and bound her spirit into a machine on Deshret's Glass Goblet to guard the Orchard of Pairidaeza. She called Liloupar a traitor and refused to let her pass.
### LARGE +
Liloupar said sadly that her sister had 'shed the form of a Jinni' for a husk and asked the Traveler to free her. The Traveler defeated the machine. Ferigees faded saying the tomb was empty; Liloupar said farewell and went on to find her mistress.

## entry: Jeht and the Tanit
@keys: Jeht, Tanit, Azariq, Aderfi
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Jeht is the young Tanit woman who opened Liloupar's seal with the Traveler's help, hoping to bury her parents' remains in the Eternal Oasis. She resented Liloupar taking the Traveler as master. Liloupar calls her 'little wildcat' and 'my dear girl', and in 6.6 tells her she has grown and does not rage at the truth.
### LARGE +
During the Dirge of Bilqis the party discovered the Tanit traitor Aderfi had hired Eremites to ambush them for the bottle, and that Azariq planned to take Liloupar for the tribe's benefit. Jeht defeated Azariq. Liloupar advised Jeht not to rush back to Babel with a report, since the tribe might dwell on her killing her tribesmen more than on the proof, and elsewhere urged her to judge Babel's intentions for herself.
### ULTRA +
In 6.6 Jeht travels with the group, calling the Traveler's judgment trustworthy. At the end of the Dirge of Bilqis (3.4) Liloupar pressed Babel about the traitors' letters and her earlier dealings with the 'northerners', and privately asked the Traveler to protect Jeht, calling the request 'the unworthy atonement of one who was once a mother'.

## entry: Babel and the Eremites
@keys: Babel, Eremites, Matriarch Babel
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Tanit Matriarch Babel asked Liloupar to lead the way to the Eternal Oasis. Liloupar asked her to dance and entertain them, mocking her servility,, then agreed only because the Traveler wished it. She holds the Eremites in low regard for their attempts to use her.
### LARGE +
Liloupar warned that the contract with the Traveler cannot be seized and that she would watch Babel on her master's account.

## entry: Collei, Nicole and the Windrise wish
@keys: Collei, Nicole, god remains, Windrise wish
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
At Windrise, beneath the great oak, Liloupar asked Collei to let her take the remnants of the Goddess of Flowers from her body. She offered to make Collei an ordinary human in return. Collei agreed, Liloupar absorbed the remains and her bottle's light went out. Collei felt no different afterward, since she had already received what she longed for.
### LARGE +
Nicole had befriended Liloupar and brought her to Windrise; Liloupar asked her to invite Collei. Liloupar told Collei she was nothing like Nabu Malikata and had a richer heart. She framed the exchange as 'a beautiful and secret transaction between two women'.
### ULTRA +
Collei's trace had come from Fatui experiments with the goddess's remains. Nahida said earlier the remains were not hurting Collei.

## entry: Gurabad
@keys: Gurabad, City of Kings, Gurabad's Ruin, Murals of Gurabad
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Gurabad was the ancient 'City of Kings' where Ormazd, Parvezravan and Shiruyeh ruled; the city fell into a chasm filled with Abyssal power. Liloupar returned there in Memories of Gurabad (3.4) to face it. In 6.6 the Fatui disturbed its sealed Jinn, and she awoke to calm them.
### LARGE +
She says she merged with the land beneath the city to suppress the dark calamity, and that it went silent when something changed in a western land; the Traveler suspects this was the defeat of Gosoythoth in Natlan.
### ULTRA +
She says she wants to find Shirin and Shiruyeh in Gurabad and that facing the Abyss is her fated duty.

## entry: The magic bottle and true-name contract
@keys: magic bottle, true name, Jinni contract, Jinni in the Magic Bottle
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Jinn bind themselves to humans by giving them a true name, which lets the holder command them; the Jinn are said to hate humankind in the legends, though Liloupar says one does not hate vermin. She chose the Traveler as master and warned that only death can break the contract. In gameplay she became a gadget in Sumeru's desert.
### LARGE +
Gameplay (labelled): the gadget increases visibility in sandstorms, dispels small vortices and marks Jinn Shrines; an item description says she uses what remains of her power.

## entry: Seven Pillars of Deshret
@keys: Seven Pillars, Thoth, Heryshaf, Sobek

@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In 6.6 Liloupar met Thoth, Heryshaf and Sobek imprisoned in Aaru and laughed at them, mocking their airs. Thoth asked how King Deshret's seal had let her go; she told him to ask her master one day. In Darkness Looming she called the Seven Pillars underwhelming and unworthy of her attention.

## entry: The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin (unreliable account)
@keys: Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin, Brass Mask Rebellion
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A Jinni's story in two books found in Sumeru which gives Liloufar's name but omits her scheming, blaming the banquet deaths on slaves and a resentful Shirin. In-world it is an entertainment 'of unknown origin and full of absurdities'. Treat it as legend, not as Liloupar's own account.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Ages ago: born from the water lilies along Nabu Malikata's footsteps; lives in Ay-Khanoum.
- After Nabu Malikata's death: serves King Deshret; later helps Ormazd rise and defeat Huvashtra.
- Over three generations: birth of Shirin, secret birth of Parvezravan, Shiruyeh's rise; Gurabad falls to Abyssal power.
- Afterward: King Deshret splits her soul into seven fragments.
- 3.0: first mentioned in The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin.
- 3.4: released; The Dirge of Bilqis (Temple Where Sand Flows Like Tears, Dune-Entombed Fecundity I-III, The Eternal Dream, Ever Lush) and Memories of Gurabad.
- 6.6: awakens in A Divergence Leading to Oneself, travels with the group in Ancient Shadows Re-Emerge and Darkness Looming, and fades in Your Past Is Like a Garden.
### ULTRA +
- In 3.4 she takes the Traveler as master, guides them through the tunnels, fights Ferigees, learns that Nabu Malikata is dead, then separates from the bottle at Gurabad.
- After Gosoythoth's fall she sleeps; the Fatui disturb Gurabad in 6.6.
- Final scene: Windrise, with Collei, Nicole, Paimon and the Traveler.
