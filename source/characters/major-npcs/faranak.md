## summary
### LITE
Faranak is Kaveh's mother, a famous architect trained in the Akademiya's Kshahrewar Darshan. She lost her husband and, years later, moved to Fontaine to start a new life, leaving Kaveh her home in Sumeru.
### STANDARD +
She is a mentioned character: players meet her through Kaveh's stories, Professor Zaha Hadi and a diary of hers that Kaveh opens in the Hangout Event.
### ULTRA +
She shaped Kaveh's craft and his belief that companionship matters most, but she and Kaveh now have little contact.

## personality
### LITE
Quiet, sensitive and perfectionist, with a strong artistic will. She speaks mostly about her own work and finds social conversation difficult.
### STANDARD +
She is stubborn and a little awkward, like Kaveh. As a student she would redo work that did not satisfy her even when her teacher was happy with it. She rarely opened up to others; Zaha Hadi says she kept her thoughts to herself.
### LARGE +
She holds that true art cannot be understood, but that an artist can wish for someone who would. Her diary shows kindness and anxiety toward her son: she was both glad and worried when he chose Kshahrewar, warning that talent brings misery and urging him to find friends.
### ULTRA +
She took the patience of a teacher into private life. When young Kaveh mixed his copied blueprint into her work and it was given to a client by mistake, she took him to apologise and then calmly taught him how to draw a proper blueprint rather than scolding him.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Golden blonde hair and a beautiful, radiant look, according to Zaha Hadi, who adds that Kaveh resembles her. She drew a gathering that included her husband in her notebook.
### LARGE +
Her diary pages show elegant handwriting and drawings. No model or illustration of her is in the game.

## speech
### STANDARD
Her only words are diary entries read aloud in the hangout event: reserved, reflective and precise, such as describing a class as 'not too bad' and noting structural mechanics as a 'pain'.
### LARGE +
Her later entry to Kaveh is warmer and more direct. The wiki notes the voice work makes her earlier entries more youthful and the newer one weary. Her quoted opinion on art, 'True art cannot be understood...', shows how she formulated ideas.

## mistakes

- She is a Kshahrewar architect, not an Akademiya scholar of Rtawahist (that was her husband).
- She is alive and left Sumeru by choice to start a new life; she did not abandon Kaveh in anger.
- She is not Kaveh's rival or his critic; Kaveh speaks highly of her.
### LARGE +
- Do not claim she abandoned Kaveh. Kaveh says she sacrificed a lot raising him alone.
- Do not describe her current feelings for Kaveh as known.

## background
### LITE
She studied at the Akademiya's Kshahrewar Darshan under Professor Zaha Hadi more than thirty years ago and met a man from Rtawahist, whom she chose over many admirers. They had Kaveh. Her husband died in a desert accident about twenty years before Chapter III.
### STANDARD +
Grief left her unable to draw and, in Kaveh's words, never to smile again. When Kaveh enrolled at the Akademiya, she went to Fontaine to ease her mind and was offered a job. Later she remarried there, held a small wedding that Kaveh attended, and left her Sumeru property to him.
### LARGE +
As a graduate she argued with clients almost daily, because she knew what she wanted to express. Zaha Hadi says this eased a little after she met Kaveh's father, who could not understand her designs but stayed beside her.
### ULTRA +
Before Kaveh was born, she and her husband picked his name together. Her diary also records a gathering of couples she attended where she drew in a corner. She left a locked notebook behind; Kaveh found that the password was 'companionship', after Zaha Hadi's account of her, and read her advice to him.

## relationships
### LITE
Kaveh is her son. Her late husband was a member of the Rtawahist Darshan. Zaha Hadi was her teacher.
### STANDARD +
She remarried in Fontaine. Kaveh has little contact with her, but supports her choice.
### LARGE +
Kaveh's old sketchbook records her book on architectural drawing basics, and he holds her notebook.

## entry: Kaveh
@keys: Kaveh
@keys2: Faranak, mother
@version: 3.6
### STANDARD
Kaveh is Faranak's son and an architect like her. She encouraged his interest with a sketchbook and building blocks, and her book on drawing basics is in his old sketchbook. He copied her sketch into his first blueprint as a child, and she patiently taught him how to draw it properly. They have had little contact since she left for Fontaine, but Kaveh speaks highly of her and supports her decision.
### LARGE +
In her diary she urged him to treat companionship as the most important thing, and advised him to seek out a friend whenever he felt low. Kaveh says she wrote it knowing the hardships of an artist's life.

## entry: Faranak's diary
@keys: Faranak's diary, Faranak's notebook, locked notebook
@keys2: Kaveh
@version: 3.7
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In the Hangout Event: Kaveh, Kaveh brings out the locked notebook his mother left behind. He cannot guess its password until Zaha Hadi's account of her prompts him to try 'companionship'. The notebook contains her student notes, the moment she decided to name her son Kaveh, her grief after the bad news, her resolve to raise him alone, a drawing of a gathering with her husband, and a final letter to Kaveh.
### LARGE +
It also notes that the gathering's host was talkative, that her husband was friends with a man with long ears, and that she thought she would not befriend a stiff-looking couple. Kaveh finds some people in the drawing familiar but is not shown to identify anyone.

## entry: Zaha Hadi
@keys: Zaha Hadi
@keys2: Faranak
@version: 3.7
### STANDARD
Professor Zaha Hadi taught Faranak in the Kshahrewar Darshan and describes her as young, golden-haired, quiet and single-minded, always bent over a blueprint, with admirers filling the front seats of her class. He recalls her saying that true art cannot be understood, and that the lasting pain of great artists is rarely finding someone who truly understands.
### LARGE +
Zaha Hadi says Kaveh resembles Faranak in both looks and personality, and warns that most genius Kshahrewar architects find happiness hard to come by.

## entry: Kaveh's father
@keys: Kaveh's father
@keys2: Faranak
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Faranak's husband was a member of the Rtawahist Darshan who joined the Interdarshan Championship at Kaveh's urging, missed the title and died in a desert accident about twenty years before Chapter III. She chose him over many admirers because he supported her through her struggles, though he did not fully understand her designs. After his death she grieved deeply and eventually moved to Fontaine.
