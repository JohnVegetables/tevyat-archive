## summary
### LITE
A noble-born schemer of Mondstadt's Aristocratic period, the illegitimate son of a great family, who wanted to restore the old nobility's glory and was willing to destroy people to do it.
### STANDARD +
He is known entirely through weapon texts and the Dragonspine expedition journals. Those sources describe a charming, manipulative man who treated everyone close to him as a tool. Nobody in the surviving record knows what became of him.

## background
### LITE
Eberhart was an illegitimate heir of an aristocratic house, probably Landrich's. Resentful of his status and fed on tales of the old aristocracy's glory, he came to hate the rotten state of his own class and decided to tear it down and take its place. He owned a gladiator, learned the spear from a witch, and led an expedition to Dragonspine that ended in murder.
### STANDARD +
Nobles were expected to study the sword; the spear was left to foreign gladiators and traitors. Eberhart took it up anyway and, by the weapon texts' account, enjoyed the night breeze with his spear tip catching the dew. He kept an unnamed gladiator fighting with glib promises even after the man had won his freedom, until Vennessa killed him. He drove his legitimate half-brother Parsifal toward life as a phantom thief and then deceived him into fleeing, which cleared the line of legitimacy. He became the Blue-Eyed Spear Witch's disciple to find the flaw in her art, then killed her, exploiting her love for Parsifal.
### LARGE +
On the Dragonspine expedition, which was made up mostly of young nobles, he spoke of a buried civilisation and power from the heavens. In truth he had already laid traps, and the party dwindled to himself, Ingbert and Luther. He killed Ingbert with a polearm and mortally wounded Luther, but was badly hurt himself. He made it back to his boat. His servant Priscilla was to light a beacon at Stormbearer Point if the plot failed; the aristocracy uncovered the plan and presumably killed her. Luther guessed the aim was to move up the line of succession, since Landrich was about to give Eberhart the family name.
### ULTRA +
Luther's reading of the betrayal is itself a surviving account, written while bleeding in the cold, and it may colour the motive; the weapon texts instead stress his wounded pride at illegitimacy and his dream of the old nobility's glory. His own recorded line is that he would do anything and accept the contempt of those who came after.

## status
### LITE
Fate unknown. He was gravely wounded on Dragonspine and no record shows him returning; he is presumed long dead. No appearance after the Aristocratic period.
### LARGE +
According to the wiki, the historian Stevens says in the Dragonspine quest A Land Entombed that no record of his return exists. As of 7.1 he has not been tied to any Nod-Krai or Snezhnaya content.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Parsifal and Ingbert were his half-brothers and both were wronged by him: one driven into exile, the other murdered. The Blue-Eyed Spear Witch was his teacher, and Priscilla his devoted servant, and both were sacrificed to the plan. The gladiator was a tool he kept in the arena. He saw all of them as steps.

## entry: Eberhart's Dragonspine Plot
@keys: Eberhart, Dragonspine expedition
### STANDARD
The young nobles' expedition to Dragonspine was recorded in the Ancient Investigation Journals. Eberhart steered the party to a different site than planned and used false comfort to reduce its numbers, then personally attacked Ingbert and Luther. Luther's last entry warns of him.
### LARGE +
The journal's author, Luther, writes that Eberhart's talk of a buried civilisation was bait and that the polearm he drew was unbecoming of a noble.

## entry: Missive Windspear Tale
@keys: Missive Windspear, Priscilla
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
The Missive Windspear, an event reward, tells of Priscilla and her "young master" Eberhart, who promised the winds of change and told her to light a beacon at Stormbearer Point if his plan was betrayed. The aristocracy's falcons and hounds found the plot first and no sails came home.
