## summary
### LITE
Parsifal was a legitimate heir of an aristocratic house of Old Mondstadt who became the city's gentleman thief, robbing the rich to give to the poor. Betrayed by his half-brother Eberhart, he fled to Liyue, became the Skipper's Chief Mate and died with him fighting a sea monster.
### STANDARD +
He is known only from item texts: the Alley Flash and Alley Hunter weapons, the Wine and Song ballad book, the Iron Sting weapon, the Heart of Depth artifact set and one line in a Dragonspine journal. The commoners' songs remember a happy ending; the artifacts tell a bleaker one.

## background
### LITE
A disobedient boy fond of chivalric play-acting, he was egged on by Eberhart to steal a sword from his family's vault and live as a thief among the streets. Eberhart later deceived him, forcing him to bury the sword and flee over the sea.
### STANDARD +
Nobody feared him more than the aristocrats: people then dreaded lighting fires after dark, so the darkened sword and, later, a black bow let him vanish easily. The commoners loved him, but he would rather drink with friends than court anyone, until one night he left a mansion with a stolen silver cup and saw a woman at a window whose eyes matched the sapphire in it. He pried the gem loose and gave it to her. It carried the ruling family's crest; her master punished her for owning it, she grew angry with him while still loving him, and at their next meeting she told him she would one day repay the scar he had caused. They apparently never met again.
Some of his loot went to a mysterious shopkeeper who collected rare things. Eberhart's deception ended it all; Parsifal buried his beloved sword under the moon and sailed away, having earlier vowed to use his strength to turn the dark world around, even by a little.
### LARGE +
In Liyue he arrived with only a slim, edgeless sword (Iron Sting) and a sapphire-blue feather pinned to his mantle. Locals guessed he had disgraced his house; the truth was never told. He fell in with the Skipper, a rough captain whose own crew was lost, and was made Chief Mate despite being drunk almost constantly. He hummed his own bad shanty, polished the blade at dusk and remembered the hometown girl and the promise to meet again. When the Skipper set out to take revenge on a great sea creature that had haunted him, Parsifal went too.
### ULTRA +
The Heart of Depth texts voice his bitterness: he calls the past a thing he keeps forgetting, says all deaths are in vain, and quotes the bards who say the thief could not avoid the gallows. The Skipper reminds him he signed a contract when he came aboard. His last words, as recorded on the Gilded Corsage, wonder whether he has forsaken truth for illusions, and add that perhaps this is not the worst of endings. The crew's feather washed ashore, blood-stained red, with the corpse of a great sea creature.

## status
### LITE
Dead; he and the Skipper died killing a sea monster. He has no part in 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content.
### STANDARD +
Mondstadt never learned his real ending. The ballad Wine and Song, popular in the alleys and taverns, ends with the thief and the witch toppling the nobles and warming each other's days forever. The text itself calls this a vulgar, carefree tale with a happy ending that bards offered as a motivational story.
### LARGE +
Per The First Great Magic as reported on the wiki, a playwright of Fontaine later wrote a play from the story of the thief and the witch, and its stage names inspired the magician Parsifal the Great and her sister Josephine, a link kept in their own entries.

## relationships
### LITE
Eberhart, his half-brother, betrayed him. The Blue-Eyed Spear Witch was his love and was later killed by that same half-brother. The Skipper gave him a place and a purpose in his last years.
### STANDARD +
Parsifal never knew how Eberhart used the witch's love for him to kill her. His brothers (Ingbert among them) advised him to abandon his vow before he sailed. His father Landrich headed the house he left.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Texts give no face or measurements. He is described as a gentleman thief in a black-bladed noble sword's company, later a hard-drinking Chief Mate in a tricorne hat, a slim sword at his waist and a blue feather on his mantle.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Aristocratic period: leaves home with the Alley Flash; becomes a vigilante thief; gives the sapphire to the witch.
- Later: Eberhart deceives him; he buries the sword and sails for Liyue.
- Liyue: joins the Skipper; follows him against the sea monster; dies.
- Roughly five hundred years later (per the wiki, from The First Great Magic): a Fontainian play based on him and the witch.
- 1.2: the Heart of Depth set and Journal Part III first tell his fate (wiki change history).
