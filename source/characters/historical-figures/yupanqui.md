## summary
### LITE
Natlan's legendary Firethief: a boastful young swordsman who grew into a quiet, one-eyed veteran and died after stealing the Turnfire from the tyrant Och-Kan. The Scions of the Canopy claim him as their ancestor.
### STANDARD +
He is known only through tales and relic texts, never in person. The tribe tells it as the story of a hero who set his people free and paid with his life.

## background
### LITE
A brash young duelist, he was tricked into a mire by Och-Kan and joined Xbalanque's party out of wounded pride. Later he served the Grand Alliance as an ordnance officer until the Scarlet-Eyed Youth asked him to join a rebellion.
### STANDARD +
The Earth Shaker's text says his contributions to Xbalanque's party were outweighed by the trouble he caused: he ignored advice, put himself in danger and annoyed the quiet artisan Ixquieh, yet his companions tolerated his youth. He lost an eye on that journey. His greatsword felled countless enemies. He remembered the party's silent priest telling him that he would one day understand why he walked the "verdant path", and that each person answers to the fire for their deeds.
### LARGE +
Years on he was an aging hero who hoped that humans and Saurians could live together in peace. It was a time when Saurians were subjugated and protecting them was condemned as a crime, because of Och-Kan's hatred of dragons. Och-Kan, his only surviving friend of old, still lamented that his own dream of humans and dragons coexisting was out of reach. Yupanqui could not bring himself to rebel against him, accepted the idle post of ordnance officer, and waited for the day he would return to the Sacred Flame.
### ULTRA +
The Scarlet-Eyed Youth came alone at night, after saving the Saurians from the shadow of the sword on the Isle of Tozoz, and pleaded with him to break the chains of the broken oath. The old man did not answer directly; he glanced at the dusty greatsword beside him, and in his dull eyes a spark flashed. The Dark Fruit of Bright Flowers text lists him as the taciturn warrior who came after the young girl Sakkuk and the hero twins, willing to lend his strength because the Youth had saved the Saurians. When the Youth said the plan needed someone who knew the city's layout better than anyone, Yupanqui named a craftsman he had heard of through rumour: the Masterful Craftsman, whose name history later lost.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon gives little: he lost one eye, and he is described as a taciturn old man in his later years. His weapon was a heavy greatsword, the Earth Shaker.
### LARGE +
His remaining eye is where the Crownless Crown text places his last sight: the new world, which the text calls his greatest reward.

## personality
### STANDARD
Young: reckless, loud, proud, quick to challenge anyone, and later embarrassed by it. Old: steadfast, reticent and reserved, weighed down by loyalty to an old friend and by regret. The shift is the point of the tale.
### LARGE +
The Dark Fruit text calls him a powerful hero who hoped Saurians and humans would live together in peace. The Moment of Attainment text calls him the great hero trusted by all warriors, taciturn to the end.

## abilities
### STANDARD
A heavy-blade fighter whose swings felled countless enemies, by the Earth Shaker's account. He is also said to have stolen the Turnfire, a fire that burns people from behind. Canon states no Vision or element for him.

## relationships
### LITE
Companion of Xbalanque; old friend, then opponent, of Och-Kan; joined the Scarlet-Eyed Youth's rebellion; ancestor-hero of the Scions of the Canopy and source of the Malipo name Kinich bears.
### STANDARD +
Och-Kan was the old friend he could not bring himself to fight until the end. The tribe remembers that he was friends with both Och-Kan and Xbalanque, and that he did not like Och-Kan's cruelty.
### LARGE +
In the uprising he stood among the heroes who rose together: the young Sakkuk, the twins Atawallpa and Waskar, and the Scarlet-Eyed Youth as leader. Sakkuk's Crownless Crown text calls him the "silent hero".

## status
### LITE
Long dead, he is remembered as a hero and as a cautionary story about looking back.
### STANDARD +
His fire burned a hundred days, according to the Scions' story, until it pierced the Ley Lines and fell into the Night Kingdom, where it is said to burn still. The elders say it lights the way to the next life and that the dead must be purified by it before they are reborn. Children say it is just a story.
### LARGE +
Each Turnfire Night the tribe honours its heroes, Burkina among them. As of 7.1 there is no sign that he returned or that anyone claims to have met him. In the present-day story the Abyss Lector Enjou was trying to learn about the Turnfire.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Youth: duels passers-by; beaten by Och-Kan; joins Xbalanque's party; loses an eye.
- Later: Grand Alliance ordnance officer, loyal to his old friend Och-Kan.
- The uprising: joins the Scarlet-Eyed Youth, breaks his tribe's Saurian free, steals the Turnfire; dies.
### ULTRA +
- 4.6: first referenced in the Unfinished Reverie artifact texts.
- 5.0: the Earth Shaker weapon text and the Yupanqui's Turnfire story chapter tell his story; Kinich's Deal discusses the price of the Turnfire.

## knowledge
### STANDARD
He is dead; he knows nothing after the uprising. In the legend he never learned whether his burning was the price of the theft or of turning back.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- He is not the Scarlet-Eyed Youth, nor the second Pyro Archon. He is a human hero.
- He did not devise the rebellion: the plan came from a craftsman, and the Youth led the party.
- Do not state as fact that he was the first Malipo or one of a fixed Six Heroes.
- Canon names no spouse, children or romance for him.
### LARGE +
- The Turnfire is not an ordinary fire: its victims feel it behind them, and looking back means being burned.
- Och-Kan was not there when Yupanqui turned; only a burned city and an army in ruins were behind him.

## scenario

## entry: Turnfire (Malipo)
@keys: Turnfire, Malipo, Turnfire Night
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
The Turnfire first appeared in the era of the Grand Alliance and was used by Och-Kan to rule and to burn cities. Anyone hit by it feels a terrible burning from behind and is burned to a crisp if they turn to look. Yupanqui stole it, and the soldiers it was thrown at were turned to ash. As he left the city, he heard Och-Kan call from behind him, turned, and saw only a burned city and an army in ruins. The flames then burst from inside his eyes. The tribe says he burned for a hundred days before the fire fell into the Night Kingdom.
### LARGE +
Malipo, the ancient name tied to the fire, passed to Burkina five hundred years ago and now to Kinich, whom children call "Turnfire Kinich". The Scions commemorate their heroes on Turnfire Night. Some elders insist the ceremony must be led by the bearer of the name. Kinich himself says everything in the world comes at a price, even for the Fire-Bringer who stole the Turnfire.
### ULTRA +
In the story quest a stranger wonders aloud whether Yupanqui paid for stealing the flame or for turning back, and the children say nobody knows. The elders say the fire lights the path to the next life: the dead must accept the flames and be purified first, like a last look back at their lives.

## entry: Earth Shaker
@keys: Earth Shaker, Yupanqui's greatsword
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
A heavy claymore that people claim the Firethief loved. They say even the flames of the Lord of Cinder Keep could not burn it away. Its two description pages tell Yupanqui's youth and old age, and in the second the old hero glances at the dusty sword when the Scarlet-Eyed Youth asks for his help.
### LARGE +
The Moment of Attainment text says that on the day of the uprising Yupanqui used his sword to shatter the shackles on his tribe's Saurian. Mavuika is seen wielding it in the Version 5.0 trailer and Archon Quest, where she had borrowed it from Kinich.

## entry: Yupanqui and Och-Kan
@keys: Och-Kan, Sacred Lord, Lord of Cinder Keep
@keys2: Yupanqui, Firethief
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
Och-Kan, the half-human hero who followed Xbalanque, tricked the young Yupanqui into a mire. They remained comrades for a long time. When Och-Kan became tyrant, Yupanqui disliked his cruelty but did not rise against him for years, because he was Yupanqui's only surviving friend of old. In the end Yupanqui stole the Turnfire and used it on Och-Kan's army. See Och-Kan's own entry for the Sacred Lord's story.
