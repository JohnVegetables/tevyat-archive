## summary
### LITE
Aurelius was one of the four Harmosts, the governors who ruled the ancient kingdom of Remuria beneath the God King Remus. A warrior-musician and military commander, he is remembered for promoting Boethius and for dying at sea when traitors sank his flagship.
### STANDARD +
Almost everything known about him comes from Remurian inscriptions and notes found in Fontaine's ruins, not from any scene, so he is a figure of reputation rather than personality.
### ULTRA +
His name also survives in a very different form: a modern Fontainian stage trilogy shares it, though its hero is regarded as invented.

## background
### LITE
Remuria's Harmosts were its governors, military commanders, diplomats and educators, and in a realm where music was a source of power they were trained as musicians as well as soldiers. Aurelius is credited with pacifying many tribes for the empire, playing the melodies of ten legions, and raising Boethius up the ranks.
### STANDARD +
A note preserved at the Collegium Phonascorum records that Aurelius first divided all phenomena into four categories: Substance, Property, Quality and Relation. Quality was treated as the keystone, covering memories, wishes, the soul and persona. The same notes use this scheme to argue about music, the will and the power called Phobos. They are the Collegium's writings rather than Aurelius's own words, and they argue a harsh position that those who do not follow Phobos are not equals.
### LARGE +
The Remurian epitaph says he passed order and civilisation to those who succeeded his authority. This is Remuria praising its own: it calls the northern peoples barbarians who repaid kindness with treason, and promises that their savagery will be repaid twelve times over.
### ULTRA +
The wiki reports that his research and theories were later used by the Narzissenkreuz Ordo. Treat this as the wiki's link, not an in-universe statement found in the notes themselves.

## relationships
### STANDARD
His best-attested bond is with Boethius, whom he promoted. The God King Remus appointed him; the other Harmosts were Euergetia, Cassiodor and Boethius, and the canon read here shows no direct interaction with the first two.

## status
### LITE
Dead. Traitors on Remuria's imperial flagship sank the ship with him aboard, and his grave is unknown; the epitaph calls the sea itself his tomb.
### STANDARD +
Nothing in canon through 7.1 shows him alive, and no relic of his is known. His name now also belongs to the invented hero of a Fontainian play trilogy.
### LARGE +
The Alta Semita engraving, which calls on Phobos to curse the traitors for all eternity, is the only account of his death, and it says his flagship had forty rows of oars.

## entry: Harmosts of Remuria
@keys: Harmost, Harmosts
@keys2: Remuria, Remus, Aurelius
@version: 4.0
### STANDARD
The Harmosts were the four governors of Remuria, appointed by the God King Remus, with authority second only to his. Aurelius, Boethius, Cassiodor and Euergetia held the office. They served as commanders, envoys and educators and were skilled in both music and war.

## entry: The Saga of Aurelius
@keys: The Saga of Aurelius, Saga of Aurelius
@keys2: Coppelius, Fontaine, play
@version: 4.0
### STANDARD
In modern Fontaine, the trilogy "The Saga of Aurelius" is credited to the playwright Coppelius. The Silvershower Heartstrings text calls its Aurelius the later form of an older hero, and describes tales of his maddening love and a bowstring that snapped. The wiki holds that the hero has no historical counterpart. Do not present the play's romance as the Harmost's real biography.
