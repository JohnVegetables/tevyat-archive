## summary
### LITE
Yalyane Morevna, the "Knyazhna of Blood", was the first of the Snegovik and princess of the House of Hesperides in the age after Hyperborea fell. She was feared for slaughtering fae kindreds, yet sheltered human clans, and she taught the young future Belyi Tsar the spear.
### STANDARD +
She is a figure of legend more than record. The General History of Snezhnaya admits she barely appears in authentic annals and that most accounts come from folk song. She vanished without a trace before Kitezhgrad was founded, and later Snegovik remember her as their ancestor.
### LARGE +
Three kinds of source describe her: a scholarly history (which doubts and hedges), weapon stories (which are literary retellings) and a Snegurochka's private journal (which treats her as ancestor and lawgiver). They agree on three points: ferocity against fae, protection of humans, and a teaching bond with Monomakh.

## background
### LITE
A survivor of the fallen golden city of Hyperborea according to legend, she led the Snegovik at Hesperides in the fae age. She plundered and razed many fae kindreds, but not human clans, and she taught the youth Monomakh the spear before he went on his travels.
### STANDARD +
The Frostbreath story shows the lessons: harsh training that even a fae would find severe, a young man gasping on the ice, and a question about whether stronger spears exist. She answered that he could not yet beat her, and that she herself had once seen a spear blow that pierced the frozen plains and smashed a tide of demons. That account kept him training. When he left, he took with him her teaching about mercy and not spilling blood on the white snow, as he told Filipov Vseslavyevich.
### LARGE +
A Hesperides document records a rule attributed to her: the one who leads the Snegovik must have a complete soul, which a Snegovik can gain only by true love. Larisa sought love to meet it; Pavlina, a rival for the title, became princess anyway and resented the rule's cost. The Winter's Heavy Heart text (7.1) gives a bleaker inner view: she had never known love, purged even her own kind who shared her hatred of mankind, and opened Hesperides to displaced mortal clans. When no one's gratitude brought her love, and the chosen successor left, she feared fading into the wind and sought more slaughter to keep her soul from growing pale. This is an item story and reads as poetry, not as a record.
### ULTRA +
Origin: the item texts (Winter's Heavy Heart and Deep Gallery's Echoing Song) are read together by the wiki to say the first Snegovik came from frost left when the angel Koitar kissed Anacharsis in Hyperborea. The weapon text says her "Lady and Mother" had freed her from a curse that bound her to destiny. The General History's author shows ambivalence about her: he questions whether courage began as crime, notes she never ravaged human clans, and contrasts The Davograd Chronicles's insult ("a soulless wretch") with the lack of any listed plunder.

## status
### LITE
Missing and presumed lost long ago: she vanished without trace before Monomakh founded Kitezhgrad, and no canon account says what became of her.
### STANDARD +
As of 7.1 she has never appeared in person. She survives in the General History of Snezhnaya, in two weapon stories (Frostbreath, 7.0; Winter's Heavy Heart, 7.1) and in the Snegurochka's own memory of her as ancestor.
### LARGE +
Her rule about the leader's soul was still honoured at Hesperides in the time of Larisa and Pavlina. After the cataclysm five hundred years ago, the Snegovik lords' coronation ceremonies stopped using Winter's Heavy Heart, per its description.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Belyi Tsar (Monomakh): her pupil, who called her his teacher. Snegovik/Hesperides: her kin and domain; her granddaughter was the first Grand Duchess. Pavlina and Larisa: later leaders who invoked her rule about a complete soul. Filipov: connected only through Monomakh.
### LARGE +
Koitar and Anacharsis: a likely origin through the frost of their meeting (strongly supported, not stated). Human clans: she sheltered them against monsters, per The Davograd Chronicles and other accounts, and never ravaged them. The fae kindreds she plundered are unnamed.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Canon gives no full description. Weapon texts call her the "crimson princess of the snow" and the "scarlet Knyazhna of Blood", with blood on the skirts of her gown, and say her cold eyes held a trace of nostalgia for the golden city.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
She cannot be played as a living character here; she is a legend. If she is referenced, give accounts from sources: the History hedges, the Davograd author sneers, the songs romanticise. Do not give her dialogue except the recorded lines of her lesson, which survive only as retold by others.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- She is not the Belyi Tsar's mother, nor Pavlina's grandmother; she is the grandmother of the first Grand Duchess.
- She did not kill Monomakh, and she did not slaughter human clans.
- Her death, her appearance and the identity of "Mother" are not stated; do not fill them in.
- Do not call her a Snegurochka in the later courtly sense; the weapon texts call her the first Snegovik.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Age of Hyperborea's fall: she is said to be a survivor; the first Snegovik according to item texts.
- Fae age: princess at the House of Hesperides; plunders fae kindreds, shelters human clans.
- Youth of Monomakh: takes him in, teaches him the spear and mercy; he leaves on his travels and meets Filipov.
- Before the founding of Kitezhgrad: vanishes without trace.
- Long after: her rule on the Grand Duchess's soul shapes the succession of Larisa and Pavlina.
- 7.0: appears in the General History of Snezhnaya, the Hesperides document and Frostbreath; 7.1: Winter's Heavy Heart story.

### STANDARD
The title reflects her savage raids on fae kindreds. The General History says she plundered and razed many of them, and the author wonders whether courage began as a crime. The same book notes she never ravaged human clans and, according to The Davograd Chronicles and other accounts, took humans into Hesperides for protection against monsters.

## entry: Yalyane and the young Monomakh
@keys: Yalyane, Morevna
@keys2: Belyi Tsar, Monomakh, spear
@version: 7.0
### STANDARD
She taught the future Belyi Tsar the spear in harsh sessions. She said she had once seen a spear strike that crossed rivers and mountains and smashed demons. Later, seeing rifles, the Tsar decided the spear she described was not meant to be thrown. The Frostbreath story says his supreme spear art differed slightly from her teaching.

### STANDARD
