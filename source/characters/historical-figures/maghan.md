## summary
### LITE
Maghan, first called Dinga, was the attendant of the last Holy Sovereign of a valley in the Tezcatepetonco Range and later a hero beside Xbalanque. About 2,000 years ago he killed his master on Och-Kan's order, then founded the Masters of the Night-Wind (Mictlan).
### STANDARD +
He is known mostly through weapon and item texts (the Flute of Ezpitzal, Starcaller's Watch, Grainfruit) rather than quests, and the accounts do not fully agree on whether he was a dragon slayer or a man who let the dragons slip away.
### ULTRA +
In Natlan's tellings, his is a story of obedience used as an act of love: the order he carried out was also his master's own last request, and the tribe built on that deed keeps the dream-pact with the dragons that he never himself ended.

## background
### LITE
As Dinga he served a girl made Holy Sovereign of a village protected by a dragonlord. She sent him to follow Xbalanque; when Och-Kan later condemned the village, Maghan led the attack that killed her. He then retired and founded Mictlan.
### STANDARD +
The village lay in a deep valley that later took the name Mictlan. Its people owed their existence to the Weaver of Secrets, who built a labyrinth of mirrors and a fortress of mist around them in return for a Sovereign who would guide the dragons into dreams. Such a Sovereign was chosen as a child with a "star-bright soul", blinded and had her ankles maimed, and was expected to die young. Dinga was her only companion. Item text says the schemer Kukulkan wanted the Sovereign to turn on the dragons and Dinga sent away, but she had already foreseen it. She told Dinga to spread the stories of heroes among the tribes, record memories on woven scrolls, and, when they met again, pierce her heart. She gave him the Starcaller's Watch, later a relic of the tribe.
### LARGE +
After the dragon civilisation fell, with Xiuhcoatl slain and Cinder City founded, Och-Kan branded the valley's people traitors for having served the dragons. Dinga, now called Maghan the dragon slayer, led the attack, destroyed the maze and fortress and killed everyone inside, including his master, who became the tribe's Wayob. Granted an audience, he turned down the king's reward, saying he had failed to catch the beasts that escaped into the smoky valleys, and asked to retire because he was old. This is the origin of Mictlan. He was a priest-king as well, and the Grainfruit legend has him following the night spirits' guidance, planting animal teeth in silt and watering them with his own blood so that a famine ended.
### ULTRA +
Later accounts add that he was said to wear a blindfold all his life and insisted that scroll weavers cover their eyes, a custom ended by the fourth high priest, Hanga, because the scrolls came out a mess. His successor, the young priest Wagadu, renewed the pact with the Weaver of Secrets after Cinder City fell, which brought forth the first Iktomisaurus. At his own wish Maghan was buried among unmarked graves and not returned to the Sacred Flame. The Flute text adds a prophecy his master gave him: the old pact would be broken and a new one made, a city of jade would arise, and a master of flames would watch over humans and dragons.

## relationships
### LITE
His master the Holy Sovereign; Xbalanque (journey companion); Och-Kan (ordered the killing); Kukulkan (schemer she warned him about).
### STANDARD +
The valley's patron was the dragonlord Ix Kemonel Jasjatem Oox, whom Kukulkan despised as decadent. His successor Wagadu and the later high priests carried on the tribe he founded.
### LARGE +
His master told him to trust neither the Lord of the Night, nor the "saints", nor the self-styled sage, because humans can rely only on themselves. The Watch text suggests this advice shaped the tribe's independence.

## status
### LITE
Dead about two thousand years; buried in an unmarked grave, remembered as Mictlan's founder.
### STANDARD +
His tribe still guards the Night Kingdom's lore, and his Grainfruit legend is told in Natlan today. No appearance or mention was found in Nod-Krai or Snezhnaya content as of 7.1.

## entry: The Holy Sovereign of Mictlan
@keys: Holy Sovereign, Maghan's Master, Starcaller
@keys2: Maghan, Dinga, Mictlan
@version: 5.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Item texts describe the last Holy Sovereign of the valley as a girl chosen by the Lord of the Night for her star-bright soul, blinded and lamed in tradition. She played a flute, guided the dragons into dreams, and learned from the Weaver of Secrets what lay ahead. She sent Dinga away and asked him to kill her when they met again, using her death to anchor her people to the land. Afterwards she became the tribe's Wayob. The wiki calls her the first wielder of the Starcaller's Watch and says she likely bore the Ancient Name Ukumbuko, but that is inferred.
### LARGE +
She warned him that the self-proclaimed sage was untrustworthy and gave him a vision of a future in which his descendants would live without fear.

## entry: The Grainfruit legend
@keys: Grainfruit
@keys2: Maghan, priest, teeth, famine
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
In the tale printed on Grainfruit, a famine struck because the soil refused seed. The priest Maghan, guided by night spirits, dredged silt from a riverbed, planted animal teeth, stood in it and watered it with his own blood. The teeth grew upwards along his body and became the plant. The description adds that the shaft must not be cut casually, and the wiki treats it as the tale of his sacrifice and death.

## entry: Starcaller's Watch and the Flute of Ezpitzal
@keys: Starcaller's Watch, Flute of Ezpitzal
@version: 5.3
### STANDARD
The Watch is an eagle statuette given to Dinga by his master and later a sacred relic of the tribe; the Flute is a jade priestly flute-like blade passed among the Masters of the Night-Wind. Their texts are the main source for the story of Dinga, the Holy Sovereign, Kukulkan's scheme and Maghan's audience with Och-Kan.
