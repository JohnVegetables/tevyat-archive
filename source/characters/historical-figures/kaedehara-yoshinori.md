## summary
### LITE
Kaedehara Kazuha's great-grandfather and the last head of the Kaedehara Clan's Isshin Art, one of the five Raiden Gokaden sword-smithing traditions. He survived the Case of the Eccentric, kept its truth secret and quietly let the family trade end.
### STANDARD +
He is known through a brief voiced flashback and a hidden letter, found in the limited 2.6 event Hues of the Violet Garden. Once an industrious smith, he later gave up forging and kept bonsai.
### LARGE +
His silence is the centre of his story. He believed a vengeful stranger might return to destroy the Gokaden, so he put his family's safety above his art, even though it cost his son's trust.
### ULTRA +
The same silence made the Kaedehara decline look like plain failure for generations, until his great-grandson read the letter. Two canon accounts of the attacker exist (see status and the entries below).

## background
### LITE
Born into the Niwa Clan, he was adopted into the Kaedehara Clan after his father disappeared and rose to be clan head and leader of the Isshin Art. In his generation the Raiden Shogun's order to forge a Goshintou from a supplied diagram failed; every swordsmith's blade was defective and they fled. He and the Kamisato commissioner of the Yashiro Commission pursued them to a beach and were attacked there.
### STANDARD +
After the incident he and the wounded Kamisato head told a false story, that fleeing smiths had hurt them, to avoid accusations of colluding with the culprit. The Kamisato head died not long afterwards. The Kaedehara and Kamisato reputations suffered. Yoshinori closed the forging business, took up bonsai, and years later left a will asking his son not to throw away one particular plant. Beneath it was a compartment holding a letter written in water-revealed ink.
### LARGE +
His son Hisamichi tried to revive the art, produced the same failed blades from the old diagram and heard his father's rebuke that he must find "the secret within" or let the art fade. In his rage Hisamichi smashed his father's cherished bonsai (a flowerpot thrown against a screen, by Albedo's reading of the soil) and left Inazuma, and the two never spoke again. Albedo later reads that phrase, rather than a complaint about skill, as a hint that the diagram, not the smith, was at fault; Kazuha agrees it is likely. Hisamichi did not take it that way.
### ULTRA +
Generations later the Tenryou Commission seized the Kaedehara estate after Kazuha became a wanted man. In the limited 2.6 event Kazuha, the Traveler, Ayaka, Albedo, Venti and others searched the stored belongings, found the old pot and revealed the letter at the very beach where the encounter took place. Kamisato Ayato had already guessed the truth and used the Five Kasen story at the Irodori Festival to point Kazuha towards it.

## status
### LITE
Dead; the letter was written near the end of his life. His clan's smithing practice ended with him, though Kazuha learned some Kaedehara techniques.
### STANDARD +
His great-grandson Kazuha now knows the truth and has made peace with it. The Isshin Art lives on in a limited way through Kazuha, and the sentient blade Kagotsurube Isshin is tied to the Akame smith Kanenaga, who fled to Snezhnaya after the incident.
### LARGE +
In the 3.3 Interlude Chapter (Act III) the Wanderer erased himself from Irminsul and the story of the attack changed: the culprit is now a descendant of the Tatarasuna blacksmith, killed by the Kaedehara and Kamisato samurai. Yoshinori's letter in the game's current form reflects that. Nothing about him appears in 6.x or 7.0-7.1.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Hisamichi (son, estranged), Kazuha (great-grandson who read his letter), the unnamed Kamisato commissioner (co-pursuer and secret-keeper) and the attacker.
### LARGE +
Ayaka's account is that he was industrious when young, and that the failure took a toll on him. Albedo's reading of his phrase "the secret within" is what pointed the group to the letter. Ayaka supposes that the Kamisato head never told his family out of similar safety concerns.
### ULTRA +
The wiki says Kazuha's father Kageharu was Yoshinori's grandson, but no canon text read here shows them together.

## knowledge
### LITE
He knows the diagram was tampered with and that the Raiden Gokaden was threatened. He does not know who would later read his letter.
### STANDARD +
He believes the safest course for his descendants is to avoid revenge and not be shackled by old feuds, writing that they should not "blindly pursue past foes."

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- He is not a Kamisato; he belonged to the Kaedehara and was born a Niwa.
- Do not give him a fixed attacker without a version: the 2.6 letter says Kunikuzushi, the 3.3 text does not.
- He did not stop forging out of cowardice or lost skill; the letter says it was to protect his children.
- He is not a playable or card character.

## chronology
### LARGE
- About 100 years ago: the Goshintou order fails; Yoshinori and the Kamisato commissioner are attacked on a beach; the cover-up begins.
- Later: Yoshinori ends the family smithing business, takes up bonsai, argues with Hisamichi, who leaves Inazuma.
- At death: a will protects one plant, hiding the letter.
- 2.6 (limited): Hues of the Violet Garden; Kazuha reads the letter.
- 3.3: the Irminsul revision changes the account of the attacker.

## entry: The Case of the Eccentric
@keys: Case of the Eccentric, Goshintou, Isshin Art, Raiden Gokaden
@keys2: Kaedehara, Yoshinori
@version: 2.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Isshin Art was ordered to forge a Goshintou for the Raiden Shogun from a diagram she supplied. Every blade came out defective, the swordsmiths feared punishment and fled, some taking refuge with the Fatui. Yoshinori and the Yashiro commissioner investigated, suspected the diagram had been altered and followed them to a beach near Byakko Plain. The incident became known as the Case of the Eccentric.
### LARGE +
Original account: the culprit was one eccentric stranger who beat the party and left a message for "her," naming himself Kunikuzushi. Later account (3.3): the culprit is the embittered head of a family descended from a Tatarasuna blacksmith, who killed bladesmiths of several schools until he was stopped by the Kaedehara and Kamisato. The Futsu, Hyakume and Senju Arts were lost; only Amenoma and a reduced Isshin line survive.

## entry: Yoshinori's letter
@keys: Kaedehara Yoshinori's Letter, hidden letter, bonsai pot
@keys2: Kazuha, Yoshinori
@version: 2.6
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
A blank sheet in the bottom of a bonsai pot that shows its writing only when wet. It tells of the beach encounter, the cover story, the Kamisato head's advice to stay silent, and a plea that descendants not pursue old enemies or lose themselves to old duty. It was found during the limited Hues of the Violet Garden event; the wording changes slightly after the 3.3 Interlude Chapter.

## entry: The Irminsul revision (3.3)
@keys: Irminsul revision, Wanderer erased, A Dance of Destruction
@keys2: Yoshinori, Kaedehara, Gokaden
@version: 3.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the Interlude Chapter Act III the Wanderer, learning he had been used, removed his own traces from Irminsul. The effects were small and reached some Inazuman history: the Gokaden story now has a vengeful descendant instead of him as the culprit. The Kaedehara line still lost the Isshin Art.

## entry: Hisamichi and the argument
@keys: Kaedehara Hisamichi, Hisamichi
@version: 2.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Yoshinori's son and Kazuha's grandfather. He wanted to bring back the Isshin Art and failed to forge the Goshintou, which led to the quarrel with his father. He left Inazuma and regretted never reconciling, keeping the dead plant exactly as it was.
