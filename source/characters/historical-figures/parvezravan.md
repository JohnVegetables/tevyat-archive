## summary
### LITE
Kisra, called Parvezravan, was a feared conqueror known as the Spirit of Victory and the second-to-last king of Gurabad. Born of the Jinni Liloupar and raised by a divine bird, he took the throne after Ormazd died and his rule ended with Gurabad swallowed by the earth.
### STANDARD +
Eremite history remembers him as both a hero and a tyrant. His fear of his own son brought about his death and the city's destruction.

## background
### LITE
Liloupar bore him as part of her revenge on Ormazd and left him in the wild, where a giant divine bird raised him. As Kisra he grew ambitious, conquered many places for Ormazd and was adopted by him as son and heir. Ormazd named him Parvez and gave him his daughter Shirin.
### STANDARD +
He did not love Shirin and used the marriage to reach the throne. When Ormazd and his heirs died at a banquet, Kisra took the crown, added 'Ravan' to his name and ruled as vassal king under Deshret. He then asked Liloupar for more power. She designed machines run by Jinn fragments, and in a dream pushed him to build a colossal machine that spat out goods. Shirin, now insane, was made to create the Jinn, which were mindless and malformed. The machines made him rich and he appeared to end slavery.
### LARGE +
The end of slavery was hollow. Slave owners no longer had to feed their workers and the freed slaves had no livelihood, so poverty and hatred spread. Gurabad's records show a Council of Nobles that refused funds for irrigation or relief while ordering General Hibehrous to put down the 'Brass Mask' rebels with Jinn-fragment weapons and constructs in the king's name. Gurabad's oases were shrinking, the clergy blamed fading faith in Deshret and the poor of places like Panjvahe suffered long-term poverty.
### ULTRA +
He had been warned that a son would surpass him, so he feared his and Shirin's son Shiruyeh and banished him from the city as an adult, forbidding him to return. In exile Shiruyeh wore a brass mask, took the name Khorramdin and presented himself as the emissary of Nabu Malikata and a saviour of commoners and slaves. Liloupar supported him. After the Great Revolt, which Gurabad's records say ran for thirteen days or more, Liloupar used the dark power of the Abyss and the city was swallowed by a chasm. A plague then rose from the chasm and killed half the city's inhabitants. Accounts of Parvezravan's death differ (see the death entry).

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years; Gurabad fell with him.
### STANDARD +
His reign is preserved in the ruins of the Desert of Hadramaveth: the Records of Gurabad, the Dogma of the Brass Mask, labour records in the Hypostyle Desert and the Nameless Tablets, readable after the Dirge of Bilqis (3.4).
### LARGE +
No mention of him was found in Nod-Krai (6.x) or Snezhnaya (7.0-7.1) material.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Liloupar is mother, adviser and secret destroyer. Ormazd was his adoptive father. Shirin was his wife and, by Liloupar's account, his sister by blood. Shiruyeh is his son and nemesis.
### LARGE +
General Hibehrous, a cavalry general born to mountain shepherds and trusted by the king to quell rebellions, was held in contempt by the nobles. His fate is not given in these sources. Tulaytullah paid an annual tribute to Gurabad in his reign as King Deshret's primary vassal city.
### ULTRA +
The divine bird Ghoghnus nurtured him in its nest. Much later, the hidden base of Thutmose at Lamb-Devourer Rock uses 'Marg bar parvezravan' (Persian for 'down with Parvezravan') as its password.

## appearance
### STANDARD
No in-game portrait of him is known. The Tale mentions the magnificent bed adorned with sapphire and golden animal horns where, in the Eremite account, he was killed.

## abilities
### LITE
A celebrated war leader and conqueror. His power came from armies, Jinn machines and Liloupar's schemes.
### STANDARD +
He is said to have destroyed or emptied ninety-nine cities, beaten ninety-nine tribal leaders and chained ninety-nine star-reading sages, and brought them to Gurabad. These figures come from the desert songs in a storyteller's tale.

## knowledge
### LITE
He knew that a son of his would one day surpass him and acted on the warning.
### STANDARD +
Nothing shows that he knew the Jinn machines served his mother's revenge, that Liloupar was his mother, or that the masked rebel was his own son.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- He is not the founder of Gurabad; Ormazd was.
- He did not poison Ormazd himself in the quests; Shirin is shown as an unwitting cause and Liloupar as the author, though the book calls him a reluctant co-conspirator.
- 'Parvezravan' is a title-name; his birth name is Kisra.
### LARGE +
- He is not the same as the later 'kings of kings' who rebuilt Gurabad.
- Do not give him heroic motives; sources show him as ambitious, fearful and ruthless.
- The Eremite view of Shiruyeh as a coward who caused the plague is one account, not a quest conclusion.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Raised by Ghoghnus; renowned conqueror; adopted by Ormazd; marries Shirin.
- Ormazd dies; Kisra becomes king and takes the name Parvezravan.
- Jinn-machine economy; Shiruyeh banished.
- Brass Mask rebellion and the Great Revolt; Gurabad falls.
### ULTRA +
- 3.0: the Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin; Transparent Ruins Records (3.1).
- 3.4: Memories of Gurabad, Dune-Entombed Fecundity and the Dirge of Bilqis reveal Liloupar's role and his parentage.

## trivia
### ULTRA
- His name and epithet come from the Sasanian king Khosrow II Parviz, 'the Victorious'.
- An achievement named for him, Parvezravan Khwarrah, means 'Glory of Parvezravan' and is for restoring Primal Obelisks in the Desert of Hadramaveth.
- Wiki editors see echoes of Astyages in the failed attempt to avert a prophecy about a grandson, which only brought it about.

## entry: Death of Parvezravan

@keys2: Gurabad, Shiruyeh
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The sources conflict. In The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin, Eremite history says his masked son stabbed him to death in his bed. In Liloupar's memories, as the Abyss power engulfed Gurabad, Kisra turned into a dark monster. Present both as accounts rather than a settled fact.

## entry: Jinn Machines of Gurabad
@keys: Jinn machines, Jinn-driven machines, Jinn fragments
@keys2: Gurabad, Parvezravan
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
At Parvezravan's request Liloupar designed machines driven by Jinn remnants and inspired him to build a colossal one in a dream. The Jinn that ran them were made by Shirin and were mindless and malformed. They produced great wealth and displaced slave labour; the records speak of workshops, flying shuttles, 'springs of power' and slaves who could earn release. The nobles also used Jinn-fragment weapons and constructs against dissenters.
### LARGE +
Liloupar calls the 'abolition of slavery' the start of a crueller servitude: owners were freed of the duty to feed their slaves and slaves lost even small mercies.

## entry: Khorramdin and the Brass Mask
@keys: Khorramdin, Brass Mask, Shiruyeh, brass-masked king
@keys2: Gurabad
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Shiruyeh ('unweaned lion cub') was banished by his father, wore a brass mask and called himself Khorramdin. He promised justice to slaves and commoners as an emissary of Nabu Malikata; Liloupar supported him and the revolt she fuelled led to the Great Revolt and Gurabad's fall. The Tale says he later fell into the chasm in the earth; Liloupar calls him a gifted charlatan who led followers to their doom. His plague is called Shiruyeh's Plague.

## entry: Spirit of Victory
@keys: Spirit of Victory, Spirit of Unceasing Victory
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
The epithet comes from Kisra's conquests. The Tale credits him with ninety-nine cities, tribes and sages, and records of his reign use it in his name when conscripting workers and building pleasure gardens, workshops and fortified camps.
