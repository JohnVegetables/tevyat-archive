## summary
### LITE
King Deshret (personal name Al-Ahmar, divine name Amun) was the god-king of Sumeru's desert thousands of years ago. With Nabu Malikata, the Goddess of Flowers, and Greater Lord Rukkhadevata he was one of the Three Eternal Companion-Gods. Grief for Nabu Malikata drove him to forbidden knowledge, which ruined his civilization and gave rise to the Withering. He gave his life to end the disaster.
### STANDARD +
Desert people still revere him. The Eremites descend from his subjects and some still want him back; Aaru Village preserves the last embers of his people. His ruins, including the Mausoleum, Khaj-Nisut and the Primal Constructs, cover the Great Red Sand. His greatest work, Aaru, was built from celestial technology. In 6.6 it was opened and became the new hub for Teyvat's memories after Irminsul was destroyed.
### LARGE +
He is remembered as proud, idealistic and ambitious enough to "shock even the wisest of gods", and also as the Mourning King, who looked at the sky and grieved for the past. Canon presents him from several angles. Apep calls him hubristic. Heryshaf calls him a stern ruler and a teacher of wisdom. Sobek reads his death as an act of love for humanity, and Thoth says that in the end he simply wanted to return to the days when Nabu Malikata was alive.

## background
### LITE
He met Nabu Malikata, a surviving angel, and with her built the city of Ay-Khanoum. He then shared his throne with her and Rukkhadevata. When he resolved to defy the Heavenly Principles, Rukkhadevata left. Nabu Malikata gave him knowledge from Celestia and was burned to ashes for it. In mourning he ruled alone, tried to build a paradise, released forbidden knowledge and finally sacrificed himself to contain it.
### STANDARD +
According to item lore he refused a "gift" from Celestia, which the wiki reads as the Archon seat, in order to pursue his own ambition. After Nabu Malikata died he gathered her scattered remains and buried them at the Eternal Oasis, and he took her Jinn as his servants. Ruling through his Seven Pillars, vassal kings and priests, he founded the royal city of Akhtamun and backed the human kingdom of Gurabad under the shepherd-king Ormazd. He dreamed of a "golden slumber" with no sorrow or parting, and after Gurabad fell he conceived Aaru, an artificial afterlife. When the forbidden knowledge broke loose, Rukkhadevata came back to help, but it persisted, and he chose to die to end it.
### LARGE +
The disaster filled minds with whispers, spread grey scales over people's bodies and withered the land; Rukkhadevata had priests raise temples infused with the power of life to slow it. Before building his kingdom he struck a bargain with the Dendro Dragon Sovereign Apep, who ruled the desert. She would let him build undisturbed if she could swallow him after his death and take his knowledge. When his kingdom collapsed she did so, and the forbidden knowledge he carried corrupted her. Apep says he set this up deliberately as the final fail-safe of his plan. Sobek, his former minister, says he tricked her so that the corruption would not spread to the land.

His research produced the Primal Constructs, built to "rebirth" the paradise he dreamed of, and a mechanism at Khaj-Nisut that let a person enter the golden slumber by reciting a prayer on his throne. Aaru's architect was a survivor of Gurabad. Human artisans had to build key parts, and Bennu later said this was less trust than caution on Deshret's part. When Liloupar engineered Gurabad's destruction, he had her split into seven parts and hidden by his sages.
### ULTRA +
Item and artifact text describe the three gods' shared kingdom as an era of peace in which "the banquet" belonged to Nabu Malikata, "authority" to Deshret and "life" to Rukkhadevata. Each held a different view of wisdom, and they debated as friends. Nabu Malikata warned him of what Celestia had done to her people. She agreed to help only once she saw that his resolve might break the world's "absurd shackles", and she told him that humans would be the true hope of any rebellion against the divine. Thoth reports that Deshret told "the Lord of Dendro" (Rukkhadevata) that, because of their eternal covenant, he would never be her enemy and would not devour "the fruit of wisdom from the heavens"; he could become wisdom's enemy, but he cherished both goddesses too much. Alhaitham read this as affection placing limits on an imperious god.

Thoth says Deshret first wanted Aaru to "rival the very world itself". In the end it held only part of the souls and was meant to bring back the time when Nabu Malikata lived. Late in his reign he imprisoned Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf inside it. He gave Thoth three questions that came from Nabu Malikata and said the seal would open when humans answered them correctly. The Temple of Silence remembers that Hermanubis, the Tighnarian he raised to sage and familiar, led his followers to Tulaytullah after the fall.

## relationships
### STANDARD
- Nabu Malikata: his love and the turning point of his life. He never got over her death.
- Greater Lord Rukkhadevata: companion-god who refused his rebellion yet saved his people at great cost to herself. Cyno, learning the truth in 3.1, realised the two were "never enemies at all".
- Apep: neither ally nor subject. He listed her as a Pillar; she despised gods and bore his corruption for millennia.
- Seven Pillars: Heryshaf (Goat King, his deputy), Sobek (Crocodile King), Thoth (Ibis King), Hermanubis (Greatest of Sages), the constructs Bennu ("soul", guardian of Aaru's gates) and Shesepankh ("will", overseer of the Jinn), and Apep in name only.
### LARGE +
- The Jinn: Nabu Malikata's children, whom he made his servants and messengers. Some were sealed in a silver vase for arrogance; Liloupar served as his envoy until he punished her.
- Ormazd and Parvezravan: kings of Gurabad recorded as his envoys. How directly he ruled Gurabad is unclear, but Liloupar says he charged it with guarding the Eternal Oasis, and he built canals for the vassal kingdoms.
- Ferigees: left to watch over the Eternal Oasis.
- His people: the Eremites, Aaru Village (Candace's people are metaphorically called his descendants) and the old priesthood all count themselves as heirs of his civilization.
### ULTRA +
- Nefer: never met him, but in 6.6 she found the answer he wanted to Thoth's questions, "time", reasoning that the proudest beings believe only time may judge them.
- Nahida: inherited a land shaped by his catastrophe. In 3.6 she helped heal the corrupted Apep, and in 6.6 Heryshaf compared her gentle rule with Al-Ahmar's "majesty and might".
- Collei: carried a fragment of Nabu Malikata's remains, which Dottore had tracked down among the goddess's unaccounted-for remains (revealed 6.6). In 6.6 the Jinni Liloupar gave her life to remove the last fragment from Collei.

## abilities
### LARGE
Sobek describes him at his height as a god whom the three future Pillars could not match, so they submitted. Even Apep could only fight him to a draw. He commanded the Jinn, ran a civilization of advanced technology (Primal Constructs, Aaru, the Khaj-Nisut mechanism) and could imprison lesser gods.
### ULTRA +
His deepest power came from celestial knowledge Nabu Malikata obtained for him, and from forbidden knowledge that eventually consumed him. Legends say he sought the Celestial Nail of the sands and used its power with the Jinn's help to build the Eternal Oasis. The Lay of Al-Ahmar claims he separated his mind from his body; the Scarlet Sand Slate also says his body was left in his mausoleum.

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years. He sacrificed himself to stop the spread of forbidden knowledge, and Apep swallowed his body.
### STANDARD +
His legacy is still active. The Withering descends from his disaster. In Version 6.6 (Song of the Welkin Moon Acts IX-X), with Sumeru in crisis, Nefer answered his three questions and freed Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf from Aaru. With Cyno speaking for Hermanubis and Apep already helping Nahida, all Seven Pillars approved opening Aaru. Its souls, Jeht's father among them, returned to the Ley Lines, and its gates opened "by the grace of His Majesty".
### LARGE +
Akademiya scholars then modified Aaru to restart the Akasha. After Irminsul was destroyed, Aaru took over its role as the hub of Teyvat's information, and Nahida's existence is now bound to it. As of 7.1 the freed Pillars are guests of the Temple of Silence, Bennu and Shesepankh help maintain Aaru, and Heryshaf says the old alliance of the three gods "remains in force" even though Al-Ahmar is gone.

## accounts
### LARGE
Canon judges him in very different ways:
- Kasala's record (3.1): the Scarlet King "brought this forbidden knowledge into our world", yet was a "proud king" and "eternal lord" who chose to sacrifice himself.
- Apep (3.6): an arrogant upstart who leaned on "the heavens' glow". His madness exceeded even hers, and he used her as a trap.
- The Lay of Al-Ahmar (a Jinni's tale of unknown truth): a melancholy, "arbitrary" king led astray by three flattering retainers.
- Thoth (6.1, 6.6): a proud king whose ambition "was not destined to be allowed in a closed-off world". After Kaveh calls Aaru "both a prison and a monument", Thoth adds: "Love is the same."
- Bennu (6.6): Aaru is "the pride of Al-Ahmar"; King Deshret believed in humanity but was also wary of it.
- Some of his devout followers, like Rahman in 3.1, long blamed Rukkhadevata for his death and refused to believe their god erred, until Kasala's record showed otherwise.

## mistakes
### LARGE
- He is not an Archon and not a former Dendro Archon. Rukkhadevata was the Dendro Archon; he reportedly turned down Celestia's gift.
- He and Rukkhadevata were never enemies; desert grudges against her rest on a false rumour.
- He did not survive and is not "sleeping". No speaking ghost of him appears in canon; do not invent one.
- Do not present a romance with Nabu Malikata as mutual or confirmed.
- "Scarlet King", "Al-Ahmar" and "Amun" are the same person; the Eremite clan Ayn Al-Ahmar is a different thing.
### ULTRA +
- His three imprisoned retainers' crime is never specified; do not invent one.
- Don't treat The Lay of Al-Ahmar as established history.
- Avoid inventing his appearance beyond the canon detail of a gold-inlaid headband (imitated by his priests) and the "sun and eye" symbol.

## trivia
### ULTRA

- His symbol, "the sun and the eye", marks desert architecture.
- His people were promised a paradise where none would have to drink the "bitter salt water".

## entry: Aaru
@keys: Aaru, Golden Slumber, Aaru's Shut
@keys2: Deshret, Al-Ahmar, Seven Pillars, Thoth, Heryshaf, Irminsul, Akasha
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Aaru was King Deshret's greatest creation, an artificial afterlife hidden inside his Mausoleum. It was built by divine and mortal artisans from celestial technology Nabu Malikata obtained for him, and it was never finished. It held the Golden Slumber, where many souls rested. Opening it required the unanimous consent of the Seven Pillars. In 6.6 it was opened: the souls returned to the Ley Lines, Akademiya scholars modified it to restart the Akasha (which Aaru had originally inspired), and after Irminsul's destruction it became the new store of Teyvat's memories, to which Nahida's existence is now bound.
### LARGE +
Thoth says Deshret first intended Aaru to "rival the very world itself" and that his last hope for it was to return to the past while Nabu Malikata lived. Alhaitham read Thoth's account as Deshret's affection for the two goddesses restraining his imperious ways, and Kaveh concluded this is why Aaru was "both a prison and a monument" and why the answer to its riddles was "time".

Apep let Al-Ahmar build his kingdom in her desert on condition that she would devour him after death and take his knowledge, which she hoped to use against the Heavenly Principles. When his kingdom collapsed she swallowed him and gained only "the apocalypse": the forbidden knowledge he carried poisoned her for millennia. She says she fell into his trap, a final fail-safe. Sobek adds that Deshret insisted on naming her one of his Seven Pillars without asking, that they fought many times to a draw, and that she hated him and everything gods stood for.

## entry: Thoth's three questions
@keys: three questions, Thoth's questions, Ibis King's questions
@keys2: Deshret, Al-Ahmar, Thoth, Nefer, Aaru
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Before sealing Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf in Aaru, King Deshret gave Thoth three questions that had come from Nabu Malikata. Humans who answered them correctly would free him. Goudarz and Makhaira answered the first two and Thoth rewarded them with power. Nefer's first answer to the last ("what determines truth and falsehood"), "a liar's soul", did not open the seal. In 6.6 she gave the answer Deshret wanted, "time", as the answer to all three, and the three retainers walked free.

## entry: The fall of Deshret's kingdom
@keys: Scarlet King, Kasala, King Deshret and the Three Magi
@keys2: forbidden knowledge, Withering, Rukkhadevata
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
A record left by a priest named Kasala, found in 3.1, tells how the Scarlet King brought forbidden knowledge into the world. People heard maddening whispers, grey scales covered their bodies and the land withered. Greater Lord Rukkhadevata came from the forest and had priests build temples infused with the power of life, which slowed the disaster and kept Aaru Village alive. The knowledge still endured, so the king sacrificed himself; Rukkhadevata spent so much strength that her form became that of a small child. Nahida later confirmed that forbidden knowledge was repelled thanks to his self-sacrifice and Rukkhadevata's near-exhaustion. Its lingering effect is the Withering.
