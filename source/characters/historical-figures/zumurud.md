## summary
### LITE
Zumurud, called the Verdant Lord, was a ruler of several desert city-states in the Great Red Sand, in Sumeru, thousands of years ago. He is known solely from Primal Obelisk inscriptions. He was the son of Muzaffar, one of the Deys of Orghana (the city later called Port Ormos), and was raised in the forest, so he did not speak the desert language. He dreamed of turning the desert green and fathered 99 children.
### STANDARD +
Nothing is recorded of his personality, appearance or death; what survives is a dream, a royal duty to produce heirs, and the glory later claimed for his children. He is long dead and cannot be portrayed as a speaking character from canon voice material.

## background
### LITE
Zumurud's father Muzaffar was one of the Twenty-Nine Deys of Orghana and conquered cities of the Great Red Sand. Zumurud grew up in the forest rather than the desert. In the second year of his reign he had a dream that the scholars wrote down in the desert language, and he ruled over several city-states.
### STANDARD +
Muzaffar instructed him to leave as many heirs as a pomegranate tree bears fruit, so that the realm would not suffer another power vacuum like the one that followed the fall of King Saleb Dowleh. Zumurud accordingly had 99 children, said to be 50 princes and 49 princesses. The Valley of Dahri obelisk lauds the princes as valiant and crowned in gold, and the princesses as wearing garlands of green leaves that servants replaced the moment one withered.
### LARGE +
The obelisk says Muzzudin and Badanah, brothers of one mother, drove their slaves to plant trees in the desert and turn barren soil into thriving forest, in the name of the Verdant Lord's dream. It concludes that his children walked the land like gods and that his realm was as indestructible as the Mausoleum of King Deshret.
### ULTRA +
The text also says the children spoke with an elegance of speech like nobles under Al-Ahmar, so that only the children of the most loyal retainers could serve them. Another obelisk, written under Badanah, praises Badanah as king over his brothers' folly and speaks of ninety-nine kingdoms falling under one ruler once more. This implies the realm had been divided among the children and later reunited under Badanah, though the text does not narrate the events.

## relationships
### LITE
Father: Muzaffar Dey, who set him the duty of begetting heirs. Children: 99 sons and daughters, of whom Muzzudin and Badanah are named. Badanah later ruled as king.
### STANDARD +
A later inscription claims that the mother of Badanah and Muzzudin was the most beloved of the Verdant Lord. A grandchild named Faramarz appears in an inscription at Khaj-Nisut.

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years. He is mentioned only in ruin inscriptions and has no known presence in the present day.
### STANDARD +
Nothing about him appears in Version 6.x (Nod-Krai) or 7.0-7.1 (Snezhnaya) content that this entry could confirm.

## entry: The Verdant Lord's dream
@keys: Verdant Lord, Zumurud, Verdant Lord's dream
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
An obelisk in the Duat Hall records that in his second year the Verdant Lord dreamed of a sun rising over a barren, yellow land with no birds. He sighed and resolved that the land should become a green paradise like the forest: springs welled from the desert and trees touched the sky. He saw a thousand ships, ten thousand roses and countless caravans woven into gold. On waking he saw an arrow of sunlight pierce a mountain summit. Because he was raised in the forest and did not know the desert language, the greatest scholars were ordered to write the dream out and carve it.
### LARGE +
Parts of the inscription are damaged, and some green gems that inlaid the king's name have been pried out.

## entry: Children of the Verdant Lord
@keys: Badanah, Muzzudin, Hanzada Sultan, Verdant Lord's children
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
The Verdant Lord had 99 children. The Valley of Dahri inscription names Muzzudin as the wisest prince and Hanzada Sultan as the loveliest princess, and Badanah as Muzzudin's mighty brother. The Sobek Oasis inscription, from Badanah's own time, glorifies Badanah as king and mocks Muzzudin's schemes, hinting at a rivalry between the brothers. These texts are partisan praise.
