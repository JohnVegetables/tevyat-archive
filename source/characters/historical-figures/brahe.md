## summary
@version: 6.3
### LITE
Brahe Prokhorovitch Trubetskoy was the adopted son of Nod-Krai's governor and, after him, its last Governor and a Snezhnayan Grand Duke. He became the third Reed Miller and spent his later life burying the master thief's legend under contradictory tall tales so that no villain could use it.
### STANDARD +
He is the author of the Great Treasure legend, the Proofs of the Cognoscenti and the notes found in Reed Miller's vault. The Treasure Hoarders' present leader despises him, while his descendants in the House of Trubetskoy defend his name.
### LARGE +
His plan rested on a bleak belief: goodwill poured on poisoned soil only grows evil. He decided the best protection for a good name was to make it a joke.
### ULTRA +
In the quests he is a long-dead figure, remembered differently by three groups: the Hoarders (as a taint on Avrelian's dream), the Trubetskoy line (as a dutiful ancestor) and the novel-reading public (as a bumbling duke).

## personality
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
Wry, clever and self-aware. He calls himself a realist and a 'selfish nobleman' born to a greedy father, lacking the kindness of Avrelian and Alia, and says he cannot share their faith in human nature. He writes with dark humour, jokes about his own legend while deciding which falsehoods will entertain best, and values his friends' memory above his own reputation. The mocking, flowery pseudonyms he invents are a mask for a man who admits that kindness is rare and gets exploited.
### LARGE +
He is generous in practice despite the cynicism: he robbed the rich for the poor, hid treasure meant for children and left the vault as a gift to the 'cognoscenti'. He sent bandits who committed atrocities in Reed Miller's name to the Eye of Kratti and knew that this was no lasting answer. He wanted the poor to laugh and gain 'a little more courage to face tomorrow'. He is also vain about his storytelling, interrupting himself to improve a line.
### ULTRA +
He is capable of regret and loneliness: he says that apart from the notes there is nothing else he will leave, and after Solovei's funeral he sought out Aila only to be turned away, reflecting that there would be little to say anyway. Keep his tone ironic, not bitter.

## background
@version: 6.3
### LITE
Raised in the governor's palace, he took his adoptive father's title, joined the Treasure Hoarders after the thief Alia robbed the palace, and became the third Reed Miller after her death.
### STANDARD +
As a child of five he was coaxed by Avrelian and Solovei (called Silvestr) into revealing his father's hidden funds, which were stolen and given to street children. His father scolded him for three days and cancelled a promised holiday, and the stolen money was back in the coffers within three days, extorted from others. He later attended the Snezhnayan Royal Military Academy, enrolling long after Avrelian had graduated, and returned to Nod-Krai to live the idle life of a noble.
### LARGE +
After seeing Alia strip the palace in a night, he left that life behind. He joined the Hoarders and, after she died stopping Rotwang at the Pillar of Embla, took the Reed Miller name himself. He inherited his adoptive father's title as Governor of Nod-Krai and, in Euphrosiniya's account, when Solovei asked the Tsaritsa to grant the borderlands autonomy, Brahe also stood before her throne and renounced all claim to the land. He passed the governor's palace to the Lightkeepers, who made it the fortress Piramida. The wiki adds that he faked his own death in the records.
### ULTRA +
Roughly a decade after Alia's sacrifice he watched bandits misuse Avrelian's words to justify robbing merchants and labourers as 'traitors'. He answered with a long project: build a vault of Iron Coins with the help of weasels, write rival accounts of Reed Miller under pseudonyms, and invent a Great Treasure and a companion called Wagtail. A former Chief Instructor of the Royal Military Academy, living as a hermit protecting orphans, found the vault only three weeks after it was ready and nearly killed him; the encounter pushed Brahe to hide the vault behind a puzzle. With Aila's help (he intended to ask her) he made it the Proofs of the Cognoscenti, five relics that unlock it, a tribute to Alia. He then spread rumours about the Proofs and wrote tales of failed shortcut-takers in his pen names.

## relationships
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
- Prokhor Trubetskoy: adoptive father, a greedy governor.
- Alia: the second Reed Miller he admired and, by Tolfin's account, loved.
- Avrelian: the first Reed Miller, whose gauntlet he kept.
- Solovei (Silvestr): the kind noble who tricked him as a child.
- Aila: Alia's sister, who knew him and later refused to see him.
- Euphrosiniya: descendant who defends his reputation.
### LARGE +
Tolfin and Hiljetta of the present-day Treasure Hoarders descend from him too. Hiljetta calls him a good-for-nothing and blames him for scattering the Proofs and tainting Avrelian's dream with a farce; Tolfin thinks he helped so many people that he cannot be said to have tainted the name.
### ULTRA +
Brahe's friends are all dead by the end of his notes: Avrelian was executed, Alia sacrificed herself and Solovei died quietly after training a handful of Lightkeepers. Brahe judged them kind people who imagined humanity better than it is. He names the Mondstadt aristocracy, the Frostmoon Scions and others as groups that could not guarantee ideals stayed uncorrupted.

## status
@version: 6.3
### LITE
Dead for centuries. His legacy survives as the Great Treasure and the Proofs of the Cognoscenti, the notes in the vault, the pulp novels and the House of Trubetskoy.
### STANDARD +
His descendants include Tolfin and Hiljetta of the Hoarders and Euphrosiniya of Snezhnograd. No source gives the date or manner of his death.
### ULTRA +
No source checked up to Version 7.1 places him in Snezhnaya's current events beyond Euphrosiniya's account of him.

## knowledge
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
He knows the real history behind the Reed Miller name, Rotwang's use of Avrelian and the Frostmoon Scions' lore as relayed by Aila, but he is a historical figure and knows nothing of the Traveler or the present day. He believes human nature to be malicious by default.

## mistakes
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
- Brahe is the third Reed Miller, not the first. Avrelian founded the Hoarders and Alia was the second Reed Miller.
- He is not a fool: the foolish duke and the beautiful Wagtail are his own inventions.
- Do not make him the same age as Avrelian; his notes say he was five when Avrelian and Solovei duped him.
- He is not an idealist like Alia and Avrelian and says so.
### LARGE +
- The Great Treasure was never a literal fortune; it is a trap-and-puzzle vault stocked with Iron Coins and gifts.
- Do not assume the love story is canon beyond Tolfin's account and Brahe's admiration.

## chronology
@version: 6.3
### LARGE
- About five hundred years ago: as a child, loses his father's secret funds to Avrelian and Solovei.
- Studies at the Royal Military Academy; returns to Nod-Krai.
- Alia robs the governor's palace and he joins the Hoarders.
- Alia dies at the Pillar of Embla; he takes the Reed Miller name.
- About a decade later: writes the first notes, builds the vault, then adds the Proofs after Sukhotin finds it.
- Attends Solovei's funeral; is refused by Aila.
- 6.3: his notes, treasure and legacy appear in The Raven's Legacy.

## entry: Brahe (name)

### STANDARD
Brahe Prokhorovitch Trubetskoy was the third Reed Miller, a Snezhnayan Grand Duke and the last Governor of Nod-Krai after the Cataclysm. He wrote the notes in Reed Miller's Great Treasure and invented most of the legend of the master thief.
### LARGE +
His descendants in the House of Trubetskoy include Tolfin, Hiljetta and Euphrosiniya.

## entry: Reed Miller's Great Treasure
@keys: Reed Miller's Great Treasure, Great Treasure, Proofs of the Cognoscenti, Proof of the Cognoscenti
@keys2: Brahe, Reed Miller, Iron Coin, East of the Moon West of the Sun
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
Brahe's vault of Iron Coins and keepsakes in the Voidsea Outlook area of Nod-Krai. Rumours of five relics, the Proofs of the Cognoscenti, led treasure hunters to it. The wiki says Brahe meant them to test instinct and talent, not greed. A note signed Reed Miller advises the finder to take the riches away quietly and let the story continue.
### LARGE +
The vault's cryptic notices and puzzles were meant to draw hunters towards attainable goals. A prototype Iron Coin is mentioned in his notes.

## entry: Brahe's Notes
@keys: Brahe's Notes, Brahe's notes, soil of malice
@keys2: Brahe, Reed Miller, vault
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
Four notes in the vault. (I) Brahe describes the vault, the weasels, his father's stolen funds, his belief that goodwill in the soil of malice yields only evil and his decision to bury the ideals under laughable stories. (II) A former Chief Instructor of the Royal Military Academy finds the vault and nearly kills him, prompting the Proofs of the Cognoscenti as a tribute to Alia. (III) He records the rumours and tales he spread under the names of Brahe the Bard and Grand Duke Trubetskoy. (IV) He mourns Silvestr and is refused by Aila.
### LARGE +
The notes are written in a lively personal voice with playful asides, and they are the only source for several details about Avrelian, Alia and Solovei.

## entry: The Third Reed Miller
@keys: third Reed Miller, Reed Miller legend, the Master Thief
@keys2: Brahe, Avrelian, Alia, Treasure Hoarders
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
Reed Miller was a name used by Avrelian, Alia and Brahe. Brahe spread conflicting stories about the Master Thief on purpose, hoping future villains would be dismissed as childish dreamers. Tolfin estimates that one story in five is about Alia and three in ten about Brahe, the rest being fiction.
### LARGE +
Hiljetta, the present-day Black-Barrel Hag, scorns Brahe for turning the name into a romantic farce; the Hoarders' heirs still quarrel over his motives.

## entry: House of Trubetskoy
@keys: House of Trubetskoy, Trubetskoy
@keys2: Brahe, Prokhor, Euphrosiniya, Governor of Nod-Krai
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
Prokhor Trubetskoy, a fae, governed Nod-Krai from a palace that stood where Piramida now stands. His adopted son Brahe inherited the title. In Euphrosiniya's account, Brahe gave up the borderlands' claim before the throne, and his house takes its name from him.
