## summary
### LITE
The Skipper was a sea captain of Liyue, about a thousand years ago, who made his name hunting sea monsters and hanging their bones from his ship. He lost his crew and his beloved navigator to one enormous beast, rebuilt a crew, and years later went out to kill it. His real name is unknown.
### STANDARD +
He is remembered through the book series Tales From the Waves, the greatsword Serpent Spine and the Heart of Depth artifact set. His chief mate in the last voyage was the exiled Mondstadt aristocrat Parsifal.
### LARGE +
Both versions of the man, the grim hunter of the book and the jovial captain of the artifact lore, belong to the same legend. The book ends in his grief; the artifacts show what followed.

## background
### LITE
He tracked and hunted terrible sea monsters from a mighty ship that anchored at Liyue Harbor, its hull adorned with their skeletons. He knew only wind and waves, and his crew feared him more than they admired him. The one person who could soften him was his young navigator, a maiden who sang endless sea shanties.
### STANDARD +
Their match came in a maelstrom. The crew attacked a monster so large that every beast they had killed seemed like a cub beside it, but after a long fight the ship was ruined, half the crew eaten and even his sword broken in two. As the monster rose to swallow the wreck, the maiden's song drew it away, and it dragged her under. The monster spared the Skipper, who woke on a merchant ship with nothing left but wounds and a vow to avenge her.
### LARGE +
Afterwards, per the Heart of Depth lore, he became carefree and jovial. He rebuilt his crew and did not speak about his thirst for revenge. He gave important work to the drunken Parsifal, who had signed a contract to follow him even to death, and did not reprimand him. The artifact text has him saying that they were both people without anything to their names.
### ULTRA +
When the Skipper judged the conditions right, he set sail against the monster once more. Parsifal died in the fight, and the Skipper and his men likely did as well, since neither he nor the ship came back to Liyue Harbor. Some time later, the corpse of a great sea monster, torn apart by the marks of a greatsword, washed up on Liyue's shore. Serpent Spine, a greatsword fashioned from a sea monster's spine, preserves the story, and Iron Sting's lore mentions that the boat captain deflected curious questions about his chief mate's blade.

## status
### LITE
Dead for about a thousand years; his ship and crew were never seen again.
### STANDARD +
His revenge succeeded: the monster's body was found. Folk tales and the book series keep his story alive in Liyue.

## relationships
### LITE
His nameless navigator (the maiden who sang shanties); his crew; Parsifal, his chief mate.
### STANDARD +
The Skipper and Parsifal bonded over losing everything; Parsifal, once an aristocrat of the Mondstadt Aristocracy driven out by his half-brother Eberhart, hummed his own shanty on voyages and was loyal to the end.

## entry: Tales From the Waves
@keys: Tales From the Waves, Rising Tide, Wild Storm, Receding Torrent
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
A three-volume book: Volume 1, Rising Tide (found in Wangshu Inn), Volume 2, Wild Storm and Volume 3, Receding Torrent (both at Wanwen Bookhouse in Liyue Harbor). It is a literary retelling of the Skipper's legend, ending where he wakes on the merchant ship and vows revenge. Its crew avoids singing about hunting monsters because they hold that ballads of blood and water are the unluckiest of all.

## entry: Serpent Spine and Heart of Depth
@keys: Serpent Spine, Heart of Depth, Ocean Conqueror
@version: 1.2
### STANDARD
Serpent Spine is the Skipper's greatsword, said to be fashioned from a sea monster's spine. The Heart of Depth artifact set (called Ocean Conqueror in the Adventurer Handbook) tells the later voyage, the chief mate and the final fight. Together with the book, they give the full legend. The pieces include a wine-stained tricorne, a gilded corsage, a gust of nostalgia, a copper compass and a goblet of thundering deep.
