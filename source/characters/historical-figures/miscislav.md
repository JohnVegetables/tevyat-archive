## summary
### LITE
Miscislav, the Blood-Devourer, was a gigantic Volkodlak and king of the Miscislavli Alliance. His war against Emperor Monomakh ended when Monomakh killed him, which let Monomakh become the Belyi Tsar and the undisputed ruler of Snezhnaya.
### STANDARD +
Fae chroniclers called him Gorynishche, "Son of the Mountain", for his size. His skull is said to be kept in the Zapolyarny Palace Treasury.

## background
### LITE
Miscislav ruled the Volkodlak and the Miscislavli Alliance in the age of the Pale Star Court. Vila fiefs paid him tribute until Monomakh's envoys told them to stop.
### STANDARD +
Three years later, he accused Monomakh's people of breaking the peace and demanded ten years' tribute in grain, gold and slaves, over thirty times what the Vila had paid. Monomakh refused. Some later readers believe Miscislav wanted war because he feared the Pale Star Court's growth.
### LARGE +
Miscislav gathered the Miscislavli clans, the bovine Volkodlak of Nyholmgarthir, the wolf-like Volkodlak of Cirnareka and the Water Imp clan of Dalegrad, with their retainers. They marched on Kitezh. The war had three battles: at Kitezh, in the foothills, at the frozen lake and on the plains.
### ULTRA +
He took personal command in the three campaigns. A flanking legion under the Cirnareka Grand Duke was misled by sudden icy fog and failed to cut off the imperial forces, and some scholars suspect treachery without evidence. Legend says Miscislav then reached for the power of the Shadow Realm and failed. Monomakh killed him in the third campaign with a thrown spear through the skull. A bear-shaped skull as large as a chariot, held in the Zapolyarny Palace Treasury, is said to bear the spear's mark.

## relationships
### LITE
Monomakh Snezhnyi, who killed him; the Vila fiefs that paid him tribute.
### STANDARD +
The Vila Grand Duchess Vaselisa von Strivozha paid him tribute and later betrayed him. Fae chroniclers record the Grand Dukes Seriviiki, Skoldyryt and Vodyanoy as his allies; Seriviiki alone escaped.
### LARGE +
In the story (Breezeborne Refrain, 7.1) told by the weapon Breezeborne Refrain, a human general of his own army, urging him not to slaughter the allied Water Imps, was stripped of rank, and his wife and children were put to death. The man later reached House Strivozha and died there.

## status
### LITE
Dead for about three thousand years, killed in the third campaign of his war.
### STANDARD +
His family survived; the wiki lists the Snezhnograd NPC Bogatov as a descendant. Some of his kin held power under the Tsar.
### LARGE +
The war made Monomakh the Belyi Tsar. Of the Grand Dukes, Seriviiki entered the Pale Star court; Skoldyryt and Vodyanoy fell to the emperor's spear and were returned to their clans for burial.

## entry: War of the Miscislavli Alliance
@keys: Miscislavli Alliance, Miscislavli, Kitezh war, War of the Pale Star
@version: 6.3
### STANDARD
In the third year of the 428th Cycle, Monomakh's envoys told the Vila to stop their tribute to Miscislav. In the sixth year Miscislav demanded ten years of tribute, which Monomakh rejected. In the seventh year Miscislav's host, with Volkodlak and Water Imp clans, attacked Kitezh and lost all three battles. Afterwards, Monomakh sought out the dead Grand Dukes and returned their bodies to their clans, and the remaining fae clans sought an audience with him.
### LARGE +
The book records Monomakh's argument that Miscislav's peace offers hid a wish for war. It also notes that Kitezh was the first city of Snezhnaya and that Monomakh later moved his seat to Snezhnograd.

## entry: Breezeborne Refrain and the Strivozha plot
@keys: Breezeborne Refrain, Strivozha plot, Alrekr Jarizleivich
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The weapon text says that a general named Alrekr Jarizleivich, who had defeated Pale Star armies, lost everything for opposing Miscislav's slaughter of Water Imp allies. He fled to Veneter, where Vaselisa von Strivozha welcomed him. His death was then presented to Monomakh as poisoning by Miscislav's spies, intended to rouse the emperor's hatred. See the entry for Vaselisa von Strivozha for her side of this.
