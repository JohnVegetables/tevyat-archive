## summary
### LITE
Makhaira was a sword dancer of Tulaytullah in the Great Red Sand, Sumeru, living some thousands of years ago after King Deshret's fall. Her mother was a Jinni. Sold to a vassal king, she helped end his reign, became queen-regent in the wars that followed, and died by viper venom. Later tales call her the Temptress of Woe or the evil queen.
### STANDARD +
She wielded the blade remembered as Makhaira Aquamarine, and the Thoth slab calls her a Jinn-blooded dancing girl. Accounts disagree on whether she was murdered or chose her own end.

## background
### LITE
Makhaira learned a seven-fold sword dance from her Jinni mother and lived in Tulaytullah. Because of her beauty and skill, the rulers of Tulaytullah sold her as a concubine to the vassal king Badanah. With Badanah's son Faramarz she plotted his death: she killed him with her sword dance, and the two staged it as a fall from a tower too frail for his weight.
### STANDARD +
According to Tulaytullah's Remembrance she had been the lover of Xiphos, the lost prince who became Tulaytullah's protector, before she was sold. At some point she wandered into the ruins where Thoth was imprisoned. He asked her what obliterates love and hate; she answered that with nothing left to lose, only vengeance could quench both, and he granted her the power to be loved.
After Badanah fell, Faramarz was crowned but young, so she became queen-regent. The court then passed the crown to his uncle Balash, master of Tulaytullah, until another uncle, Goraz, raised an army and restored Faramarz, with Goraz and Makhaira as co-regents. Goraz then took the throne, made her his queen and exiled Faramarz. Tulaytullah, angry over Balash, rebelled, and she handed Goraz's head to the rebels. Faramarz returned to the throne a third time; when Saleh sought independence she appeased it, and twelve more uncles claimed the crown in a long war that Faramarz won.
### LARGE +
Faramarz, said to love her, came to distrust how she aligned herself with whoever best served her ambitions. The Lamp of the Lost text has a prince say he closed her lips with a viper's kiss and buried her in sand. Slave-warriors and city dwellers then conspired and deposed him for good, and he was exiled to the forest.
The Old Blind Poet, a general and poet who had served Badanah, outlived much of that era and recounted these stories; a Desert Pavilion artifact set carries his chronicle. In the present day desert dwellers still tell of the treasure of the evil queen Makhaira.
### ULTRA +
Her mother's Jinn ancestry is stated only in connection with the sword dance; canon does not say how much Jinn power she held. The riddle exchange with Thoth was the second of three he posed to chosen humans; the first went to Goudarz (the power to deceive) and the third to an unnamed woman whom the Traveler takes to be Nefer. Thoth hoped correct answers would satisfy King Deshret's rules and free him; as he is still imprisoned, they did not.

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years. She is remembered as a schemer and as the Temptress of Woe, and nothing in canon through Version 7.1 revives or revisits her. Her fate is told in two ways: killed with viper venom by Faramarz, or a self-chosen death by snake venom after a coup.
### STANDARD +
Her story survives in weapon and artifact texts, the Primal Obelisk inscription at Khaj-Nisut and a Thoth slab found during Headlong Into the Sands, where Kaveh and Alhaitham read her tale. Kaveh says she is mentioned in many papers about the desert.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Faramarz is both partner in the plot against Badanah and, later, the man said to have killed her. Xiphos is her earlier lover in Tulaytullah's Remembrance. Thoth, the imprisoned Ibis King, gave her the power to be loved. Badanah, Balash and Goraz are the kings and uncles she served, outlived or helped remove. The Old Blind Poet is the surviving witness of the era's intrigues.
### LARGE +
Goraz briefly made her his queen after exiling Faramarz. Thoth later told the Traveler's party that he will not say whether her answer or his gift determined her end.

## entry: Faramarz and the Fall of Badanah
@keys: Faramarz, Badanah
@version: 3.1
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Badanah was a tyrant vassal king whose greed Faramarz, his son, and Makhaira judged ruinous. Makhaira killed him with her sword dance and the pair arranged for him to appear to have fallen from a tower while pursuing a falcon. The tower was demolished and his hunting falcons were buried with him. Faramarz was crowned, lost and regained the throne several times, and was finally exiled to the forest after killing her.
### LARGE +
Faramarz, in the Lamp of the Lost text, describes his rule as tossed about by treachery and says he had someone to love who longed to be queen.

### STANDARD
Thoth, held in ruins sealed by King Deshret's rules, offered three humans a riddle and a power. Makhaira was the second; she answered about vengeance and was granted the power to be loved. Her story is told on a slab that Alhaitham and Kaveh read in Headlong Into the Sands.
