## summary
### LITE
Mouun was a shrine maiden of Watatsumi Island's Yuna Clan and, in the island's folk-tunes, the greatest master of whalesong. She won the great whale Daikengyou to Watatsumi's side, led the island's first navy with her twin sister Ayame, and was killed in the war against the Raiden Shogun.
### STANDARD +
She is known through a book, a weapon description and a second text on Touzannou, all set in the Archon War, so her story comes as legend. She is long dead and does not appear in any present-day scene.

## background
### LITE
Born to the Yuna Clan, famed for whalesong, she entered Sangonomiya Shrine young. In the songs she is wise and gentle, a mediator of quarrels.
### STANDARD +
The Yuna were one of the great houses that first followed their Omikami into the sunlight. At the shrine she learned the maiden's etiquette and rites, history, governance and island folk-tunes.

By the time news came that the Shogunate had unified the other Inazuman islands, she and Ayame were already well known on the island. When Watatsumi Omikami set out on the eastern expedition, the then Divine Priestess entrusted the first navy to her. Part of the task was to win over Daikengyou, a huge blind whale that none of the island's whalesong masters had faced and returned from whole. Mouun dived into its phosphorescent palace as the moon rose; on the third moonrise afterwards the whale surfaced with her on its back, and nobody learned how she persuaded it.

She and her sister then took the navy to fight at Omikami's side. When word came that Omikami and Touzannou had been killed together, she tried to withdraw but was ambushed by subordinates of the tengu Sasayuri and killed together with Daikengyou. The Shogunate took their remains.
### LARGE +
Daikengyou is described as a blind whale of immense age (the book gives it 'five hundred years and four hundred years before that'), served by deep-sea fish and moonglow jellyfish, with five hundred narwhals guarding its left flank and five hundred humpback whales playing music on its right. Folk-tunes say it could swallow ten coral islands in a gulp. The Mouun's Moon text says she befriended the monsters of distant seas and fought beside them against the thundering clouds, rode the waves with them and sang with her sister and the great whale of dismal days in the deep and the dark ever-night.

The same weapon text records two quoted exchanges: words accepting that Omikami's war may have been doomed from the start, yet worth it if memories and sacrifice are left behind, and a banter in which a young man promises to bring back a legendary Great Tengu's mask and she threatens to have a whale raise a wave to wash his mouth clean. The text does not name the man here; linking him to Touzannou is the wiki's reading.
### ULTRA +
After her death the people of Watatsumi kept singing of her. The Yuna Clan's songs, quoted in the book about the Viceroy of the East, say she invited her student for a night swim in waters lit by moon and stars, where he sensed the speech of sea creatures, heard her mournful whisper and chose his course. He afterwards developed the sword arts Getsumouun and Yuushio.

## abilities
### LITE
She was the finest whalesinger in Watatsumi's songs, able to speak to whales and sway the greatest of them, and she was skilled in shrine rites and mediation. A bow, Mouun's Moon, is named for her.
### STANDARD +
Her lasting weapon is the shell-and-coral warbow now called Mouun's Moon. Its text describes her riding a sea creature into battle and gives the bow a pale, moonlit gleam.

## status
### LITE
Dead since the Archon War. Her songs, the Mouun Shrine on Watatsumi and the bow named for her remain.
### STANDARD +
She is not seen in any quest as a living person. Her sister Ayame disappeared into the sea after a last stand, and canon gives no further word of either of them.
### LARGE +
The Sangonomiya Chronicles record the Watatsumi side's surrender to the Shogunate after the war; they do not mention Mouun. Her story survives chiefly in the island's folk-tunes, which are still sung today.

## relationships
### LITE
Her twin sister Ayame fought with her, and Touzannou, who became the champion of Orobashi, was her student.
### STANDARD +
She served the Divine Priestess of Sangonomiya Shrine and followed Watatsumi Omikami (Orobashi), the Great Serpent, into the eastern campaign. Ayame was valiant and cheerful where Mouun was calm and wise, and the two are paired in the songs. Daikengyou was her close ally until it died with her.
### LARGE +
The unnamed boy later known as Touzannou learned whalesong and rites from the sisters. Orobashi and Touzannou were cut down together by one stroke of the Raiden Shogun's sword, which left what is now called Musoujin Gorge; it was this news that sent Mouun into retreat.

## entry: Mouun's Moon
@keys: Mouun's Moon

### STANDARD
A four-star bow of the Watatsumi series, released in version 2.2. Its text calls it a longbow made of shell and coral that shines white as seafoam under the moon, and tells of the shrine maiden and her sea-monster friends.

## entry: Daikengyou
@keys: Daikengyou
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
The giant blind whale that Mouun persuaded to join Watatsumi, escorted by narwhals and humpback whales. It was killed with her in the ambush after the war went badly. Its name echoes a title for the highest-ranking koto master, a naming note from the wiki rather than from canon.
