## summary
### LITE
Takamine the Mistsplitter was a swordsman and inveterate gambler of Inazuma who lived about five hundred years ago. Nicknamed Konbumaru, he served the Raiden Shogun as a Hatamoto, wielded the sword Mistsplitter and loved the shrine maiden Asase Hibiki. He vanished fighting the dark forces during the cataclysm.
### STANDARD +
He is remembered in a children's rhyme listing famous martial artists (Arataki of the Front Gate, Iwakura the Successor, Kitain the Serpent, Takamine the Mistsplitter). His story, a promise and a wager that went unfulfilled, is told through the texts of Mistsplitter Reforged, Thundering Pulse, Shimenawa's Reminiscence, Emblem of Severed Fate and Hamayumi.

## background
### LITE
In his youth he wandered Mt. Yougou and met the Great Tengu Reizenbou. He bet his young body against Thundering Pulse, a bow given by the Shogun; the bet ended three wins to three, so he became her servant but kept the bow. Under her he learned tengu archery and swordsmanship, then entered the Shogun's service on her recommendation and became a Hatamoto, though he never gave up wandering and gambling.
### STANDARD +
He followed Reizenbou to the Grand Narukami Shrine where the Kitsune Saiguu lived, met the Yougou Three there and grew close to Asase Hibiki, a closeness the Kitsune Saiguu liked to tease her about. He was prouder of his swordsmanship, and far better known for it, than of the archery he had learned; finding it a waste, he taught tengu archery to Hibiki. Hibiki scolded him for wandering and gambling even after he became a Hatamoto with weighty duties and (it is said) a sweet wife.
### LARGE +
Once he came to Hibiki to have his broken seal cage mended, and he dragged her to the harbour at Koseki Village for a day off, speaking about Teruyo of the shrine and about terrible nightmares in which he cut off his own head. The text says both knew these were words to hide the melancholy of adulthood.

## personality
### STANDARD
Cheerful, boastful and reckless: a gambler who, by the item texts, never regretted a wager and was fearless with the Tengu's bow. He joked about his own archery being wasted because he was "too good with a sword". Hibiki's affectionate insults call him an idiotic old gambler, a headache, "the idiot Konbumaru". Beneath that he kept a firm promise: he tied his return from the Abyss to a bet with Hibiki and meant to win it.
### LARGE +
His mannerisms and manner of speech (turning his back, a certain tone) were distinctive enough that Hibiki saw him in the pirate captain Zaemon. He confided terrible nightmares in which he cut off his own head, though Hibiki understood these as words covering ordinary adult melancholy.

## abilities
### LITE
A famous swordsman wielding Mistsplitter, a sword the Shogun gave to her Hatamoto, said to call on lightning to cut mountain mist and night fog. He also knew tengu archery.
### STANDARD +
The sword had been shattered once and reforged; its blade shows a cloud-like hamon. He used it to slay countless demons and Tatarigami, and in the Abyss kept slashing at a flood of foes like mist until it broke. Thundering Pulse, the Tengu's thunder-charged bow, was his favourite weapon before he left it with Hibiki.

## relationships
### LITE
Beloved of the shrine maiden Asase Hibiki; servant and archery pupil of the Great Tengu Reizenbou; samurai of the Raiden Shogun.
### STANDARD +
His route into the Shogun's service was Reizenbou's recommendation letter. The Kitsune Saiguu treated his romance with Hibiki as a source of gentle teasing. His wager with Hibiki is the central tie of his legend; see Hibiki's entry for the aftermath.

## status
### LITE
Dead for about five hundred years. He fought the dark forces with the Shogun in the cataclysm, was cut off and overwhelmed, and did not return for many years.
### STANDARD +
Years later something returned from the Abyss to the forest where he and Hibiki had agreed to meet. She, no longer young, loosed an arrow charged with power through it. Kagura's Verity, in Yae Miko's voice, says what returned was the darkened remains of his soul and that wiping it out was needed to protect Inazuma. Hibiki appears to have seen it as saving him (Shaft of Remembrance).
### LARGE +
After Hibiki returned to Asase Shrine she spoke fondly of him to Neko, usually as "Konbumaru". Neko took it as a good name and has since named a cat Konbumaru once per generation.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Youth: wanders Mt. Yougou; bets with Reizenbou; draws; becomes her servant and wins the bow.
- Under Reizenbou: learns tengu archery, meets the Yougou Three and the Kitsune Saiguu, and grows close to Hibiki.
- Later: enters the Shogun's service by recommendation and becomes a Hatamoto; marries (or is about to marry) a samurai's daughter.
- Cataclysm: leaves Thundering Pulse with Hibiki and goes with the Shogun against the Abyss; the Mistsplitter breaks; he is lost.
- Many years after: returns changed and is killed by Hibiki.
- Legacy: remembered in a children's rhyme; Neko's cats named Konbumaru; Mistsplitter Reforged is reforged from the fragments.
### ULTRA +
A separate thread: Hibiki's later aid to the pirate captain Zaemon (Ako Domeki) against the Shogunate fleet, partly because he reminded her of Takamine, led her to undo the great barrier at Seirai with the "true magic" she learned on Mt. Yougou.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
Do not call him the Raiden Shogun's lover or equal; he was her retainer. Do not claim canon gives a first name, an age or a wife's name. Do not state that Hibiki and he had a son as a fact. Do not say he was a villain or that he became a monster; item texts say the returned figure was the darkened remains of his soul. Keep romance factual and non-sexual.

## entry: Mistsplitter Reforged
@keys: Mistsplitter Reforged, Mistsplitter, Mistsplitter's Edge
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
A violet-glowing sword said to cut through mist and night fog, bestowed by the Shogun on her Hatamoto. It was shattered into a thousand pieces and later reforged from the fragments so that the weapon would inherit the Mistsplitter name; its hamon resembles flowing clouds. Takamine's sword broke in the Abyss, and the text says Mistsplitter had no successors and now lives only in stories, artworks and fairytales.

## entry: Takamine's wager with Asase Hibiki
@keys: Konbumaru, Takamine's wager, Takamine's bet
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
Before leaving for the Abyss Takamine left Thundering Pulse with Hibiki and bet he would return alive and claim "the future" along with the bow; if he lost, she kept it. He did not return in time, so she won the bow. When what came back from the Abyss reached her she was no longer young and, in Shaft of Remembrance's telling, shot it to save him and kept their promise as her own.

## entry: Takamine in the children's rhyme
@keys: Arataki of the Front Gate, Kitain the Serpent, Iwakura the Successor
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
Inazuman children who love martial arts list the famous warriors as Arataki of the Front Gate, Iwakura the Successor, Kitain the Serpent and Takamine the Mistsplitter. The rhyme appears in Mistsplitter Reforged's description.
