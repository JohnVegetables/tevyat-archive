## summary
### LITE
Xiphos was a legendary hero of Tulaytullah, a prosperous desert city of sapphire domes and emerald gardens, in the age after King Deshret's kingdom fell. Born a crown prince and hidden as a servant, he became its famed "Blade of Tulaytullah" and was killed in a duel by an exiled prince hired by the city's rivals.
### STANDARD +
He wielded a sword said to hold a bloodthirsty Jinni, saved many maidens from monsters and took treasure from them in the process. After his death Tulaytullah fell within a few years, leaving only its garden, which survives as the Garden of Endless Pillars.
### LARGE +
Everything about him is legend. The weapon texts that tell his story disagree on details, including who killed him, and the wiki warns against reading them as history.

## background
### LITE
He was born to a queen of one of the kingdoms in the Great Red Sand after King Deshret's kingdom fell. To save him from her kingdom's destruction, she dressed him as a servant and burned his crown and royal clothes, leaving him only the Key of Khaj-Nisut as proof of his birth.
### STANDARD +
Her parting words told him to use the sword as the key to the gate of paradise and rebuild the kingdom amid emeralds and pomegranates. After years as a wanderer he became the apprentice of the Old Blind Poet, a former retainer of the desert's vassal-kings, who realised he held one of the keys to the Eternal Oasis and urged him to unite the desert peoples.
### LARGE +
Xiphos then travelled between city-states and became the hero of Tulaytullah, home of the Tighnarians. In some accounts he was Makhaira's lover before she became a tribute dancer at Badanah's court.

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years, slain in a duel.
### STANDARD +
His killer left the desert for the rainforest afterwards. The Key of Khaj-Nisut was lost in the desert, and Tulaytullah fell within a few years.

## relationships
### STANDARD
- Old Blind Poet: his master and teacher, who described the people and world to him as eyes.
- Makhaira: lover in some accounts, later the dancer whose scheming is blamed for the exile of the prince who killed him.
- The unnamed killer: a fallen foreign prince hired by Tulaytullah's resentful rivals; some legends say Faramarz.
### LARGE +
- The Jinni of his sword: a cruel spirit that sharpened the blade with each slaughter and sang to its master of wine and blood. The weapon text calls it "now-silent".

## mistakes
### LARGE
- Do not state as fact that Faramarz killed him or that he and Makhaira were lovers.
- Do not portray him as a purely noble hero; the text has him taking treasure from the maidens he saved, and a Jinni's bloodlust on his blade.
- Do not give him lines or a known personality.

## chronology
### LARGE
- After Deshret's fall: born prince, hidden as servant, years of wandering, apprenticed to the Old Blind Poet.
- Journey across the desert city-states; becomes the hero of Tulaytullah.
- Duel with the hired exile and his death.
- Few years after: the Key is lost and Tulaytullah falls.

## trivia
### ULTRA
- The name echoes xiphos, an ancient Greek double-edged sword, as makhaira echoes a Greek sword; the wiki also notes parallels with Hannibal and Homeric themes. These are observations, not canon.
- The Old Blind Poet may be modelled on Homer, per the wiki.

### STANDARD
One of a paired set of obsidian scepters said to prove the right to rule the desert and to open the gate of the paradise at the end of the ocean of sand. Xiphos' mother left it to him when she disguised him. It was lost in the desert after his death.

## entry: The fall of Tulaytullah

### STANDARD
In weapon lore, Tulaytullah was a city of sapphire domes and emerald gardens, home of the Tighnarians, prosperous in the age after King Deshret's fall. Rival city-states resented it and hired an exiled prince to kill Xiphos. Within a few years the city fell, leaving only its garden as the Garden of Endless Pillars.
