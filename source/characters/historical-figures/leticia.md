## summary
### LITE
Leticia Randolph was the eldest daughter of House Randolph, a powerful noble house of Fontaine. She slipped into Fleuve Cendre in disguise, fell in love with a man named Reed, gave up her family name and took up an axe. The claymore Verdict tells her story.
### STANDARD +
She is known only from that one weapon description and its cross-references. Her end is not recorded.

## background
### LITE
Her father taught her that nobles' falls take commoners with them, and that she must keep a calm, pure bearing: the family name could bring a commoner prosperity or send him to Fleuve Cendre. Behind her father's and brother's backs she put on a disguise and went down into the sunless undercity out of curiosity. A local man, Reed, saw through the disguise at once, and the visit became an escape from her family.
### STANDARD +
Reed called her a poor liar, noted that her clothes bore no soot, oil or blood, and asked her to set aside, for a while, being a Randolph and see how her fellow Fontainians lived. He repeated the 'Boss's' words, that everything the sun touches should belong to them and Fleuve Cendre to Fleuve Cendre's people, and told her that, should he be lost, her hand should decide his fate. She fell in love with him, left her surname behind, and, years later, was no longer used to fine dresses but to the weight of an axe.
### LARGE +
When her father found her again she had taken 'the name of the rose' and had a child. He pleaded with her to come home: he lit a candle for her every day, would not abandon his grandchild, and hinted that 'Randolph magic' could do something for her husband. She answered that she had made a pact with her beloved and would not let the Randolph name be stained on her account. Only a shard of memory survives of the Siege of Poisson, in which she recalled that the hunters' secret paths should have been unknown to outsiders.
### ULTRA +
The text opens with her fascination with Parsifal's magic, showing she knew Fleuve Cendre's stage culture from the surface. In the Fleuve Cendre Ferryman photograph, a woman named Rosa holding an infant stands beside her husband Thompson and near Parsifal; the wiki reads her as Leticia, which fits 'the name of the rose', a husband called Reed and a baby.

## status
### LITE
Fate unknown. Last seen in the sources at the Siege of Poisson, over 500 years ago.
### STANDARD +
Not mentioned in any quest as a living person; as far as checked she is not part of 5.x, 6.x or 7.0-7.1 story content.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Her father, a gentle, aged noble, loves her and wants her home; her husband Reed, a Fleuve Cendre man, was the first to see through her disguise. Her brother and servants are only mentioned as the ones she hid from.
### LARGE +
The 'Boss' of Reed's slogan is presumably Eduardo Baker, whose followers she joined at Poisson. The Poisson photograph by journalist Karl Ingold places 'Rosa' among Baker's circle, so Parsifal the Great, Baker's son and the Petrichors were all near her.

## entry: Verdict
@keys: Verdict, Leticia Randolph
@version: 4.3
### STANDARD
A five-star claymore whose description tells Leticia's story. Its text moves from her father's lecture, through her disguised visit and Reed, to the later reunion with her father at Poisson.
### LARGE +
Treat the ending as open: it leaves it ambiguous whether she accepted her father's offer, and says only that in a dream she remembered the hunters using secret paths that outsiders should not have known.

## entry: Rosa Reed
@keys: Rosa Reed, Thompson Reed

### STANDARD
Rosa and Thompson Reed appear in Karl Ingold's photograph of Baker's circle at Poisson (Fleuve Cendre Ferryman, 4.0). Rosa holds an infant. The wiki identifies Rosa with Leticia and Thompson with Reed, but canon does not state it.
