## summary
### LITE
Mikoshi Nagamasa was an Inazuman who lived around the time of the cataclysm, the adopted son of the oni warrior Mikoshi Chiyo and one of the Yougou Three. As Inspector at Tatarasuna he forged the nagamaki Daitatara Nagamasa and, to end the Tatarasuna Mystery, executed his loyal yoriki Katsuragi with it. Both man and sword became known as "Katsuragikiri Nagamasa".
### STANDARD +
He is remembered as upright, stubborn and obsessed with cleansing his family name. His story connects directly to the Wanderer, who once lived among the workers at Tatarasuna as the nameless "kabukimono".
### LARGE +
Almost everything about him comes from Tatarasuna's Rather Aged Notes, a weapon description, the Archon Interlude chapter that exposed the truth in 3.3, and a few spoken memories. He is never met in the present.
### ULTRA +
He speaks only a few times across canon: a short exchange in the Wanderer's memory (3.3), a half-remembered promise to his adoptive brother in a Kitsune Statue voice memory, and a nightmare cry in a limited 5.4 event cinematic. The rest is reported by others: Tatarasuna workers, the Sumeru scholar Aqaba, the Inazuman novelist Sawada, and the Narukami Trail's local historians.

## personality
### LITE
Stern, principled and obsessive about honour; a harsh man who knows right from wrong but will not bend once he has decided.
### STANDARD +
A worker's note calls him harsh but knowing right from wrong, "not amenable to reason", and says his name indicates "one obsessed with purity", and says the writer and some Tatarasuna households trusted him despite his mother's disgrace. Forging seemed to lift his temper; the same notes say his mood improved whenever he worked a blade. Local legend in the Narukami Trail calls him extremely just and upright, even stubborn to a fault.
### LARGE +
His defining drive is the desire to wash away the Mikoshi stain, which the notes say wore on him. Niwa judged him more cautious and by-the-book than himself about Escher, and Escher attributed that caution to his wish to redeem the family's honour. When Tatarasuna's disaster needed a culprit, his vow to clear the name meant he could not allow himself to die. An old note hopes he will someday remember the joy of forging with his own hands. Weapon text calls his loyalty and self-admitted stupidity the cause of his devotion to the Mikoshi name.
### ULTRA +
Even afterwards, Sawada's closing lines imagine him as an old man dreaming of the night the Daitatara Nagamasa was forged, with painting, dancing and drinking turning to red clouds in the furnace. Sawada reasons that the sword in Nagamasa's final memory was the Daitatara rather than the Katsuragikiri because he still had fond memories of it; this is the novelist's imagining, not an official record.

## background
### LITE
Fostered by the oni maiden Chiyo and raised beside her son Michihiro, he trained with the Yougou Tengu with Asase Hibiki and Kamuna Harunosuke. After the cataclysm destroyed Chiyo's name, he joined the Shogunate to restore the Mikoshi honour.
### STANDARD +
He rose to Inspector and was posted at Tatarasuna, where he took up bladesmithing, sought instruction from Vice Armory Officer Miyazaki and, with Katsuragi's and Miyazaki's help, forged a nagamaki. When Tatarasuna's furnace began killing workers and Armory Officer Niwa vanished, blame fell to Nagamasa as second-in-command.
### LARGE +
Five hundred years ago the cataclysm struck. Of the four people who mattered to the Yougou Three (Takamine, Reizenbou, the Kitsune Saiguu and Chiyo), only Reizenbou came back intact: the Saiguu died, Takamine vanished and Chiyo was corrupted and fled. The three students parted. Harunosuke went to Liyue, Hibiki returned to Asase Shrine, Nagamasa entered the Shogunate. Some time later (the Tatarasuna Mystery is dated about 400 years ago, canon not stating his age), in Tatarasuna, he took Katsuragi's report of a nameless young man found in a cave sealed by a landslide, agreed to call him "the kabukimono" as the workers already did, and sent Katsuragi to tell Niwa someone new was joining them. He knew the youth carried a gold ornament granted by the Shogun, told him he was "neither man nor mechanism" and apologised for dealing with him as he must.
### ULTRA +
When a Fontaine mechanic calling himself Escher introduced a new smelting process at the Mikage Furnace, workers began falling sick. Niwa told Escher that Nagamasa may also have noticed the troubles tracked him, but was more cautious and did things by the book; Escher replied that caution suited a man seeking to redeem his family's honour. Escher (Dottore) then murdered Niwa, and the people came to believe Niwa had fled. Sawada's unfinished novel The Isle of Darkling Clouds dramatises the aftermath, with Nagamasa leading a search party and drawing his blade on a laughing veiled apparition until Katsuragi pulls him back from the furnace's edge; the book is openly fantastical and is not a record. In the end, Katsuragi insisted on taking the blame; Nagamasa killed him with the Daitatara Nagamasa and cast the blade into the furnace; Nozomu, who could not accept the order, drew the melted blade out of the furnace, was horribly burned and died of his burns that night. Kinjiro hid the remains and Nozomu's painting in the Arsenal. He wrote the Anthology of Inazuman Blades (when is not stated), which records Haran Geppaku Futsu of the Futsu school, and Sawada heard that he lived to a ripe old age.

## relationships
### STANDARD
His family: Chiyo, whose fall defined his life, and Michihiro, who left him alone with the name. At Tatarasuna: Katsuragi, whom he saved and later killed; Niwa, whose disappearance condemned him; Miyazaki, Nozomu and Kinjiro, who forged and mourned beside him. Toward the youth called the kabukimono, he was guarded and apologetic.
### LARGE +
Niwa's last words, spoken to Dottore for the kabukimono, say that both Nagamasa and Niwa see the kabukimono as "one of us". A Tatarasuna worker's note says some households trusted him despite his mother's disgrace. Michihiro's name survives in the Iwakura Clan, while descendants of Nagamasa are said to be the present-day Mikoshi in Inazuma City.

## status
### LITE
Dead long ago. He is a figure of history who survived the Tatarasuna Mystery, is said to have lived to old age, and is known now only by the epithet that still clings to him and his sword.
### STANDARD +
Tatarasuna's notes, the Arsenal and the sword's diagram are what remain of him. According to Inagi Hotomi, most people surnamed Mikoshi in Inazuma City today are his descendants.
### LARGE +
No appearance after Version 3.3's revelations changes his story. Searches of Nod-Krai (6.x) and Snezhnaya (7.0-7.1) material found no new mentions as of 7.1.

## entry: Daitatara Nagamasa
@keys: Daitatara Nagamasa, Katsuragikiri Nagamasa, Katsuragi-Slashing Nagamasa
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The nagamaki Nagamasa forged at the Mikage Furnace with Katsuragi and Vice Armory Officer Miyazaki. Its name means "Great Tatara Nagamasa". After he used it to execute Katsuragi he threw it into the furnace; Nozomu pulled out the melted remains and died of his burns. Kinjiro hid the remains with Nozomu's painting in the Arsenal. The epithet "Katsuragikiri" then passed to both Nagamasa and the weapon. The playable weapon version is a different item (see the item entry); this entry is the in-world sword.
### LARGE +
Workers' notes record that the completed blade lifted everyone's mood: Nozomu painted it, Katsuragi and the kabukimono danced a sword dance, and Sawada's epilogue later imagines the forging night as one of painting, dancing and drinking. The weapon text calls it tough and simple, says he designed it after consulting Miyazaki while obsessed with "the grim loveliness of steel", and says its diagram is as simple and upright as its maker.

## entry: Tatarasuna Mystery
@keys: Tatarasuna Mystery, Tatarasuna incident, Mikage Furnace sickness
@keys2: Nagamasa, Katsuragi, Niwa, kabukimono
@version: 3.3
@spoiler: major
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
About 400 years ago the Mikage Furnace's new smelting process sickened and killed Tatarasuna workers. Armory Officer Niwa was secretly murdered by "Escher" (Dottore), and the story spread that Niwa had fled. Responsibility fell to Inspector Nagamasa. Katsuragi insisted on taking the punishment, and Nagamasa executed him. The Wanderer's kabukimono, who had left Tatarasuna, came to believe in a "second betrayal" through Dottore's lie. Nothing in canon shows Nagamasa ever learning who the mechanic truly was.
### LARGE +
The truth surfaced in the 3.3 Archon Interlude when the Sumeru scholar Aqaba, with the Inazuman novelist Sawada, studied the Rather Aged Notes and Nahida drew on Dottore's memories. Afterwards the Wanderer erased his own name from history; the wiki infers that the record now shows a foreign mechanic where the kabukimono had been. Sawada decides to end his novel with an epilogue narrated by Nagamasa as an old man.

## entry: Yougou Three
@keys: Yougou Three, Asase Hibiki, Kamuna Harunosuke
@keys2: Nagamasa, Yougou Tengu
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Three Inazumans who trained under the Yougou Tengu about five hundred years ago: Asase Hibiki, Kamuna Harunosuke and Mikoshi Nagamasa. They lodged for a time in Araumi and found the mansion of the Great Tengu Reizenbou. After the cataclysm cost the four people who most shaped them (Takamine, Reizenbou, the Kitsune Saiguu and Chiyo) they separated: Harunosuke to Liyue, Hibiki to Asase Shrine on Seirai Island, Nagamasa into the Shogunate. Their motivations for studying are partly unknown.
### LARGE +

## entry: Mikoshi Clan and Chiyo's disgrace
@keys: Mikoshi Clan, Mikoshi Chiyo, Torachiyo
@keys2: Nagamasa, Michihiro, Mikoshi
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The oni Mikoshi Chiyo (also called Torachiyo) was close to the Raiden Shogun until the cataclysm's Abyssal corruption drove her to attack Ei on Mt. Yougou; struck down by Ei, she fled and her fate is disputed in legend. Her biological son Michihiro, heir of the clan, withdrew to Konda Village in shame and later adopted the Iwakura name; her adopted son Nagamasa joined the Shogunate to rebuild the family's reputation.
### LARGE +
Because of Chiyo's disgrace, Nagamasa's every cautious decision at Tatarasuna can be read as protecting the clan's name, Niwa calls him more cautious and by-the-book, and the disguised Dottore explicitly ties that caution to his wish to redeem the family's honour.
