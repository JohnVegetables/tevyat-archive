## summary
### LITE
Ahpub was a hero of ancient Natlan, the first bearer of the Ancient Name Uwezo and one of the original six Heroes of Natlan. With his twin sister Ixquieh he swore to end death, destruction and pain, joined the warrior Xbalanque against the dragons, and later became the first Chief of the Collective of Plenty. He is remembered for his explosive temper and his blowdarts.
### STANDARD +
He and Ixquieh are the only heroes of the Alliance era whose end is told three different ways. The Collective of Plenty still honours him as its founder.

## background
### LITE
Ahpub and his twin Ixquieh were born while dragons still ruled Natlan. Their father died through the Dragonlord's whims and their mother gave her life to shield them; the twins were parted and endured countless trials before meeting again before the corpse of an evil dragon. There they vowed to end death, destruction and pain and to stop the strong from bullying the weak.
### STANDARD +
Their reunion was only possible, the weapon text says, with the help of the Collective of Plenty and the clans of the mines (the Children of Echoes). Ahpub was the "explosive" one and Ixquieh the calm one; he fought with blowdarts and she with forge-fire. Following the paths of "strength" (Uwezo) and "blessings" (Baraka), they once tricked the earthquake-causing dragon Aj Imuch' Paxlaq Lahun, Tenth of the Thirteen Sovereign Lords, into drinking an elixir that stripped its power, using an imitation gem forged with a fire mortals had not known, and buried it alive among the mountains. They then met Xbalanque and pledged their weapons to his cause for as long as it meant executing fell dragons and stamping out suffering.
### LARGE +
The pledge extended to Och-Kan and the Grand Alliance. When Och-Kan became King of Ochkanatlan, Ahpub became Chief of the Collective of Plenty and Ixquieh Chief of the Children of Echoes. He never publicly opposed Xbalanque's ban on harming Saurians, but his hatred of dragonkind was well known. When the new king ordered fleeing dragons hunted down, only Ahpub among the remaining heroes answered, leading Collective warriors to the mine-clan's door and demanding the "ferocious beasts" sheltered there.
### ULTRA +
In the 7.0 weapon story Heretic's Molten Blade, which is narrated from Och-Kan's side, Ahpub appears in the celebration after the heroes swept a stronghold of draconic descendants. He is a drinking companion at the bonfire, praised for dodging claws, fighting one-handed in mid-air and even riding a dragon's head into a cliff; the tale says he is the only one pleased by the answer Och-Kan gives to Xbalanque's question about which weapon he would choose.

## status
### LITE
Dead or vanished roughly two thousand years ago, depending on the account; he does not appear in the present. After the fire he is not seen again. His Ancient Name Uwezo passed on within the Collective of Plenty and is now carried by Iansan. The Collective of Plenty keeps his story alive in its customs.
### STANDARD +
Tribe members today say the tragedy is why Collective of Plenty fighters prefer bare-handed wrestling in daily life: it keeps contests open and non-lethal, though they still use weapons against the Abyss.

## relationships
### STANDARD
**Ixquieh** was his twin and equal partner; their shared vow bound them until the dragon question drove them apart, and the manner of her death is told three ways (see below). **Xbalanque** was the leader they chose; the twins offered blowdarts and forge-fires to him. **Och-Kan** was the king whose later order Ahpub alone obeyed; Collective members now call that trust misplaced. The **Collective of Plenty** claims him as founder, and its smith Xona uses him as the cautionary tale behind the tribe's wrestling culture. **Sundjatta**, a later hero, is the source of one of the competing death accounts.

## entry: Ahpub and Ixquieh's Falling Out

### STANDARD
The most detailed version comes from the Ring of Yaxche (2nd page). After Och-Kan became king and ordered fleeing dragons pursued, Ahpub marched on his sister's territory demanding the dragons they sheltered. After days facing each other he asked her to a private meeting, hoping sincere argument would bring her back to the vow. She called the king and her brother the real oathbreakers, said his vengeance had become blind bloodlust, and reminded him of Xbalanque's ban on harming peaceful Saurians. In a rage he put his sword to her neck merely to cow her, but struck her throat by accident and she bled out in his arms. He carried her into the incinerating fire. Both chiefs vanished; no war between tribes followed, though the king later demanded names of those who sheltered dragons.

## entry: Competing Accounts of the Twins' End

@version: 5.5
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Three accounts disagree. In the Ring of Yaxche, Ahpub's blade accidentally kills Ixquieh and he follows her into the fire. In the Flamelets text (5.8), Sundjatta says Ixquieh burned her workshop and writings and took her life while forcing her brother to do the same, to spare him the tarnished legacy of Och-Kan. Xona (5.5) says Ahpub, trusting Och-Kan after Xbalanque entered the Sacred Flame, led an army against the Children of Echoes; Ixquieh threw herself on his sword to force a shared death; but "legends claim" the siblings survived, saw through the plot to stir up discord and fled together to a distant land. No source resolves this.
