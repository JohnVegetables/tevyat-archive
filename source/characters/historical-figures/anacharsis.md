## summary
### LITE
A Chronicler from the sea of stars who warned Nibelung of the Abyss, then lived in Hyperborea as the Exalted Saint, co-ruling with the angel Koitar in rebellion against the Heavenly Principles until she left Teyvat.
### STANDARD +
She has no speaking role in any quest. She is known from artifact and weapon texts, a Liyuean folk tale of the seelie, a Frostmoon hymn, a Hyperborean time-capsule letter and the Belyi Tsar's notes, and these are sympathetic, legendary or hostile by turns. Item texts use "she" for her original form and "he" while she inhabited a Hyperborean youth.
### LARGE +
Later ages remember her in different shapes: the Frostmoon Scions as Seutervoinen, a Snezhnayan songbook legend as the "King of Hyperborea", and the Belyi Tsar as a prophet whose rebellion he first blamed, then understood. Her mark on the present is the Shadow Realm, which the Tsar's notes credit to her and which slowly holds back the Abyss.

## background
### LITE
A Chronicler of an ancient interstellar civilization, bound never to speak with the peoples she recorded, she broke that rule to warn Nibelung of the Abyss. Returning later, she lived among mortals in Hyperborea inside a youth's body.
### STANDARD +
After ten millennia of meditation she sensed a glimmer from a small world at the edge of a spiral arm: the will of Nibelung, which shone brighter than any civilization she had seen. She told him she had seen the Abyss tear through star clusters and drown all songs, and begged him to leave with her. He answered that his people were the meaning of the universe to him, that his bones would shield the realm, and that he would lead them to the stars. When she came back, the Heavenly Principles ruled, the earth was bound by the Four Shades and Nibelung had vanished.
Unwilling to disturb the new ruler, and against the laws of her kin, she cast her mind into a humbly born Hyperborean youth with star-shaped pupils to listen to the city's debates. The priests were quarrelling over how to appease the angels after the city questioned the progress of knowledge. He slipped into their chamber and refuted their charges of sin, and the chief priest, wearing the white-branched crown, sent him to plead his case before the first angel beneath the Irminsul. There he told Koitar the origin he came from. Her crown broke, she told him the secrets of creation, and he swore to be her ally against her ruler. The three moons witnessed the oath.
### LARGE +
As Exalted Saint and Koitar's partner she ran Hyperborea for thirty years. Under them the city taught advanced Mekhaneikos and Tekhneikos, forged fae spirits on the bones of giant beasts and, in a letter from the Elysium Project's tenth year, raised a tower to knock on the heavens' gate. They urged exploration of the sea of stars beyond the false sky. A time-capsule letter from that era, written by a Hyperborean, credits the Exalted Saint and Dawn's Navigator with guiding the work and imagines descendants visiting the Saint's home planet.
The Belyi Tsar's notes say the Saint came to Hyperborea seeking the light from the world's genesis to learn whether it was truly extinguished, and that this is the light behind the Tsar's own birth. Hyperborea's destruction, he concludes, was decreed before the Saint arrived, and those who stayed faithful to the future could only delay it thirty years.
### ULTRA +
When Celestial Nails fell on the city at the end of the unified civilization, the rebel leaders chose to face the end calmly. Koitar spoke her last words to the "first awakened" at a small preaching hole on the outskirts, called Anacharsis her dear companion and told her to return to the world she belonged to, since this was never her war. The host body, worn by the ages, answered with silence, and Anacharsis is understood to have left it. Koitar was stripped of name and form and her kin were cursed, so that any angel who loves one person alone loses body and mind and rises as a Seelie. 
The Tsar later reached the ruins through the Shadow Realm. His notes treat the Saint as the creator of the Shadow Realm, whose shielding of Teyvat from the Abyss may be an unintended result of the Saint's memories, and warn that the Abyss has been seeping in since the Tsar opened its passage.

## status
### LITE
Left Teyvat when Hyperborea fell, about 6,000 years ago. Whether she lives is not stated through 7.1. Surtalogi sent Skirk after the voyager's trail; no meeting is shown.
### STANDARD +
Her host body, the youth, was left behind on the platform. The Etherlight Spindlelute text gives a young woman's lament in the abandoned Seelie court, recalling the tower's apostle and "those star-like eyes". No text through 7.1 says the two reunited.

## relationships
### STANDARD
- Nibelung: the friend she warned of the Abyss. He chose to stay with his people; she left, came back and found him gone.
- Koitar: the first angel and her spouse. Their love broke Koitar's crown and began the rebellion; Koitar later urged her to flee.
- Heavenly Principles: ruler of the false sky, whom she chose not to disturb until the rebellion.
- Hyperborea's people: they called her the Exalted Saint and followed her and Koitar for thirty years.
### LARGE +
- Belyi Tsar: his notes first condemn her as a deluded prophet, then admit he was mistaken after reading the true records.
- Skirk and Surtalogi: Surtalogi told Skirk of a voyager in the north; the Finale of the Deep Galleries texts frame her trek as a search for that trail.
- Frostmoon Scions: their hymn praises the ship of Seutervoinen beside the arrows of Ianikuinen.

## entry: The Saint's Notes
@keys: Notes by a Certain Noble Individual, Belyi Tsar notes, Pale Crown Palace notes
@keys2: Anacharsis, Saint, Hyperborea
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Belyi Tsar wrote six sets of notes in the Pale Crown Palace about his expeditions into the Shadow Realm. In the second he blames the Saint's folly for Hyperborea's destruction. In the third he suspects the Shadow Realm was made by the Saint, as a film separating Teyvat from the Abyss. In the fourth he reverses himself: the city's fate was fixed before the Saint came, the Saint sought the light of the world's genesis, and those faithful to the future bought only thirty years. In the fifth he calls himself the posthumous child of that first light.
