## summary
### LITE
Ormazd was a shepherd who, favoured by King Deshret and the Jinni Liloupar, became the first vassal-king of Gurabad. He grew into an ordinary tyrant, and Liloupar's revenge started with his death at a banquet.
### STANDARD +
He is remembered in the desert as the founder of the City of Kings and, in the Eremite tales, as the doomed father of Shirin and the adoptive father of the hero Parvezravan.

## background
### LITE
Ormazd began as a shepherd of a nomadic clan. King Deshret sent Liloupar to help him gather the desert tribes, and with her blessings he beat his rivals, overthrew a tyrant named Huvashtra and built Gurabad.
### STANDARD +
Liloupar loved him and hoped he would turn out an enlightened ruler. She gave him a hundred generations of blessings at the price of 'sharp retribution'. He took slavery and sacrifice for granted and shrugged off her warnings, so she resolved on three generations of ruin.
### LARGE +
Deshret was his patron and he built palaces and temples for his worship, taking slaves from tribes and demanding service from states. His own tablet says he dedicated half of his kingdom to Liloupar and half to Deshret, and that he would leave his throne in white vestments with a sacrifice of a thousand slaves. When Liloupar's daughter Shirin was born, she predicted that Shirin would fall for a hero whose child would surpass its father, that many relatives would meet a sweet end, and that only Shirin would hold Gurabad. She told him that her joy would bring tears to his eyes, that he should no longer eat at the same table as her after her wedding and that her heir would be an ill omen for the land. He only smiled.
### ULTRA +
He adopted the orphan Kisra, who was raised by a giant divine bird and became a famous conqueror. As reward he gave Kisra the name Parvez and Shirin's hand. Then the night of the banquet came. He had barred Shirin from his table as Liloupar's warnings advised, yet poisoned honey reached the hall; accounts differ on how (see the banquet entry). The Tale's storyteller says all 300 of his children died with him, and that Kisra took the crown as the new king. In the Dirge of Bilqis (3.4) Liloupar calls it a punishment for her lover and for herself, and Shirin is shown as an unwitting cause.

## status
### LITE
Dead for thousands of years. His remains and those of his heirs are said in the Tale to lie in crypts beneath the sands of Gurabad.
### STANDARD +
Gurabad itself fell two generations after him. His murals and tablets in the ruined Temple of Deshret and the Desert of Hadramaveth are the main surviving record.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Liloupar loved him and judged him; Shirin is the daughter he feared; Parvezravan is the son he adopted without knowing whose child he was. Deshret is his patron.
### LARGE +
Liloupar's earrings were a gift from him, and she took them off when she decided on revenge. Nameless Tablet I, addressed to Kisra and apparently Liloupar's, says retribution for Ormazd's ambitions will come; Tablet II, addressed to Shirin, asks her forgiveness.
### ULTRA +
His grandson Shiruyeh, child of Shirin and Kisra, is the 'ill omen' of Liloupar's third warning. The book says Ormazd had 300 children besides, all dead.

## speech
### STANDARD
No voice-overs or dialogue by him survive except tablet text in a formal, prayerful register that praises Deshret's grace. He is recorded as using the terms 'my immortal partner' for Liloupar and 'my lord' for Deshret.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- He is not Parvezravan; Parvezravan was his adoptive son and successor.
- Ormazd did not know Kisra was Liloupar's son.
- Do not name Gurabad's later 'kings of kings' as Ormazd's heirs; they are later claimants.
### LARGE +
- Not a Khaenri'ahn, Eremite chief or a god. He was a human vassal king.
- The 'villain Shirin' story is the book's account; the quests show a different version. Preserve both.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Thousands of years ago: Deshret sends Liloupar; Ormazd overthrows Huvashtra, builds Gurabad.
- Birth of Shirin; Liloupar's prophecies; adoption of Kisra.
- The banquet: Ormazd and his heirs die; Kisra becomes king.
### ULTRA +
- 3.0: the Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin introduces him as Ormazd Shah.
- 3.3: Desert Pavilion Chronicle artifacts tell of his rise.
- 3.4: Memories of Gurabad and Dirge of Bilqis quests; murals and tablets readable.

## trivia
### ULTRA
- His name is a variant of Ahura Mazda and draws on the Sasanian king Hormizd IV, whose son Khosrow II is the model for Parvezravan.
- The desert's shepherd-to-king story echoes biblical David, per the wiki.
- The wiki notes that the Tale omits Liloupar's role, which the later quests reveal.

## entry: Liloupar and Ormazd
@keys: Liloupar, Jinni Liloupar, Ormazd's lover
@keys2: Ormazd, Gurabad
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Deshret sent Liloupar from Ay-Khanoum, the city of the Moon Maiden, to help Ormazd. The Jinni fell in love with him. As an attendant of the Goddess of Flowers she believed she had picked the ideal king, and she gave him advice in private that he took for pillow-talk. When he became a tyrant she imposed 'three generations of ruination'.

## entry: The Banquet of Poisoned Honey
@keys: poisoned honey, banquet, Night of Honey
@keys2: Ormazd, Gurabad, Shirin
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The book The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin says slaves poured scorpion venom into musk honey served to Ormazd and his 300 heirs while Shirin, barred from the hall by her father, and her reluctant co-conspirator husband Kisra were the only ones left with dreams to enjoy. The Dirge of Bilqis quest shows another version: Liloupar's honey scheme used Shirin as an unwitting carrier, and Shirin's mind broke when she saw the result. Both agree that Kisra took the throne.
### LARGE +
The book also says the slaves were drowned in the honey pot by the new king. Quests do not confirm this.

## entry: Huvashtra and the founding of Gurabad
@keys: Huvashtra, founding of Gurabad, Valivija
@keys2: Ormazd
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Ormazd's own tablet says he overthrew a tyrant named Huvashtra by Deshret's grace, built Gurabad with Liloupar's help and gathered the wandering desert tribes. Nothing else is said of Huvashtra. The Tale describes the age as the century of the vassal kings, after one of three living gods fell and before Al-Ahmar and the Sage-King of the Greenwood restored the oasis paradise.
