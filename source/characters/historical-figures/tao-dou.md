## summary
### LITE
Tao Dou, divine name Bune, was a god of Liyue who lived before and during the Archon War. It took the form of a giant tree on the summit of a divine mountain and stood before the realm of the dead, where it was called the god of the underworld who could pacify the forces of life and death. Dragged into the Archon War, it was defeated and purified by the Eight Adepts, and its remains became the border between the living and the dead.
### STANDARD +
Liyue still tells the story as the tale and opera "The Sanctification of Tao Dou". Its remains are said to lie beneath Mt. Qingce, held down by the Hidden Palace of Zhou Formula and the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method. Most of what is known comes from storytellers and from Liyue characters repeating legend, so treat details as folk history rather than recorded fact.

## background
@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
### LITE
Legend places a divine mountain in Liyue with the god Tao Dou, a giant tree, on its summit. People believed the realm of the dead lay behind the tree, and that a soul weighed down by grave sins could not pass beyond it into the afterlife. When the Archon War reached Liyue, Tao Dou was pulled into the fighting. The boundary between life and death blurred and death began to spread into the world of the living.
### STANDARD +
The Eight Adepts answered the crisis: the Three Hermits of Guizang, the Two Sages of Lianshan and the Three Beasts of Tao Dou, a band said to be mostly ordinary humans plus non-human adepti. Together they laid down a great array to purify the god. Tao Dou's remains sank into the ley lines and became the border separating the living of Liyue from the dead. The Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method was used for the purification, and by one account one of the Three Beasts, the Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame, gave her life to set the array working; her art was later taken up by the Wuwang Kid and passed to the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor. This account is told during the Springtime Charms event (5.3, limited-time) and is not all verified elsewhere.
### LARGE +
The appearance is described as a twisted trunk, cracked to show a bright golden interior, with golden foliage at the tips of its branches. The mountain where the Three Beasts of Tao Dou retreated shares its name. In Chenyu Vale it is still a gross insult to say "You'll never cross Mt. Tao Dou when you die." Lan Yan warns the Traveler not to repeat it.

### ULTRA +
Accounts of the Three Beasts differ in framing. Keqing and Lan Yan present the Eight as a legend whose members were mostly human, while Xingqiu remarks elsewhere that the Three Beasts of Tao Dou were all adepti. After the purification, Wind Reader and the Jade-Plumed Silverwing are said to have travelled together and met every autumn. The knowledge of the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method was later lost, and Yuehai Pavilion holds no record of it; only the name survived, in legend.

## status
@version: 5.3
### LITE
Dead in the sense of having been defeated and purified long ago; its remains survive as the border between life and death. Those remains lie beneath Mt. Qingce, suppressed by the Hidden Palace of Zhou Formula. As of 7.1 no new information has appeared.
### STANDARD +
During Springtime Charms (5.3, limited-time) fetor leaked from that border near Wuwang Hill, sickening the elderly of Qingce Village. Hu Tao, with the Ministry of Civil Affairs and friends, re-enacted a scaled-down version of the Sanctification to repair the affected stretch of the border. In the later Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon epilogue, Zibai recognises the name Bune when she visits Wuwang Hill with the Traveler.
### LARGE +
Its last mention found was that epilogue, set in Liyue. Nothing was found tying Tao Dou to Nod-Krai (6.x) or Snezhnaya (7.0-7.1) content.

## relationships
### STANDARD
The Eight Adepts defeated it, and storytellers celebrate them for it. Zibai knew it as Bune. The Wangsheng Funeral Parlor, Hu Tao's family business, carries on a secret art tied to guarding the border it left behind, and Lan Yan's Lan clan of Chenyu Vale claims descent from the hermit Wind Reader, one of the Guizang Hermits who took part.

## entry: Eight Adepts and the Sanctification
@keys: Sanctification of Tao Dou, Eight Adepts, Three Beasts of Tao Dou
@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
Liyue's legend of the Eight Adepts says they came from three groups: the Three Hermits of Guizang (including Wind Reader, the Yunlai Angler and the Wuwang Kid), the Two Sages of Lianshan, and the Three Beasts of Tao Dou, who were not human. They purified Tao Dou in the Archon War with the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method. Told in the Springtime Charms event (limited-time).

## entry: Border between life and death

@keys2: Tao Dou, Bune

### STANDARD
Said to be formed from Tao Dou's remains sinking into the ley lines. Its leaks of fetor near Wuwang Hill were the crisis of Springtime Charms (limited-time event). A furnishing from that event says the remains rest beneath Mt. Qingce, keeping balance between the realms of the living and the dead.
