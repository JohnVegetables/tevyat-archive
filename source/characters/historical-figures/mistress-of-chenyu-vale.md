## summary
### LITE
The Mistress of Chenyu Vale is the unnamed god who once ruled Chenyu Vale and the ancient city of Chizhang, presiding over its beasts, spirits and adepti. When the Archon War came, she turned cruel, fought Morax, and was defeated and sealed. Her name has been forgotten.
### STANDARD +
She is known only through legend: Fujin's recollection, a book of mountain ballads, and a few item texts. Fujin insists she was not evil and once made many dreams come true, and blames the Archon War for what she became.

## background
### LITE
In the distant past she ruled Chenyu Vale as its god and kept the balance between mortals and the wild, controlling the ebb and flow of the Bishui River. Three adepti served her: the carp Fujin, the snake called the Herblord and the suanni Lingyuan. She was among the first gods that ancient Liyue worshipped, and was associated with jade.
### STANDARD +
When the Archon War began she lost herself to a hunger to become an Archon, or to survive, and stopped granting the gentle dreams Fujin remembers. She declared war on Morax and the gods of the Guili Assembly. Her power was far below his, and she grew more desperate. As a last gamble she sent the Bishui River in flood to wipe out her enemies downstream, knowing the flood would also drown Chenyu Vale and its people.
### LARGE +
Her three adepti turned on her. Lingyuan drove the people into the shelters Fujin and the Herblord had prepared, the Herblord fought her, and Fujin climbed Mt. Lingmeng to hurl the Votive Rainjade into the waters. Together they saved the vale, at heavy cost: the Herblord was torn apart and lived on in a tiny form, and Fujin sank to Carp's Rest and lost most of her strength. A local weapon text speaks of monsters that fled to the mountains pursuing refugees, grieving a fallen master, which the wiki connects to her. In a folk retelling by Little Mao the war turned the Bishui crimson.
### ULTRA +
After Morax brought peace the people of Chenyu Vale forgot the old god, and the ancient jade rites were forgotten too, until Fujin and the Traveler revived the Rainjade Rite. The song of lament still sung in the vale is the only evidence of what followed. It asks who will leave herbs on the sandbank, where the godly banners are now and how terrible the Xuanwen's wail is, and it is paired with the tale that she lies sealed deep beneath Mt. Lingmeng, wearing a skirt of coagulated jade-blood, leaning on a long-destroyed lunar chariot beside a black, bottomless pool. A mariner in another legend glimpsed glowing mountain spirits in a cavern, throwing jade into such a pool while singing of lonely malice and of a lone lord lamenting lost years.

## relationships
### STANDARD
Her adepti servants Fujin, the Herblord (now Changsheng) and Lingyuan were close friends to one another and loyal to her until the flood. Her chief enemy was Morax. Fujin speaks of her with sorrow rather than hatred.
### LARGE +
Lingyuan, who never became close to humans, also calls her 'our lord'. The Herblord lost her memories of this past and is a different person now as Changsheng.

## abilities
### STANDARD
Not directly shown. Legend says she governed wild beasts and adepti, controlled the Bishui River's flow, and was bound to jade. She could also grant dreams, as Fujin recalls.
### LARGE +
Her final stroke was the ability to flood the Bishui on a scale that would have submerged the vale. Canon gives no further feats and does not call her an Archon.

## status
### LITE
Sealed, according to local legend, beneath Mt. Lingmeng since the Archon War. Her fate after the war is not confirmed in-game. Not mentioned in 6.x Nod-Krai or 7.0-7.1 Snezhnaya content.
### STANDARD +
Her people forgot her. The traces left are the lost Rainjade Rite, the ancient ruins at Chizhang Wall, the ballad, and the old shelters and murals. The miasma Lingyuan later stirred up by disrupting the spirit veins, in an effort to restore Fujin, belongs to Lingyuan, not to the mistress.

## entry: The Flood of the Bishui
@keys: Bishui River flood, Rainjade Rite, Votive Rainjade
@keys2: mistress, Fujin, Chenyu Vale
@version: 4.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Mistress of Chenyu Vale raised the Bishui River in a last attempt to defeat Morax, though the water would also have destroyed Chenyu Vale. Fujin, who organized the Rainjade Rite for her lord, threw the Votive Rainjade into the flood from Mt. Lingmeng; the Herblord fought the goddess while Lingyuan led people to shelter. The vale survived. Fujin's own account, in An Ancient Sacrifice of Sacred Brocade, is the main source.
### LARGE +
Little Mao's tale in Floating Jade, Treasure of Chenyu adds that the river ran red in the fighting, and that the vale's ancestors threw magic jade into the water to ensure good weather and fertile soil. This is a folk account, not an eyewitness one.

## entry: The Song of Lament
@keys: song of lamentation, Mt. Lingmeng Gossip, skirt of coagulated jade-blood
@keys2: Chenyu Vale, mistress, demon lord
### STANDARD
Mt. Lingmeng Gossip, Volume 3, says Chenyu Vale songs tell of a demon lord of the distant past who hid in an ancient cave, wearing a skirt of coagulated jade-blood and leaning on a long-destroyed lunar chariot beneath a black pool. The same book names her as the mistress of Chenyu Vale and notes that both indigenous and migrant folk say she began a war from incurable obsession, was defeated, sealed and plunged into dreaming slumber, though the historical details can no longer be verified. A three-line lament is the only supporting evidence.
