## summary
### LITE
Hermanubis, the Greatest of Sages, was a Tighnarian priest-warrior raised to be one of King Deshret's Seven Pillars. He founded the Temple of Silence and, to save his followers, broke himself into fragments of power.
### STANDARD +
His power outlived him. Two Ba Fragments live on in Cyno, General Mahamatra of the Akademiya. He is revered by the Temple of Silence as a founder of desert civilization and remembered by the other Pillars with respect.

## background
### LITE
King Deshret chose him from the Tighnarians as sage and divine familiar, the only Pillar born human. After the king's death he led his followers to Tulaytullah and founded the Temple of Silence.
### STANDARD +
At Tulaytullah the Temple became keeper of the knowledge that survived the fall, while Hermanubis used his power to hold back the remnants of forbidden knowledge. He took in many people who had nowhere else to go. When the desert's city-states and Eremite tribes went to war, a king of Gurabad besieged the city and demanded the Temple as proof of his victory; Tulaytullah's ruling elite sided with him and blamed the Temple's knowledge for the world's corruption. By then Hermanubis had spent much of his strength, so he shattered himself into fragments of spirit and power, the Ba Fragments, and gave them to his followers, who drove the invaders off.
### LARGE +
The victory did not make the Temple's people heroes. Their strange power frightened others and they were shunned, so the Temple and the Tighnarians who followed it left the city, which fell into ruin. Later the Temple spent time in the rainforest alongside the Akademiya before mistrust drove it back to the desert, where it now lies in a large underground temple near Thamud Oasis (the wiki dates the move to about 400 years ago).
### ULTRA +
Of the old fighting companions, Sethos reports that Hermanubis arrived in Tulaytullah with two companions and sparred with the city's residents for days, winning the support of many; those supporters were among the Temple's first members, and the matches became its founding Rite of Duels. Bamoun's dying words describe him as once a warrior of Tighnar who came from the barren sands to serve the God King. The Temple dates its founding to more than a thousand years ago.

## status
### LITE
Deceased: he shattered himself long ago. His power survives as Ba Fragments.
### STANDARD +
Two fragments were held by the Temple of Silence under its leader Bamoun. They were implanted in two children, Sethos and Cyno, and at the start of the Traveler's story they were split between them. In the Cyno story quest Oathkeeper (4.6), Cyno won both in the Rite of Duels; the Temple's custom is to follow whoever holds the most fragments, but Cyno handed leadership to Sethos.
### LARGE +
Cyno calls himself a fellow vessel of Hermanubis. In the limited 6.1 event Headlong Into the Sands he says Hermanubis can possess him but that he cannot talk to him whenever he likes and could sense nothing when Sethos asked him to try; Thoth, hearing this, wondered aloud whether Hermanubis was gone and said he would have liked to speak with him. In a 6.6 quest Heryshaf refers to Cyno as Hermanubis's representative while the Seven Pillars agree to open Aaru.
### ULTRA +
He has no known active presence at the 7.1 cutoff. Nothing in Nod-Krai or Snezhnaya content changes his status.

## relationships
### STANDARD
King Deshret chose him and he served the king as Pillar and sage. Among the other Pillars, Heryshaf, Thoth and Sobek were the most senior; Bennu and Shesepankh are the king's soul and will, and fondly think of him. Cyno and Sethos are the living vessels of his power; the Temple of Silence is his institution and its members praise his name.
### LARGE +
Official notes say all the other Pillars, whether or not they admit it, held a deep respect for him, and that mention of his name recalls days when they gathered in fellowship. Bennu has frankly said that Hermanubis is the Pillar he admires most. Shesepankh thinks fondly of his old colleague. Thoth credits him with giving shelter at Tulaytullah, whose refugees' descendants are now scattered and more numerous than Thoth had imagined.
### ULTRA +
Tighnari, who descends from the Valuka Shuna, was greeted by Bamoun as kin to the Tighnarians who stood by Hermanubis from the Temple's founding to Tulaytullah's fall. Apep was also counted among the Pillars by the king but never accepted the title nor met the others.

## appearance
### STANDARD
Depicted as a humanoid figure with a jackal's head, usually in purple garments. Statues and murals show him among the Seven Pillars.

## abilities
### LITE
A renowned sage, priest and warrior. His power held forbidden knowledge at bay and, as Ba Fragments, lets a vessel host his strength.
### STANDARD +
A fragment works as an indwelling presence: Cyno's lightning-wreathed state and Sethos's Ba Fragment are both tied to it. Vessels must be suitable; the fragments rejected adult bodies, which is why children were used. Bamoun links the light of the fragment to Hermanubis's will.
### LARGE +
Each fragment is said to stand for something different, such as might, glory or secrets not yet revealed. How much the sage himself could do before he shattered is not detailed.

## knowledge
### LITE
He knew King Deshret's world first-hand and nothing after Tulaytullah.
### STANDARD +
He would know the forbidden knowledge that destroyed the old civilization and what the Temple was made to guard. He does not know the Akademiya, the Traveler or the fate of the Temple.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- Hermanubis is not Cyno, nor does he speak through Cyno on demand.
- He is not the god of the dead from Egyptian myth; in canon he is a Tighnarian sage.
- He did not serve Apep or Nabu Malikata; his lord was King Deshret.
### LARGE +
- The king who besieged Tulaytullah was a later king of Gurabad, not Ormazd or Parvezravan.
- Do not invent dialogue or a personality beyond 'sincere, steadfast, kind and steady' from the official notes.
- Not an Archon or angel; the Pillars are King Deshret's sages and constructs.

## chronology
### LARGE
- Before Deshret's death: chosen from the Tighnarians as sage and familiar; serves as one of the Seven Pillars.
- After Deshret's death: leads followers to Tulaytullah, founds the Temple of Silence.
- Later: Gurabad's king besieges Tulaytullah; Hermanubis shatters into Ba Fragments.
- Over a thousand years later: Bamoun implants fragments in Sethos and Cyno; Cyrus takes Cyno to the Akademiya.
- 4.6: Cyno defeats Sethos in the Rite of Duels and takes both fragments.
### ULTRA +
- 3.0: first named in the Lay of Al-Ahmar, where the text says even the greatest of sages, Hermanubis, could never have foreseen the later ruin of the desert tribes.
- 3.5: Windblume event dialogue notes that the power of Hermanubis once brought Cyno great suffering.
- 6.1: limited event Headlong Into the Sands, where Thoth and Sethos speak of him.
- 6.6: Heryshaf calls Cyno Hermanubis's representative.

## trivia
### ULTRA
- His name comes from the Greco-Egyptian god Hermanubis, a blend of Hermes and Anubis who is jackal-headed; he is the only Pillar with a Greek hybrid name.
- Sethos's naming ceremony says the wisdom of Hermanubis will bestow its favour on the child.
- In her 6.x voice-over about Cyno, Nefer jokes that Hermanubis is a good judge of character for recruiting so diligent a servant, and wonders how Cyno unwinds with a tireless 'boss'.
- Some Akademiya people believed Cyno had been raised by a Hermanubis priest in a desert temple; Cyno's Vision story and the Windblume dialogue show this is false.

## entry: Ba Fragments
@keys: Ba Fragment, Ba Fragments, fragments of Hermanubis
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
When Hermanubis shattered himself, the pieces of his spirit and power were called Ba Fragments and passed to his followers. By the present only two were left, held by the Temple of Silence under Bamoun. Adult bodies rejected them, so he offered his adoptive grandson Sethos and found a second suitable child, Cyno. After the two children took the fragments, Cyrus ended the experiment and left with Cyno. In the Rite of Duels Cyno won Sethos's fragment, so both are with him.
### LARGE +
Sethos says each fragment stands for something different. The Temple's custom is to follow whoever has the most fragments, which is why Cyno was named leader before choosing Sethos. Cyno suspects Bamoun designed the duel so the power of Hermanubis would end up concentrated in a single person whichever of them won.

## entry: Siege of Tulaytullah
@keys: Tulaytullah, City of Sapphire, siege of Tulaytullah
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
Tulaytullah, a city of canals, sapphire domes and gardens, grew famous as a city of wisdom under Hermanubis's Temple of Silence. A king of Gurabad laid siege to it and demanded the Temple as proof of his victory. The ruling elite colluded with him by painting the Temple's knowledge as the root of the world's corruption. Hermanubis, his strength largely spent, divided himself among his followers, who beat the army back; the people then turned on the Temple, the Tighnarians left and the city declined.

## entry: Rite of Duels
@keys: Rite of Duels, duel with Sethos
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
According to Sethos, when Hermanubis first came to Tulaytullah he and two companions sparred with the city's residents for several days and won supporters, who became the Temple's first members. The sparring was kept as a founding rite. In Oathkeeper Cyno and Sethos used it to decide who would hold the fragments: three fighters per side, both fragments at stake, with the challengers needing to win every bout. Cyno's side won.
### LARGE +
Duaenre presided, calling Hermanubis the witness whose power and authority were present. Bamoun died moments after the result.

## entry: Seven Pillars
@keys: Seven Pillars, Pillars of King Deshret
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
King Deshret's Seven Pillars were Heryshaf the Goat King, Thoth the Ibis King, Sobek the Crocodile King, Hermanubis, Bennu, Shesepankh and the Dendro Dragon Apep, who never accepted the title. Hermanubis was the only one of them born human. Thoth, Sobek and Heryshaf are held in Aaru in the present day, while Hermanubis, after the king's death, went to Tulaytullah.
