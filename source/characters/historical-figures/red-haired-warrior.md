## summary
### LITE
A nameless red-haired warrior who wandered into Decarabian's walled Old Mondstadt, became one of the first to plot against the God of Storms, and stood with the Nameless Bard, the wind spirit, Amos and Gunnhildr when the tower fell.
### STANDARD +
He is remembered in song but not by name, and the later Ragnvindr Family is generally taken to descend from him. After the victory he turned away from the new god.
### ULTRA +
Texts place him among the very first conspirators and credit him with spreading the secret sign of the Windblume flower. The 1.0 Carmen Dei cinematic gives him a small, emotional moment as he holds the dying bard. His existence as a distinct fifth rebel only became clear when later items corrected the early assumption that the knight and he were the same.

## background
### LITE
A wanderer who came to live in Old Mondstadt under Decarabian, he disliked the god's rule and began to plan resistance. When the great uprising came, he went up the tower with the bard, the wind spirit, Amos and Gunnhildr to confront Decarabian.
### STANDARD +
Around 2,600 years ago, with the aid of Gunnhildr and her clan, a full rebellion rose. Decarabian died in the fighting, but the bard and Amos were mortally wounded. In the cinematic, the warrior props the bard up so that he can watch the old god fall. The wind spirit then became Barbatos in the bard's likeness (Boreas, Decarabian's rival, chose to pass over the position).
### LARGE +
Windblume Ode says he was first among those who passed the Windblume sign, a flower given with a promise of hope and stood for courage; he wove the "threads of dawn" through the long night. On the ruined tower's summit, amid the crowd's songs, he turned his back on the newborn god, "hidden like a single raindrop in a tidal wave of humanity". His name, the text adds, has since been lost. The wiki describes his appearance as a long head of red hair, a small red goatee, a leather coat with a fur collar and a red gem at the ascot; this is drawn from the cinematic.
### ULTRA +
Dawn's Brilliant Oath (6.3) tells of a warrior clad in iron who had never seen the open sky and was ordered to crush the rebels in the alleys. At the young bard's music, he laid down his sword, moved, as others were, by a desire for the sky, not by promises. The wiki identifies this warrior as him. The same text goes on to speak of the huntress Amos casting off duty and name to gather the winds for the bard. The wiki additionally says that after the victory he built an underground intelligence network for Mondstadt, but the texts it cites do not state this clearly, so it is not repeated here as fact. The wiki also notes the Ragnvindr connection: a thousand years later, the Windblume Ode text says, "another red-haired knight" would follow his path.

## status
### LITE
Long dead or lost to history; canon gives no date, place or manner of his end, and no one living remembers him by name.
### STANDARD +
His memory survives in the Windblume Festival, whose flower was once his side's secret sign, and in the red-haired line of the Ragnvindr Family.
### LARGE +
No 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content adds to his story beyond the 6.3 artifact text.

## relationships
### STANDARD
- The Nameless Bard: the friend whose song moved him and whose death he witnessed.
- The wind spirit who became Barbatos: ally in the uprising, whom he later left.
- Amos and Gunnhildr: the other two rebels of the group of five.
- Decarabian: the tyrant they overthrew.
### LARGE +
- The Ragnvindr Family: his presumed descendants. Ragnvindr, a thousand years later, is also described as red-haired and as a rebel against tyranny.
- The Gunnhildr Clan: their chief's daughter rallied her people behind the revolt.

## chronology
### LARGE
- About 2,600 years ago: lives as a wanderer in Old Mondstadt; helps plan and spread resistance.
- The rebellion: storms the tower with the bard, the wind spirit, Amos and Gunnhildr.
- Decarabian dies; the bard falls; the wind spirit ascends as Barbatos. He turns away.
### ULTRA +
- 1.0: first seen in the cinematic of Should You Be Trapped in a Windless Land.
- 1.4 (limited): named as "red-haired warrior" in Windblume Ode.
- 1.6: Freedom-Sworn separates him from the knight in Amos' Bow, making the group five.
- 6.3: Dawn's Brilliant Oath adds the account of the warrior who laid down his sword.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- He has no name; do not use one.
- He is not Ragnvindr, who lived 1,600 years later, although both are red-haired and the clan is thought descended from him.
- He is not the knight of Amos' Bow (probably Gunnhildr).
### LARGE +
- He did not betray Barbatos; the texts say only that he turned away.
- Do not describe an organised spy network as established fact.

## entry: Windblume sign

@keys2: Decarabian, rebellion, Red-Haired Warrior
@version: 1.4
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
In Windblume Ode, a text from the limited Invitation of Windblume event, the Windblume was a lonely flower that grew strong in storms, and rebels gave it to one another as a secret sign of courage. The red-haired warrior was first to pass it. In peace it came to mean love and gladness.

## entry: Rebellion against Decarabian
@keys: Decarabian's tower, Boy and the Whirlwind, rebellion against Decarabian
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
About 2,600 years ago the rebels, five by Freedom-Sworn's count (the youth, the spirit, the archer, the knight and the flame-haired warrior), climbed Decarabian's tower while the weak below sang a drinking song of defiance. Decarabian died, as did the Nameless Bard and Amos; the wind spirit became Barbatos.

## entry: Ragnvindr Family link
@keys: Ragnvindr Family, red hair, red-haired knight
@keys2: Ragnvindr, Dawn Winery
@version: 1.4
### STANDARD
Windblume Ode ties the red-haired warrior to Ragnvindr, who in the Aristocratic age rose against another tyranny, and a character teaser draws a similar figure among the family. Crepus and Diluc are descendants of Ragnvindr, and the wiki notes the red-haired resemblance, but canon never states the kinship.
