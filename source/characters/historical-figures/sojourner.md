## summary
### LITE
Community name for the Fontaine-born lyrist of the Wanderer's Troupe, told in the Resolution of Sojourner artifacts. He left privilege to wander, fell in love in Mondstadt, and died covering his comrades' retreat when the troupe's uprising failed, about a thousand years ago.
### STANDARD +
Canon never gives him a personal name and refers to him as the traveler, the lyrist or the harpist. His instrument, The Stringless, was both a harp and a bow.

## background
### LITE
He left a privileged life in Fontaine, adored by the Court's ladies, to wander unburdened and seek his destiny.
### STANDARD +
On the road he met the Conductor; the girl who used a flute as a sword and then Kreuzlied, a defector from the Lawrence Clan, joined them, forming the Wanderer's Troupe. They travelled Teyvat, even to dangerous places such as Mare Jivari, before reaching Mondstadt. The artifact text describes these as probably the happiest days of his wandering life.
### LARGE +
In Mondstadt he met a maiden at a clear lake. She was already chosen as the Ludi Harpastum maiden, the girl the Lawrence Clan claimed against her will, and she asked him not to flatter her with empty praise or song. He promised to break her chains and asked only to see her smile. The Heart of Comradeship text says he pinned a flower to his chest to remind himself to love no one, yet resolved to remember her and walk into the fire for her.

## chronology
### LARGE
Ordered from item texts: he leaves Fontaine unannounced; meets the Conductor, the flute-sword girl and Kreuzlied; the troupe travels Teyvat including Mare Jivari; arrives in Mondstadt and meets the maiden by a lake; learns of the aristocracy's tyranny and joins the attempt to storm the city; is repelled and dies covering the retreat. No years are given beyond roughly a thousand years ago.
### ULTRA +
The Widsith lore is the source for the assault on the City of Mondstadt. The Sundial text frames the end through time: the nobles' halls and the homeless troupe are both ground down by it. Items mention the Wanderer's Troupe weapons (The Flute, The Widsith) as sharing the same story.

## personality
### LITE
Playful, determined and drawn to freedom; a singer whose lyre-song revealed his real feelings even when he did not understand why. He distrusted his own pretty words.
### STANDARD +
Texts describe him as both playful and resolute, like the wind that carries a harp's tune. He wished to love no one and be attached to nothing, but loyalty to his comrades and to the maiden overrode that wish.

## relationships
### LITE
The Wanderer's Troupe; an unnamed Mondstadt maiden he loved; the Lawrence Clan, his enemy.
### STANDARD +
The Goblet text shows the troupe as the first company he felt he need never leave. The maiden's smile became the stated aim of his rebellion.

## abilities
### LITE
A lyrist and archer who played The Stringless, a harp-bow.

### STANDARD +
The troupe was said to bring birds down, sometimes with the tune and sometimes with arrows that followed it. In the last stand he plucked strings and rained arrows until he ran out of both music and arrows.

## status
### LITE
Dead for about a thousand years. After the uprising he stayed behind, repelling pursuit so that his comrades could flee, and fell in the fighting. He does not appear in 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content as far as the sources read show.
### STANDARD +
Most troupe members were slain or captured and enslaved, and the aristocracy later cut the instrument's strings to silence it. Legend says he had no regret about dying nameless in a foreign land, only that he found true love and never got to sing another love song. Mondstadt's sky, the texts note, was the same as the sky of his homeland.

## appearance
### STANDARD
No canonical description of his face or build exists in the sources read. Items mention a flower worn on his chest, a crown of willow branches kept as a memento, and a lyre-bow with strings of different materials.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
Do not treat "Sojourner" as his in-world name, do not name the maiden, and do not merge him with the Wanderer (Scaramouche) or the Traveler. He did not found the troupe alone; the Conductor and others were present.
