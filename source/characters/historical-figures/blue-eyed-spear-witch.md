## summary
### LITE
The blue-eyed spear witch was a sapphire-eyed sorceress of Mondstadt's late Aristocratic period, about a thousand years ago. She hunted the cruel with a spear of her own design and loved the gentleman thief Parsifal. A sapphire he gave her got her branded as a thief, and she was later killed by her own disciple Eberhart.
### STANDARD +
Her name is not revealed in-game (the wiki suggests Josephine). She is known only through weapon and book texts, never in a quest.

## background
### LITE
Her origin is unknown. She already hunted aristocrats and wrongdoers with a spear and magic when she met Parsifal, a noble-born thief. His sapphire gift, still bearing a noble crest, exposed her as the thief and earned her a criminal's brand.
### STANDARD +
Parsifal pried the sapphire from a stolen silver cup to match her eyes; she blamed him for the brand yet stayed bound to him in love. She designed the Crescent Pike, a needle-like spear with a crescent blade. Item text says she could see the fatal flaw in every living thing, none in herself, until music and then love gave her one. At some point she took Eberhart as a disciple and taught him her spear art.
### LARGE +
One version of the story (the book Heart's Desire: Sapphire) says that years later she traded the sapphire for the silver cup that had once held it, and that she had been a servant in a noble house who was punished by her master; other texts do not support the servant account. Popular songs such as Wine and Song gave the pair a happy ending, which the narration admits is not how things went.

### ULTRA +
Weapon texts give three views of her: Alley Hunter shows Parsifal never managed to make her smile despite his gifts; Wine and Song shows the cheerful public legend; Crescent Pike shows her inner regret. The Favonius Lance text adds that Eberhart's spear skill may have been learned from her for the purpose of killing her, and that nobles of the era were expected to learn the sword while the spear was left to foreign gladiators and traitors.

## status
### LITE
Dead. Weapon text says she died at the hands of Eberhart, her disciple, while she was distracted by longing for Parsifal. Her legacy lives on in tales, in the Crescent Pike, and in a play by the Fontainian playwright Coppelius that later inspired aliases of Parsifal the Great and her sister Josephine five hundred years afterwards.

## relationships
### LITE
Parsifal is her great love and the cause of her scar; she blamed him but never stopped caring. Eberhart was her pupil and, according to item texts, her killer.
### STANDARD +
Her dying wish in Crescent Pike's text is to see Parsifal and hear his song once more.

## entry: Crescent Pike (the witch's spear)
@keys: Crescent Pike
@keys2: witch, Parsifal, Eberhart, Aristocratic period
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Crescent Pike is a long, needle-thin spear with a crescent blade, designed by the blue-eyed spear witch to slip through the gaps in an opponent's guard. Item text says it could seem to fly from her hand toward a fatal flaw. Her own flaw, she realised at the end, was caring for someone.

## entry: Sapphire gem and branding
@keys: sapphire gem, sapphire, criminal's brand
@keys2: Parsifal, witch
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Parsifal stole a silver cup and pried its sapphire out as a gift, since it matched the witch's eyes. The crest of the noble house was inlaid in the gem, so she was identified as the thief, captured and branded on the face. Heart's Desire: Sapphire says she later traded the gem back for the cup.
