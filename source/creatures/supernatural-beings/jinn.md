## summary
### LITE
The Jinn are elemental spirits of Sumeru's deserts. They can take human or other shapes and wield great power, and they were created by the goddess Nabu Malikata. After her death they served King Deshret, typically housed in silver magic bottles, weapons or lamps. Liloupar is the best known; the Traveler meets her in Sumeru, and in 6.6 she fades away.
### STANDARD +
They are remembered both as proud, dangerous spirits and as exiles who remain loyal to a lost mistress. Most individual Jinn are unnamed in canon, and several famous ones appear only in tales that may be fictional.

## nature
### LITE
Jinn are elemental spirits who can assume humanoid and non-humanoid forms and hold considerable power. Legendary Jinn are drawn with human features, and the Jinni in a Sumeru folk tale is a translucent, human-like dancer with hair, eyes and clothing. They normally dwell in objects such as magic bottles or weapons rather than walking the world freely.
### STANDARD +
They are said to be able to possess a human body, which the tale's Jinni uses as a threat. Plumpy, the Jinni living in Dori's lamp, shows that the form varies: it is a round, lilac, grinning creature with small floating arms. Jinn are described as proud and give their allegiance only to those they judge worthy, as Liloupar shows when she chooses the Traveler over the Eremites who tried to command her.
### LARGE +
A Jinni's soul can be divided. King Deshret split Liloupar into seven fragments as punishment and had his sages hide them across the Great Red Sand; the most dominant one was kept in Liloupar's Cell. Her fragments could be gathered again in bottles, and in the present day she appears as two orbs inside a floating bottle. The elite Eremites who summon "spirits of omen" from their weapons have appearances matching the Jinn seen in the Archon Quest As All Falls to Emptiness; the wiki suggests a link, but canon does not state it.

## origin
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
According to the Flower of Paradise Lost artifact text, the Jinn were born after Nabu Malikata was cast aside by the heavens. She wandered the barren wastes for seventy-two nights; the blood from her torn heels became springs and streams that fed gardens, and the Jinn were born from the water lilies there. They begged her to stay and she agreed. With King Deshret she built Ay-Khanoum, whose name means City of the Moon Maiden in the Jinn's ancient tongue.
### LARGE +
In stories that leave out the goddess, King Deshret instead learned the Jinn's story after capturing them and built Ay-Khanoum as a gift. In return they built the entrance to his Mausoleum. These are rival accounts; see the uncertainties.

## history
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
After Nabu Malikata died, the Jinn turned to serve King Deshret. The folk tale says they willingly entered silver bottles he made for them and were marked by his seal, then created and explored on his behalf. Liloupar says he deceived them: he claimed the goddess was not dead but asleep in the Orchard of Pairidaeza and could be woken with their help.
### LARGE +
Liloupar, who was born of the same lilies, loved the shepherd king Ormazd and, after he turned tyrant, brought ruin on Gurabad over three generations by twisting the fates of her children and grandson. That story is told in Liloupar's own entry and in The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin. In the present day the Traveler finds her bottle during the Sumeru desert quest The Dirge of Bilqis, and the fragments' story ends with Memories of Gurabad.

## recent_events
@version: 6.6
### ULTRA
During A Divergence Leading to Oneself, the Traveler seeks Liloupar to calm the Jinn and sandstorms that the Fatui have disturbed, and she quells them before travelling with the group. In Your Past Is Like a Garden she asks to absorb the remains of Nabu Malikata embedded in Collei so that her soul can finally rest, and fades away once Collei agrees. Whether other Jinn were affected by her passing is not stated. Separately, in Nod-Krai (Special Operation, 6.1) the kuuvahki energy made Dori's Jinni lose consciousness.

## society
### STANDARD
The Jinn had no known government. Most lived in Ay-Khanoum, and later served King Deshret in his bottles. Descent matters: Liloupar is called the Mother of the Jinn and an ancestor of the Jinn (the Jinni in The Shepherd and the Magic Bottle calls herself a descendant of Liloufar), and half-human children such as Shirin and Parvezravan came from a Jinn mother. Makhaira, the figure of the Makhaira Aquamarine weapon story, is described as the daughter of a Jinni.
### LARGE +
Jinn offspring with humans are possible in canon. The wiki notes that the female Jinn of legend echo the shape-shifting Sila of Arabic folklore, but that is the writers' inspiration, not an in-world fact.

## abilities
### STANDARD
Jinn have shapeshifting and great spiritual power. Bottles can carry on their benefits after the occupant leaves: the bottle Liloupar leaves with the Traveler still gives the same benefits it did while she was inside. Dori ensures that her lamp Jinni's wishes are granted after it is used in battle. Jinn can also be harmed by their environment: in Nod-Krai the turbulent kuuvahki energy made Dori's Jinni lose consciousness, and Nefer saved Dori.
### LARGE +
Liloupar used the power of the Abyss in the destruction of Gurabad, which shows Jinn can be tied to dangerous powers, not merely elemental ones. How the Jinn relate to specific elements is not stated; they are called elemental spirits generally.

## relations
### STANDARD
Jinn owe their existence to Nabu Malikata and, in later ages, were bound to King Deshret. Their relation to humans is ambiguous: some are servants, some lovers or parents, some avengers. The Eremites tried to use Liloupar for their own ends and she held them in low regard. Dori keeps a Jinni in her lamp after tricking it into entering.

## individuals
### STANDARD
Known Jinn: Liloupar, the Mother of the Jinn; Ferigees, whom Liloupar calls sister (whether they are kin is unclear) and who indwelt a guardian machine at the Eternal Oasis until the Traveler destroyed it; Plumpy, the lilac Jinni in Dori's lamp; the mother of Makhaira; the Jinni in the Xiphos' Moonlight weapon story; and the Jinn of folk tales in The Shepherd and the Magic Bottle and The Tale of Shiruyeh and Shirin, who may be fictitious. Half-human descendants include Shirin, Parvezravan and Shiruyeh.

## entry: Liloupar
@keys: Liloupar, Liloufar, Mother of the Jinn
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Liloupar was a Jinni who served Nabu Malikata and then King Deshret. She loved Ormazd, then took revenge on his betrayal by ruining Gurabad over three generations. King Deshret split her soul into fragments. The Traveler gathers them in the Sumeru desert and she takes the Traveler as her master. Her full biography is in her own entry.

## entry: Silver magic bottles
@keys: magic bottle, silver bottle, Jinni bottle
@keys2: Jinn, Jinni, Deshret
### STANDARD
King Deshret made silver bottles for the Jinn, who entered them and were marked with his seal. In the folk tale the bottle's Jinni calls itself an eternal prisoner content with the illusion of comfort. Liloupar's soul fragments were also kept in bottles, and the bottle she leaves with the Traveler still gives the same benefits it did while she was inside.

## entry: Ay-Khanoum
@keys: Ay-Khanoum
@keys2: Jinn, Jinni, Nabu Malikata
### STANDARD
Ay-Khanoum, the City of the Moon Maiden in the Jinn's ancient language, was built by Nabu Malikata and King Deshret in one account and by Deshret alone, as a gift to captured Jinn, in another. The Jinn lived there and in return built the entrance to his Mausoleum.

## entry: Dori's Jinni
@keys: Plumpy, Dori's lamp
@keys2: Dori, Jinni
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Plumpy is the Jinni who lives in Dori's lamp, a round lilac creature with small floating arms. Dori tricked it into entering the lamp; it stays there and she makes sure its wishes are granted whenever it is used in battle. It appears in the Luna VII quest A Divergence Leading to Oneself. Canon gives it almost no further profile.
