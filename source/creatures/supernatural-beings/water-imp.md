## summary
### LITE
Water Imps are a less common kind of Snowland Fae from Snezhnaya, also called Rusalka or Sirens. They have fin-shaped ears, a fish tail in water that becomes two legs on land, very sharp hearing and a gift for song. Most left Snezhnograd long ago; Vodyanitsa, the prima donna of the Korolevskiy Troupe, stayed.
### STANDARD
Water Imps are one of the less common kinds of Snowland Fae, with the Augury Birds and Lantern Fae. Their old role was singing, carrying messages and healing along Snezhnaya's rivers, bound by a covenant that no Water Imp should again be made to fight. Over time almost all of them left Snezhnograd for a faraway unfrozen lake. The playable Vodyanitsa, Prima Donna of the Korolevskiy Troupe, is the kind's best-known member and the last one still singing in the city.

## nature
### STANDARD
Like the other Snowland Fae, Water Imps came from Hyperborea's creation of the fae and are native to Snezhnaya. They normally have a mermaid-like tail, which at will they change into working human legs to walk on land. Vodyanitsa has fin-shaped ears, pale crystalline scales and, in water, a dark-teal tail, with ribbon-like fins appearing on her back.

They live in or near water and look after their tails. Vodyanitsa's guidance note tells readers to soak the tail in fresh, clean water, with no milk, petals or fruit, and says the best water is from Fontaine. She dislikes dry land: she says her skin dries out after too long ashore and that "for us Water Imps, leaving the water behind is a big enough price to pay already".

Water Imps hear extremely well. Vodyanitsa says she can make out whispers from afar, boasts that she hears "clear as a bell" whatever is whispered, and says that Water Imps can understand and even learn the songs of Frostfin Whales.
### LARGE +
Water Imps' songs are said to be sincere. In a quest Odette says the song of a Water Imp is "the most sincere sound in the world", with no lies or tricks, though sometimes all emotion and no technique. Vodyanitsa's profile also mentions a rumour of an otherworldly allure in her voice, but she insists it is only good singing.

In a character story the young Vodyanitsa sits unharmed among railway workers, where sparks do not even scorch her dress, and sings with them through a blizzard. Paimon wonders whether Water Imps feel the cold at all; canon does not answer.
### ULTRA +
Old Water Imp songs remain in the Korolevskiy Theater's repertoire only because Vodyanitsa keeps singing them. In "Upstream Variations" she says she sings for every Water Imp whose voice ever graced the theatre, and she talks to her departed kin Paraskeva and Xenia while rehearsing. These parts of the quest present them as having left the city, not necessarily as dead; Vodyanitsa's wording about "ghosts" is a joke and a lament, and the text leaves it so.

## history
### STANDARD
Vodyanitsa's character stories give the Water Imps' own account. The Water Imps' childhood is placed in Dalegrad, and they once roamed freely, singing blessings to every living thing. Their lord Vodyanoy Dombertiev, "the Lackland", allied with King Miscislav, and the fast, fearless Water Imps became the strength of Miscislav's rise, until his daughter Vaselisa Vodyanovna could not bear the sight of her kin's cold blood frozen on the lake.
### LARGE +
Vaselisa Vodyanovna, called the "Zmey Maiden", left with those who would no longer bear arms and came to the court of Emperor Monomakh Snezhnyi, the Tsar of the Pale Star. She refused crown, treasure and lands. She asked only that the Water Imps stop sharpening their blades to songs of sorrow and sing for every soul with a true love. The Emperor agreed, and they bound themselves by oath: no Water Imp would be made to serve another's desire, and even a Water Imp who offered to go to war would not have to take up steel.
### ULTRA +
Afterwards, according to the story, the Water Imps walked among armies weaponless, as couriers of dispatches and of lovers' letters, as carriers of the wounded and as singers; the blood-pink flowers of ice never rose again on the banks of Dalegrad; and Snezhnograd rang with their songs. The covenant text also says the Water Imps never forgot the protection the Emperor granted them. Later, the city's people noticed the songs fading as the Water Imps left, until Vodyanitsa announced that her seven-day performance would go on and sang alone.

## society
### STANDARD
Water Imps are a small, close-knit people. Their work was song in many forms: stage performance, street melody, and ambushes that splash passers-by at the water's edge. Vodyanitsa's parents were celebrated singers who held intimate concerts at their estate where fae and townsfolk sang together, and they taught her to think only of the next song. Water Imps include aristocrats: in the 7.0 Archon Quest Vodyanitsa says her Water Imp parents belonged to the aristocracy that the Tsaritsa drove out of Snezhnaya long ago, and that they now live somewhere new that the postman cannot reach. Her profile separately remembers them as celebrated singers.
### LARGE +
When the city turned too restless, nearly all Water Imps left for a lake untouched by winter. Vodyanitsa's relatives invited her to join them, and still write by river fish to call her home. She stays with a rule: when she feels like leaving, she looks for ten Snezhnayans, and if at least one truly loves her singing she stays. Someone always has.
### ULTRA +
A flashback in Winter's Mysterious Silence shows the kind under pressure from a different side: a mob demanding that all Water Imps leave a theatre, performers and audience alike, during a violent period in the human-fae conflict. A human, Shram, lost a Water Imp lover, Litiya Ozerova, in it. Vodyanitsa says the aristocrats among the Water Imps were driven out of Snezhnaya by the Tsaritsa long ago. How these exiles relate to the lake migration is not explained.

## abilities
### STANDARD
Water Imps use elemental power without a Moon Wheel, like other fae. Vodyanitsa, however, carries a Hydro Vision, which she treats as jewellery. Song is central to their identity. In the covenant story, Water Imps lulled anxious soldiers to sleep with their songs and carried the wounded swiftly downriver on rafts. Vodyanitsa's profile hints at an allure in her voice; see the uncertainties.
### LARGE +
The kind swims quickly and can follow rivers across the world. Vodyanitsa has travelled to Liyue, Fontaine, Inazuma, Sumeru and Natlan, noting that legends of mermaids and sirens elsewhere probably come from people noticing Water Imps. In Fontaine a Special Security and Surveillance Patrol officer once thought she was drowning and tried to rescue her.

## relations
### STANDARD
Water Imps are on good terms with the Snezhnayans who love song. Fae history ties them to Dalegrad, the Volkodlak king Miscislav and the Tsar of the Pale Star. Among the rare kinds, Celaeno lists Water Imps alongside Augury Birds and Lantern Fae, but no personal connection between their known members is shown. Vodyanitsa herself is friends with Odette and Alyosha through the Rokot.
### LARGE +
Snezhnayan humans often misread them: the "siren" legend portrays them as dangerous, and Vodyanitsa deliberately debunks it. Her kin write that most of their kind are unlikely to thrive in a city where hearts burn hot and restless.

## individuals
### STANDARD
- Vodyanitsa: Prima Donna of the Korolevskiy Troupe, playable Hydro character from 7.1, the last Water Imp still on stage in Snezhnograd.
- Vodyanoy Dombertiev, "the Lackland": lord of the Water Imps of Dalegrad, who fought for Miscislav (ancient figure).
- Vaselisa Vodyanovna, the "Zmey Maiden": his daughter, who led weaponless Water Imps to the Tsar.
- Litiya Ozerova: a Water Imp performer loved by the human Shram, mentioned in Winter's Mysterious Silence.
- Paraskeva and Xenia: Water Imps whose music Vodyanitsa memorised and whom she names when rehearsing.
- Vodyanitsa's parents: celebrated singers, unnamed in the sources read.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
As this project's interpretation: portray Water Imps as theatrical, warm and a little teasing, with a deep love of ordinary listeners and a dislike of being treated as a status symbol. They are not sea-monsters or temptresses; the kind themselves refuse that label. Keep the tail, wet hair and water imagery, but do not make siren charm literal unless the scene asks for it.
