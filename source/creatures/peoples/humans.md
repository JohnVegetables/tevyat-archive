## summary
### LITE
Humans are the ordinary peoples of Teyvat: the ones who farm, trade, fight, worship and write. By the most complete account in the setting, the Heavenly Principles made them after conquering the dragons, and set them under a covenant with the heavens. Their fates are written in the stars, and their power over the elements comes only from a Vision, a gift tied to a strong desire. The Traveler is an outsider to this arrangement, and so are the Descenders.
### STANDARD +
Almost every named person in Teyvat is human, so this entry is not about any one nation. It records what canon says about where humans came from, how their fate is held, and who stands outside that rule. Most humans know little of it: they live by their nation's own legends, which differ from one another and from this picture.

## nature
### STANDARD
Humans have no single appearance or culture. Canon treats "human" as the default state of the Human Realm, the world the Heavenly Principles remade by diluting the Light Realm's power into seven elements. Ordinary humans cannot command the elements without a Vision or a Delusion. Those who do get one are called allogenes: people with a strong desire who might one day ascend to Celestia.
### LARGE +
Not everyone who looks human is one. Fontainians were Oceanids shaped into human form by Egeria. Melusines, adepti, Khaenri'ahns, angels, Descenders and synthetic people each have their own entries or exceptions, and a character should know which kind they are talking about.

## origin
@version: 2.4
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The Byakuyakoku Collection preserves a banned account in which the Primordial One (whom the wiki identifies with the Heavenly Principles) fought and defeated the seven dragon sovereigns, then made humans. With one of its Shades it created animals, plants and trees, then humans, "numerous as the stars". They made a covenant with it and entered a new age, the unified civilization.
### STANDARD +
Under the covenant the heavens answered hunger with food and rain, poverty with riches and sorrow with their voices. The one taboo was to succumb to temptation. Angels served the heavens as divine envoys to humans. The text dates the creation "four hundred years after the Held Branches", which the wiki reads as about 400 years after the Heavenly Principles arrived.
### LARGE +
Other evidence supports the idea that the Heavenly Principles holds authority over human creation. In 4.2 Neuvillette explains that the Hydro Archon, Egeria, never had permission from the Heavenly Principles to create a new human race, and that this was the "original sin" behind the Prophecy of Fontaine. In 5.2 (Moment of Awakening) a dragon says that "the Descender", whom the wiki links to the Heavenly Principles, tossed humans into Teyvat.
### ULTRA +
Artifact texts add a hidden rule: "cyclical eternity", under which human civilisations flourish and are destroyed and remade. Humans who asked the angels about it were met with silence. The War of Funerary Flame and the Celestial Nails that followed destroyed civilisations such as Sal Vindagnyr, Hyperborea and Lang-Gan, which fits this pattern but is never confirmed as its enforcement.

## fate_and_stars
@spoiler: major
### LITE
The stars hold human fate. A Vision bearer's fate is tied to a constellation, a pattern of real celestial bodies called Stella Fortuna. The Heavenly Principles are said to hold humans' strings like a puppeteer. The Traveler and other Descenders are not bound, and neither are dragons, who predate Celestia's rule.
### STANDARD +
Mona reads these patterns and says fate can only be accepted, not changed. Special constellations depicting the person exist for the Archons, adepti, the Traveler and a few others who stand apart or defy the world. Fate is anchored in Irminsul, which the Heavenly Principles remade for that purpose, according to Apep (6.6).
### LARGE +
In the limited-time event Unreconciled Stars (1.1, quest Where Ancient Stars Align), the constellation of Leonard, an adventurer from about two thousand years ago, fell as meteorites carrying elemental energy and, in Mona's words, his will. Citlali, a Natlan shaman, says the heavens move humans like a puppeteer moves a marionette, and she can bind her own Stella Fortuna into physical objects. A ruler in the Chamber of Deliberation records (Natlan) calls humans soulless creatures bound to star signs, a voice from a hostile court, not a verdict of canon.
### ULTRA +
Ordinary people's fates are also reflected in the stars but are not linked to named constellations. Remuria, Hyperborea and Sumeru each tried to build systems that stood outside fate, and in 6.6 Irminsul was burned and Aaru took over its record-keeping. As of 7.1 no source says Fate has ended.

## vision
### STANDARD
The Vision system is a relatively recent rule. Before the War of Funerary Flame, Celestia ruled humans through angels. After the war the angels fell and the link was cut, so the Heavenly Principles set up new arrangements: the Gnoses, the Seven Archons and Visions. A Vision is given to an allogene with a particularly powerful desire; the Archons do not choose the recipients. A bearer who dies leaves a masterless Vision, and one who loses their Vision loses the ambition with it.
### LARGE +
Visions are tied to fate: the constellation of a bearer marks their path. The gods are said to receive an "abundant gift" when an allogene fulfils their desire, a detail the Seven may not discuss. Raiden Ei's Vision Hunt Decree, which collected Visions in Inazuma, shows how closely they are linked to a human's identity.

## relations
### STANDARD
Humans stand under several kinds of beings. Gods and Archons lead nations. The Heavenly Principles rules from above through Celestia and the Shades. Dragons once ruled the land and in Natlan fought humans; in Enkanomiya the Dragonheirs used to "graze upon humanity". Angels guided humans in the unified civilization before they fell. Descenders stand outside Celestia's rules and can alter fate.
### LARGE +
The Heavenly Principles is said to have a "deep love" for humanity, which Rhinedottir states in 7.1, and also to destroy civilisations that test its rules. Humans can take parts of the world into their own hands: Khaenri'ahns tried to surpass nature with the Art of Khemia and were destroyed in the cataclysm, and the alchemist Rhinedottir succeeded in producing a synthetic human, Albedo.
### ULTRA +
Hilichurls are the cursed remnant of humans: many are Khaenri'ahns of mixed or foreign ancestry struck with the curse of the wilderness. They are covered in their own entry.

## khaenriahns
@version: 7.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Khaenri'ah's people are a special case. Dainsleif calls it a nation built purely by humans, without a god. In 7.1 Rhinedottir says all Khaenri'ahns descend from a bloodline that began with Anastasya, now the Tsaritsa, and the Third Descender, Saarelainen. He bargained with the Heavenly Principles to save her and protect his descendants. The wiki calls pure-blood Khaenri'ahns a separate race, but a quest does not use that wording.

## status
@version: 7.1
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 humans still live in seven nations (Mondstadt, Liyue, Inazuma, Sumeru, Fontaine, Natlan and Snezhnaya) and beyond, including Nod-Krai. The Heavenly Principles is dormant, Celestia still acts through the Shades, Visions still work and no one has proved Fate has ended. Irminsul burned in 6.6.

## roleplay
### STANDARD
Roleplay guidance (this project's recommendation, not canon text): ordinary humans do not know they were made by the Heavenly Principles or that stars hold their fate. They know their nation's legends. Do not let a farmer discuss Heimarmene or the Shades. Characters who can speak about this are few: Mona and Hexenzirkel witches, Nahida, Nicole, Fatui Harbingers, the Abyss Order and Dainsleif.
### LARGE +
Treat "human" as a broad category, not a monolith. Nations, Visions, Delusions and curses all give humans very different relations to gods and fate. When a scene touches the Heavenly Principles, remember that characters speak from their own legend or their own grievance.

## mistakes
### STANDARD
- Do not say all humans have Visions. A Vision bearer's fate is tied to a named constellation; other people's fates are reflected in the stars but are not linked to named constellations.
- Do not say the Traveler or Descenders are bound by fate.
- Do not claim Fate is destroyed or that the Heavenly Principles is dead; it is dormant and Fate's status is unresolved.
- Do not treat the Primordial Human as an ordinary human. It is a title, covered in its own entry.
- The "soulless creatures" line is a hostile ruler's opinion, not a fact.

## entry: Creation of humanity
@keys: creation of humans, creation of humanity, Primordial One, Phanes, covenant of the heavens
@keys2: humans, humanity, mankind
@version: 2.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Byakuyakoku Collection, a history banned in Enkanomiya, says that after the Primordial One defeated the seven dragon sovereigns and made four Shades of itself, it and one Shade created birds, beasts, fish and plants and then humans, "our ancestors". The humans made a covenant with it and entered a new age. The text suggests the Primordial One may have been Phanes, a winged, crowned, androgynous being born from an egg whose shell became the barrier between the universe and the world.
### LARGE +
Under the covenant, the heavens fed the hungry and answered sorrow with their voices; the only forbidden thing was temptation. The same text calls the "path to temptation" already sealed. It is an in-world account, not objective history.

## entry: Humans and the stars
@keys: Stella Fortuna, star sign, constellation of a Vision bearer, fate written in the stars
@keys2: fate, humans, Vision
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Each Vision bearer's fate is tied to a constellation made of real celestial bodies called Stella Fortuna. Constellations describe a trait: Jean's, Leo Minor, stands for strength and responsibility shouldered too young. Several Vision bearers may share one. Special constellations depict the Traveler, the Archons, adepti and certain others. Descenders and dragons are not subject to this binding, though they can be given a constellation later, as Ineffa was.

## entry: Allogenes and Visions
@keys: allogene, allogenes
@keys2: Vision, Visions
### STANDARD
Venti tells the Traveler that those who receive a Vision are allogenes with the potential to ascend to Celestia and become gods (Ending Note, 1.0). The wiki notes that the Chinese word for allogene is the same as the game's title, "primordial god". Visions are given as a form of recognition by Celestia, and the Archons themselves do not choose who receives one.

## entry: Fontainians as made humans
@keys: Fontainians were Oceanids, original sin of Fontaine, Egeria's sin
@keys2: Fontainian, Oceanid, human
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the Archon Quest Apocalypse (4.2), Neuvillette explains that Egeria could not create new human life as one of the Seven. She poured water from the Primordial Sea into the blood of her Oceanid familiars, giving them human bodies. If a Fontainian touched Primordial Seawater, the power would escape and they would revert to Oceanid form. The Heavenly Principles had given no permission, and the "original sin" became the Prophecy of Fontaine. The wiki says Neuvillette later absolved the Fontainians' descendants and made them true humans.

## entry: Beings outside fate
@keys: Descender, Descenders, outside fate
@keys2: fate, constellation, humans
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Descenders are beings from beyond the false sky who stand outside Teyvat's rules and can influence fate. The Traveler is one. The Heavenly Principles was the first, and Nibelung is also counted as one. Rene hypothesised that a native being might become a Descender, and tried twice and failed. His research held that a Descender is a being with a will that can rival a world.

## entry: Synthetic humans
@keys: Art of Khemia, synthetic human, Primordial Human Project
@keys2: Albedo, Rhinedottir, human
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Khaenri'ahns were "obsessed with mimesis", seeking to copy and surpass natural forms by alchemy. The Art of Khemia is attributed to Rhinedottir and her student Albedo, who is her successful synthetic human. The Primordial Human Project that produced Albedo is a separate subject, covered in the Primordial Human entry.
