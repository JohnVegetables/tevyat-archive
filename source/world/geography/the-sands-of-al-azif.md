## summary
### LITE
The Sands of Al-Azif are a comparatively flat part of the Desert of Hadramaveth in Sumeru's Great Red Sand. Wenut, the huge eyeless sand-worms of the desert, roam the surface, and the Wenut Tunnels run in the earth beneath. The City of the Deceased, a one-time domain, lies within.
### STANDARD +
The area came with the desert in version 3.4. Its viewpoint, the Moonblue Canals, preserves a desert tale that three canals once met here and watered fertile fields and rich gardens; nothing of that age remains.

## geography
### STANDARD
One of the flatter, more open stretches of the desert, with the Wenut Tunnels, a system of caverns, below it. On the surface, Wenut rise from the sand to strike at travellers.
### LARGE +
As a game mechanic, in parts of the Hadramaveth the Wenut erupt from the ground to attack with area blows (true physical damage), and a player can lure their strikes onto Weathered Rocks to shatter them. This is gameplay, not a separate lore claim.
### ULTRA +
The desert boss Setekh Wenut, the Last Prince of the Sands, is placed in the Wenut Tunnels (see that entry).

## places
### STANDARD
The Wenut Tunnels (the caverns of the Wenut), the City of the Deceased domain, and the Moonblue Canals viewpoint site.
### LARGE +
The City of the Deceased is a one-time domain whose single level is called Suspiration of the Deceased. Its in-game blurb calls them halls of death that do not welcome a flood of living things, and sends the visitor through winding corridors to clear overgrown fungi, slimes and hilichurls that should not be there.

## history
### STANDARD
Canon gives the sands no dated history. The Moonblue Canals viewpoint relays an old desert tale: this was where three canals met, feeding fields and gardens, but only the faded shade of that dream remains.
### LARGE +
The Wenut Tunnels viewpoint adds that the mortal palaces have long fallen and the Wenut now rule there, making it a court and garden to their liking. Wenut legend (loading screens and the Setekh Wenut archive text) recalls a green age of grass and great trees that desertification ended.

## dangers
### LARGE
Wenut (and, in the tunnels, the boss Setekh Wenut); the fungi, slimes and hilichurls of the City of the Deceased.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Scene guidance rather than canon: flat dry sand, a low hum of vibration as unseen Wenut move below, and the memory of vanished canals and palaces. The Wenut hunt by sound and vibration, so a careful traveller treads quietly.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the sands are an open, permanent part of the Desert of Hadramaveth.
### STANDARD +
No change to the area is recorded after version 3.4.

## entry: City of the Deceased
@keys: City of the Deceased, Suspiration of the Deceased
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
A one-time domain in the Sands of Al-Azif (the wiki lists the Wenut Tunnels as its point of interest). Its single level, Suspiration of the Deceased, is a walk through winding corridors with fungi, slimes and hilichurls, and Dendro and Anemo are recommended. Its blurb: the halls of death do not welcome the deluge of living things.
### LARGE +
The game never says whose dead the city holds or why hilichurls have taken it over; treat the identity of the deceased as unknown.

## entry: The Moonblue Canals
@keys: Moonblue Canals
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Viewpoint in the Sands of Al-Azif. Ancient desert tales say this was where three canals met and nourished fertile fields and rich gardens, but only the dulled shades of a dream remain.
