## summary
### LITE
Enkanomiya is a sunken realm deep beneath Watatsumi Island in Inazuma, entered through a pool beside Sangonomiya Shrine. Once a surface nation called Delphi Pytho, it fell into the depths during the ancient war of funerary flame and became Tokoyokoku, then Byakuyakoku, lit only by an artificial sun, the Dainichi Mikoshi. The Watatsumi people descend from its inhabitants, whom the serpent god Orobashi led back to the surface. Today it is an empty ruin of ghostly Shades of Tokoyo and hostile Bathysmal Vishaps.
### STANDARD +
Its name means "Palace Below the Depths". The artificial sun can be switched off, flipping the realm between bright Whitenight and dark Evernight. Its old records, including the forbidden chronicle Before Sun and Moon, preserve a version of world history that is otherwise almost lost.

## geography
### STANDARD
A dark undersea expanse with no mountains, in which ruined islands, terraces and temples float or stand amid open darkness; the Dainichi Mikoshi rises at the centre. Main subareas: Dainichi Mikoshi, The Narrows, The Serpent's Heart and Evernight Temple, plus The Serpent's Bowels near the entrance and three hidden islands at the realm's corners (Yachimatahiko's Locus, Yachimatahime's Locus and Kunado's Locus).
### LARGE +
Byakuyakoku's borders were marked by three corners, the front line of its long struggle with the vishaps. The Narrows were once squeezed between a mountain face and the Roundlands, until strange tectonic shifts dropped the Roundlands into the depths and widened them. Space itself overlaps at the Serpent's Heart. Its scholars said that even this deep, the "undying wind" (personified as Tokoyo Ookami) and "water" (the might of the vishaps) still held sway, so engineering had to reckon with both.

## places
### STANDARD
Dainichi Mikoshi: the artificial sun, once called Helios or Hyperion. The Serpent's Heart: anciently Delphi, "land of snakes", built to guard secrets, hold prisoners and worship the cosmic serpent Ouroboros; its altar is a sealing device. Evernight Temple: holds the Tokoyo Reisen, a stream once called the River Lethe. The Narrows: a long, widened passage. The three Loci: islands on which stood the hidden Towers of the Three Realms.
### LARGE +
Other points of interest include the old library ruin where the Traveler meets Enjou, a hidden Vishap Research Lab reached through a phase gate north of the Dainichi Mikoshi, and the Emanant Skylights circled by schools of airborne ghostfish. The realm is full of Byakuyakoku mechanisms: phase gates, triangular devices, daises and barriers (Shield-Lights of Tokoyo) that appear or vanish depending on whether it is Whitenight or Evernight, and Sunfire Phantasms, mirage-like distortions that hide or reveal things.

## people
### STANDARD
No living community remains; the Enkanomiyans moved to Watatsumi Island long ago, and some great Watatsumi houses, such as the Yuna Clan, trace their lines back to Byakuyakoku. The "inhabitants" now are Shades of Tokoyo (Sinshades): afterimages of past people's memories and feelings that appear during Evernight, among them the sage Aberaku, the seven Sunchildren, the shrine maiden Eboshi (Erebos) and Aru, a "sinless" shade made to help with the Watatsumi ritual.
### LARGE +
A shade that realises it is only an afterimage soon fades, since "to know sin is to be free of it". Visitors in recent times: the Traveler and Paimon (sent by the Sangonomiya shrine maiden Tsuyuko), the Abyss Lector who called himself Enjou, and Abyss Order mages and hilichurls.

## governance
### LARGE
Byakuyakoku was nominally ruled by the Sunchild, a child-king chosen and controlled by a cabal of corrupt nobles who also taught the people to worship the Dainichi Mikoshi as a god. Orobashi overthrew the Sunchildren and ruled as Watatsumi Omikami; its descendants' Sangonomiya Clan now leads Watatsumi Island. Today entry is controlled from Sangonomiya Shrine: Kokomi, as Divine Priestess, must authorise it.

## culture
### LARGE
Enkanomiyans revered serpents even before Orobashi, worshipping the "great imaginary serpent" Ouroboros since the earliest times; after Orobashi came, art showed Ouroboros as a scaleless serpent and Orobashi as a coral-adorned one. They also practised "triangle worship": they held that three realms met in their land and built three watchposts, later trial sites, at its corners. Triangles recur in their architecture, including the three pillars of the Dainichi Mikoshi.
### ULTRA +
The original Enkanomiyan language has been lost; as the people merged with Inazuma, names were rendered in Inazuman form (Abrax became Aberaku no Mikoto, Helios became the Dainichi Mikoshi, Erebos became Eboshi). One surviving riddle book from the transition still gives Narukami-style names with Byakuyakoku glosses, and records that livestock such as cattle and horses died out within two generations of the fall, so later Enkanomiyans knew them only from books. Watatsumi still keeps some customs, such as fortune-telling with Deep Sea Spiritherbs transplanted from Enkanomiya. Enkanomiyan priests venerated Istaroth under the names Kairos and Tokoyo Ookami, the God of Moments, though they dared not speak her true name (the scribe of Before Sun and Moon writes it only once, in reverse).

## history
### STANDARD
As Delphi Pytho it belonged to the single unified human civilization that followed the Primordial One's victory over the Seven Sovereigns. When the "second throne of the heavens" (Nibelung) returned and the war of funerary flame broke out about 6,000 years ago, the land fell into the depths. In the darkness the Dragonheirs of the Depths (Bathysmal Vishaps) preyed on the people, and lanterns could not hold them back. Only Istaroth answered their prayers, enlightening the sage Abrax (Aberaku), who led the building of Helios, the Dainichi Mikoshi; its light drove the vishaps off and began the "Years of Sun and Moon". The people then found their way to the surface barred, and nobles installed the puppet Sunchildren. Ages later a child found the fugitive god Orobashi in a cave and asked it to be their god; Orobashi overthrew the Sunchildren, sealed away the vishaps and grew a coral path to the surface, creating Watatsumi Island, to which the people migrated.
### LARGE +
The chronicle Before Sun and Moon dates events by year: in the "Third Year of Darkness" Istaroth heard the people; in the "Year of Blindness" Abrax's wisdom awoke; in the "Year of Sight", the First Year of Sun and Moon, Helios was finished and Whitenight banished Evernight. In the Second Year the first Sunchild, Rikoru, had Abrax imprisoned for life on a false charge of plotting to usurp him; by the Tenth Year Abrax was dead. His bones, crown and robes were enshrined in separate places to honour him, which split his shade apart. Others, such as Spartacus, also resisted the Sunchildren and were jailed. Orobashi had fled the continent after defeat in the Archon War; after Enkanomiya it moved to Watatsumi and was slain by the Raiden Shogun (Ei) at Yashiori Island about 2,000 years ago. Under Orobashi, the people renamed their old home Enkanomiya and the new land Sangonomiya.
### ULTRA +
The people's own chroniclers concluded that the Primordial One had laid a ban on their return, and inferred from it that the Second Who Came had been defeated. The same chronicle names Phanes as possibly the Primordial One, and describes how it used the "eggshell" to separate the universe from the world. Orobashi read Before Sun and Moon, learned of a prophecy that the Dragon Sovereign of Water would be reborn in human form (a prophecy since borne out by Neuvillette), and personally directed experiments on the Dragonheirs so that it would not happen in Enkanomiya; the records say no vishap died in them. The experiments stopped as vishap intelligence approached human levels. On the last day before the people left for the surface, envoys from Khaenri'ah with a Ruin Guard came to seize the chronicle; the guard Antigonus (Antei) stopped them at grave cost (World Quest "Antigonus").

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Silence, ruins and water-dark sky. In Whitenight the artificial sun bathes the pale, Greek-looking colonnades in a flat, timeless glow; in Evernight the realm sinks into blue darkness and murmuring shades replay their past. Fish swim through the air. The overall feeling is of a beautiful, forgotten civilisation sleeping beneath the waves.

## dangers
### LARGE
Bathysmal Vishaps, which hate man-made light and can drain a fighter's strength; a group of them, the Coral Defenders, guards the depths beneath the Dainichi Mikoshi. Ruin machines, hilichurls, Abyss Mages and Abyss Heralds roam the ruins.
### ULTRA +
The Abyss Order has taken an interest in Enkanomiya's records. Every few thousand years the realm's pull bleaches Watatsumi's soil into barren Holy Soil, and legend warns that the sealed Dragonheirs and other monsters could break loose if this is not checked.

## travel
### STANDARD
Enkanomiya is sealed from Watatsumi Island by five Goshou Rocks, the Eye, Fang (Tooth), Fin, Tail and Heart. The Spirit Pearls from the first four unlock the Heart, which yields the Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep, said to be forged from Orobashi's blood. With the spirit veins between the realms flowing and a bearer "as clear as water", the key opens the deep pool directly across from (east of) Sangonomiya Shrine, the Moon-Bathed Deep, into the descent.
### LARGE +
Inside, travellers move between islands by gliding (riding ghostfish shoals) and through ancient phase gates; the three corner islands can only be summoned by shrine maidens or Vassals of Watatsumi.

## quests
### LARGE
"The Moon-Bathed Deep" (Tsuyuko asks the Traveler to break the seals for the Watatsumi Goryou Matsuri); "From Dusk to Dawn in Byakuyakoku" (recovering the Bloodbranch Coral, meeting Enjou, who turns out to be an Abyss Lector); "Erebos' Secret" (Eboshi's three trials at the corners); and many world quests among the shades, including "Hyperion's Dirge", "Antigonus", "Lotus Eater", "The Phaethons' Syrtos" and "Collection of Dragons and Snakes" (which unlocks The Byakuyakoku Collection). "The Moon-Bathed Deep" dates from 2.1; the others are from 2.4.
### ULTRA +
The limited 2.5 event "Three Realms Gateway Offering" (story chapter "Aphotic Diffusal") covered Enkanomiya in an abyssal darkness; see the sub-entry.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 Enkanomiya remains an empty, sealed ruin beneath Watatsumi, visited mainly by the Traveler. Its people's descendants live on Watatsumi Island.
### STANDARD +
The Traveler retrieved the Bloodbranch Coral for the Watatsumi Goryou Matsuri and defeated Enjou, but the Abyss Order knows the place. No later Archon Quest returns there, and it is little known outside Inazuma: when the Traveler mentions it in Fontaine, Wriothesley has never heard of it and had meant to ask Neuvillette about it.
### LARGE +
In 6.5, the Uplifted philosopher Istoria in the Temple of Space recalled a student, Abrax, who had gone to Delphi Pytho; he did not know what became of him or what the place is now called.

## entry: Dainichi Mikoshi, Whitenight and Evernight
@keys: Dainichi Mikoshi, Whitenight, Evernight, Helios, Hyperion, Byakuya no Hikari
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
The Dainichi Mikoshi ("Great Sun Mikoshi") is Enkanomiya's artificial sun, a tower of three tall pillars around a triangular interior at the realm's centre. Built by the sage Aberaku (Abrax), who called it Helios, "the divine chariot of the sun", it was later known as Hyperion. Its light drove off the Dragonheirs and created Whitenight; with it switched off, the realm returns to Evernight, when hidden walls, mechanisms and shades appear. Nobles later made it an object of worship, and the Sunchildren's Rite of Solar Return took place in its inner sanctum.
### LARGE +
Hydrological Studies in Byakuyakoku says it stood for earth, the element between wind and water, and notes that, as prophecy foretold, the sun the sage revealed was also used to cast a great shadow over the nation. Per 2.4 promotional material, Orobashi later strengthened it so day and night could be toggled. Under it, special Bathysmal Vishaps absorbed the Bloodbranch Coral the Traveler needed.

## entry: The Sunchildren
@keys: Sunchild, Sunchildren, Phaethon, Phaethons, Rite of Solar Return, Rikoru, Divine Bridle
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Sunchildren (Phaethons) were child rulers whom a noble cabal set up and manipulated after the Dainichi Mikoshi was built. There were seven: Rikoru (the first), Ion, Isumenasu, Orupeusu, Piramumon, Risutaiosu and Surepio. They were hated for the cruelties done in their name, though people knew they were puppets. Their attendants were forbidden to speak to them, and before a Sunchild grew old enough to see the truth, the Rite of Solar Return burned the child to death inside the artificial sun. The Divine Bridle was the badge of their office. Orobashi ended their rule. Their shades can still be found in Evernight.

## entry: Aberaku, the Helios-Forger
@keys: Aberaku, Abrax, Helios-Forger, Aberaku no Mikoto
### STANDARD
Aberaku no Mikoto (originally Abrax) was the sage who, enlightened by Tokoyo Ookami (Istaroth), designed the Dainichi Mikoshi and led its construction. When he saw how the nobles used the Sunchild, they convinced Rikoru that Aberaku wanted his throne; he was charged with high treason and held in a high, dark, cold prison for the rest of his life, dying by the tenth year of Sun and Moon. His enshrined bones, crown and robes split his shade into fragments; the Traveler reunited them in "Hyperion's Dirge". Proud and theatrical, he introduces himself as "Helios-Forger" and bears no hatred for the child who imprisoned him.
### LARGE +
In 6.5, Istoria of the Temple of Space says his student Abrax went to Delphi Pytho, that he does not know whether Abrax survived the war, and that no one has found Abrax's name in historical records.

## entry: Shades of Tokoyo
@keys: Shade of Tokoyo, Shades of Tokoyo, Sinshade, Sinshades, Ghostfire Phantasm, Sunfire Phantasm, Eidolon
### STANDARD
Because Byakuyakoku is a "nether realm", it sometimes projects afterimages of the past from people's lingering thoughts: the Shades of Tokoyo or Sinshades, ley line phenomena born of strong feelings called "Sins of Tokoyo". It is said that when the God of Moments looks with favour on someone, their feelings and memories remain forever. They appear only in Evernight. Ancient Enkanomiyans called both these and the mirages of Whitenight "Eidolons", and only after Orobashi came did they tell them apart as Ghostfire Phantasms and Sunfire Phantasms. A shade that realises what it is soon fades.

## entry: Dragonheirs of the Depths
@keys: Dragonheir of the Depths, Dragonheirs of the Depths, Dragonheirs, Bathysmal Vishap, Bathysmal Vishaps, vishap-people
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Bathysmal Vishaps, called Dragonheirs of the Depths, are deep-sea vishaps that held the depths when Byakuyakoku fell and hunted its people in the dark. They shun man-made light; one in-game description says this is because they were robbed of the true sun and the surface. They are proud elemental creatures that never bowed to any god and can adapt fast, passing chosen traits and new elements to their young within a generation.
### LARGE +
Under Orobashi the Enkanomiyans experimented on them. Intelligence tests showed fourth-generation vishaps approaching the language ability of a twelve-year-old, and the researchers urged a stop. An attempt to graft Orobashi's blood into them eventually succeeded, and the records note they were beings of the Light Realm, at odds with the Human Realm. The researchers even worried they might be proved narrow-minded for dismissing an earlier writer's "fantastical tale" about vishap-people.

## entry: Towers of the Three Realms and Eboshi
@keys: Towers of the Three Realms, Yachimatahiko's Locus, Yachimatahime's Locus, Kunado's Locus, Eboshi, Erebos, Three Great Martial Trials
### STANDARD
At Byakuyakoku's three corners stood towers that harmonised the three realms meeting there and steadied the land's winds and waters. They were so important that they were hidden by secret arts, summonable only by shrine maidens and Vassals of Watatsumi; their ancient names are lost, and the islands are now called Yachimatahiko's, Yachimatahime's and Kunado's Locus. The shade of Eboshi (Erebos), a shrine maiden and auxiliary of Sangonomiya, raises them and sets the Traveler three martial trials after the Bloodbranch Coral reaches Watatsumi ("Erebos' Secret").

## entry: Orobashi in Enkanomiya
@keys: Orobashi, Watatsumi Omikami, Profane Serpent, Orobaxi
@keys2: Enkanomiya, Byakuyakoku, Tokoyokoku, Sunchild, Dragonheir, coral
### STANDARD
Defeated in the Archon War, the serpent god Orobashi fled the continent and ended up in a cavern of the depths. A child of Enkanomiya, diving past the three corners on a dare to look for a Dragonbone Flower, found it and felt kinship rather than fear. Calling itself the Profane Serpent with no mortal followers left, it agreed to become their god. It overthrew the Sunchildren, sealed the Dragonheirs, taught the people, and broke off its own coral branches to give them light and build a ladder to the surface, the origin of Watatsumi Island. Its life force lives on in the Bloodbranch Coral.

## entry: Before Sun and Moon
@keys: Before Sun and Moon, Byakuyakoku Collection, Hydrological Studies in Byakuyakoku, Bathysmal Vishap Experimental Records
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Before Sun and Moon is an Enkanomiyan chronicle, forbidden to ordinary readers, mixing fable and history from the world's beginning to the building of the Dainichi Mikoshi; it is Vol. 2 of The Byakuyakoku Collection. It tells how the first throne of the heavens, the Primordial One (possibly Phanes), defeated the Seven Sovereigns, made four shining shades, and created humanity, with whom it made a covenant; how the second throne came and the funerary war cast Delphi Pytho into the depths; and how only Istaroth heard the people. Its scribe stops writing in the Tenth Year of Sun and Moon on hearing armour outside.
### LARGE +
The Abyss Order sought it to prove that the gods and Celestia came from outside Teyvat, and long ago Khaenri'ahn envoys tried to seize it. Other volumes record Byakuyakoku's geography, the vishap experiments and a children's riddle book that repeats the prophecy that the Dragon of Water would return in human form.

## entry: Enjou in Enkanomiya
@keys: Enjou
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
In Enkanomiya the Traveler meets Enjou, a polite man who claims to be clerical staff assisting the trial and explains that the place's true name is Byakuyakoku. He helps them break into the Dainichi Mikoshi, but is really an Abyss Order agent searching for records from before the artificial sun; after the vishaps beneath the tower fall, he reveals himself as an Abyss Lector, fights the Traveler, and leaves after hinting that Paimon may someday betray them. He later reappears in Natlan under other names.

## entry: Watatsumi Goryou Matsuri and the descent
@keys: Watatsumi Goryou Matsuri, Bloodbranch Coral, Jade Coral Branch, Goshou Rocks, Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep, Moon-Bathed Deep, Tsuyuko
### STANDARD
By legend the depths of Enkanomiya pull at Watatsumi's spirit every few thousand years, bleaching the soil into infertile Holy Soil. The Watatsumi Goryou Matsuri pushes the spirits back down using a Jade Coral Branch (Bloodbranch Coral) broken off in the depths, said to be made from Orobashi's own body. The shrine maiden Tsuyuko, who studied at the Sumeru Akademiya, believes seawater salinity causes the bleaching and that the coral's nutrients restore the land. She recruited the Traveler to break the Goshou Rock seals and, with Kokomi's permission, fetch the coral.

## entry: Aphotic Diffusal (Three Realms Gateway Offering)
@keys: Three Realms Gateway Offering, Aphotic Diffusal, Tsumi, Bokuso Box, Sentou, Reptilian, Reptilians
@version: 2.5
@availability: limited
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
During the limited-time event "Three Realms Gateway Offering" (2.5), a darkness laced with abyssal energy engulfed Enkanomiya and complicated the Watatsumi ritual. Kokomi sent the Traveler back down, where they met Tsumi, a snake-masked shrine maiden who had been tending wounded Watatsumi soldiers. Enjou had promised the vishaps the darkness they craved in exchange for safe passage for the Abyss Order, then rebuilt the towers to spread a darkness poisonous even to them. The Traveler dispelled it and persuaded Tsumi, who was raised by the vishaps and could have destroyed the Dainichi Mikoshi, not to do so; she warned that the vishaps would return. Back at the shrine, Kokomi took Tsumi to be the last Vassal of Watatsumi (Orobashi's half-serpent familiars), rejected Enjou's theory that she was one of the "vishap-people" (Reptilians: vishaps that imitate humans and differ only in their vertical pupils) as pure fiction, since Watatsumi's ancient documents say nothing of them, and resolved to find a way to stop the soil bleaching that would not keep disturbing the vishaps.
