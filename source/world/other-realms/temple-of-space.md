## summary
@version: 6.5
### LITE
The Temple of Space, which its maker named "Segment-Domain Tau", is a vast temple floating unseen in the sky above Windrest Peak in northern Mondstadt. It belongs to Asmoday, the Ruler of Space. She turned it into an exhibition hall for vanished civilisations. People, relics, landscapes and even whole ecosystems from past ages were "uplifted" into it and kept there, unchanging, as living memories. Inside, time stands still: nobody ages, hungers, tires or dies. Asmoday has been gone for a long time. The Watcher Melanta then ruled the temple harshly until the Traveler defeated him in 6.5.
### STANDARD +
The way in is the Statue of The Seven at the Old Sanatorium Site, a ruined building on Windrest Peak that stands on what the temple left behind on the ground. Loading-screen lore says wind may not enter the Master of Space's hall, which is why the mountain winds below are so fierce.

## geography
### STANDARD
The temple is a huge structure of halls, walkways and display cases hanging above the clouds over Windrest Peak. Paimon is amazed that something so big floats over Mondstadt without anyone seeing it. Its centre is the Pillar Hall of Space, where the Watchers once kept watch over the world. Around it are three themed exhibition areas: Luyang Academy, the Mahavaipulya Chamber and the Desert Pavilion. At its highest point is the Cage (Atrium) of Suchness, home of Asmoday and her Watchers. The Corridor of Nothingness separates the Cage from the lower, inhabited temple, which is called the Hall of Emptiness. Below and apart from it all is the Apathic Interval, a transitional space.
### LARGE +
Paths take shape under people's feet as they walk, and stone walls vanish to reveal hidden galleries. The architecture can be moved, turned and stretched by a Watcher's power. The Mahavaipulya Chamber has pages that float and fly, and Luyang Academy has paintings that hold whole spaces inside them. A plain doorway can open onto a hall far larger than the space behind it should allow.

## places
### LARGE
- **Pillar Hall Central Zone**: the heart of the temple. The Memory Core stands here, a safe spot that the Suppression-type guards cannot enter, and Istoria stays here after the story.
- **Luyang Academy**: taken from ancient Liyue. It has become a place of painting and art, where the residents painted, taught and exhibited.
- **Mahavaipulya Chamber**: a library of ancient texts, plus new novels written by uplifted residents.
- **Desert Pavilion**: a desert-style complex based on Sumeru's ancient temples, rebuilt from memory by Venipi.
- **Apathic Interval**: the first stage of uplifting, where newcomers must give up every desire.
- **Cage of Suchness**: the summit where Asmoday and the Watchers lived, reached only by the Path to the Heavens.
### ULTRA +
The first galleries the Traveler passes through display creatures as museum pieces with plaques. Two are Fontemer Aberrants (an Armored Crab and a Blubberbeast) whose captions mention the Golden City of Hyperborea, the Great Mother Naberius and Remuria, and a serpentine skeleton hangs from a ceiling. Display cases double as punishment cells: Domain Keepers shut people inside them, and the Traveler frees Josephus from one.

## people
### LARGE
The residents are the "Uplifted", also called Aiones. They are people chosen from particular eras or vanished civilisations and kept as segments of human culture. Notable residents include Istoria, a scholar from the age when the Dark Dragon King tore open the sky, and his friend Josephus. There are also the painters Daozi and Bosch at Luyang Academy; Yu Huangyi, Calcerius and Okikuni near the Mahavaipulya Chamber; and Venipi, once a stone hauler for King Deshret's mausoleum, at the Desert Pavilion. Dex, a juvenile Watcher, was raised among the residents. Domain Keepers and the various Segment-Domain Keepers act as guards and staff.
### ULTRA +
Long stagnation has made the residents oddly gentle with each other. Josephus says everyone there is everyone's friend, because over such long spans of time anything else would drown the world in sorrow. Istoria insists that all are equal in frozen space-time, whether saint or commoner, young or old. A painter the residents call "Maukie" (Escher) was exiled by Melanta and wanders Windrest Peak with no memory. Istoria believed the uplifting had already ended, so he was surprised to see new humans arrive.

## governance
### STANDARD
Asmoday made the temple a place of preservation only. She created two Watchers, Nihilita and Melanta, to keep order from the Cage of Suchness, bring chosen people in and observe Teyvat in silence. She also made the Domain Keepers as lesser guardians. Her rule forbade innovation and change of any kind. Leaving was possible only by building the Path to the Heavens, which costs all of one's memories, so that nothing learned in the temple could return to the mortal world.
### LARGE +
Istoria says the Corridor of Nothingness draws its power from the temple itself and is its "rule of departure", and the Watchers exist to uphold that rule. He adds that Dex is too immature to control it. Melanta says another rule is built into him: when a human gives up everything to seek an answer, he must answer in the Creator's place and hold nothing back. Breaking the temple's laws also harms the Watchers themselves. Melanta's acts against the rules set off his own self-destruction.

## history
### STANDARD
The people of Great Arcadia worshipped both the Master of Time (Istaroth) and the Master of Space (Asmoday), and raised a temple to each; the two were once joined. A loading screen says Asmoday never visited while the temple flourished. After a disaster the Temple of Space was lifted into the sky and hidden, and only then did her gaze rest on it. She remade it into Segment-Domain Tau, a silent watchtower over the world. The ruins on the ground were later built over into the sanatorium. At some point Asmoday vanished. Nihilita "opened his eye", left his post and was branded a traitor. Melanta stayed and enforced her rules ever more harshly.
### LARGE +
While Asmoday was away, her sister Shades brought in things they found interesting and allowed small changes. Ronova installed the Maranasati Caskets that guard the Apathic Interval. Naberius, Ronova and Istaroth had Venipi rebuild a Sumeru desert complex from memory, which became the Desert Pavilion. Melanta, longing for his creator, then tried to undo every change. He pressed the Mahavaipulya novelists into paper between pages for blurring history with fiction, and turned Luyang Academy's people into ink blots inside paintings. He turned the Desert Pavilion's people to sand and exiled "Maukie". As its laws began to fail, the temple's boundary with Teyvat weakened, and Domain Keepers started falling from the sky onto Windrest Peak.
### ULTRA +
The exhibits named as preserved include records of Hyperborea, Lang-Gan, Delphi Pytho (Enkanomiya), Ochkanatlan, Remuria and Arcadia. Istoria says the temple was probably never meant as a paradise. Its purpose was preservation, not evolution, which is why the novelists were the first to be punished.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Inside it is silent and windless, and time feels strange. Zeitlind says arriving felt like a very long while and also only an instant. The temple feels like a museum after closing time, with plaques, glass cases, bookshelves and paintings. Its people are centuries or millennia old, frozen at the age they were taken, and they treat newcomers with a dry, weary friendliness. When Melanta loses control, the sky and every structure turn red and Asmoday's red cubes swarm.
### ULTRA +
Its residents describe the temple as an "anthology of the past" with no future. To them, a visitor from the present has a future they lack, so that visitor's needs come first.

## dangers
### LARGE
Under Melanta, Domain Keepers hunted residents who broke precedent and locked them in display cases, which meant a new exile to the Apathic Interval of at least fifty years. Anyone cast into the Interval must reach total mental emptiness to leave, which can take years or centuries. It changes a person's personality and stops them from ever obtaining a Vision. Leaving the temple for good costs all of one's memories.
### ULTRA +
A visitor's belongings can be classed as "relics of this age" and taken for the exhibits: Zeitlind's suitcase was stolen and its contents scattered across the three halls. Nihilita shielded Zeitlind from "apathic afflictions" on the way up, which suggests the ascent itself is dangerous for an unprotected human.

## travel
### LARGE
The temple is reached through the Statue of The Seven at the Old Sanatorium Site, where Anemo power opens a portal that lifts the traveller into the sky. Paimon and the Traveler have to be escorted in by Nihilita's arrangement the first time. The way out to the ground is not free: Istoria says sending someone home was once nearly impossible, but became easy as the temple's laws weakened.
### ULTRA +
Gameplay (not in-world lore): the temple has its own map tab and exploration progress. Dex's "Spatial Dragging" moves, rotates and stretches architecture, opens Restoration Cores and reshapes the spaces inside pages and paintings. Harpasta turn the player into a Plume Moth to fly between areas. Memory Factors are offered to the Memory Core for rewards. Co-op is locked until the quest series is finished.

## quests
### STANDARD
The temple is the setting of the world quest series A Long Day in the Mountains (6.5). It begins with The Wind Has Ceased at Dornman Port and the Old Sanatorium Site and continues through Luyang Academy, the Mahavaipulya Chamber, the Desert Pavilion and the Apathic Interval. It ends with the confrontation with Melanta in The Final Judgment.

## status
@version: 6.5
### LITE
As of 7.1 the temple is free of Melanta's rule and still floats hidden above Windrest Peak, with its uplifted residents still living there. Asmoday has not returned.
### STANDARD +
Melanta self-destructed after his defeat. Nihilita is out in the mortal world. Istoria remains at the Memory Core. Dex, the juvenile Watcher the residents raised, keeps helping the Traveler explore the temple (in gameplay terms, its manipulation abilities stay available).

## entry: The Uplifted and the Apathic Interval
@keys: the Uplifted, Aiones, Apathic Interval, Maranasati Casket
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
"Uplifted" is the temple's term for people taken from a particular era or a vanished civilisation and preserved there forever as living memories. The Watchers first cast them into the Apathic Interval. To leave it, a person must give up every desire through meditation and mastery of their thoughts until their mind is completely empty, which takes anywhere from a few years to several centuries. The process changes their personality and stops them from ever gaining a Vision. Once inside, they no longer age, hunger, tire or die. Istoria says the uplifting is thought to have ended.
### LARGE +
Ronova placed the Maranasati Caskets in the Interval to guard it. When Melanta threw the Traveler, Istoria, Zeitlind and Dex into it during the story, they broke out without giving up their desires by destroying the Caskets with Dex's help, and also freed a man named Alara.

## entry: The Watchers Nihilita and Melanta
@keys: Nihilita, Melanta, Watcher of Nothingness, Watcher of Melancholy, The Open-Eyed
@keys2: Temple of Space, Segment-Domain Tau, Asmoday, Dex, Windrest Peak
@version: 6.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Asmoday created two Watchers to keep the temple and named them after the two things she could least bear: Nihilita (nothingness) and Melanta (melancholy). After she vanished, Nihilita "opened his eye", saw the truths of the world and left, so Melanta called him the traitor, the Fallen One. Melanta stayed in the Cage of Suchness and tried to restore the original rules by punishing the residents. Nihilita waited on Windrest Peak for someone who could free the temple. He sent first Raumhilda and then the Traveler, each time lending them the juvenile Watcher Dex.
### LARGE +
After his defeat, Melanta understood that "when the divine departs the throne, all creations are free". He admitted that he had acted out of jealousy of Nihilita's freedom and out of hope for his creator's praise, and wondered why she had not asked him to come with her. His own rule-breaking made him self-destruct, and his last words were addressed to Nihilita. Istoria explains that "Watcher" originally meant one who is awakened, and that a Watcher who keeps its eye closed is swallowed by darkness.

## entry: Path to the Heavens and the Corridor of Nothingness
@keys: Path to the Heavens, Corridor of Nothingness, Cage of Suchness, Atrium of Suchness
@version: 6.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Corridor of Nothingness separates the inhabited Hall of Emptiness from the Cage (Atrium) of Suchness at the temple's summit. The only way to an audience with the temple's master, and so the only way out, is to build the Path to the Heavens across the Corridor by giving up all of one's memories and wisdom, so that the temple's truths never reach the mortal world. Walkers on the Path get one last look back at their memories. With Asmoday gone, a path once built does not vanish after use. Melanta, however, destroyed half of the Corridor and cut the Path that Raumhilda had built.
### LARGE +
In The Final Judgment, Zeitlind gave up her memories to rebuild the Path. They joined with what was left of her sister's, and the Traveler, crossing it, saw the dreams the two sisters had shared. Back at the sanatorium Zeitlind's memories faded; the Traveler and Paimon told her who she was and that her sister was alive somewhere in the world, and pointed her to her suitcase for clues.

## entry: Raumhilda and Zeitlind in the Temple
@keys: Raumhilda, Hilda, Zeitlind, Lind
@keys2: Temple of Space, Istoria, Melanta, sanatorium, Path to the Heavens
@version: 6.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Raumhilda ("Hilda") met Nihilita on a midnight walk while she was staying at the Windrest Peak sanatorium. He entrusted Dex to her and sent her to the temple, where she befriended the residents (she called Istoria "Grandpa"). She also learned painting from Daozi and Bosch. Moved by the residents' suffering, she gave up her memories to build the Path to the Heavens and faced Melanta. They could not agree, and she promised that her younger sister would one day come and defeat him. She left with no memories, and her grave at the sanatorium is empty. Her younger sister Zeitlind later came looking for her, and with the Traveler she freed the temple and paid the same price in memories.
### LARGE +
Items Zeitlind brought to say goodbye to her sister were scattered through the halls as "relics". Among them were Kamera photos from Hilda's vacation, the letters the sisters exchanged while Hilda was at the sanatorium, and Hilda's amethyst brooch. Hilda's watermelon still life, "Long Live Life", stays in Luyang Academy at Zeitlind's wish. Zeitlind chose "To be continued" as her own epitaph, echoing the line "One lifetime doesn't count" on her sister's empty grave.

## entry: The Damaged Coffin in the Cage of Suchness
@keys: Damaged Coffin, Cage of Suchness, Atrium of Suchness
@keys2: Traveler, sibling, Asmoday, Temple of Space
@version: 6.5
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
At the summit of the temple lies a damaged coffin with every trace of its occupant worn away by time. Even so, the Traveler senses that the person who once rested there was the one most important to them. The wiki takes this to be the Traveler's sibling, whom Asmoday took during the Cataclysm. The text urges the Traveler to keep travelling until they meet again. Canon does not show when the sibling was placed there or how they left.
### ULTRA +
Melanta, asked why a god took the Traveler's sibling, said he did not understand it himself. He had once thought it was punishment for transgressions, but now feared a deeper meaning, and he called the Traveler a being outside the order of the "children of humanity". A note found in the Cage of Suchness, written in both the language of the Andromeda Empire and the local tongue, was left by a self-described novelist who has wandered many worlds. The writer says they slipped out of a "containment facility" after years of tinkering, objects to being locked away without explanation as an illegal entrant, notes having already been paid royalties in Mora by their publisher (Yae Publishing House), and mentions finally working out the ending of "VM" while confined.

## entry: Shades' Additions to the Temple
@keys: Desert Pavilion, Venipi, Luyang Academy, Mahavaipulya Chamber
@keys2: Temple of Space, Segment-Domain Tau, Ronova, Naberius, Istaroth, Melanta
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
Asmoday's rule was pure preservation, but after she vanished her sister Shades softened it. They brought in things they liked and allowed small acts of change. Luyang Academy, a segment of ancient Liyue, grew into a working art school. The Mahavaipulya Chamber filled with new novels by residents. Naberius, Ronova and Istaroth commissioned Venipi to rebuild a Sumeru desert complex from memory, which became the Desert Pavilion. Melanta treated all of this as corruption and punished it until the Traveler restored each hall.
### LARGE +
At the Desert Pavilion, Melanta's Suppression-type Keepers and their leader, the Punisher of Gomorrah, kept the residents turned to sand until the Traveler's group defeated them.
