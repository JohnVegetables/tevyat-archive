## summary
### LITE
Spiritwall Cave is a point of interest in Toyac Springs, Natlan. It is a closed-off cave where someone left a half-written, half-drawn note describing years spent making wall paintings.
### STANDARD +
The writer says a rockfall trapped them there, and that they had run out of the phlogiston used for engraving. The note is unsigned.

## history
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
The note, titled Picturesque Records, is a piece of paper well preserved because of the sealed surroundings. Its author asks how many days have passed since the rockfall trapped them. They regret not seeing an ancient excavation site once more, and speak of the wall paintings they were making.
### LARGE +
The author was a graffiti maker who turned away from Phlogiston Engraving, the formal practice their friends and elders urged, in favour of pictures and graffiti. They preferred the soft lines of light and wind, flowing water and falling leaves to what they saw as rigid lines.
### ULTRA +
By their own account they had used up all the phlogiston available to them and had spent ten-odd years on the art form. They hoped it would one day be enjoyed or popularised, and half-jokingly considered the storytellers' claim that stones can speak and that new life might grow from pictures after centuries. They also admitted the paintings might be erased by wind and rain, and ended by resolving to finish the walls.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Quiet, enclosed and dusty, with a creative loneliness: pictures on the stone, a note in a hopeful and wry voice.

## entry: Picturesque Records
@keys: Picturesque Records, Spiritwall Cave

### STANDARD
Picturesque Records is an unsigned note in Spiritwall Cave, Toyac Springs. Its author, trapped by a rockfall, wrote about turning Phlogiston Engraving into free pictures and graffiti and hoped the art would outlast them.
