## summary
### LITE
Reed Miller's Great Treasure is a hidden cave beneath Dreadshade Mire in Nod-Krai, holding a huge pile of Iron Coins heaped on a ship. In folklore it is the great hoard of Reed Miller, the Treasure Hoarders' legendary master thief. In truth it was arranged about 500 years ago by Brahe, the third man to use that alias, as part of a plan to bury the real Reed Miller under harmless tall tales. The Traveler finds it by collecting the five Proofs of the Cognoscenti.
### STANDARD +
Entry is through the world quest The Raven's Legacy, the final part of East of the Moon, West of the Sun. The vault is a trial: three locked doors, riddling notices, a Weasel Thief statue and a guardian monster. Inside, the ship's helm triggers a vision of two thieves in the past, and Brahe's Notes explain the whole scheme.

## geography
### STANDARD
Beneath Dreadshade Mire in the Voidsea Outlook area, reached through a hole opened under a giant Weasel Thief statue. The map shows three levels: the Entrance, an Entrance Hall and the Destination, where the ship and its Iron Coins lie.
### LARGE +
Along the way are three doors, each with two solutions (picking the right key from keys offered by Weasel Thieves or attacking the door; digging or using kuuvahki to pull Unipolar Crystonite from the ceiling; tossing Iron Coins or luring out the weasel holding the key with food). A Radiant Lunapod guards a chest. Completing the quest unlocks a teleport waypoint and the viewpoint Elysium's Final Treasure.

## places
### STANDARD
The Weasel Thief statue with five Iron Coins hanging from its tail, which the Traveler feeds the five Proofs of the Cognoscenti; the three doors; a stone tablet with a verse about a raven felled in a net amid thorn-bushes; and the ship under its mound of Iron Coins.
### LARGE +
Three Suspicious Notices offer cryptic hints about the gate, a strange rock and the weasels' tastes. Brahe's Notes (four of them) sit in the vault and are available to read after the quest.

## people
### STANDARD
Brahe is the author of the vault and of Brahe's Notes. Tolfin, the storyteller who guides the quest, and his grandmother Hiljetta (who fights the Traveler as a friendly spar) lead the way to it. Two thieves named Relya and Silve appear in the helm flashback.
### LARGE +
The wiki gives Brahe as the adoptive son of Prokhor Trubetskoy, the governor of Nod-Krai. Tolfin and Hiljetta are described as his descendants. Weasel Thieves, named by Brahe, helped him carry the Iron Coins.

## history
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Brahe, last Governor of Nod-Krai after the Cataclysm, saw his friends Avrelian and Alia gone and their ideals twisted by bandits who used the name Reed Miller to justify cruelty. He decided to smother their history under contradictory legends. He placed a vault of Iron Coins, with the help of wild weasels, to give the name a harmless, absurd legend of a Great Treasure and keep the tales alive for future Treasure Hoarders.
### LARGE +
Brahe wrote that the secret funds first hidden here by his father were stolen long ago by Avrelian and Silvestr, when Brahe was five, to return the money to poor children, and that his father recouped them within three days. He turned the site into a puzzle later called the Proof of the Cognoscenti, a test of instinct and talent. A final message in the vault, written in the voice of Master Thief Reed Miller, advises whoever solves it to take the riches quietly, remove the note, and let the story of scattered proofs keep circulating so that the wealth stays safe.
### ULTRA +
The wiki adds that Brahe faked his death in official records and wrote contradictory accounts under pseudonyms such as Brahe the Bard and Grand Duke Trubetskoy. Three weeks after he set the vault up, Dmitri Dubrovich Sukhotin, a former Chief Instructor of the Snezhnayan Royal Military Academy, found it and attacked, believing Brahe a common thief; both stopped when Sukhotin recognised a gauntlet that had belonged to Avrelian. This encounter, per the wiki, deepened Brahe's wish to leave something meaningful for those who need it. These details come from the wiki's Brahe page and were not all traced to in-game text.

## culture
### STANDARD
The Treasure Hoarders revere the legend of Reed Miller, and the Great Treasure is its crowning tale. Popular versions appear in The Legends of Reed Miller, Thief Extraordinaire. Tolfin estimates 20 percent of the legend comes from Alia, 30 percent from Brahe, and the rest is invention.
### LARGE +
Others have taken the name since, such as Netochka in The Shoemaker's Children Go Barefoot, and two petty thieves who tried to steal Tolfin's harp. Vaino, a former treasure hunter, jokes that he never found any of Reed Miller's old treasure.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
A theatrical puzzle-cave: riddling notices, scurrying weasels, coin-strewn passages and a playful but sober closing message. The flashback at the helm is solemn.

## dangers
### STANDARD
A Radiant Lunapod, Guard of Lost Treasures, attacks when the chest is opened. The doors can also be solved without combat.

## travel
### STANDARD
Begin The Raven's Legacy in Piramida with Tolfin, spar with Hiljetta, and bring the five Proofs of the Cognoscenti to the statue. Afterwards a teleport waypoint marks the entrance.

## quests
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Raven's Legacy (World Quest, final part of East of the Moon, West of the Sun): after Tolfin points the Traveler and Paimon to the vault, they solve the door puzzles, find the strange Iron Coins, defeat the guardian, and take the ship's helm. The flashback shows Relya and Silve, two thieves of the past, taking treasure from a governor and leaving a raven insignia in its place. The Traveler receives The First Iron Coin.
### LARGE +
Tolfin identifies the raven insignia as Avrelian's, derived from the family crest of his noble-born academy friend, and gives the Traveler an insignia as keepsake. The quest rewards the achievement East of the Moon, West of the Sun.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the vault is open to explore. The hoard of Iron Coins is still on the ship, and nothing after 7.1 is recorded.

## entry: Reed Miller
@keys: Reed Miller, Master Thief Reed Miller
@keys2: Nod-Krai, Treasure Hoarders, Great Treasure, Brahe
### STANDARD
In Nod-Krai folklore Reed Miller is the founder of the Treasure Hoarders and a master thief, but the name was an alias used by at least three people: Avrelian, Alia and Brahe. Brahe deliberately seeded contradictory legends, including the Great Treasure, so that those who might misuse the name would not be taken seriously.
### LARGE +
Others have since adopted the name for themselves or drawn on the stories. Popular accounts are collected in The Legends of Reed Miller, Thief Extraordinaire.

## entry: Brahe's Notes
@keys: Brahe's Notes, Brahe, Brahe the Bard, Proof of the Cognoscenti, Proofs of the Cognoscenti
@keys2: Reed Miller, Great Treasure, Weasel Thief
### STANDARD
Brahe's Notes are four writings found in the vault in which Brahe explains how he arranged the Iron Coins with the help of Weasel Thieves, how Avrelian and Silvestr once took his father's hidden funds, and why he felt compelled to cover Avrelian and Alia's legacy in harmless legends. He saw goodwill as wasted on poisoned soil, in a thought he attributes to Alia's gloomy sister Aila.
### LARGE +
Brahe says the Proofs of the Cognoscenti and the Great Treasure were meant to make the Reed Miller name generic and harmless; he writes that bandits were already misusing Avrelian's words within a decade of Alia's sacrifice.
