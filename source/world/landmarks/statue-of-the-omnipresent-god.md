## summary
### LITE
The Statue of the Omnipresent God is a vast statue built in front of Tenshukaku in Inazuma City as the "Symbol of Eternity". It was raised after the Vision Hunt Decree so that every confiscated Vision could be embedded in it. Its Visions were taken away and returned to their owners after the Traveler defeated Ei in Chapter II. Its earlier English name was the Statue of the Thousand-Armed, Hundred-Eyed God.
### STANDARD +
Ninety-nine Visions had been seized by the time of Amidst Stormy Judgment. Who the statue shows is never stated: many Inazumans simply call it the Shogun's statue.

## geography
### STANDARD
It stands before Tenshukaku, the Raiden Shogun's residence on Narukami Island,. Visions are inlaid across its body, including Arataki Itto's, set near the bottom just above a Pyro Vision whose owner is unknown. After Stillness, the Sublimation of Shadow, a Patrol Soldier stops anyone approaching it.
### LARGE +
Its base is damaged. When the Traveler first reaches Narukami Island the statue is still under construction, according to Thoma, and a teaser for the story shows it hauled from the water with ropes. Wings, hooded robes and a great necklace on the figure are what the debate over its identity leans on.

## history
### STANDARD
The Vision Hunt Decree was issued by the Raiden Shogun and the Shogunate about a year before the story, to seize Visions across Inazuma and inlay them in the statue; the Tenryou Commission carried it out. It began as a Fatui plot to cause unrest and create a market for Delusions; Kujou Takayuki agreed to help in exchange for their promises of support, and deceived the Shogun into continuing it. Ei, the true Shogun, knew of the Fatui involvement but approved, believing individual ambition would only lead to loss and suffering and was incompatible with her ideal of eternity. The Traveler challenged Ei in her realm by showing her the power of the seized Visions' aspirations, and Ei repealed the decree. The Visions were removed from the statue and returned to their owners.
### LARGE +
In a year 99 Visions were seized, and those who lost them suffered a loss of ambition, except Arataki Itto and Kuki Shinobu, who seemed unaffected. Those serving the Shogun, such as Kujou Sara, kept theirs. The war between the Shogunate and the Watatsumi Army grew from the decree and the Sakoku Decree issued at the same time.
### ULTRA +
The wiki notes the statue echoes the Great Buddha of the historic sword hunt, whose nails and clamps were forged from confiscated weapons. Arataki Itto's character story also refers to a Vision being inlaid on the Shogun's statue, and an NPC named Yamada speaks of a Vision that was meant to be part of the "Almighty Shogun's" statue.

## quests
### STANDARD
Stillness, the Sublimation of Shadow (Chapter II, Act II) is the quest in which the Vision Hunt Ceremony is the reason the decree is being carried out. In The Omnipresent God (Chapter II, Act III) and the quest Omnipresence Over Mortals, the Traveler defeats Ei and the Visions are returned to their rightful owners.
### LARGE +
The achievement The Aspirations of All refers to the statue as the symbol of Eternity, as does Thoma in Three Wishes.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
A towering, many-eyed figure over the capital, gleaming with stolen Visions in the early part of the story; after Chapter II its inlays are empty, a monument to a policy that was reversed.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the statue's Visions are gone: they were returned to their owners after Chapter II. The decree was repealed.
### STANDARD +
Its identity remains debated, and no 6.x or 7.x content on the wiki changes this.

## entry: Vision Hunt Decree
@keys: Vision Hunt Decree, Vision Hunt Ceremony, Megari-rei
@keys2: Inazuma, Statue of the Omnipresent God, Tenryou Commission
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
The decree ordered all Visions in Inazuma seized and inlaid in the Statue of the Omnipresent God. The Tenryou Commission enforced it. People who objected hid Visions or made decoys; many joined the Watatsumi Army. The decree is repealed in Chapter II, and the Visions are returned.

## entry: Debate over the Statue's Identity
@keys: Omnipresent God, Thousand-Armed Hundred-Eyed God, Symbol of Eternity
@keys2: Statue, Tenshukaku, Ronova, Shogun
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
Canon does not name the figure. Candidates include the Raiden Shogun (people say it is her statue; a quest description calls Ei the Omnipresent God), Ronova (the design shares her wings and hairstyle and is covered in eyes, and a text describes her as someone who sees, hears and judges all), the Heavenly Principles or Four Shades as a whole, or a personification of Eternity. Treat these as unproven.
