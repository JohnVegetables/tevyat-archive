## summary
### LITE
Chioriya Boutique is a fashion shop and label owned by Chiori, an independent designer from Inazuma, on a busy street in the Quartier Lyonnais of the Court of Fontaine. It looks small, but passers-by stop to look in, and its clothes have a good reputation. Its owner has a blunt, lightning-quick manner with troublemakers. The wiki also records a nickname for it, the 'intelligence boutique', because customers talk freely about their business there.
### STANDARD +
Chiori is rarely in the shop while she is designing, and when she is there it usually means she is in a good mood.

## geography
### STANDARD
The shop fronts one of the main thoroughfares of Quartier Lyonnais in the Court of Fontaine. A bell rings above the door when customers enter. The interior can be entered after starting the story quest Stained Spots.
### LARGE +
The sign uses Fontaine script. Before Chiori's story quest the sign read 'Couture' (the French word for dressmaking), and after The Woven and the Cut it reads 'Chiori', a sign put up by Kirara and Navia.

## places
### STANDARD
Inside, a table near the front door holds letters addressed to Chiori, and her work desk holds notes she has not sent.
### LARGE +
Letters on the table are from Emilie (thanks for fragrance feedback, plus a sample of a Romaritime scent), Navia (an invitation to a Spina di Rosula party, asking that Eloffe be brought along), Kirara (a customer asking for a child's outfit, with sizes) and Chevreuse (a request to watch a judge's wife, who shops there). On the desk are Chiori's travel plans, noting a new clothing trend in Natlan and Snezhnayan winter-wear designs for reference, a letter to Chevreuse about a Maison Gardiennage employee overheard discussing a deal, and a letter to Charlotte about decorations for Monsieur Vérité that politely refuses interviews over tea.

## people
### STANDARD
Chiori owns the shop and designs the clothes. The staff the wiki names are Eloffe, Graciette, Trudaine and, inside, Laetitia.
### LARGE +
Friends who write to her include Navia, Kirara, Emilie, Chevreuse and Charlotte.

## culture
### STANDARD
The shop is a social and fashion hub. Well-dressed merchants and officials come to show off what they know as much as what they wear, and Chiori's profile treats the intelligence as something that arrives on its own rather than something she hunts for.
### LARGE +
Chiori then decided to pass on hints to friends in the form of letters or a quiet word when someone's interests or Fontaine's safety are involved. She compares this to tailoring: placing each thread where it belongs. The brand itself grew from her wish to make a name in Fontaine, a name she first tried out in Inazuma while turning down a place at Ogura Textiles & Kimonos.

### ULTRA +
Chiori's profile adds that she draws her designs casually, brings the drafts to the boutique, copies them onto toile, and then the originals end up as tea-stained wrapping paper for taiyaki. The drafts are for customers to admire and for fashion magazines. The brand name itself came to her in Inazuma, when an offer from Ogura Textiles & Kimonos made her realise she did not want to work under someone else's shop name.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Rowdy drunks sometimes burst in, as the profile's opening shows, and Chiori deals with them herself.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
A modest-looking shop on a busy street. The door bell chimes for each customer, the greeting is confident if not exactly enthusiastic, and customers speak admiringly of the handiwork.

## travel
### STANDARD
The boutique is a marked point of interest in Quartier Lyonnais in the Court of Fontaine.

## quests
### STANDARD
Chiori's story quest, When They Talk About Tonight (Cisoria Chapter Act I), runs through the shop in Stained Spots and The Woven and the Cut (4.5).

## status

Open and trading as of 7.1, as far as the sources show.

## entry: The Intelligence Boutique
@keys: intelligence boutique
@keys2: Chiori, Chioriya
@version: 4.5
### LARGE
Chiori's profile says that no one is sure when the shop gained the nickname 'intelligence boutique', and Chiori is as puzzled as anyone. She came to see that information flows through her door of its own accord, because well-dressed merchants and officials boast about what they know. She finds it distracting, but she began to pass on warnings as a favour. Letters on her table show the same pattern: Chevreuse, a law-enforcement friend, asks her to watch for a regular customer, and Chiori writes back about overheard talk of a deal.
