## summary
### LITE
The Baiju Guesthouse is an inn near the entrance to Liyue Harbor, where the waitress Xiuhua greets guests asking whether they want a meal or a room for the night. It is a modest, ordinary lodging house rather than a grand establishment.
### STANDARD +
Its name means roughly "White Horse Guesthouse" in Chinese. It is one of the places in the Harbor where travellers can stay, alongside the better-known Wangshu Inn outside the city.

## people
### STANDARD
Xiuhua is the guesthouse's serving girl; by her own account she is just one of the service staff and "nothing special". She has a twin sister, Yuhua, who waits tables at the Wangshu Inn, and the two sometimes swap places at their respective inns, so a guest may meet one while expecting the other.
### LARGE +
The wiki attributes the guesthouse to Lu Weng, a Liyue Harbor local; see the entry on ownership below. The Wangshu Inn's bulletin board mentions that a Prince has booked a room at the guesthouse, a passing detail with no follow-up.

## status
### LITE
Open and operating as of 7.1.
### STANDARD +
Zibai, the White Horse Adeptus, took lodgings here after her soul was restored during the Lantern Rite event story, and told the Traveler she would live there for a while.

## entry: Zibai's lodging at the Baiju Guesthouse
@keys: Baiju Guesthouse, White Horse Guesthouse
@keys2: Zibai, Lu Weng, Lu Xiangxiang, Lantern Rite
@version: 6.3
@availability: limited
### LARGE
In the 6.3 Lantern Rite event story "A Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon" (epilogue "White Horse's Leisurely Journey"), Zibai said she would stay at the Baiju Guesthouse for a while, and the old man Lu Weng later confirmed she holds a long-term booking; Zhongli had arranged for her expenses to be charged to the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor. Lu Weng noticed she resembled the pictures of the White Horse Adeptus in his family's history, since both have golden eyes, and she answered that fate may have had a hand in it. Lu Weng gave her tea from his village garden as a welcome gift.
### ULTRA +
Earlier in the epilogue, Zibai remarked that the inn's new proprietor felt he could not compete with the Wangshu Inn and was thinking of renaming the guesthouse; Paimon joked he should call it "Rex's Rest". Lu Weng, who prays to the White Horse Adeptus, later says his son came to his senses and kept the old name, and credits the Adeptus with his granddaughter's overnight-grown tea tree. Zibai does not correct him; she urges him to record the miracle in the family history. This was an event scene: it is canon but not something every Liyue resident knows.

## entry: Ownership of the Baiju Guesthouse
@keys: Baiju Guesthouse owner, Baiju Guesthouse proprietor
@spoiler: minor
### ULTRA
Sources disagree on who runs the inn. The wiki lists Lu Weng as owner, while the 6.3 event dialogue calls Lu Xiangxiang's father the "new proprietor" who nearly renamed it, and Lu Weng calls that man his son. A safe portrayal: the Lu family runs the guesthouse, with Lu Weng the elder patriarch and his son now in charge of day-to-day decisions; do not state more than that as fact.
