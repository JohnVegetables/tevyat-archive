## summary
### LITE
Mouun Shrine is a shrine in the west of Watatsumi Island, the Inazuman island ruled by the Sangonomiya Shrine and the Watatsumi people. It holds an Offering Pool in which, locals say, the wishes of the faithful collect and dissolve into the water, and it is the place where Tsuyuko, a Sangonomiya shrine maiden, first asks the Traveler for help in "The Moon-Bathed Deep". Its name may honour Mouun, a shrine maiden of Watatsumi legend.
### STANDARD +
The old shrine buildings are partly broken, but the site is a high spot overlooking the sea, and Komaki of Bourou Village points to it as the island's lucky high place where fortune is found. A Statue of The Seven stands there, and the wiki lists Kumi, Anisa and Kaushik among its NPCs.

## geography
### STANDARD
Mouun Shrine sits at the west of Watatsumi Island. Its features include a Statue of The Seven, a Waverider Waypoint, Electro Seelie and Electric Lamps, Lightning Strike Probes, a Mysterious Pillar puzzle, Thunder Barriers and Thunder Sakura Boughs. Local specialties include Sea Ganoderma. The wiki suggests it was inspired by the real Naminoue Shrine in Okinawa, a cliff-top shrine overlooking the ocean; this is design inspiration, not canon.
### LARGE +
One puzzle there uses three Lightning Strike Probes. The player interacts with the Mysterious Pillar, an Electro Seelie above the pillar and two others orient the probes, and following the first Seelie leads to a chest. Paimon wonders whether these Electro Seelie are here to protect or to watch the place.

## places
### STANDARD
The Offering Pool is the shrine's best-known feature. Komaki says that everyone's wishes accumulate in the pool and dissolve into the water, like honey: no one can see them, but they can be felt in the water's sweetness and viscosity. She says the wishes' power starts to dissipate when water leaves the pool. During the world quest "Divine Plant of the Depths", the Traveler draws water here and carries it back to Bourou Village within two minutes to feed the Spiritherbs there.
### LARGE +
After the quest, Komaki's "Fortune of the Day" hint says that luck lies at the highest point of Watatsumi Island, in an ancient place dedicated to the gods, which leads to the shrine. Inside the broken temple, an Electro Seelie lights an Electric Lamp, then three more Seelie must be touched, two on a torii gate and one on top of the temple, to open a chest.

## people
### STANDARD
Kumi, Anisa and Kaushik are listed at the shrine. Kumi, who moved from Narukami Island to Bourou Village and mourned her friend, the umibouzu Kuntira, now shares her home with the researcher Anisa Purbiruni, a trainee Dastur from the Sumeru Akademiya who studies marine life and the island's puzzles. Kaushik, another Sumeru scholar who has stayed since before the Sakoku Decree, researches Watatsumi culture and history.
### LARGE +
Kaushik's pet theory, drawn from a totem he dug up, is that ancient Inazuma had two snake gods: one fixed in coral and forming the Sangonomiya Shrine, and another that tried to invade Narukami Island and was cut down by the Electro Archon. He also claims there are five Four Winds in Mondstadt, and says the shrine maiden Mochizuki is blinded by faith for rejecting his conclusions. These are his claims; the wiki treats them as wrong. Tsuyuko, the shrine maiden at the quest start, says she gave guided tours to visitors in the past and that she looks at folklore critically.

## history
### STANDARD
The shrine's own history is not told. Its name echoes Mouun, a shrine maiden of the Yuna Clan, a master of whalesong who, with her twin sister Ayame, led the first Sangonomiya navy and befriended the giant blind whale Daikengyou. She fought beside Watatsumi Omikami (Orobashi) in the war against the Shogunate and was killed in an ambush by the subordinates of the tengu Sasayuri, together with Daikengyou.
### LARGE +
The book "The Life of Mouun the Shrine Maiden", found in Bourou Village, says her family was one of the great houses that first followed their Omikami into the sunlight, and that she was known for gentleness and for steering people away from pointless conflict. The wiki names her as the original wielder of Mouun's Moon.

### ULTRA +
According to the book, Daikengyou was a blind whale of great age that commanded narwhal bodyguards and humpback whale musicians, with deep-sea fish and moon-bright jellyfish as servants. At the Divine Priestess's direction Mouun dived into its palace; on the third moonrise the people saw it surface bearing her on its back. Nobody knew how she persuaded it. After the battle went badly, the Shogunate captured the remains of Mouun and the whale.

## quests
### STANDARD
The Moon-Bathed Deep begins here when the Traveler talks to Tsuyuko, who asks for help with Watatsumi Island's "Holy Soil" problem. The commission "Operation Thunderous Ignition" has a Watatsumi version just north of the shrine, and the Offering Pool figures in "Divine Plant of the Depths".
### LARGE +
Tsuyuko explains that tradition requires diving to Enkanomiya every few centuries, when the spirit veins flow smoothly, to retrieve a Jade Coral Branch left by the Omikami and use it for a ritual that stops the soil from bleaching. She sends the Traveler first to the Goshou Rocks, five nodes of the barrier between Sangonomiya and Enkanomiya.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Wooden Shield Hilichurl Guards, Hydro Samachurls, Geo and Hydro Specters, Electro Abyss Mages, Thunderhelm Lawachurls, Crackling Axe Mitachurls, Ruin Destroyers and Ruin Scouts roam the area. Treasure Hoarders appear in the Operation Thunderous Ignition commission.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Sea air, torii gates and a broken temple on a high cliff, with Electro Seelie drifting about. It feels solemn but faintly playful: a place of prayer where small wishes are said to settle in the water.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the shrine is open to visitors with its Statue of The Seven and Offering Pool; the wiki records no change since 2.1.

## entry: The Offering Pool and the Spiritherbs
@keys: Offering Pool, Spiritherb, Divine Plant of the Depths, Water Welling With Wishes
@keys2: Komaki, Mouun Shrine, Bourou Village
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
The Spiritherbs of Bourou Village are long-lived plants that give off a gentle light because they lived in darkness. Komaki uses their glow to judge whether wishes will come true, and says they need water from the Offering Pool at the island's shrine because it holds "wishes" that are lost soon after leaving the pool. The younger shrine maidens doubt the divination and refuse to help with it, which Komaki calls the wisdom of the old that cannot be reduced to theory.

## entry: Mouun, the Whalesong Shrine Maiden
@keys: Mouun, Yuna Mouun, Daikengyou, Umigozen Ayame
@keys2: Watatsumi, shrine maiden, whale
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Mouun was a Yuna Clan shrine maiden from Watatsumi Island who learned the rituals, history, governance and island folk-tunes from the Divine Priestess and became a master of whalesong. She persuaded the great whale Daikengyou to join her, and with her sister Ayame, the diver called Umigozen, she led Sangonomiya's first navy. She was killed in an ambush by Sasayuri's subordinates while retreating after Omikami and Touzannou fell; Ayame vanished into the blood-red sea. Her full entry is her own page; the shrine's name may honour her but this is not stated.
