## summary
### LITE
The Yashiro Commission Headquarters is the working seat of the Yashiro Commission, the branch of Inazuma's Tri-Commission that manages shrines, festivals and cultural events. It occupies the main building of the Kamisato Estate in Inazuma City. The Kamisato Clan leads the Commission: Kamisato Ayato is clan head and Yashiro Commissioner, and his younger sister Kamisato Ayaka, the "Shirasagi Himegimi", is the public face loved by the people. The Traveler first meets Ayaka here, and Thoma, her attendant, is nearly always at hand.
### STANDARD +
The Yashiro Commission is regarded as the least controversial of the three Commissions, since its duties are civic and ceremonial rather than military or financial.

## geography
### STANDARD
The headquarters is inside the main building of the Kamisato Estate and is marked on the map with the Estate's own icon. It is an enterable interior. A bookcase in the left-hand room holds volumes of A Legend of Sword (Vol. V), New Chronicles of the Six Kitsune (Vol. III) and Princess Mina of the Fallen Nation (Vol. V).
### LARGE +
Furnishings sold in game refer to its rooms: the "Yumemiru Decorum" kimono rack, which holds the robe Ayaka wears when practising calligraphy, and the maple-wood bookcases "Inkheart" and "Trove of a Thousand Tomes". In the 5.7 anecdote "Kamisato Ayato: Business", freshly cut bolts of fabric can be inspected inside it.

## people
### STANDARD
Kamisato Ayato is the Commissioner; Ayaka, the Commission's public figure, lives in the Estate; Thoma serves as her attendant and a member of staff. The wiki also lists the Commission's representatives Hirano, Hirotatsu, Madarame Hyakubei, Takami, Furusawa, Kazumasa and Ogawa. Soldiers of the Shogun's Army assigned to the Commission are relaxed compared with those at the Kanjou and Tenryou Commissions.
### LARGE +
The Commission also owns the members-only Komore Teahouse, which the Kamisato Clan uses as a local base and meeting place (staff: Taroumaru the boss, Kozue, Nobumori and Nakanishi), and commands the Shuumatsuban, a clandestine ninja group used for gathering information, which includes Sayu. Takami manages festivals and the Naganohara Fireworks Show on Amakane Island for the Commission.

## governance
### STANDARD
The Yashiro Commission is one of three Commissions under the Raiden Shogun, with the Kanjou (finances, borders) and Tenryou (security, military) Commissions. It manages shrines, festivals and cultural events, so it has less influence than those two on paper. All three share equal authority and can check each other. A map text on the Estate says its duties are so pervasive that even Shogunate officials cannot make sense of them.
### LARGE +
The Commission is the only one of the three that stayed out of the Fatui's dealings during the Vision Hunt and Sakoku Decrees, and Ayato turned down the Fatui's offer. As a result the events of the Inazuma Archon Quests largely left it unaffected, and it gained slightly more influence and sympathy afterwards. In the 5.7 anecdote Ayato says the Commission's choices of textile patterns draw public scrutiny and that merchants advertise "Yashiro Commission-approved" labels after its annual procurements; he now oversees such purchases personally after a delegate was once offered bribes.
### ULTRA +
Ayaka explains during the Vision Hunt Decree that the Commission serves the Shogun but is close to the people because of the contact its duties require, and that a Commission's power rises and falls with the trust of its people, so it cannot stay indifferent to the decree's effects.

## culture
### STANDARD
Receiving guests is ritualised. Ayaka first receives the Traveler from behind a screen, which Thoma calls a time-honoured tradition within the Yashiro Commission. She puts the screen aside only for those she considers friends.
### LARGE +
Ayato also receives visitors from behind a curtain before stepping out. The Commission treats official functions as matters of detail: even fabric colours are chosen with care, and weddings between commission families are treated as affairs of state.

## history
### STANDARD
In Three Wishes (Chapter II Act I, 2.0) Thoma brings the Traveler to the Estate, where Ayaka, behind the screen, asks for help against the Vision Hunt Decree and offers an introduction to the Raiden Shogun on one condition: that the Traveler fulfil three small wishes for people who lost their Visions. In A Flower Blooms in a Prison, the Traveler returns to the headquarters, Ayaka steps from behind the screen, declares the Traveler a friend, and dispenses with the screen for Thoma too.
### LARGE +
In New Shoots from Old Branches (Cypressus Custos Chapter Act I, 2.6) the Traveler enters the headquarters, finds Thoma studying a chessboard and meets Ayato, who emerges from behind a curtain and receives the Traveler in Ayaka's absence. The two discuss the coming marriage of the Kujou and Hiiragi heads, a matter that concerns the Commission because it handles ceremonial affairs.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Quiet, formal and refined: a manor interior of bookcases, screens and curtains, where visitors are expected to be courteous and conversations carry weight. Thoma's easy friendliness softens it.

## quests
### STANDARD
Appears in Three Wishes and A Flower Blooms in a Prison (Chapter II Act I), in New Shoots from Old Branches (2.6), and in the 5.7 anecdote "Kamisato Ayato: Business".

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the headquarters remains the Yashiro Commission's seat. Ayato and Ayaka are its leading figures, Thoma serves there, and the Commission has the most public goodwill of the three. No content from 5.x to 7.1 changes it.

### STANDARD
Ayaka meets visitors behind a screen as the Yashiro Commission's daughter, so the public sees her only through her reputation as Shirasagi Himegimi. The Traveler's help with the three Visionless people persuades her to appear in person. Thoma, whose friendship with Ayaka predates his post as attendant, notes that she dispenses with the screen for him and for those she calls friends. When she steps forward she is warm, a little amused by Paimon, and surprisingly different from her formal manner behind it.
