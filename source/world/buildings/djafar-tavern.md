## summary
### LITE
Djafar Tavern is the main place to eat and drink in Port Ormos, Sumeru's big harbour. Its owner Eymen tends the bar, promising the best wine, food and service and treating every customer like family. Merchants, tourists, sailors and former Eremite mercenaries mix here. In the Archon Quest it is where Ayn Al-Ahmar members, devotees of King Deshret, sold false information to would-be buyers.
### STANDARD +
The staff are Eymen (bartender and owner) and two hostesses, Nesmil and Roshana, who both used to be Eremites.

## people
### STANDARD
Eymen: bartender and owner. He was a travelling merchant in the desert until he made enough money to open the place. Roshana says he does not want to offend customers and will try to meet any demand. Nesmil: cheerful hostess who talks up the tavern's wine and appetizers; a former Eremite mercenary who says she left them for the tavern because of Roshana, and that she enjoys more freedom here than she would freelancing for the Akademiya. Roshana: dry, unfussy hostess who does not make cocktails or wait on customers ("talk to the bartender"), another ex-Eremite who prefers this to dealing with stiff researchers. Other people seen here include the merchants Abbanes and Behrouz and the researcher Parisa; at night the Mahamata Mahshid and the Eremite mercenary Matari are also present.
### LARGE +
Roshana jokes that a stronger drink is enough to make researchers drop their rules. Nesmil boasts of her mercenary past but admits sea monsters are too tough to punch, and says she could handle drunks easily. Eymen's idle thoughts wonder whether he should start something more profitable.

## quests
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
In Lost in Prosperity (Chapter III Act I), Akademiya students in Port Ormos say that the Eremite group Ayn Al-Ahmar likes to set up shop at Djafar Tavern and will sell information on anything for half a million Mora. The Traveler and Paimon go there and Eymen shows them to a table. Ayn Al-Ahmar men (Tariq and Tumart) talk openly about reclaiming King Deshret's power. Tariq takes 500,000 Mora and hands over an address, then explains the group's faith in King Deshret and their grievance against Greater Lord Rukkhadevata. Alhaitham interrupts and calls Tariq a fraud: the address is fake, and the group lures people with promises of information so they can squeeze them for money.
### LARGE +
After completing that quest Eymen drops his pretence when the Traveler returns: he admits he helped the outlaws because Eremites looked after him in the desert, says he now has nothing to do with them, and offers wine or, failing that, a sword as an apology (the Traveler receives the sword Harbinger of Dawn). The tavern serves only as a meeting point in the story; the quest goes on elsewhere in the port.

## event
@availability: limited
@version: 5.1
### LARGE
During the Sabzeruz Festival event Chromatic Ode of Candies and Roses, Nesmil offers Sabzeruz special sweet wine to customers, and the parade's Flower Carriage is kept near the tavern at the end of its route. Roshana points out that the carriage has nothing to do with the tavern, though many first-time tourists ask about it as if it did.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Noisy and cosmopolitan: sailors and merchants from many lands, mercenaries off duty, and a bartender quick to make everyone feel welcome. Business of all sorts, including dubious business, is done at its tables.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 Djafar Tavern is open and unchanged.
### ULTRA +
No story content after the Chapter III quest alters the tavern. Eymen is shown running it in later event dialogue too.
