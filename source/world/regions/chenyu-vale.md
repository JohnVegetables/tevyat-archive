## summary
### LITE
Chenyu Vale is the fog-wrapped mountain country of western Liyue, on the border with Fontaine, famous for its tea. Its people live in Qiaoying Village, the tea-making heart of the vale, and trade through Yilong Wharf, a busy river port that Fontainians visit for a taste of Liyue. Long ago a nameless goddess ruled the vale, and three adepti served her: the white snake Herblord, the golden carp Fujin and the suanni Lingyuan. Legends of jade sunk in the river give the vale its name.
### STANDARD +
The vale has three areas: the Upper Vale (the village, the wharf and the Bishui River), the Southern Mountain (old adeptal and ancient sites around Mt. Yaojun), and Mt. Laixin, the sacred mountain where the vale's spirit veins meet. The first settlers came from the Chasm, and their ancestors were refugees of the ancient civilisation of Lang-Gan.

## geography
### STANDARD
Steep, misty karst peaks, terraced tea slopes, waterfalls and jade-green rivers. The Bishui River starts near Yilong Wharf in the northwest, flows through the vale past Qiaoying Village, then narrows and drops by a waterfall into central Liyue. The climate is noticeably more humid than in the Hills of Barsom nearby. Mt. Mingyuan, west of Yilong Wharf, lies directly beneath the floating island of Celestia, which hangs overhead.
### LARGE +
Upper Vale: Qiaoying Village, Yilong Wharf, Mt. Lingmeng (west of the village and southeast of the wharf), Mt. Mingyuan, and the Jademouth, huge slabs of jade that flank the river. Southern Mountain: Teatree Slope south of the village, Yaodie Valley (the "valley of vibrant butterflies") with the Herblord's old Lingshu Courtyard, the ruined Chizhang Wall, and Mt. Xuanlian further south. Mt. Laixin holds Chiwang Terrace and Fujin's hidden abode, Carp's Rest.

## places
### STANDARD
- **Qiaoying Village**: the tea village, sending its leaves to Liyue Harbor and to other nations.
- **Yilong Wharf** (also Yilong Port): a trading port across the water from Fontaine.
- **Jademouth**: giant jade slabs in the Bishui River. Legend says an adeptus threw them down to stop a flood.
- **Mt. Laixin**: the sacred mountain. Whoever controls it controls all the land of the vale.
### LARGE +
- **Chiwang Terrace** (Mt. Laixin): an ancient altar where, by local tradition, the vale's ancestors spoke with the heavens.
- **Carp's Rest**: Fujin's abode, holding the Votive Rainjade.
- **Lingshu Courtyard** (Yaodie Valley): the Herblord's former home. Only empty medicine jars remain.
- **Teatree Slope**: home of the vale's first tea tree, which Fujin planted.
- **Chizhang Wall**: ruins of Chizhang, the ancient city the Mistress governed. Murals and ancestral records survive there, and a wild Solitary Suanni roams nearby.

## people
### STANDARD
Tea growers, roasters and merchants. Grandpa Lu, a village elder, and Uncle Luo, who oversees a tea workshop, speak for Qiaoying, and Jin works at the Chunming Teaworks. The adeptus Fujin and the suanni Lingyuan (nicknamed "Bluey" by the local boy Little Mao) still live in the vale. Yilong Wharf has Fontainian tea merchants, a Millelith sergeant, hired guards and boatmen.
### LARGE +
Lan Yan, a master rattan weaver of the Chenyu Vale Artisans Association, comes from the vale; her family's ancestors lived near the Chasm. Yelan says the Chasm was once home to the people of present-day Chenyu Vale. Hautemontagne, a Fontainian technician, services the village's tea machinery. Lu Xiangxiang, from a family with tea fields in Qiaoying Village and a guesthouse in Liyue Harbor, joined a dig in the Chasm that turned up Lang-Gan relics (6.3).

## governance
### LARGE
No local lord or office is named; the vale is part of Liyue. Village affairs fall to elders and workshop heads. The Millelith keep a sergeant and guards at Yilong Wharf, and the Feiyun Commerce Guild trades in Qiaoying tea (villagers once mistook the Traveler for a Guild envoy). In the ancient past the Mistress of Chenyu Vale ruled from Chizhang, settling disputes between mortals and the wild and controlling the Bishui River.

## economy
### STANDARD
Tea is everything. Qiaoying's tea is sold in Liyue Harbor and abroad. Fontaine imports it through Lumidouce Harbor, and Fontainian tea merchants trade at Yilong Wharf. People believe the adepti blessed the vale's land and water. A special cauldron in the village, said to be an adeptal mechanism, roasts the leaves and gives them their aroma.
### LARGE +
Named teas include Dipped Dew, Tianheng Bohea, Qiaoying floral tea, Sunglo Tea and Single-Origin Yauwan Tea. Every year the village holds a tea-serving ceremony and brews floral tea from the wilted petals of Spiritscent Flowers. The vale's original tea tree is said to grow well only here, because it "remembers" a promise between two friends. Artisans at Yilong Wharf still make simple jade ornaments, and Yilong jade, blue-green and lustrous, still turns up in ruins across Liyue. Clearwater Jade grows in the vale. The Bishui River has good water transport between Qiaoying and the wharf.

## culture
### STANDARD
Jade is sacred here. The vale's ancestors made offerings by throwing jade into the water, believing this sent adeptal energy into the spirit veins, protected their homeland and brought good harvests. The old rites were later forgotten. Families go to Yilong Wharf to honour their ancestors and the dead, and the custom of throwing jade into the water continued even after the old goddess fell and Morax came.
### LARGE +
Many legends tell of a slab of jade on a sacred mountain that could summon "sweet rains". Demons coveted it, so it was thrown into the river. In other versions, the Herblord split the jade into ornaments and hid them in rivers, mountains and shrines, and one priestly family still keeps a jade loop said to carry an ancestral pact with a deity. A loading screen says most people read "Yilong" as "the jade left behind", while some scholars think it is a miscopying of "dragon remnants", a nod to the dangerous mountains.
### ULTRA +
In Chenyu Vale an old myth tells of a giant toad in the sky that eats the moon, and some of the vale's tribes call the White Horse Adeptus the Daughter of Time. Locals once sang a lament for the forgotten Mistress, about a lost chariot in a dark bamboo wood and Xuanwen beasts wailing over a black pool. Qiaoying's buildings follow Hui-style designs.

## history
### STANDARD
When a Celestial Nail destroyed Lang-Gan (in what is now the Chasm), survivors fled and founded the first settlements here. The wiki dates the Chasm's creation to about 6,000 years ago. Fujin, a golden carp adeptus, guided them, taught them jade rites, reshaped soil and water for farming, and planted the first tea tree on the Herblord's land. In the Archon War the Mistress of Chenyu Vale fought Morax. Driven mad, she finally raised a flood down the Bishui River that would have drowned her own people. Her three adepti rebelled. Lingyuan led the people to safety, the Herblord fought the goddess, and Fujin climbed Mt. Lingmeng and threw the Votive Rainjade into the river, creating the Jademouth. The Mistress was sealed away, and Morax brought peace to the vale.
### LARGE +
All three adepti paid for it. The Herblord was torn apart and survived only as a tiny white snake that could no longer take human form. Fujin "sank into the sea" and lay weakened at Carp's Rest for thousands of years. Lingyuan returned to the wilds. In recent times Lingyuan, missing the old days, tampered with the spirit veins so the vale would "return to nature". Qiaoying's tea went bad and miasma broke out. With the Traveler's help, Fujin performed the Rainjade Rite and restored the land, but the effort forced her back into carp form (4.4, *Chenyu's Blessings of Sunken Jade*).
### ULTRA +
Ziqiao, a soul-fragment of Zibai who took up arms for Morax under contract, recalls that the two of them first subdued Chenyu Vale and then brought peace to Mt. Qingce. Before the Archon War, Fujin spent time with the southern adepti such as Madame Ping and Cloud Retainer, who once sent her a teapot realm for a trip that never happened. The desperate doctor who sought the Herblord here found only the weakened snake, and her healing contract passed down to Baizhu.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
The scent of roasting tea everywhere, mist curling around jagged peaks, river boats and teahouse chatter at the wharf, and mossy shrines and jade ruins in the hills. Fontainian visitors mingle with locals at Yilong Wharf. Up in the Southern Mountain and on Mt. Laixin it feels older, quieter and more numinous.

## dangers
### LARGE
Lingering miasma (Fujin could not clear it all), Treasure Hoarder camps, steep cliffs and waterfalls, and the territorial Solitary Suanni around Chizhang Wall. A notebook found at a Treasure Hoarder camp describes smuggling: valuable goods hidden under layers of tea and porcelain and handed to Fontainians of the "Pinky Club" on a beach.

## travel
### LARGE
The Bishui River links the vale to central Liyue, dropping by a waterfall on the way down. Boatmen carry passengers along the Bishui between Qiaoying and Yilong Wharf. Yilong Wharf faces Fontaine across the water and sits southeast of Lumidouce Harbor, which makes it a natural meeting point for Fontainian traders.

## quests
### LARGE
- *Chenyu's Blessings of Sunken Jade* (4.4): Fujin, Lingyuan and the Rainjade Rite.
- *The Cloud-Padded Path to the Chiwang Repose* and *Rainjade's Benediction*: open Carp's Rest and the offerings that restore Fujin.
- *A Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon* (6.3): ties the vale and Chiwang Terrace to Lang-Gan and Zibai.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the vale is peaceful and its tea trade thriving. The Traveler and Fujin repaired the damage Lingyuan did, though some miasma remains.
### STANDARD +
Fujin is recovering at Carp's Rest. With enough of her power restored she can take human form again, and she and Lingyuan are friends once more. The Herblord lives on as Changsheng, the snake who travels with Baizhu in Liyue Harbor. The 7.1 Celestial Nail anomaly centres on the neighbouring Chasm, not on the vale.

## entry: Qiaoying Village
@keys: Qiaoying Village, Qiaoying, Chunming Teaworks, Grandpa Lu, Uncle Luo
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
Qiaoying Village is the tea village of Chenyu Vale's Upper Vale, known across Teyvat for its tea. The smell of tea fills its peaceful streets. Grandpa Lu blamed the vale's soil and water when the tea declined. Uncle Luo oversees a workshop where Jin operates the machines, and the Fontainian Hautemontagne maintains them. The village roasts its leaves in an old cauldron said to be an adeptal mechanism, and every year it holds a tea-serving ceremony with floral tea brewed from Spiritscent Flower petals.
### LARGE +
Before 4.4 the village's tea had begun to taste "off". The cause was Lingyuan disturbing the spirit veins, and the tea recovered after Fujin's rite, in time for Lantern Rite. Zhiqiong, the cartographer, once mapped the village for a building project that was later shelved. Zhongli once collected tea seed oil there for Hu Tao's Lantern Rite plans.

## entry: Yilong Wharf
@keys: Yilong Wharf, Yilong Port, Yilong jade
@version: 4.4
### STANDARD
Yilong Wharf is a thriving trading port on the Fontaine border, southeast of Lumidouce Harbor. Fontainians come here to experience Liyue culture without travelling to Liyue Harbor. It has an upper level of teahouses (the Jiulue Teahouse), tea merchants, a storyteller and a Millelith post, and a lower level of street food, anglers and a hydrologist. Families come here to honour their ancestors, and local artisans still make jade ornaments.

## entry: The Mistress of Chenyu Vale and her three adepti
@keys: Mistress of Chenyu Vale, Herblord, Fujin, Lingyuan, Votive Rainjade, Rainjade Rite, Jademouth
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Mistress of Chenyu Vale was an ancient jade goddess whose true name is forgotten. She was among the first gods worshipped by the people of old Liyue. She ruled the beasts, spirits and adepti of the wild mountains, judged between mortals and nature, and controlled the Bishui River. Fujin says she was not evil and made many dreams come true, until the Archon War drove her mad in her bid to become an Archon. Her adepti were the Herblord, a white snake who took human form and healed the sick; Fujin, a golden carp devoted to humans and their tea; and Lingyuan, a wild suanni who never warmed to people. When the Mistress unleashed the flood, they turned against her and saved the vale.
### LARGE +
The Herblord later became Changsheng, the snake who bound her healing contract to a line of doctors ending with Baizhu. Fujin confirmed this when the Traveler told her about Liyue Harbor. Lingyuan, called "Beastlord of the Sunken Jade Realm", has sworn never to take human form again. She is still trying to decide what she thinks of humans, as she promised the Traveler she would.
### ULTRA +
At the 6.3 Lantern Rite, Lingyuan teased Fujin for avoiding the White Horse Adeptus, who had returned to Chiwang Terrace. Fujin, in carp form, pretended to be an ordinary fish because she feared being asked about their old lord.

## entry: Lang-Gan's heirs and Chiwang Terrace
@keys: Chiwang Terrace, Chiwang, Records of the Ancestors
@keys2: Chenyu Vale, Lang-Gan, Zibai, White Horse Adeptus
@version: 6.3
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Chiwang Terrace on Mt. Laixin is a copy of the jade altar where Lang-Gan honoured the Three Moons and its guardian, the angel Zibai. Survivors rebuilt it in Chenyu Vale after Zibai gave her life holding back the Celestial Nail. Lan Yan knows it as the place where the vale's ancestors communicated with the heavens. In 6.3, a photo of the moon vanishing above the terrace and Lang-Gan relics dug up in the Chasm drew the Traveler into the Lantern Rite events that restored Zibai. Fragmentary "Records of the Ancestors" at Chizhang Wall tell of villagers saved from beasts by a beautiful adeptus, of jade divided and offered at Chiwang "that the heavens may hear", and of a courtyard sealed on an adeptus's orders.
### ULTRA +
After her restoration, Zibai toured modern Liyue with the Traveler and Paimon, going from Liyue Harbor to Yilong Wharf and on to Qiaoying Village, where they stayed the night with the Lu family. At the 6.3 Lantern Rite, Zhongli showed a silk painting of Zhanzhu the Moon-Devourer. He said the work was discovered in Chenyu Vale many years ago, though its cinnabar and azurite pigments suggest it came from the Chasm, and he later admitted his piece was a copy he had made of the original.

## entry: Southern Mountain and Mt. Yaojun
@keys: Southern Mountain, Mt. Yaojun, Lingshu Courtyard, Yaodie Valley, Teatree Slope, Chizhang Wall, Chizhang
@version: 4.4
### LARGE
The Southern Mountain is the wilder, older half of the vale. Baizhu's story places Chenyu Vale around Mt. Yaojun, the sacred mountain of the Herblord. Her home, Lingshu Courtyard in Yaodie Valley, stands empty but for medicine jars, and Teatree Slope next to it still holds the first tea tree Fujin planted on the Herblord's land. The two adepti once agreed to hold a tea banquet for all the adepti, which never happened. Changsheng has said that if her contract ended she would sleep out her last days on Mt. Yaojun. Chizhang Wall preserves the ruins and murals of the Mistress's city.

@keys: Gate of Life, Qimen Arts, Rite of Homa

@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
