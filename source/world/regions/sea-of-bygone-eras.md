## summary
### LITE
The Sea of Bygone Eras is a stretch of Fontaine's waters, too deep for sunlight to reach, where the capital of ancient Remuria lies in ruins on the sea floor. It is reached through the sunken Faded Castle in the Nostoi Region and has its own map. Its ruins include the warrior city Machimos, the gated inner city Capitolium and, on the hill at its heart, the golden palace Domus Aurea, where the God King Remus once tended the "symphony" that ruled his empire.
### STANDARD +
Before it sank, this water was part of the Great Fontaine Lake. In the 4.6 world quests (Canticles of Harmony) the Traveler, the spirit of the former Harmost Cassiodor and the freed vishap prince Scylla went down into the ruins and destroyed Phobos, the Grand Symphony, which had trapped Remurian souls for millennia.

## geography
### STANDARD
A dark sea floor of coral reefs, rock towers, caverns and broken stonework, crossed by currents. Machimos, the outer city, makes up the larger, outer part of the ruins: the tall rock formations there are probably what is left of it. The only way from Machimos into Capitolium is the Initium Iani gate, which opens onto the Passage of Ianus, an underwater corridor with a current running its whole length. Capitolium contains Clivus Capitolinus (the hill of Domus Aurea), the harp-shaped aqueduct Alta Semita and the Collegium Phonascorum.
### LARGE +
Event-menu text describes the waters as lying south of a "Great Terrestrial Lake" and as once part of the same high sea. Monsieur Os (Cassiodor) adds that because these waters were once part of the Great Fontaine Lake, they have the same properties as Fontaine's waters. The outer ruins hold the sub-areas Initium Iani, Hortus Euergetis, Caesareum Palace and Portus Anticus, the last sunk into an underwater cavern. Domus Aurea has upper and lower levels; its throne room is the Sacellum Requietis.

## places
### STANDARD
- **Machimos**: the outer city, once full of temples, arenas, walls decorated with glaze and gold, statues and markets; home of Remuria's warriors.
- **Capitolium**: the inner city, a "paradise for artists" of theatres and carved palaces.
- **Domus Aurea**: the royal palace on Clivus Capitolinus, built as a giant instrument for playing Phobos and considered the "heart" of the Symphony.
- **Initium Iani / Passage of Ianus**: the gate and corridor between the two cities.
### LARGE +
- **Caesareum Palace**: a training ground of the Remurian legions, where the chains that bound Scylla were kept.
- **Portus Anticus**: the old royal port, whose bell tower once guided sailors home.
- **Alta Semita**: the aqueduct that once carried Ichor from Domus Aurea to "the great lake in the far north".
- **Collegium Phonascorum**: Remuria's school, where Harmosts and music lecturers taught the Symphony.
- **Hortus Euergetis**: an undersea garden named after the dead Harmost Euergetia.
- **Sacellum Requietis**: Remus' throne room inside Domus Aurea.

## history
### STANDARD
Remus, a god who came to Meropis, founded Remuria above the waters of present-day Fontaine and made Capitolium its centre. A prophecy said the empire would face utter destruction and its people would return to the primordial sea. To escape that fate, Remus gave his people golem bodies of stone with golden Ichor for blood and bound their souls into the Symphony, Phobos. Phobos turned the empire to war against the "barbarian" tribes and vishaps. Remus' secret final plan to destroy it failed when the Harmost Boethius sealed Scylla at the Initium Iani, and a great wave from the Symphony sank the capital and its people.
### LARGE +
According to Scylla, his vishap army's assault on the capital was part of Remus' secret plan. Scylla was to reach Domus Aurea with the Requiem, Remus' final order for Phobos to destroy itself, and to guide the power it released. Remus would sacrifice himself to start the Requiem. Boethius falsely claimed Remus had ordered the legions to stop resisting, let the Dragonborn come in, then shut the Initium Iani and turned Scylla to stone. Without Scylla, the Requiem's power became a tsunami that drowned Remurians and invaders alike. Afterwards Boethius gathered survivors as the Golden Troupe in the Faded Castle to revive the empire. Cassiodor opposed him, broke his soul into fragments, sealed them in these waters and sealed the passage to the surface.
### ULTRA +
Over the centuries Boethius' shattered soul bound itself to the remains of Phobos. Shortly before the 4.6 quests, the Symphony grew strong enough to reach the surface, replacing the souls of Petrichor's townsfolk and visiting adventurers with Remurian souls who relived the empire's last days. In Pale Crown Palace (7.0), the Belyi Tsar wrote that Sybilla, the prophet behind Phobos, had tried to build a system independent of the ley lines and Heimarmene. He believed the Remurians had somehow escaped the heavens' gaze and passed on their arts, though in distorted form. The Temple of Space (6.5) also preserves records of Remuria among its fallen civilisations.

## people
### STANDARD
No living residents. Golem soldiers of the old legions (Praetorian Golems) still guard the ruins. Scylla, the "Fell Dragon Prince", now lives free in these waters and welcomes visits from the Traveler. The souls once held in the Symphony have passed on.
### LARGE +
Scylla calls himself a prince among the Dragonborn. Cassiodor says he was really an unusually strong vishap, because no new Hydro Dragon could be born while Egeria slept. Scylla once fought Remus for thirty days before guiding him to Egeria's prison, and became his friend. Since he lost his body to the seal, he now lives in a body made of Ichor. After hearing that a true dragon (Neuvillette) has been born, he has thought of seeking an audience with him, but doubts he still counts as Dragonborn. Lesser creatures that feed on the Symphony's music live here: allochromatic anemones, wonderchime flowers, eels, "washer" octopuses and the golden Auric Anglerfish, which the Collegium's zealots built to bring non-human creatures under the Symphony.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Silent blue darkness lit by golden ruins, glowing sea life and the faint pulse of old music. White stone and bronze lie broken among reefs; engraved orders and memorials from the empire's last war still cover the walls. Since Phobos fell, Scylla finds the deep far more peaceful, and quiet enough that he is tempted to sleep.
### LARGE +
In-game text describes the citizens' belief that the empire would last for ever while the God King's melody played from the golden throne, and how that pride and hatred sank beneath the waves. Paimon thought the inside of Domus Aurea looked "more like the guts of some giant machine" than a palace.

## dangers
### LARGE
Praetorian Golems and Hunter's Rays, ancient sealed doors and defences, crushing depth and darkness, and the Local Legend Cineas, "Unreturned Envoy", a Primordial Bathysmal Vishap. The Symphony's power can still move or freeze objects. While Phobos still existed, its music could seize souls. The Traveler was immune because Phobos' melody did not sing of their destiny.

## travel
### STANDARD
Gameplay: the area unlocks after the world quest Underwater Nocturne, through the portal in the Faded Castle. Travellers explore it by diving, using the Symphony (the Ancient Autoharmonic Music Box sends a Pause Soundwave to stop objects and a Play Soundwave to start them) and, after Scylla is freed, his "Summon the current" ability to ride water currents.
### LARGE +
Gameplay: other puzzles include Autoharmonic Reed Pipes, potential energy orbs, resonant anemones, wonderchime flowers and allochromatic anemones, swaying eels in the coral reefs, washer octopuses and the Auric Anglerfish. Restoring the Alta Semita aqueduct (Golden Aqueduct Reconstruction I-II) is needed to open Domus Aurea's gates.

## quests
### STANDARD
Canticles of Harmony (4.6): Prelude "The Shadow Over Petrichor" (Faded Castle), First Movement "Underwater Nocturne", Second Movement "The Last Day of Remuria" (freeing Scylla and breaking through the Initium Iani), Third Movement "Gradus ad Capitolium" (restoring the golden aqueducts) and Finale "Fortune Plango Vulnera" (the battle in Domus Aurea and the Requiem).
### LARGE +
In the finale, Boethius tried to "take on flesh" as a new Sebastos in Domus Aurea. Cassiodor let himself be absorbed so that he could seize control of Boethius' body from within, and Phobos showed itself as "that-which-was-once-Sybilla". Cassiodor asked the Traveler to destroy both of them. Golems executed the defeated Boethius, Cassiodor faded, and the Traveler played the Requiem at the harp in the throne room. The freed souls rose like a current to the High Sea, led by a spirit-guiding golden bee. Osse, the cat Cassiodor had possessed, was left an ordinary cat. Scylla brought the group back to Petrichor, then swam off to enjoy his freedom.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the sunken capital is quiet: Phobos was destroyed in 4.6, the trapped Remurian souls have passed on and Scylla is free in the deep.
### STANDARD +
The Symphony mechanism still works for exploration, and golem guards remain. Cassiodor and Boethius are gone. Later versions refer back to Remuria in lore texts, such as the Temple of Space (6.5) and the Belyi Tsar's notes in Pale Crown Palace (7.0).

## entry: Capitolium and Domus Aurea
@keys: Capitolium, Domus Aurea, Clivus Capitolinus, Sacellum Requietis, Eternal City
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Capitolium was the inner city of Remuria's capital, open only to the finest scholars and musicians, and only a few of them served the God King directly. At its centre, on the hill Clivus Capitolinus, stands Domus Aurea, a golden palace of tall copper pillars built as a giant harp-like instrument for playing Phobos. Remus sat at its heart, listening for any wrong note anywhere in the empire and correcting it with a pluck of his strings. Scylla calls the city the "Eternal City" and says Remus' will still protects it. In 4.6 Boethius made his last stand inside the palace, and the Traveler played the Requiem in its throne room, the Sacellum Requietis.
### LARGE +
In a cavern beneath the hill, an engraving records an order from Boethius to receive Dragonborn envoys for talks. The rest has been hacked out and replaced by the word "Traitor" scratched over and over, with a final curse inviting the enemy to sink into the "immortal prison" with the city. The wiki says the palace was fashioned from Remus' ship, the Fortuna.

## entry: Machimos and the Initium Iani
@keys: Machimos, Initium Iani, Passage of Ianus
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
Machimos was the outer city of Remuria's capital, home of its warriors, with arenas, temples, gold-and-glaze walls and busy markets. The Initium Iani was the only gate into Capitolium, opening onto the Passage of Ianus. Boethius and the Harmost Euergetia sealed Scylla there by shutting the gate on him as he tried to break through. In 4.6 the freed Scylla broke the gate's stone seals and carried the Traveler through the trench beyond.
### LARGE +
An engraving at the gate notes that a humble slave named Decimus now lies at the bottom of the sea just as the exalted Harmosts do.

## entry: Caesareum Palace
@keys: Caesareum Palace, Legio VI, Rithimi Versi, Legion Athanatoi
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
A training ground of the Remurian legions where the chains that held Scylla were kept. Boethius ordered Legio VI Rithimi Versi of the Legion Athanatoi to hold back the Dragonborn here; its records say no soldier deserted and no enemy got through. A nameless stone statue still offers a challenge to visitors.
### LARGE +
The engravings contradict each other. One order tells the legion to receive the Dragonborn peacefully, and the lines after it are deliberately struck out. Another order from Boethius seals Portus Anticus and the Initium Iani behind seven layers of defence, and records that three chains forged from Euergetia's blood and bone were moved here to imprison the drake chieftain for ever. A memorial by two siblings, Aetius and Placidia, says they died fighting the "traitorous" Dragonborn at the gates, choosing "civilization and death" in the name of their benefactress.

## entry: Portus Anticus
@keys: Portus Anticus, Remurian royal port, Legio V, Faunus
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
Remuria's royal port, now sunk into an underwater cavern. Ships came in past the Ocean Pillars along the royal fairway. Its tall bell tower was said to stand at the border of reality and dreams: its bell could "pierce the barrier of ocean" and lead sailors lured to sleep by sirens back home through the mist. The imperial ship Fortuna was berthed here.
### LARGE +
Engravings record orders from Legio V's envoy Faunus withdrawing the northern harbour defences. A later order from Boethius tells Legio V to stop fighting the Dragonborn and return to Caesareum Palace, then has Legio VII and Legio IX seal the port behind them. One bitter verse condemns Faunus for razing the city of Lugdunensis.

## entry: Alta Semita
@keys: Alta Semita, golden aqueduct, golden aqueducts
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
A huge aqueduct shaped like a harp that once carried Ichor from Domus Aurea to "the great lake in the far north". Scylla remembers swimming in those waters. It broke apart after the fall. In 4.6 the Traveler restored it with fragments of the Symphony so that Domus Aurea's gates could be opened.
### LARGE +
An engraving there mourns the Harmost Aurelius, who had pacified many tribes for the empire and once promoted Boethius. He sank with his great flagship of forty rows of oars, which "traitors" working with the barbarians scuttled as it carried him north. No one knows where he is buried.

## entry: Collegium Phonascorum
@keys: Collegium Phonascorum, Harmost's Notes
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
Remuria's centre of learning, where Harmosts and music teachers taught citizens the theory of the Symphony. Scylla calls its scholars "mad believers" who tried to bring non-human creatures under the Golden Symphony, creating the Auric Anglerfish.
### LARGE +
A Harmost's notes found here set out Aurelius' four categories of all things (Substance, Property, Quality, Relation) and describe Phobos as a symphony that blends mortal melodies so that it can "vie with Fortuna". The notes go on to argue that people outside the Symphony merely play out a fate written for them, so they are not equal humans and must be wiped out. This is the ideology behind Remuria's wars of conquest.

## entry: Hortus Euergetis
@keys: Hortus Euergetis, Euergetia's garden

### STANDARD
An undersea garden named after the dead Harmost Euergetia, called "the benefactress". An engraving signed "Ouranides of Ouranopolis" (Cassiodor's alias) mourns her as colleague, friend and sister, and promises that flowers will always bloom there for her even if her name is forgotten.

## entry: Scylla in the Sea of Bygone Eras
@keys: Scylla, Fell Dragon Prince, Prince of Dragonborn
@keys2: Sea of Bygone Eras, Remuria, Machimos, Capitolium, Domus Aurea
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Scylla is a giant vishap prince who led Dragonborn vishaps and "barbarians" against Remuria. Boethius trapped him in stone at the Initium Iani, and he stayed chained for millennia. Thoughts escaping from his stony prison took the form of a large glowing fish that swam past the Faded Castle's windows. The Traveler, Paimon and Cassiodor broke his three chains in 4.6, and he helped them reach Domus Aurea and complete Remus' Requiem. Proud, booming and friendly, he calls the Traveler and Paimon "little ones" and despises Boethius. He now roams these waters freely and asks the Traveler to visit him.

## entry: The Symphony and Phobos
@keys: Phobos, Grand Symphony, Golden Symphony, Ichor
@keys2: Sea of Bygone Eras, Remuria, Domus Aurea, Remurian
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The "Symphony" is human memory and persona woven into music, a melody in which souls can live and have their wishes granted. Sybilla, the Great Golden Bee and a prophet who survived an earlier cycle, taught the secret to Remus. He poured these melodies into Ichor, which the primordial sea could not dissolve, so that people could rule their own fates. Overwhelmed, the people asked to be guided, so Remus wrote every melody into one great Symphony: Phobos. Phobos turned out to have Sybilla's selfless urge to make "everyone" happy, so it tried to grant even its Remurians' wishes to conquer. The Symphony's remaining power still moves the ruins (the Symphony mechanic) and feeds the area's strange creatures.

## entry: Golden Castellum and the Golden Troupe
@keys: golden castellum, Golden Troupe
@keys2: Sea of Bygone Eras, Faded Castle, Boethius, Cassiodor
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Faded Castle, once the "golden castellum", is the gateway to the Sea of Bygone Eras. Harmosts and scholars kept an archive there of scripts, scores and stories of things that had and had not happened, hoping to master fate. After the fall, Boethius' surviving followers, the Golden Troupe, gathered there to bring back the empire. Cassiodor sealed the way to the surface to protect Fontaine, and the seal held until he opened it for the Traveler in 4.6.
