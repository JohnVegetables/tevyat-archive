## summary
### LITE
Narukami Island is the largest island of Inazuma and the heart of the Shogunate. Its coast holds Inazuma City, crowned by the castle Tenshukaku from which the Raiden Shogun rules. Ritou is the walled-off port where all foreigners enter. Above them rises Mt. Yougou, topped by the Grand Narukami Shrine and the Sacred Sakura. Inside the island are farming and dyeing villages, fox-haunted plains and moonlit sacred forests. Inazuma's public festivals and cultural events are held here.
### STANDARD +
During the Sakoku Decree the island was the centre from which isolation was enforced. Visitors arriving by sea see the shrine and the Sacred Sakura on the mountain and the city on the shore.

## geography
### STANDARD
Mt. Yougou rises in the north of the island. Inazuma City lies on the coast below Tenshukaku. Ritou ("outlying island") is a port district set apart from the rest of the island, joined to it by a guarded road. Byakko Plain, a low and sparsely wooded field in the southwest, carries the main road from Konda Village to the city. Chinju Forest sits in a river valley just south of Mt. Yougou and is wrapped in perpetual night under an unseen moon. Araumi, the northern promontory, is cut off by the mountain. Off the coasts lie Jinren Island, a group of broken isles at the northernmost tip, and Amakane Island, a small festival island west of Byakko Plain.
### LARGE +
Thunder Sakura trees grown from the Sacred Sakura's branches stand across the island and draw filth out of the land. At the foot of Mt. Yougou lay a pool of Electrified Water, which was de-energised after the Sacred Sakura Cleansing Ritual.

## places
### STANDARD
- **Inazuma City**: the capital, with the Tenryou commercial district, the Hanamizaka suburb and Tenshukaku.
- **Ritou**: the Kanjou Commission's port and checkpoint for all non-Inazuman nationals, home to the Outlander Affairs Agency.
- **Mt. Yougou / Grand Narukami Shrine**: the mountain shrine of Yae Miko's shrine maidens and the Sacred Sakura.
- **Kamisato Estate**: residence of the Yashiro Commission's Kamisato Clan, reached through Chinju Forest.
- **Konda Village**: a quiet farming village, long the Shogunate's main source of dye, known for Konda Cuisine.
### LARGE +
- **Byakko Plain**: open fields where wild kitsune roam near the city outskirts.
- **Chinju Forest**: a sacred grove of torii, tanuki statues and abandoned shrines; legend says it was once home to many demons, and tales of the tanuki-bayashi cling to it.
- **Araumi**: ruins of an abandoned city that extends deep under the earth and sea.
- **Jinren Island**: broken isles in the mountain's shadow, once overrun by deserters turned bandits.
- **Amakane Island**: in summer, people sit among its sakura and watch fireworks in the distance.
### ULTRA +
- **Depths of Mt. Yougou**: caverns beneath the mountain, opened after the Cleansing Ritual.
- **Court of Flowing Sand**: a domain on Mt. Yougou.
- Under Araumi lie the Perpetual Mechanical Array's chamber and the domain Empty Boat of a Thousand Gates, said to have been the Great Tengu Reizenbou's mansion.

## people
### STANDARD
The Shogun, the Tri-Commission and most of Inazuma's population live here, along with the Grand Narukami Shrine's maidens under Guuji Yae Miko. Ritou hosts foreign merchants (from Liyue, Mondstadt, Snezhnaya and elsewhere), Kanjou Commission officers and the Hiiragi family. Kitsune and bake-danuki, youkai that live alongside humans, roam the forests and plains.
### LARGE +
Konda Village's chief is Konda Densuke. Tejima, a samurai and Vision bearer, has long waited in the village for a lover who promised to meet him there and never came.

## governance
### STANDARD
The island is ruled directly from Tenshukaku. The Kanjou Commission runs Ritou and its border controls, the Tenryou Commission keeps order and the army, and the Yashiro Commission handles ceremony and culture. Under the Sakoku Decree, foreigners could only pass beyond Ritou with a Travel Permit that was hard to obtain without the right connections.

## history
### STANDARD
Tradition records that Narukami was one of the islands that survived the great flood after the war of funerary flame. In that flood a kingdom north of Mt. Yougou, whose foolish ruler wished to build a high garden for the white tree, was swept away or buried in sand. After the cataclysm about 500 years ago, Araumi's survivors never returned, and Thunder Sakura were planted across the island to absorb filth. Hundreds of years ago Lord Hiiragi Hiroshi of the Kanjou Commission turned a deserted island into the trading port of Ritou.
### LARGE +
Araumi was an underground city that stayed active until the cataclysm. Then the Kitsune Saiguu guided its people through the tunnels to safety. In the present, the Traveler's arrival in Ritou began the Inazuma Archon Quests. With Hanachirusato's help, the Traveler performed the latest Sacred Sakura Cleansing Ritual.
### ULTRA +
A Kitsune statue in Araumi preserves a commissioner's long-ago plea to the Shogun to build a port there and centralise foreign trade in government hands. Under Sakoku, Ritou's foreign merchants languished and its streets lost the bustle of a century before.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Red maples and green roof tiles at Ritou. Lantern-lit streets and shop signs in the capital. Cherry blossom and the hum of Electro on Mt. Yougou. A hush of torii and tanuki statues under Chinju Forest's endless moon. Fishing and farming villages along the roads, and in festival season fireworks over the water.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Bandits and ronin, Treasure Hoarders and Kairagi linger in the wilder corners. Araumi's depths hold Ruin Guards and other ancient machines. Under Sakoku, foreigners trying to leave Ritou without a permit were turned back by its guards.

## travel
### STANDARD
Ships from abroad dock at Ritou; it is the official way in. Roads link Ritou, Konda Village, Byakko Plain and Inazuma City, and boats cross to Kannazuka and the other islands.
### LARGE +
Gameplay note: before the Travel Permit quest, guards stop the Traveler from leaving Ritou on foot. Unlocking the Statue of The Seven here reveals the sea between Inazuma and the mainland on the map.

## quests
### LARGE
The opening of Chapter II (Ritou, the Travel Permit, the Vision Hunt ceremony) and much of its finale take place here. World quests include the Sacred Sakura Cleansing Ritual and the tanuki-bayashi stories of Chinju Forest.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 Narukami Island is at peace. The Sakoku and Vision Hunt decrees are gone, foreigners move freely through Ritou, and Ei now takes a more active part in governing from Tenshukaku. No Narukami-specific story events appear in 6.x-7.1 content.

## entry: Araumi
@keys: Araumi
### LARGE
Araumi is the northern promontory of Narukami Island, cut off from the rest by Mt. Yougou. It is full of the ruins of an ancient city that extends deep under the ground and the surrounding sea. The city likely dates from the Archon War. Its tunnels held the mansion of the Great Tengu Reizenbou and stayed in use until the cataclysm. Then the Kitsune Saiguu led its people out through the passages. The survivors never returned. Its depths are now patrolled by ancient automatons, including the Perpetual Mechanical Array.

## entry: Konda Village and Byakko Plain
@keys: Konda Village, Byakko Plain, Konda Cuisine
### STANDARD
Konda Village, in the southwest of Narukami, has long been the Shogunate's main dye-producing village. It is simple, peaceful and known for Konda Cuisine, though Sakoku and the war aged it early. The road from Konda to Inazuma City crosses Byakko Plain, a low field where wild kitsune roam. Folk say a fox will not show itself twice to a traveler whose fate is not tied to its own.

## entry: Chinju Forest
@keys: Chinju Forest, tanuki-bayashi
### STANDARD
Chinju Forest is a sacred grove just south of Mt. Yougou, on the path to the Kamisato Estate. For reasons nobody explains it lies under eternal night, lit by an unseen moon. Torii gates, tanuki statues and abandoned shrines fill it. Legend says demons once lived there, and rumour holds that pranksters still play tricks on those who walk through. The "tanuki-bayashi" tales of bake-danuki are tied to it.
