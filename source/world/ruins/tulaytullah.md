## summary
### LITE
Tulaytullah, the "City of Sapphire", was an ancient desert city-state in what is now the Hypostyle Desert of Sumeru. After King Deshret's civilisation fell, the priest Hermanubis brought the Tighnarians there and founded the original Temple of Silence, and the city became famed for wisdom, canals, sapphire domes and gardens. Wars among the desert kingdoms ended its glory. Only its garden is still preserved, in the Garden of Endless Pillars.
### STANDARD +
Its story is told in fragments: weapon and artifact texts, obelisk inscriptions in the Mausoleum of King Deshret, the Temple of Silence's own history, and a few quests. Legend says the city arose from the tears of Nabu Malikata after the fall of her fellow Angels. Refugees from Tulaytullah helped found Aaru Village and spread across Sumeru; some Eremite tribes and some desert-born people still trace their descent to it.

## geography
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
The city stood at the site of today's Garden of Endless Pillars in the Hypostyle Desert, part of the Great Red Sand. Accounts describe a lattice of shimmering canals, buildings with sapphire domes and lush emerald gardens full of padisarah flowers and pomegranates.
### LARGE +
It was one of the Cities of the Seven Towers, the group of desert cities that Shah Ramshahr bound under his rule by raising seven towers near them, together with Ay-Khanoum and Saleh. Whether those towers are the later Towers of Betrayal is not settled.

## people
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Tulaytullah was home to the Tulaytullah Tighnarians, priests who had come with Hermanubis, and in later ages to an elite of nobles and scholars. Its better-known figures in the sources are King Humayun, the young noble Goudarz (later called Saleb Dowleh), the queen regent Makhaira, her lover Xiphos, the prince Faramarz and a blind poet who later led refugees to Aaru Village.
### LARGE +
Descendants are scattered: the character Layla's blue hair and pointed ears mark her as being from Tulaytullah's line, and the Eremite tribe of the Shimti traced their ancestry to the city. The Shimti were massacred by the Iab tribe about twenty years before the present; the chief's daughter Nefer survives.

## governance
@version: 3.0
### LARGE
Tulaytullah's rule changed repeatedly. It was a vassal of Deshret's heirs at Gurabad and paid yearly tribute under Parvezravan; a city led by King Humayun under Shah Ramshahr; a conquered city under foreign Khagans; later a prominent state ruled by nobles and scholars, where Balash held the title of master. The elite often put its own power ahead of the people, as when it colluded with a besieging Gurabad king, and under the Khagans it treated its own citizens harshly.

## history
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
After King Deshret sacrificed himself and his civilisation splintered, Hermanubis led the Tighnarians to Tulaytullah and set up the original Temple of Silence, which kept the knowledge that survived and held remnants of forbidden knowledge at bay. A little over a hundred years after Deshret's death war reached the desert. A Gurabad king besieged the city and demanded the Temple be handed over; the city's elite sided with him and blamed the Temple's knowledge for corruption. Hermanubis, his strength spent, broke himself into pieces and passed his power to his followers, who defeated the invaders but frightened the citizens. The Temple of Silence and the Tighnarians then left for the rainforest, and Tulaytullah fell into the wider wars.
### LARGE +
Later events, from obelisk inscriptions: after Ramshahr died, his son Artaxir and then the Khagan Turan held the throne until Humayun and other Tulaytullah nobles rebelled, took the throne by marriage into Ramshahr's family and ruled as Humayun Gurkan. He was beheaded at Saleh in the Jinn Uprising. The warlord Makani Khagan then took Tulaytullah from a thin defence and called himself ruler of the Seven Realms; Garshasp of Ay-Khanoum later restored a fragile peace and, after his death, survivors of his burned capital fled to Tulaytullah. Goudarz, a noble who gained cunning-based kingly power from Thoth, freed the city from the Khagans, renamed himself Saleb Dowleh and went to rule at Gurabad. He allied with the port city of Orghana (now Port Ormos), and its Dey Muzaffar later killed him and his line.
### ULTRA +
In the last golden age Tulaytullah was among the desert's most prominent cities, ruled by nobles and scholars, but it came under Gurabad's king Badanah, who subjugated other cities and enslaved their people. To please him the city's elite sent Makhaira to him as a concubine. She, with Badanah's son Faramarz, killed him and passed it off as a hunting accident. A power struggle followed: Balash, Badanah's brother and Faramarz's uncle, ruled with Makhaira, Faramarz abdicated to him, and Balash was slain by another uncle, Goraz, who took Makhaira as wife. The city's nobles rebelled to avenge Balash; Makhaira appeased them with Goraz's head. Faramarz was exiled and restored three times, finally killed Makhaira with viper venom, and was ousted for the last time after a conspiracy of citizens and slave-warriors. Meanwhile Xiphos, a prince of a fallen kingdom and wielder of a blade with a Jinni in it, became the city's famed protector, the Blade of Tulaytullah. His fame earned him enemies, and the exiled Faramarz, now a mercenary, killed him for payment. Without him the city withered.

## atmosphere
@version: 3.0
### LARGE
In its prime, a scholarly, wealthy desert city of blue-domed halls, canals and gardens, full of scribes, priests and nobles. In its decline, a place of intrigue, conquest and betrayal, remembered by its descendants with nostalgia and caution.

## status
### LITE
Tulaytullah is long gone. Its remembered gardens are all that remains, preserved in the Garden of Endless Pillars. It cannot be visited as a city.
### STANDARD +
Its descendants live on across Sumeru, and Aaru Village was founded by refugees from Tulaytullah and other failing desert kingdoms. As of Version 7.1 nothing found changes this.

## entry: Hermanubis and the Temple of Silence
@keys: Hermanubis, Tulaytullah Tighnarians, original Temple of Silence
@keys2: Tulaytullah
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
Hermanubis was a Tighnarian priest who brought followers to Tulaytullah and, according to Thoth, took in many who had nowhere else to go. The Temple of Silence he founded there kept the surviving knowledge of Deshret's civilisation and, through his power, kept forbidden knowledge at bay. The Tighnarians themselves came from a place called Tighnar and had left it to serve Deshret before his death. When the city turned on the Temple, the Tighnarians withdrew to the rainforest.
### LARGE +
Hermanubis had used up his strength by the time of the siege, which is why he broke himself apart to pass his power to his followers. The Temple of Silence's records about the siege are the main source for the event; they do not name the besieging king.

## entry: Makhaira, Faramarz and Xiphos
@keys: Makhaira, Faramarz, Xiphos, Blade of Tulaytullah, Badanah
@keys2: Tulaytullah
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: minor
### LARGE
In the last age of Tulaytullah a Jinni-descended woman, Makhaira, was sent as tribute to the tyrant Badanah, and plotted with his son Faramarz to kill him. She ruled as queen regent amid constant alliances. Her first love Xiphos, the Blade of Tulaytullah, was killed by Faramarz, and Faramarz in turn ended Makhaira. Their stories survive in weapon texts and inscriptions rather than any single account.

## entry: Aaru Village and the Refugees
@keys: Tulaytullah refugees, blind poet, Shimti
@keys2: Aaru Village, Layla, Eremite
@version: 3.0
### LARGE
Aaru Village began with refugees from Tulaytullah and other fading desert kingdoms. Among them was a blind poet from Tulaytullah who had been a general under Badanah, had known Faramarz as a child (and was blinded by him), and mentored Xiphos. Other refugees spread across Sumeru. The Shimti Eremite tribe's claim to Tulaytullah descent is recorded in the quest Dance of Death: Serpent and Scorpion.
