## summary
### LITE
The Tomb of Carouses is a small cone-shaped ruin in the Dune of Carouses, in the Great Red Sand of Sumeru's Hypostyle Desert. It looks like a scaled-down copy of the Mausoleum of King Deshret. Nobody in canon says who built it.
### STANDARD +
It is a point of interest rather than a settlement: a sealed tomb with a puzzle mechanism and a modest hoard. The scholar Soheil named it in his old expedition notes, and the Traveler later returns to it on his behalf.

## geography
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
The tomb stands on the Dune of Carouses within the Great Red Sand, part of the Land of Lower Setekh. The dune and its surroundings hold hilichurls and fungi, and scarabs can be gathered nearby. The tomb is a cone-shaped structure, far smaller than the Mausoleum of King Deshret.
### LARGE +
The Dune of Carouses is a subarea of the Hypostyle Desert and also holds a Dendro Elemental Monument. Soheil's notes place the tomb on his route after the Transparent Ruins and before Lamb-Devourer Rock, on the way toward the fabled oasis he was seeking.

## places
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
Inside, a mechanism called a Prism of Khaj-Nisut must be activated, as in the larger Mausoleum. Doing so changes its colour but opens nothing by itself; a second switch has to be found, after which a passage is heard opening deeper in. A chest at the end holds a stone slate.
### LARGE +
Other features recorded for the site are Seelie, Forged Primal Light, large fan blades, Obscuring and Everlight Cells, and an elite Primal Construct: Reshaper. These are the same kinds of devices met in the Mausoleum of King Deshret.

## history
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
Its origin is unknown. When Soheil's archaeology team reached it on day 45 of his expedition, they found it shut by the same mysterious power that had kept them out of the Transparent Ruins, and he gave it its name provisionally. The next day his team was attacked by Eremites calling themselves Thutmose, led by two young men, one of whom (Samail) wanted them dead while the other (Jebrael) let them go.
### LARGE +
Years later, in the world quest Desert's Remembrance, the Traveler and Paimon open the tomb while following Soheil's old notes. Paimon remarks that it matches Soheil's description of it as a lesser mausoleum of King Deshret and wonders which was built first; canon does not say.
### ULTRA +
Soheil, in the notes, finds it hard to imagine how Deshret enslaved his people to build the Mausoleum, and takes the same view of this smaller copy. That is Soheil's opinion in his notes, written while he was investigating, and not a settled fact.

## atmosphere
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
A hushed, wind-scoured sandstone cone, quiet apart from the hum of old mechanisms. It feels like a sibling of the great Mausoleum, with fewer rooms and less treasure.

## dangers
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
A Primal Construct: Reshaper is listed as an elite enemy at the site, and fungi and hilichurls roam the surrounding dune. Elsewhere in the desert, Eremite groups may be encountered.

## quests
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
Desert's Remembrance, a world quest in the Old Notes and New Friends chain that follows Soheil's Wish. The Traveler searches the tomb and brings what is found back to Soheil.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the tomb is unchanged and open to explore. No later story involves it.

## entry: Record of the Tomb of Carouses
@keys: Record of the Tomb of Carouses, The Sheep the Bird and the Crocodile
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
A stone slate found in the tomb's chest, described as a record left by a lost civilisation. It carries a fable, The Sheep, the Bird, and the Crocodile. A sheep chief sends seven lambs to find fresh pasture; a bird, for its own reasons, sends them far north; a crocodile there pities them and escorts them home. The bird, ashamed, joins them, and on meeting the chief the crocodile devours the sheep and weeps afterwards. The moral: do not trust the meek, the wise, or those who shed tears.
### LARGE +
The fable suggests its makers distrusted outward kindness, but who wrote it or why it was placed in the tomb is unrecorded.
