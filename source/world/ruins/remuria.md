## summary
### LITE
Remuria was the empire of the god Remus, who called himself the God King and was known to his people as the Sebastos or Imperator. He founded it on the waters of what is now Fontaine, before the Hydro Archon Egeria ruled. It prospered on music and art, its capital was Capitolium, and its emblem was a golden bee. It was prophesied to face utter destruction, and Remus's attempts to avoid that destiny wrecked it. It drowned in a single catastrophe and now lies in the Sea of Bygone Eras.
### STANDARD +
Remuria is the setting of the 4.6 World Quest series Canticles of Harmony, when the buried Grand Symphony Phobos stirred again beneath Petrichor. Its survivors and descendants, the Golden Troupe and the Marechaussee Hunters, shaped some of Fontaine's hidden history, and its golem bodies and ruined palaces are still found across Fontaine.

## geography
### STANDARD
The empire spread over the "high waters", the great lake of Fontaine, by conquering islands and building long fairways called the Alta Semita that carried the music of the capital outwards. The capital had two parts: the outer city Machimos, where the warriors lived and which held ports, temples and arenas, and the inner city Capitolium, reached only through the Initium Iani and the Passage of Ianus. A bell tower at the port guided ships between islands.
### LARGE +
Today the ruins are two areas: the Faded Castle, once the Harmosts' archive on the shore, and the Sea of Bygone Eras, which holds Machimos (Caesareum Palace, Hortus Euergetis, Portus Anticus) and Capitolium with Domus Aurea. These waters have the same nature as Fontaine's water.

## government
### STANDARD
Remus ruled as an authority above all others, but shared governance with four humans of exceptional ability, the Harmosts, who ruled the cities. They were Boethius, Cassiodor, Aurelius and Euergetia. Only the most gifted intellectuals and musicians served the God King directly. Everyday decisions of the "ascended" Remurians came to depend on Phobos, which handed down the course they should take.
### LARGE +
Legions with numbers such as Legio VI Rithimi Versi and Legio IX served under the Harmosts. Boethius commanded the force that fought Scylla; Euergetia pacified the northern barbarian border; Aurelius completed an earlier northern campaign among island tribes without violence.

## culture
### STANDARD
Music defined Remuria. Remus believed it separated people from other living things, and taught the early Fontainians farming, house building and the arts. Conch shells were used as instruments, and in mourning, Remurians cast harps of black iron that could not be played, their silence being symbolic. Capitolium was a paradise of theatres and palaces with carved beams, with a palace of copper pillars at its heart.
### LARGE +
Harmost writing on music in the Faded Castle distinguishes three kinds: the music of the world (Musica Mundana), the music of mortals and gods (Musica Humana) and the music of instruments (Musica Instrumentalis). The Harmosts studied scripts, scores and all possible stories in the castle in order to understand fate, which Remurians called Fortuna.

## religion
### STANDARD
Remus was god and king. The seers, among them Sybilla and her fellow golden bees, prophesied that the greatest empire would meet the most utter destruction. Remurians thought of fate as Fortuna, a rule that governs all things, and the Grand Symphony was Remus's attempt to take that power away from it. Outside their borders, northern tribes revered Egeria, the Mistress of Waters, in many forms.

## history
### LITE
After the first civilisation fell to flood, Fontaine had no real civilisation for a very long age. Remus arrived in his ship and brought agriculture, cities and music. Seers prophesied that his empire would be utterly destroyed, so he built the Grand Symphony, Phobos, and put the souls of his people into immortal bodies to escape fate. Phobos magnified the cruellest wishes of the ascended Remurians, they went to war with the other peoples of Fontaine, and in the end a tsunami drowned the empire.
### STANDARD +
Remus took the advice of the seer Sybilla and visited Egeria, then imprisoned in the Primordial Sea. Scylla, a vishap prince guarding her cell, fought him for thirty days before letting him pass. Egeria gave him a goblet of primordial water, which he mixed with an immortal stone to make golden Ichor. Remurians turned into "ascended" souls in golem bodies that the sea could not dissolve. Sybilla gave her life to make Phobos.
### LARGE +
Remuria declared war on the "barbarian" tribes, Lochfolk and vishaps. The resistance, the Lochknights, formed around Erinnyes. Remus, waking in sorrow, separated Cassiodor from the Symphony so he could act freely, and secretly arranged with Scylla for a fake betrayal. Phobos learned of it and guided Boethius to sabotage it. Scylla was lured into a trap and sealed at the Initium Iani. Boethius refused Remus's order to end the war. Remus presumably triggered the Requiem; with no one to control the power, great waves swallowed the capital and its invaders alike.
### ULTRA +
Afterwards the survivors lived as barbarians until Egeria returned to settle conflicts. Fontaine as it exists was founded by Egeria at its spring waters. Boethius survived and founded the Golden Troupe to revive Remuria; Cassiodor, as the Golden Hunter and head of the Marechaussee Hunters, opposed him and eventually broke Boethius's soul, sealing the pieces in the palace.

## people
### STANDARD
Remus; the seer Sybilla; the four Harmosts; the vishap prince Scylla; and the many golem soldiers and musicians. Este the Quick-Witted, a Remurian legionnaire seen only in memory, has a living Fontainian descendant in Petrichor. Cassiodor adopted a cat's form and the name Osse.
### LARGE +
Aetius and his sister Placidia (Legio VI) left an epitaph in Caesareum Palace. Hirtius, vicarius of Legio VI, wrote a survey of the northern tribes. The Aremorica leader Erinnyes opposed the empire, and an earlier chief, Cunoricus, bowed to it about twenty years before that survey.

## status
### LITE
Only ruins, golems and memory remain. In Version 4.6, Phobos was finally ended and its souls went to the sea; Remuria is no longer a threat.
### STANDARD +
Remurian descendants live as ordinary Fontainians. Remus's story is still told in books, many half-forgotten, and the Golden Troupe's own distortions of it confused historians.

## dangers
### LARGE
Praetorian Golems still guard the ruins, and vishaps such as the Primordial Bathysmal Vishap live in the deep. Before the Requiem, the symphony could pull souls from the living; this is no longer a risk.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Gold leaf and rust, sunk music, weighty stone voices that speak as if the war were still on, and a sorrowful calm. The prevailing mood is of an ancient dream slowly released.

## entry: The Harmosts
@keys: Harmost, Harmosts
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
The four human rulers who shared Remus's power: Boethius, Cassiodor, Aurelius and Euergetia. Euergetia is remembered by Cassiodor as kind, almost a sister; Boethius grew arrogant and contemptuous of non-Remurians, while Cassiodor, who came from a tribe Remuria conquered, joined the empire and later opposed what it became. Remus cut Cassiodor off from the Symphony to keep his free will. Each Harmost could also bend the Symphony's power; Euergetia could make conquered people vividly taste their native dishes.

## entry: Golden Ichor and the Ascended Remurians
@keys: Ichor, golden Ichor, ascended Remurians
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
Remus mixed primordial water with an immortal stone to make golden Ichor, which held the minds and souls of his people. They were moved into stone golems with Ichor for blood so they could survive the coming flood. The result was immortality, but also pride; many came to see non-Remurians as lesser. In the present day, some descendants remain ordinary Fontainians.
### LARGE +
Boethius says that he and Cassiodor were new humans crafted by Remus's own hand and are the last survivors, and that Cassiodor's soul is like insoluble Ichor. Ruined golems are still found in the wild; later folklore painted even the greatest scholars as soul-stealing magisters who bound mortal spirits into golems, which the book's author blames on the moral decay of Remuria's final days.

## entry: Phobos and the Grand Symphony
@keys: Phobos, Grand Symphony
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Remus and Sybilla made the Grand Symphony to avert the prophecy and free humans from Fortuna. Sybilla had no will of her own and wanted to make humans happy, so Phobos satisfied whatever the Remurians wished, including slaughter and conquest. In Fortune Plango Vulnera Phobos itself speaks, calling itself the sum of all souls. Remus tried to end it with the Requiem, and the Traveler eventually played that Requiem.

## entry: The War on the Tribes
@keys: Lochknights, Aremorica, Cunoricus
@keys2: Remuria, Remurian
@version: 4.6
### LARGE
The Faded Castle notes by the Vicarius Hirtius paint northern tribes as crude savages, but admit some, like Lugdunensis, ruled by a holy king, were more developed. Legio V razed that city; the Harmost Boethius destroyed another city and erased its name. The resistance formed by tribes who worshipped Egeria, led by Erinnyes, joined the rebellion that Scylla led, at Remus's secret request, against Remuria. Their agony is said to have woken Remus.

## entry: Remuria's Legacy
@keys: Golden Troupe, Marechaussee Hunters
@keys2: Remuria, Remurian, Boethius, Cassiodor
@version: 4.6
### LARGE
Boethius gathered survivors in the Faded Castle to revive Remuria; their research and goals later shaped Fontaine's hidden history. The Phobos page links Remurian arts that survived the fall to the later Narzissenkreuz Ordo, which drew on what it learned of the Golden Troupe; the sources leave most of the details unsaid. Cassiodor's Marechaussee Hunters stood against them.
