## summary
### LITE
Sal Vindagnyr was an ancient kingdom on the mountain once called Vindagnyr, which is now Dragonspine in Mondstadt. Refugees from a land of snow and strife settled the then-green mountain, and their kingdom prospered under the heavens' blessing. Then the Skyfrost Nail fell from the sky. Endless blizzards buried the kingdom, its sacred white tree was shattered, and its people died. Today only frozen ruins remain, along with murals, carved tablets and a few personal keepsakes.
### STANDARD +
Its story is pieced together from scattered texts. The chief priest Varuch climbed toward the summit to seek the heavens' guidance and did not come back. His daughter, the princess, painted prophetic frescoes and died trying to save the sacred tree. The outlander hero Imunlaukr went to look for a way to save the kingdom and returned to find everyone dead. A scribe recorded the end.

## geography
### STANDARD
The kingdom covered the mountain now called Dragonspine, which was green and fertile before the nail fell. Its capital and palace stood on the mountainside, in the ruins now called the Entombed City. The summit held a ritual site where priests went up to face the heavens. The Skyfrost Nail came down there, and the Peak of Vindagnyr domain sits there now.
### LARGE +
Ruins survive in two main places: the Entombed City's ancient palace and its outskirts. Other remains include the hidden Mural Room near the palace, Starglow Cavern, carved tablets scattered across the mountain, and the sacred white tree on the eastern slopes, now called the Frostbearing Tree. A small island east of that tree holds a withered tree with a sword driven into the ground beneath it.

## places
### LARGE
- **Entombed City**: the snow-buried capital and its ancient palace, now the largest field of Sal Vindagnyr ruins on Dragonspine.
- **Frostbearing Tree**: the kingdom's sacred white tree, an Irminsul tree under which the princess was born. A fragment of the nail shattered it.
- **Peak of Vindagnyr**: the former summit ritual site, buried in the mountain by the nail.
- **Mural Room**: a sealed great hall holding the princess's frescoes and the abandoned greatsword Snow-Tombed Starsilver.
- **Starglow Cavern**: a deep cavern where one fragment of the nail lay frozen; the scribe seems to have gone down there after the end.
- **The princess's island**: the small island east of the Frostbearing Tree where she tried to graft a branch of the shattered tree and died. The Princess' Box was found there.
- **A weathered memorial stone** west of the Entombed City outskirts, where the Traveler can lay flowers. It held the Scribe's Box, and the wiki suggests it may be the princess's grave.
### ULTRA +
The Mural Room shows two finished murals. The first shows a green mountain under a floating island, with an angel standing beside the mountain and pointing at the sky. On its other half, crowned figures and smaller people reach toward the envoy, who holds out a shining symbol. The second shows three green mountains with a castle on the central peak. Inscriptions on the murals say that the angels help the faithful. A third fresco, which was meant to show the thaw, was never finished. The princess promised Imunlaukr a fourth fresco of his likeness, but it was never painted.

## people
### STANDARD
The texts name only a few people. **Varuch** was the chief priest and the princess's father. **The princess** was born under the white tree, received a blessing that let her foresee the future, and painted what she saw. **Imunlaukr** was a hero from another land who lived in the kingdom. **Ukko** is named in the princess's last words and is generally taken to be the scribe who recorded the kingdom's end.
### LARGE +
The kingdom's annalists believed the princess's blessing, like the undying tree, would last forever. Her last words apologise to her father and to Ukko and wish she could see Imunlaukr again. The scribe's final lines are bitter. They wish fire on the frozen sky, call Imunlaukr "that outsider who abandoned the princess", and hope that people building "a new nation without gods" might be strong enough to stand against the world.

## governance
### LARGE
Sal Vindagnyr appears to have been ruled by priests. A weapon appearance released in 7.0 says a divine envoy of the heavens once gave a spear to the high priest of Sal Vindagnyr, and with it the right to lead the city-state. The first mural likewise shows crowned figures receiving something from an angel. In the kingdom's last days, the chief priest Varuch was the one who sought the heavens' guidance on the summit, and his daughter was called the princess.

## culture
### STANDARD
The people of Sal Vindagnyr revered the heavens and believed their prosperity was Celestia's lasting blessing. The white tree was sacred to them. Their priests climbed to the summit to ask the heavens for guidance. The princess recorded her visions as frescoes in the great hall, and the annalists wrote the kingdom's history on stone.
### LARGE +
Texts from Nod-Krai and Snezhnaya add more. A Nod-Krai prayer to Maadteraahka, Mother of Life (Naberius), recorded in Hymns of the Far North (6.0), says her compassion was revered "from Hyperborea to Sal Vindagnyr". An artifact text (6.3) recalls the age of the three moons, when some peoples climbed the heights of Vindagnyr to offer laurel-crowned verses to the moons, as others did at Lang-Gan and in Arcadia's sacred groves.
### ULTRA +
Starsilver ore seems to be native to the mountain, since the princess's greatsword was made of it long before Durin fell there. The scribe's carvings speak of Starsilver as a weapon's "bones".

## history
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Thousands of years ago, people fleeing a land of snow and strife found a green mountain. They raised a monument on its peak and named their new home Sal Vindagnyr. For a time the kingdom flourished. Then the Skyfrost Nail descended onto the summit ritual site. Cloud and mist sealed the city in, and snowstorms never stopped. As snow whipped across the sky, the pillar split into three. One fragment shattered the white tree, and the others froze the places where they landed. The trees withered and life on the mountain failed.
### LARGE +
Varuch climbed the peak again, hoping his daughter's third fresco would show the snow thawing. She could not paint it, because she had not seen blue sky or green grass in so long that she no longer knew which colours to use. She gave Imunlaukr the Starsilver greatsword and promised to wait for him. Then she took the most intact branch of the shattered tree to a small island to graft it, and died in the cold beside it. The scribe tried to heal the withered ley lines, buried the princess, and recorded that there was no more need for records. Imunlaukr returned to a hall of "deathly echoes", cursed the heavens, left the greatsword between the frescoes, and went down the mountain in search of war.
### ULTRA +
Weapon lore from 6.6 continues Imunlaukr's story. On the frozen plains he threw his Starsilver spear into a "black tide" of beasts to save a maiden under attack. Ancient songs say the blow shattered snow-covered mountains and leveled three thriving kingdoms; the text adds that his spear pierced "the stone that held up the earth", and the maiden died anyway. The survivors hunted him as their enemy, and he gave up the warrior's life for good. His descendants later became Mondstadt's Imunlaukr Clan. Five hundred years ago, during the cataclysm, the princess's vision came true: Dvalin killed the black Abyssal dragon Durin over the mountain. Durin's blood seeped into the ley lines, his corrosive power was held in check by Vindagnyr's unnatural cold, and the frozen tree drew in his blood. Khaenri'ahn Ruin Guards also reached the mountain; their records speak of a "skyborne power" their nation could not forgo, and of failure.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
The ruins are sad and frozen: snow-choked halls and broken towers, ice that will not melt, and a deadly cold that drains anyone without a source of warmth. The weathered memorials feel ancient. Modern Mondstadters know almost nothing of the kingdom. Even Albedo, who has studied Dragonspine for years, knows it mainly from its stone tablets.

## dangers
### LARGE
The ruins share Dragonspine's deadly cold and its Cryo hilichurls, slimes, Abyss Mages and dormant or active Ruin Machines. A Ruin Grader guards one of the old shard sites below the Entombed City outskirts. The Peak of Vindagnyr shifts between warm and subzero ground, and the subzero ground speeds up the chill.

## quests
@version: 1.2
### STANDARD
In the world quest **In the Mountains** (1.2), the adventurer Iris asks the Traveler to look into the strange ice blocking Dragonspine's routes. Thawing the three frozen fragments of the Skyfrost Nail frees the Frostbearing Tree and restores the nail above the summit. Hidden exploration objectives on the mountain gather the Priest's, Princess' and Scribe's Boxes, light the Ancient Carvings, and open the Mural Room, where the Traveler finds the greatsword Snow-Tombed Starsilver.
### LARGE +
In **Ripples That Will Never Reach** (Archon Quest, 6.2), Razmaspes, a man from an ancient age whose ancestors lived in Hyperborea, asks the Traveler's party about Sal Vindagnyr. Albedo replies that he has seen its name on Dragonspine's stone tablets but that only its ruins remain. Razmaspes sighs that there was no way to turn things around.

## status
@version: 7.1
### LITE
As of 7.1 Sal Vindagnyr is a dead kingdom whose ruins lie frozen under Dragonspine's snow. The Skyfrost Nail that destroyed it hovers, restored, above the summit.
### STANDARD +
In the 7.1 world quest Anomaly: Mondstadt, the nail has changed in the same way as the other Celestial Nails. Albedo says he had assumed that the nail's purpose ended once the Heavenly Principles passed judgment and it destroyed the ancient civilization. He now sees that it is not that simple, and he stays to observe the change.

## entry: The Princess of Sal Vindagnyr
@keys: Princess of Sal Vindagnyr, princess of Vindagnyr, Sal Vindagnyr princess
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The princess of Sal Vindagnyr was the daughter of the chief priest Varuch. She was born under the sacred white tree and blessed with the ability to foresee the future, which she painted into frescoes. She dreamed of a black dragon blotting out the sun and took it as an omen of doom. After the Skyfrost Nail fell, she gave Imunlaukr the Starsilver greatsword and promised him a fresco of his likeness. She could not finish the fresco of the thaw her father hoped for. She died on a small island, trying to graft a branch of the shattered tree, and the scribe buried her.

## entry: Imunlaukr of Sal Vindagnyr
@keys: Imunlaukr
@keys2: Sal Vindagnyr, Vindagnyr, Dragonspine, Starsilver, princess
@version: 6.6
### STANDARD
Imunlaukr was an outlander hero living in Sal Vindagnyr when the Skyfrost Nail fell. He left to find a way to break the ice; one account says he went looking for a branch of Irminsul. The princess gave him the Snow-Tombed Starsilver. He came back too late, found no one alive, cursed the heavens, and left the greatsword in the Mural Room. His descendants became Mondstadt's Imunlaukr Clan.
### LARGE +
Lore added in 6.6 says that a single blow from his spear, struck to save a maiden, is remembered in song as ruining three kingdoms. He was hunted afterwards and gave up the warrior's life.

## entry: The Ancient Carvings and the Three Boxes
@keys: Ancient Carvings, Priest's Box, Princess' Box, Princess's Box, Scribe's Box
@keys2: Dragonspine, Sal Vindagnyr, Vindagnyr
### STANDARD
Eight carved stone tablets on Dragonspine, apparently left by the kingdom's last scribe, record its founding and its end, and the Traveler can light them up. Three ornamented boxes, the Priest's, the Princess' and the Scribe's, hold their owners' last thoughts. Varuch hopes his daughter's third fresco will show a thaw. The princess cannot paint it and says goodbye. The scribe wishes ruin on the world and turns to a godless new nation. Together, the boxes open a sealed door on the mountain.

## entry: The Frostbearing Tree's Origins
@keys: Frostbearing Tree, white tree of Vindagnyr
@keys2: Sal Vindagnyr, Vindagnyr, princess, Skyfrost Nail
### STANDARD
The Frostbearing Tree was Sal Vindagnyr's sacred white tree, an Irminsul tree. The Skyfrost Nail shattered it and it froze. Five hundred years ago it absorbed Durin's blood, and after the Traveler broke its icy shell and fed it Crimson Agate, it bore one crimson, icy fruit: the catalyst Frostbearer. The fruit is dedicated to those who lived in its shade, to its priests, and to the girl who painted it.

@version: 6.2

### LARGE
Later texts place Sal Vindagnyr among the ancient human peoples of the unified-civilization era. A prayer to Maadteraahka (Naberius) says she was revered "from Hyperborea to Sal Vindagnyr", and Razmaspes, a man of an ancient age whose ancestors lived in Hyperborea, still remembered it by name. An artifact text says pilgrims climbed Vindagnyr to honour the three moons. The Amethyst Crown says the heavens sent the divine nails to "mend the land" and laid mortal realms to waste. Sal Vindagnyr, Hyperborea and Lang-Gan are each tied to a fallen nail.
### ULTRA +
The text of the Starlight of Fylkir weapon appearance, a limited reward from Stygian Onslaught in 7.0, says that in an age beyond recollection a divine envoy of the high heavens gave this spear to the high priest of Sal Vindagnyr, granting the right to lead "the city-state wreathed in verdant shade".
