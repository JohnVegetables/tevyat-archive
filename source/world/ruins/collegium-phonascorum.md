## summary
### LITE
The Collegium Phonascorum (Latin, roughly "college of music teachers") was the educational centre of Remuria, set in the inner city Capitolium under the sea off Fontaine. There the Harmosts and music lecturers taught Remurians about the symphony of Phobos. Notes left among its ruins record a Harmost's reasoning about music, will and fate, and show how a learned institution fed a brutal ideology.
### STANDARD +
Introduced in 4.6. The notes divide all things into Substance, Property, Quality and Relation, and conclude that people shaped by Fortuna must be eradicated. The notes are unsigned, so whether this was official doctrine is not stated.

## geography
### STANDARD
A subarea of Capitolium, near the Alta Semita and the approach to the Domus Aurea. Two golden Auric Anglerfish drift near it and have trapped a creature that Monsieur Os remarks on.
### LARGE +
It lay inside the inner city, which could be entered only through the Initium Iani and the Passage of Ianus.

## culture
### STANDARD
Remuria's power rested on music. The Collegium taught that the symphony of Phobos, a composite of mortal melodies, could challenge the order of Fortuna, the fate that governs mortals and gods alike. Lecturers were the Harmosts themselves and other teachers.
### LARGE +
Harmost's Notes (II) explains the Remurian philosophy in musical terms. Musica Humana (the music of a person) should match Musica Mundana (the music of the world); the "Piercing of the Ego" (to play one's own music) means piercing Substance and Quality and so controlling one's own will. The notes treat the Quadrant of Qualities as memories, wishes, soul and persona.
### ULTRA +
The notes hold that even gods and mortals are fragile, like taut strings, and so the instruments' ratios must be adjusted "to avoid profaning the melody of morality", blending mortal melodies into the symphony called Phobos. If Phobos stopped, everything would collapse, and all would sink to dreamless dreams under Fortuna.

## history
### LARGE
Canon records the Collegium only through this text and its Latin name. The notes were presumably written while Remuria still stood. The notes argue that people who stand apart from Phobos "cannot be regarded as humans equal to us", a line of thought that echoes the Remurian war against the northern tribes; canon does not say who wrote the notes or whether they were official teaching.
### ULTRA +
The notes reason that distance from Phobos is betrayal; the louder a mortal's music and the firmer a mortal's wish, the firmer Fortuna's order becomes. They argue that such people have no real choice, only a path fated for them, and conclude on grounds of "morality and rationality" that they should be eradicated like plagues and wildfires, else Fortuna's symphony will grow to a deafening roar and no one will ever again be able to choose rebellion.

## quests
### STANDARD
Part of the area is used in Golden Aqueduct Reconstruction: Part II, a child quest of Gradus ad Capitolium. Monsieur Os comments on the two Auric Anglerfish near it.
## status
### LITE
Ruined and silent as of 7.1, a sunken place whose lessons now serve only as record.

## dangers
### LARGE
Two Auric Anglerfish near the ruin; beyond it the Domus Aurea is guarded by Praetorian Golems.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
A hall of broken columns and silent instruments, still faintly resonant with fragments of painted music. The mood is solemn and a little chilling because of the notes.

### STANDARD
An unsigned lecture note recording Aurelius's fourfold division of phenomena: Substance, Property, Quality and Relation, with Quality as the "Quadrant of Qualities" of memories, wishes, soul and persona. It describes the Piercing of the Ego, argues for tuning the human symphony into Phobos, and ends in a call for eradication of those it deems merely fated players of Fortuna's score.
