## summary
### LITE
Khaj-Nisut ("Eye of the Sands" on the map before the Golden Slumber quests) is the throne temple of King Deshret, also called Al-Ahmar, buried in the Great Red Sand of Sumeru's desert. It is invisible until a visitor passes three trials, and the temple is built to recognise who is acknowledged: the scarlet slate that a visitor carries opens its doors, and its mechanisms test clearance. Inside, an immense empty throne sits at the top of a descent through lower halls. In the Golden Slumber quests the Eremite Samail was tricked onto the throne, entered the "Golden Slumber" that King Deshret's followers believe in, and Jebrael followed him in to stop him as the temple sealed behind them.
### STANDARD +
The name is Egyptian-derived for "appearance of the king", and the Chinese name uses a term for the manifestation of the sacred. The Scarlet Sand Slate calls it the sacred realm that all King Deshret's people aspired to reach, and warns that whoever acquires Al-Ahmar's wisdom loses their bodily form and cannot tell of it. The surrounding ruins are also the City of Gold domain, which the domain text describes as a stronghold once defended by seven battlements that crumbled overnight when King Deshret fell.

## geography
### STANDARD
Khaj-Nisut lies in the Land of Lower Setekh, part of the Great Red Sand, in Sumeru's desert. An oasis in front of it is a resting place for travellers. The temple does not exist on the map or to the eye until the visitor has passed the three trials set around it and operated a Plinth of the Secret Rites; then it manifests. Inside it is a descent, not a climb: a lower floor, further halls below it, then a lift that carries visitors up to the throne room under the dome of Khaj-Nisut.
### LARGE +
The Scarlet Sand Slate's guidance for Khaj-Nisut says the throne sits under the dome and that "to ascend to a high place, one must first look down". The ruins are reached from the same desert as the Mausoleum of King Deshret and Opet Hall, whose underground map points visitors toward Khaj-Nisut; the slate lists them as stages of one journey (Abdju Road, Khemenu Temple, the Mausoleum, Opet Hall, Khaj-Nisut, Duat Hall and onward). The wiki lists the point of interest as a subarea of the Land of Lower Setekh, while the Mausoleum is placed in the Hypostyle Desert; the quests treat them as parts of one complex.

## places
### STANDARD
Entrance gateway and oasis: the oasis outside is where travellers refresh themselves before paying tribute. Lower floor and descent: halls with mechanisms (Plinths of the Secret Rites, a central device that must be set in action in three steps). Throne room: the throne of Al-Ahmar on the upper floor, beside a statue of Heryshaf (as pictured on the wiki).
### LARGE +
The City of Gold domain is set in these ruins; its in-game description speaks of a desiccated temple on sundered rocks, "more akin to a tomb for an age of radiance long lost", where gold-eyed warriors once stood guard over watchtowers. Returning to the throne room after the quest, a visitor can reach it again as an ordinary exploration site.

## people
### STANDARD
Samail of the Thutmose, an Eremite faction that wants King Deshret's lost power, and Jebrael, once his partner as one of the "twin blades of Thutmose" and now a father, reach the throne in the story. Jeht (Jebrael's daughter), the Akademiya researcher Tirzad, the Traveler and Paimon enter with Benben, a small Primal Construct that responds to the ruins. Nobody lives in Khaj-Nisut. The only residents are the Primal Constructs and mechanisms of King Deshret's age and, during the quest, Samail's Eremite mercenaries and Fatui Skirmishers.
### LARGE +
The Fatui are interested in the power of King Deshret and are found in the desert ruins in general. A missive signed by Samail that players can read in a tent in The Secret of Al-Ahmar says that conditions for opening the path are close and that Khaj-Nisut remains "an essential part of the puzzle".

## governance
### LARGE
King Deshret ruled from here in the sense that the throne is his, but the texts treat the building more as a place of acknowledgment than as an administrative centre. The slate's inscription on Opet Hall says blessings gathered there reach the throne directly, and the devout would gain honour of access to the throne room; the Khaj-Nisut inscription says only those acknowledged may set foot and gaze on the temple of King Deshret.

## history
### STANDARD
Khaj-Nisut belonged to the civilisation of King Deshret, the Lord of Deserts, in the age before and during the Archon War. In the Golden Slumber quests (3.1) it is rediscovered: first the Mausoleum, then, after a series of trials, the temple itself. The throne is empty, and the "wisdom" of Al-Ahmar that texts promised there is not an object but a prayer.
### LARGE +
The slate says whoever obtains the wisdom of Al-Ahmar loses their body and cannot report it, which fits what the quest then shows. The Key of Khaj-Nisut sword (added to the game as a weapon) carries a legend of a key that sealed a homeland and hid a desert tower and "paradise of dreams" under illusions when the nations scattered; the story is told as a tale of a prince and a blind poet and does not describe this building directly.
### ULTRA +
A quest item named Khaj-Nisut, found in a chest during Dune-Entombed Fecundity: Part III, is a chess piece placed in the temple of Gurabad, built as a miniature of the Khaj-Nisut "which once belonged to King Deshret". The 6.6 Archon Quest does not return to the building itself, but it settles the fate of what its throne led into: Heryshaf reveals that the Golden Slumber is preserved within Aaru, and when the Seven Pillars open Aaru to help Sumeru against the Irminsul crisis, the souls held in the Golden Slumber return to the ley lines, ending King Deshret's afterlife for good. Jebrael's soul is among them.

## quests
### STANDARD
Golden Slumber (World Quest series, 3.1), especially The Secret of Al-Ahmar and Dreams Beneath the Searing Sand. The follow-up series Old Notes and New Friends begins with Dual Evidence, which grants the Ferryman's Clearance for Duat Hall and the Great Hall of Truths in the Mausoleum.
### LARGE +
Returning to the throne room after completing Dreams Beneath the Searing Sand grants a small reward (the achievement "Encore!" and a chest). The slate's "Clearance of Khaj-Nisut" is granted during The Secret of Al-Ahmar.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Primal Constructs and the building itself. In the quest, after Samail enters the Slumber, the temple turns hostile and the visitors have to flee through the lower floors while a "sea of consciousness" pursues them; if it touches a visitor the escape restarts in the game's time trial. Outside, Samail's Eremites and Fatui Skirmishers patrol the ruins during the quest.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Scale and silence. Gold and red stone worn by wind, deep halls lit by floating energy, a throne too large for a human body. The sense is of a place built to make a visitor feel they must earn their presence, and then of a building that still listens: mechanisms answering the slate, doors closing behind you.

## travel
### LARGE
Visitors need the Scarlet Sand Slate (obtained in Lost in the Sands) and, for the inner halls, the clearance that Tirzad's slate unlocks. The temple reveals itself only after the three trials and the operation of a Plinth of the Secret Rites. The Eremites and Fatui make the approach unsafe during the quest. Aaru Village, a refuge of King Deshret's descendants, is the nearest settlement and the base for expeditions.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the throne is empty and the temple is sealed after the Golden Slumber events. Jebrael and Samail never returned in body; Jeht kept her father's axe and went looking for the Tanit. In 6.6, when the Seven Pillars opened Aaru, the souls trapped in the Golden Slumber, Jebrael's among them, were released to the ley lines, and Jebrael spoke briefly with Jeht before departing.
### STANDARD +
No later canon through 7.1 changes the building itself, but the Golden Slumber that its prayer opened no longer exists as a refuge for souls. The City of Gold domain (3.3) remains in the surrounding ruins.

## entry: Dreams Beneath the Searing Sand
@keys: Dreams Beneath the Searing Sand, Samail's throne, Eternal Dreamland
@keys2: Khaj-Nisut, Golden Slumber, Jebrael, Jeht
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the last chapter of the Golden Slumber, Jebrael and Jeht, the researcher Tirzad, the Traveler and Paimon travel to the oasis for Khaj-Nisut while the Eremite Samail's men attack the camp. A coded letter says Al-Ahmar's throne shows itself to those who pass three trials. After the trials Khaj-Nisut manifests; the party activates its mechanisms, rides the lift to the throne room and finds the throne empty. Samail arrives holding Jeht and demands the prayer needed to sit on the throne.
### LARGE +
Jebrael recites a song that Jeht's mother Ufairah had deciphered from Al-Ahmar's texts. Samail sits on the throne, repeats the prayer and goes still: he has entered the Golden Slumber, a one-way trip. His mercenaries are beaten, but the temple turns against the visitors and Samail's voice speaks through it. Jebrael, who understands that the consciousness left in the Slumber can only be stopped from inside, goes into it while the others flee; Samail tells him he will soon become part of "us", and Jebrael answers that he is there to stop him. The door closes behind the fleeing party. Back at Aaru Village, Jeht keeps his axe and her mother's notes and plans to look for the Tanit tribe, which she learned was her father's. Tirzad prepares a paper and leaves the original slate with the Traveler.

## entry: The Scarlet Sand Slate on Khaj-Nisut
@keys: Scarlet Sand Slate, Clearance of Khaj-Nisut, Ferryman's Clearance
@keys2: Khaj-Nisut, Mausoleum of King Deshret
@version: 3.1
### LARGE
The slate Tirzad brings is an etched relic of King Deshret's civilisation that commands devices in his ruins. It has "clearance levels" unlocked by lighting symbols in different places; the clearance of Khaj-Nisut is granted during The Secret of Al-Ahmar. Its text on Khaj-Nisut says an oasis lies before the temple, that the illusion appears at the end of a threefold trial, that it is the sacred realm that all King Deshret's people hoped to enter, and that recitation of the prayers opens the way to a "golden slumber where wisdom gathers"; whoever gets the wisdom loses a body and cannot tell of the wonders. The slate is the in-game reason players can see the ruins at all.

## entry: Heirs of Thutmose
@keys: Thutmose, Twin Blades of Thutmose, Samail the Straight Shooter
@keys2: Khaj-Nisut, Eremites
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Thutmose is a group of Eremites, devout believers in King Deshret, who sought the secret of Al-Ahmar with help from researchers and even the Fatui; their base is at Lamb-Devourer Rock. Samail and Jebrael, both originally of the Tanit tribe and the survivors of an expedition to the ruins of Gurabad, founded or joined it and were known as its twin blades. Jebrael left after the birth of his daughter Jeht; the Golden Slumber quest says Samail killed Ufairah, Jeht's mother, because he saw her love as an infection weakening Jebrael. See the characters' own entries for their lives.
