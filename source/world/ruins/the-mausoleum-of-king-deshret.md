## summary
### LITE
The Mausoleum of King Deshret is the huge pyramid in the Hypostyle Desert of Sumeru's Great Red Sand that has long been taken for the tomb of King Deshret (Al-Ahmar), the Lord of Deserts. For years a great cyclone turned on its summit and researchers never learned why. In the Golden Slumber quests the Traveler, Paimon and Tirzad's expedition find the way in through a mural passage in Khemenu Temple and discover that the pyramid is only one corner of a far larger palace: a mausoleum with an empty sarcophagus, an underworld-like Duat Hall, a Great Hall of Truths and, at its highest point, Aaru's Shut.
### STANDARD +
The Scarlet Sand Slate describes it as the place where King Deshret left a golden husk while his intelligence lies elsewhere, with many more temples beneath it. After Dual Evidence the cyclone is permanently gone and the summit is open.

## geography
### STANDARD
Rises from the Hypostyle Desert in the Great Red Sand. Three levels are mapped: Main Hall, Middle Level and Lower Level. Opet Hall is an underground chamber just north; Duat Hall is reached by an underground waypoint directly south of the Mausoleum; the Great Hall of Truths connects to Duat Hall's south side; Aaru's Shut is the highest point.
### LARGE +
A stone tablet on a plateau north-east of the Sobek Oasis waypoint in the Hypostyle Desert carries an inscription about the sandstorm and burning sun exorcising darkness and guiding the dead back to the Orchard of Pairidaeza. The Scarlet Sand Slate says the hall under the sand cannot be seen whole and craftsmen "strung natural caves with thousands of corridors", so a traveller who knows the directions will still lose their way.

## places
### STANDARD
Entrance and Main Hall: giant doors, guardian statues and a sarcophagus chamber. Opet Hall: where blessings were said to reach the throne directly. Duat Hall: the bottomless hall, named after the Egyptian realm of the dead, where the guilty or disrespectful meet their destiny and those who pass the trials fly across an invisible river. Great Hall of Truths: where, by the slate, the wise and unafraid who bring praises to the king obtain unique gifts. Aaru's Shut: the summit.
### LARGE +
The slate names further parts of the complex: Sekhem Hall (the source of servants who rendered the king's opponents to a handful of sand), and the three trial halls Place of Rebirth, Place of Breath and Place of Swallowing. The slate also names Abdju Road, the entrance to the king's territory, and Khemenu Temple, whose murals show Deshret's reign, as earlier stages of the journey. See their own entries.
### ULTRA +
The wiki notes that Duat is the Egyptian-derived name for the realm of the dead, the Opet in Opet Hall echoes an annual Theban festival, and "shut" in Aaru's Shut is from the Egyptian word for shadow; the Chinese name of Aaru's Shut is "Aaru's Reflection". The Mausoleum's Chinese name simply means "mausoleum of the Scarlet King". The wiki also records that Opet Hall seems to connect to the Great Hall of Truths on its south side, though players cannot cross that way and must use Duat Hall.

## people
### STANDARD
Nobody lives there. In the quests the expedition of the Akademiya researcher Tirzad, with the desert guide Jebrael and his daughter Jeht, Benben (a small Primal Construct that is evidently connected to the ruins), the Traveler and Paimon enter first. Fatui forces and Eremites, including Samail, "the Twin Blades of Thutmose", are also working in the ruins. The Vahumana researcher Soheil of the Usaibia family, who explored the desert in his youth and never found Khemenu Temple or Khaj-Nisut, later learns of the interior through Dual Evidence.
### LARGE +
Soheil says generations of Vahumana researchers tried to open the mausoleum without success. He is accompanied by Hulwani of the Corps of Thirty, who reminds him that the sage has prohibited researchers from entering the desert without permission.

## governance
### LARGE
The slate's text on Opet Hall and the Mausoleum presents the king as a ruler of spectacle and devotion: the devout gain the honour of reaching the throne room, citizens travel freely and praise him, and Al-Ahmar "had no enemies" because those who paid him no homage were destroyed by servants issuing from Sekhem Hall. These are the slate's own words and reflect the king's followers' voice rather than a neutral record.

## history
### STANDARD
The building belongs to King Deshret's civilisation. Little is dated: the Record of Aaru's Shut, a stone slate left near the summit, is the fragment of a diary by someone who says Al-Ahmar made them the architect of Aaru, a project meant to protect the people after a disaster that killed the writer's father; the writer's mother blamed Al-Ahmar for the calamity in Gurabad, which the writer rejects. The writer hopes Aaru will encompass the desert, restore Gurabad and carry out Al-Ahmar's ambition, and the record breaks off when they hear a loud explosion.
### LARGE +
Soheil explains that nearly all records of the later period of Al-Ahmar's civilisation were lost, and that even occasional finds are fragments; the Record is a perfect slate. The Golden Slumber series (3.1) shows the pyramid as part of a complex with an elevator, a hidden passage in Khemenu Temple, and three trial places that Tirzad and the slate call the king's "organs" (Rebirth, Breath, Swallowing). King Deshret himself was one of the Three Eternal Companion-Gods and died during the Archon War, according to his wiki page.

## quests
### STANDARD
Golden Slumber (3.1), especially The Secret of Al-Ahmar: the party enters through a mural passage in Khemenu Temple, rides an elevator and reaches the Mausoleum, fights Primal Constructs, lights four Primal Torches to open a sarcophagus that proves empty, reaches the highest floor, falls into what seems to be a trap that separates the party, defeats Samail and the Fatui, and then pushes deeper into Opet Hall. Old Notes and New Friends (3.1): Dual Evidence and Soheil's Wish.
### LARGE +
In Dual Evidence the Traveler, with the stone slate and the Ferryman's Clearance, goes through Duat Hall and the three trial places (rebirth, breath, swallowing) to the Great Hall of Truths, activates four Plinths of the Secret Rites, passes through a portal, lights three Primal Torches to activate a second device that carries them to Aaru's Shut at the top, beats a guardian construct and finds the Record. Soheil, shown the Record, says he has never seen so perfect a slate. After this the cyclone no longer turns. In Soheil's Wish, visiting the sarcophagus room afterwards, Paimon notices a hole in the floor that was not there before.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Primal Constructs (Repulsors, Prospectors and guardians), traps that separate a party, mechanisms that need clearance, Fatui Skirmishers and Eremites in places, and Duat Hall's bottomless drop. Before Dual Evidence, the summit's cyclone made the pyramid unclimbable.
### LARGE +
The slate's guidance for the Mausoleum warns visitors not to tarry on account of the golden vessel, to measure the buried hall by foot and not step hastily down the wrong path, and notes that thousands of corridors will confuse even a skilled navigator.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Immense stone rising out of the sand, a column of wind on the roof before Dual Evidence, then corridors, murals and statues beneath. The Mausoleum is meant to make a visitor feel small. In the quests it also feels watched: doors respond to Benben, lights wake as torches are lit, and the slate speaks in short verses.

## travel
### LARGE
Soheil says generations of Vahumana researchers failed to open the Mausoleum; the Traveler's party succeeds with the Scarlet Sand Slate, whose clearance grows as its symbols are lit. A waypoint is unlocked at the Mausoleum and another underground, directly south of it, gives access to Duat Hall. The Old Notes and New Friends quests begin at Aaru Village.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Mausoleum is open to those with the slate. The cyclone is off, the summit changed, and Aaru's Shut is a permanent destination through the Great Hall of Truths.
### STANDARD +
Soheil studied the Record and counts it as a major find. In the 6.6 Archon Quest the Mausoleum and Aaru's Shut became the allies' working base during the Irminsul crisis: the Seven Pillars agreed to open Aaru, and the reconfigured Aaru has since taken over Irminsul's role of collecting and storing information. No later change to the site is shown through 7.1.

## entry: Aaru's Shut and the Record
@keys: Aaru's Shut, Record of Aaru's Shut
@keys2: Mausoleum, Deshret, Great Hall of Truths
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Aaru's Shut is the highest point of the Mausoleum. The slate's description says it should have been paradise on earth and the crown jewel of the desert but now looks very different. The word Shut in the name is, according to the wiki, from the Egyptian word for shadow. At the summit the Traveler defeats a guardian construct and finds a chest with the Record of Aaru's Shut.
### LARGE +
The Record's author, never named, calls Aaru's primary form nearly complete and promises that visitors will cross a river between the underworld and Aaru in the form of a vulture and falcon, pecking three organs and burning "embers of the sun" with feathers at three places. The text matches what the slate told the Traveler. Nothing says who wrote it beyond their self-description as the child of a fugitive and a maid.

## entry: The Great Hall of Truths and Duat Hall
@keys: Great Hall of Truths, Duat Hall, Ferryman's Clearance, Plinth of the Secret Rites
@keys2: Mausoleum, Deshret, Scarlet Sand Slate
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
Duat Hall needs Ferryman's Clearance, granted when Dual Evidence starts. It is a bottomless hall, linked to the trial places of Rebirth, Breath and Swallowing. Visitors who pass reach the Great Hall of Truths, where a giant device and four plinths open a path. According to the slate, those who bring praises before the king, who are wise and who do not fear the dark may obtain unique gifts here. A portal beyond the plinths leads to a second giant device; once its three Primal Torches (the "embers of the sun") are lit, the device lifts the party to the summit.
### LARGE +
The three trial places promise, by the slate, an unalterable form (Rebirth), everlasting bliss (Breath) and freedom from hunger and thirst (Swallowing).

## entry: Dual Evidence and Soheil's Wish
@keys: Dual Evidence, Soheil's Wish, Soheil, Kalantari
@keys2: Mausoleum, Deshret, Aaru Village
@version: 3.1
### LARGE
Aaru Village, after Tirzad's paper on the Golden Slumber (a study of governance in Al-Ahmar's later period), draws forty-six Akademiya students who want to enter the desert, and the Vahumana researcher Soheil is sent to turn them back. Kalantari, who asks for the Traveler's help, is the last. Soheil first dismisses Tirzad's paper, partly because the sandstorm at the top of the Mausoleum looked as he remembered it from his youth, then formally apologises, on behalf of himself and his family, once the Traveler returns with the Record. In Soheil's Wish he gives the Traveler his old archaeology notes as a "gift" so they can explore further sites for him, since the sage forbids researchers from entering the desert.

## entry: The Mausoleum in the Irminsul crisis
@keys: Seven Pillars, Aaru mainframe
@keys2: Mausoleum, Aaru's Shut, Deshret, Aaru
@version: 6.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
In Song of the Welkin Moon Act IX (As All Falls to Emptiness, 6.6) the Irminsul crisis brought the Traveler, Nicole, Nefer and the Akademiya's sages and scholars into the Mausoleum, where they investigated Aaru's Shut and met Thoth, Heryshaf and Sobek. Once Nefer gave Thoth the answer "time", the three were released and, joined by Shesepankh and Bennu, with Cyno speaking for Hermanubis and Apep already aiding Nahida, the Seven Pillars agreed to open Aaru; the soul of Jebrael was among the first to emerge before it dissipated. In Act X (Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana) the Mausoleum served as the allies' base: Bennu handed Kaveh Aaru's mainframe, Kaveh and Alhaitham worked out how to override Aaru, and Nahida joined them at Aaru's Shut. After Nahida burned Irminsul, the reconfigured Aaru took over its role of collecting and storing information.
