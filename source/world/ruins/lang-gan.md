## summary
### LITE
Lang-Gan was a great nation of Teyvat's unified civilisation, on what is now the western coast of Liyue. Its people lived by the mines and were guided by Zibai, an angel who served Celestia as a Moon Envoy and whom they worshipped as a god of soil and grain. In the war of funerary flame the Abyss tainted it, and Celestia sent a Celestial Nail to purify it. Zibai died holding the Nail back so that her people could flee, and the impact made the Chasm.
### STANDARD +
The Inverted City and Nameless Ruins under the Chasm are what is left. Some survivors went east and founded Chenyu Vale, taking their old rites; Zibai was later remembered in Liyue as the White Horse Adeptus.

## geography
### STANDARD
A coastal nation whose settlements ran from peak to peak across the crystal-veined mountains where the Chasm now yawns. Wiki-cited texts say it was a great and lovely land, made to bless the children of humanity. A weapon legend places it on the western coast before Mt. Tianheng rose.
### LARGE +
The same legend says the Celestial Nail left "chasms deep as the whale dives". The nail itself is the Spiritstone, still visible in the Chasm's depths.

## people
### STANDARD
The Lang-Gan people mined jade and ore, and their lives and families revolved around the mines. Zibai taught them silk painting, farming, spinning and weaving. At the dawn of the nation she gave the longbow Yayu to the high priest, which gave him the right to lead as emperor. The people had no books; they kept history in ballads that poetry collectors later wrote down for the court.
### LARGE +
Chenyu Vale families such as those of Yelan and Lan Yan descend from refugees who fled the Chasm. The elders of Qiaoying say the vale's ancestors came from a chasm to the south.

## culture
### STANDARD
Lang-Gan honoured the Three Moons, and above all Zibai, at a finely carved jade altar, with offerings and sacrifices. Its people held the Three Moons in awe along with other civilisations of the Moon era such as Hyperborea, Arcadia, Sal Vindagnyr and Meropis. In memory visions, Lang-Gan's people released lanterns at the altar when a Trilunar Syzygy filled the sky.
### LARGE +
Item texts imagine roasted millet and boar offerings and earthen drums. In the Lanternlit story Zhongli muses that some of Lang-Gan's jade may have become part of the foundations of Liyue Harbor; this is speculation, not a stated fact.

## governance
### STANDARD
A priest-emperor ruled under Zibai's guidance; Zibai herself was the divine envoy of Celestia and the people's patron deity.

## history
### STANDARD
After the Eternal and Iridescent Moons shattered in the war of funerary flame, Abyssal corruption tainted Lang-Gan. The Heavenly Principles answered by sending Celestial Nails to heal the land. Istaroth advised Zibai to let it fall, but she refused to abandon her people, held the Nail back for a time at the cost of her life, and let the inhabitants flee. Istaroth preserved her soul, splitting it into the Three Deadly Selves. The refugees travelled east.
### LARGE +
Some survivors settled in Chenyu Vale, carrying their jade altar, rites and the memory of the Chasm; the vale's murals at Chizhang Wall show their departure under a Celestial Nail. The Nail ultimately created the Chasm and buried Lang-Gan under the hills.
### ULTRA +
Millennia later, Liyue folklore turned Zibai into the White Horse Adeptus of the Eight Adepts. In the Lanternlit story the Traveler and Zhongli reunited the pieces of her soul, and Zibai was brought back. In the Archon Quest of 2.6 Dainsleif had already recognised the Inverted City as the work of an older civilisation, without knowing it was Lang-Gan.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Memory of Lang-Gan comes as moonlight on jade: lanterns on a terrace, silver light over mountains, an unhurried people trusting their patron. Today it is a hush under the Chasm, a lost city glimpsed upside down.

## dangers
### STANDARD
The ruins are under the Chasm's Underground Mines, with Abyss Order agents, Ruin Guards and corrupted mines. Nothing remains of the nation as a place to visit.

## travel
### STANDARD
Not directly reachable; one sees its remains through the Inverted City and the Nameless Ruins in the Chasm.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 Lang-Gan is a buried ruin and a memory; its descendants live in Chenyu Vale and beyond, and Zibai has been restored.

## entry: The fall of Lang-Gan
@keys: Lang-Gan, Langgan, fall of Lang-Gan
@keys2: Zibai, Celestial Nail, Chasm
@version: 6.3
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
This account comes from the "A Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon" story (Lantern Rite 2026, 6.3, since kept as a story quest), shown as a memory by Zizhi, the wrathful fragment of Zibai's soul. A Celestial Nail appeared in the sky over Lang-Gan. Istaroth told Zibai that everything tainted by the Abyss must be purified, Lang-Gan included, that this was heaven's will and that she was only an emissary; Zibai answered that her people honoured and prayed to her and that she would die among them rather than live with the guilt. Her Jadelight Canopy halted the Nail's descent for a time and the people fled. She fell and Istaroth caught her, splitting her soul into the Three Deadly Selves; the survivors looked up at the one remaining moon. Zizhi says the tainted remnants of the war falling from the sky devastated Lang-Gan and that the descendants of Lang-Gan made their home throughout Liyue.

## entry: Zibai as Lang-Gan's guide
@keys: Zibai, White Horse Adeptus, Moon Envoy
@keys2: Lang-Gan, Liyue, adeptus
@version: 6.3
### LARGE
Zibai is a Geo-wielding former angel and Moon Envoy who governed Lang-Gan and later became one of the Eight Adepts of Liyue. She taught Lang-Gan its crafts, was called the God of Soil and Grain, and gave the first emperor the Yayu bow. See her character entry for personality and later life.

## entry: Lang-Gan's descendants in Chenyu Vale
@keys: Chenyu Vale ancestors, Lang-Gan descendants, Jadefall
@keys2: Lang-Gan, Chiwang Terrace, Chizhang Wall
### LARGE
Elders of Qiaoying say their ancestors came from a chasm to the south; Lan Yan says in a Chenyu Vale quest that her ancestors lived near the Chasm before moving. The Votive Rainjade rite, the Chiwang altar and the murals of Chizhang Wall carry their memory. Their old religion was lost; later generations accepted Rex Lapis's rule and became part of Liyue's people.

## entry: Lang-Gan as a name
@keys: Lánggān, Langgan etymology
@keys2: Lang-Gan
### ULTRA
The name matches an ancient Chinese word, lánggān (琅玕), for a precious gemstone variously identified with blue-green malachite, coral, chalcedony, red spinel or red jade, which fits a nation tied to jade.
