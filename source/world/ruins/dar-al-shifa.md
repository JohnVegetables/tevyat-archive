## summary
### LITE
Dar al-Shifa is an abandoned hospital in the Land of Lower Setekh, not far from Aaru Village. It once specialised in treating patients with Eleazar, the disease that covers the body in scabs and ulcers. Old notes found inside suggest that a doctor there was using patients as test subjects for dangerous treatments. In the Archon Quest the Traveler, Paimon and Alhaitham investigate it after villagers report crying at night.
### STANDARD +
The name means "house of healing" in Arabic, and the in-game viewpoint says it is named for a prayer for patients. It is now silent, missing both the doctors' bustle and the songs of recovered patients.

## geography
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
A weathered desert building, half full of sand, with a barrier hiding an older underground wing. A trapdoor leads down to a lower level that looks like a second hospital tucked inside the first. Tunnels beneath hold the remains of dead hilichurls. Eremites roam the surroundings.
### LARGE +
In the Archon Quest the entrance and the lower level are sealed by barriers that open only when three Dendro monuments are activated, with Alhaitham's Vision starting the process. Hidden Exploration Objectives in the area include the Eleazar Hospital Notes and Abbas' Escape.

## history
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
The hospital treated Eleazar patients, and rooms, labels and patient items such as bowls bearing patients' names remain. A medical file records a farmer called Abbas under a doctor named Amoudi, then continued by a doctor whose name is blacked out. The treatment documents describe replacement of a patient's skin and limb, and a physician's note links high elemental quantities in the body to the disease. The doctor writes of control without elemental power, a possible military use, and sees the patients as specimens.
### LARGE +
Specimen IV, Abbas, is said to have attempted several times to leave, citing a guiding "God's voice", which the doctor recorded as a delusion. An emergency notice in the hospital mentions a 23-year-old patient with a mole on his left forehead and encouragement not to give up hope. A weekly meal and duty schedule lists Ibrahim and Amoudi as physicians. The date and cause of the hospital's closure are not given.
### ULTRA +
The wiki treats the redacted doctor as likely Dottore, but the text of the notes only blacks the name out. A linked Hidden Exploration Objective, Abbas' Escape, retraces Abbas's escape route from the hospital.

## status
### LITE
Abandoned for years. In Cry From the Eleazar Hospital the party learns that the crying heard from the village at night came from here, and finds Razak, an Akademiya scholar and a senior of Alhaitham, in a mindless state.
### STANDARD +
The people who had been held here were already taken away when the party arrived, leaving drag marks from a cart. The hospital is later used as evidence in Alhaitham's reasoning about the Akademiya.

## dangers
### LARGE
Hilichurls and an Eremite presence nearby; a locked lower level that requires the Dendro monuments to open.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Quiet and dusty, with sand piling in the corridors. It feels like a place hurriedly left, with old schedules, vases and tableware still in place.

## entry: Razak and the night crying
@keys: Razak
@keys2: Dar al-Shifa, Eleazar hospital, Alhaitham
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the quest Cry From the Eleazar Hospital, the village girl Shani tells the party she hears faint crying at night coming from far away, and that the guards have heard similar sounds. Alhaitham leads the party to the hospital, where they find Razak, an Akademiya scholar whom Alhaitham says never trained in the forest or reached the Satyavada Life stage, and who now cannot speak and has unfocused eyes. Alhaitham links his state to that of the Eremite Mizri at Port Ormos.
### LARGE +
Alhaitham's reasoning: the Akademiya spread a false rumour of King Deshret's resurrection so that the Radicals would round up the exiled "mad scholars" for them, turning them from exiles into missing persons who could be used as material for Divine Knowledge Capsules. He is explicit that this is a deduction and that he is not acting out of pity.
