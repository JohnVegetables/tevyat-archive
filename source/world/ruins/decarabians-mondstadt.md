## summary
### LITE
Decarabian's Mondstadt, usually called Old Mondstadt, was the original home of Mondstadt's people: a walled capital built in a deep valley by the God of Storms, Decarabian, who ruled from a great tower. His people rebelled and killed him about 2,600 years ago, after which the new Anemo Archon Barbatos led them to Cider Lake. The valley is now the ruin-filled area called Stormterror's Lair.
### STANDARD +
Texts describe the city as a "dream of prosperity" created wholly by Decarabian's divine might. To his people it was a prison of wind.

## geography
### STANDARD
The capital lay in an extremely deep valley that may be partly artificial, in the area now called Stormterror's Lair in the Brightcrown Mountains. Tall bridges are a signature of the city and its surroundings.
### LARGE +
Ruins in Decarabian's style show that his realm spread across the Brightcrown Mountains and Windwail Highland, with ruin clusters in Brightcrown Canyon (leading into the city) and around Cecilia Garden, and as far south as the waypoint between Liyue and Dawn Winery. Architecture inside the lair suggests the city once opened to the northeast and northwest; a mountain now blocks the northeast gateway and the northwest waterway is dammed. How far north or west the realm reached is not known. The Wolvendom part of Windwail Highland has none of these ruins.

## places
### STANDARD
Decarabian's Tower stood at the middle of the capital and was his seat. The residential quarter is still clearly marked out in the ruins.
### ULTRA +
Cecilia Garden, a major settlement of the realm, still holds ornate Decarabian-style ruins. The Decarabian-themed weapon ascension materials (tile, debris, epic fragment, dream piece) are gathered at Cecilia Garden, and their item texts describe the city as his dream of prosperity.

## culture
### STANDARD
Decarabian micromanaged the capital, even deciding where each resident lived, and banned certain tunes and chords because they might signal dissent. His ruins show a distinct style: plant-like margent ornaments and a curled-ended cross (a cercelée) instead of the Celtic knots and triquetra found on other Teyvat ruins. The stonework has survived 2,600 years in good condition.
### LARGE +
Decarabian honestly believed his rule was loving and that his subjects bowed in adoration. His people bowed because the harsh winds forced them to, and the rebels used the windblume flower as a secret sign of solidarity.

## history
### STANDARD
During the Archon War the land was a frozen waste under Boreas, the wolf-god of the north, and Decarabian raised an impenetrable storm wall around his capital to shut out the cold. His subjects came to see themselves as locked in. About 2,600 years ago they rose in rebellion and slew him; the wall fell, and for the first time they saw the sky beyond it. A wind spirit among the rebels ascended as Barbatos, cleared the snow, and led the survivors to found the new city on Cider Lake, leaving the old capital empty.
### LARGE +
The rebels, led by the Nameless Bard, the wind spirit, Decarabian's former lover Amos, the knight Gunnhildr and a wandering red-haired warrior, won at a cost: Amos and the Nameless Bard died. Decarabian's death blew apart part of his tower. The Gunnhildr Clan had left the city some years earlier, found shelter through a weak wind spirit's help, and rejoined the revolt. Mondstadt's later histories call this era the Age of Kings, naming Boreas and Decarabian the Kings of Ice and Frost.
### ULTRA +
After the fall, a prototype Khaenri'ahn Ruin Guard (the first Field Tiller), damaged in a clash with Boreas' spirit, took up residence in the tower and for about a year fired on anyone who approached. Locals thought it was Decarabian's wrath and called it the "Tyrant's Final Fury"; it stopped once its power ran out. Five hundred years ago the wounded dragon Dvalin made the tower his lair, and Mondstadt, having forgotten him, came to call him "Stormterror" and the place Stormterror's Lair.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Silent, wind-scoured stone in a deep valley: tall arched bridges, broken pillars, plant-patterned carvings and strong air currents. It feels like a once-controlled city left to the weather.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Hilichurls, slimes, Fatui, an Eye of the Storm and Ruin Guards roam the lair, and its wind currents and heights are hazardous.

## status
### LITE
Abandoned ruins as of 7.1.
### STANDARD +
The storm barrier fell with Decarabian, and during the Prologue the ruins were sealed by a storm barrier of Dvalin's lair until Venti dispelled it, after which Dvalin was freed from the Abyss Order's hold. The area is open to travellers.

## entry: Decarabian's Wind Wall and Rebellion
@keys: Decarabian's storm wall, storm barrier, Age of Kings, Kings of Ice and Frost, Windblume rebellion
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Decarabian walled his city in gales that even Boreas could not scratch, because blizzards covered the land. The wall barred exit and entry, and his people felt imprisoned despite the warmth inside. The rebellion's symbol was the windblume. When Decarabian fell the wall dispelled and revealed clear blue sky.

## entry: Decarabian-style ruins
@keys: Decarabian-style ruins, Decarabian ruins, cercelée, Tyrant's Final Fury
### STANDARD
Ruins in Decarabian's style are recognisable by margent plant motifs and the curled-ended cross, and they lack the triquetra. They appear in Brightcrown Canyon, around Cecilia Garden, and at the southern waypoint of Windwail Highland. A Field Tiller sitting in the tower once fired on visitors, a year-long episode remembered as the "Tyrant's Final Fury".
