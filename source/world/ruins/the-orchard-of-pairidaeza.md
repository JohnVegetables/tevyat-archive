## summary
### LITE
The Orchard of Pairidaeza, or Eternal Oasis, is a hidden garden beneath Mt. Damavand in the Desert of Hadramaveth, Sumeru. King Deshret built it on a divine nail as a cemetery and memorial for the Goddess of Flowers, preserving a single moment forever: animals, trees and even the water are frozen in place. It can only be entered through Deshret's Glass Goblet above it.
### STANDARD +
Desert tribes tell of it as a paradise where the slumbering goddess is guarded by Ferigees, the last mother of the Jinn. When the Traveler, Jeht and the Jinni Liloupar reach it in The Dirge of Bilqis, Liloupar finds that the goddess is not there.

## geography
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Beneath the Goblet, the oasis is a still, bright garden of trees and a lake. Everything is fixed in place, including the water, which can be walked on. A central island holds three chairs, one for each of Sumeru's three god-kings, with abbreviated names written in Deshret Script. A withered Irminsul-like tree stands beside the island, which the wiki reads as showing that a Celestial Nail fell on Mt. Damavand.
### LARGE +
Three flower buds in the garden trace trails of purple flowers; moving them through the oasis reveals memories that can be set into the three floral thrones. The Dendro Treasure Compass does not work here.
### ULTRA +
The oasis lies under Mt. Damavand, whose name in the desert dwellers' lost language means "the mountain from which smoke and ash arise"; a vast tornado whirls around the mountain's center, which the later quest Homecoming reveals to be where Apep resides. The in-game viewpoint text for the oasis quotes a verse about leaves never seized by autumn and newborn spring-joy never subject to the curse of years.

## history
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
In a time only the Jinn could recall, King Deshret built a cemetery for his beloved, the Goddess of Flowers. With gems under the deep sands as a power source and the Jinn's help, he made an eternal oasis. Later tribes spread legends of it as ever-flourishing, ruled by the slumbering goddess and guarded by Ferigees. The Tanit, Uzza, Shimti and other matriarchal tribes call themselves Daughters of the Flower Goddess and seek the oasis for its endless knowledge.
### LARGE +
Ferigees, once a Jinni who served Nabu Malikata, chose to serve Deshret after her mistress died, because he promised a mausoleum. She feared the consequences of Liloupar's schemes in Gurabad and gave up her Jinn body to inhabit a machine on the Goblet, guarding both the mortals and the slumber she believed was there.

## accounts
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Three views of the place exist side by side. The tribes believe in a living paradise with a sleeping goddess. Ferigees believed the goddess lay in the oasis and defended it for that reason. Liloupar, after seeing it, concluded the goddess is gone and that the Lord of the Sands lied to the Jinn. The artifact text calls it a cemetery and argues that eternity is no paradise.
### LARGE +
The artifact text imagines a conversation among Deshret, Nabu Malikata and Rukkhadevata in which one voice questions why the dunes should be commanded to stop flowing and argues that eternity is no paradise. It is a poetic scene carried by the desert winds, not a record of a real meeting.

## status
### LITE
In The Eternal Dream, Ever Lush the equilibrium device that kept the oasis still was broken as the party entered, and the Jinni Liloupar says the oasis is destined to be buried by the sands in time. Jeht left her father's axe and her mother's notes beside a tree there.
### STANDARD +
Liloupar says the Goddess of Flowers is not there and feels no hatred, only emptiness. Jeht, a member of the Tanit tribe, finds calm in the place. Babel, the Tanit matriarch, is told of the oasis afterwards. After the Akasha Pulses quest, one throne bears Rukkhadevata's name in Deshret Script, according to the wiki (version 3.6 or later).

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Utterly still and bright: the air does not move, water has no ripples, and every living thing is paused mid-breath. Visitors report feeling calm and peaceful without knowing why. Beneath the peace lies a sense of loss, since the garden was made to keep something that is gone.

## entry: Ferigees and the Glass Goblet
@keys: Ferigees, Guardian of the Eternal Oasis, last mother of the Jinn
@keys2: Orchard of Pairidaeza, Goblet
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Ferigees, a Jinni who served Nabu Malikata and later King Deshret, guarded the oasis from a machine atop Deshret's Glass Goblet. She called Liloupar a traitor who had brought Gurabad down with honeyed words, refused her entry, and was destroyed when the Traveler fought her mechanical defenders. Her last words say the empty tomb will crumble. Desert legends call her the last mother of the Jinn, a gate guardian who blesses every mortal who comes to her.

## entry: Jeht at the Oasis
@keys: Jeht, Ufairah, Jebrael
@keys2: Orchard of Pairidaeza, Eternal Oasis
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Jeht came seeking the place her parents had spoken of. At the center of the oasis she set down her mother's notes and her father's axe beside a tree, which can be found there later. She said she might return after her adventures to lie down beside her parents' memory. Her mother's notes contain a lullaby of the eternal oasis written for her daughter.
