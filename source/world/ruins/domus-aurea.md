## summary
### LITE
Domus Aurea ("Golden House") was the royal palace of the kingdom of Remuria. King Remus, the Sebastos and God King, had it made from his ship Fortuna and designed it as a giant instrument on which the Grand Symphony, Phobos, could be played. It is the "heart" of Phobos and is protected by that power. It lies on the hill of Clivus Capitolinus in Capitolium, deep in the Sea of Bygone Eras, and looks strangely well preserved for something thousands of years sunk.
### STANDARD +
In Version 4.6 the Traveler and Paimon fight their way in with Scylla and Cassiodor to stop the Remurian Harmost Boethius, who had used Phobos to steal the souls of the people of Petrichor. The palace was the place where Phobos was finally destroyed.

## geography
### STANDARD
Seen from outside, the palace resembles a giant harp, with pipes extending outwards that once broadcast the symphony to the four corners of Remuria. Inside it feels less like a palace than the guts of an enormous machine. Its main levels are a lower level and an upper level; the upper level holds the throne room, the Sacellum Requietis, and a podium with the great harp.
### LARGE +
Golden aqueducts (the Alta Semita) once carried the will of Phobos through Remuria; they lie broken and have to be rebuilt before the gates open. The old book History of the Decline and Fall of Remuria describes a golden palace of towering copper pillars at the heart of Capitolium, with the king resting in its centre and hearing every note of the empire.

## places
### LARGE
Gates of Domus Aurea: they open only when the broken golden aqueducts are reconnected and the old symphony is played, matching what the score said.
Central chamber: where Boethius made his stand.
Sacellum Requietis: the throne room on the upper level.
The podium and harp: where the final Requiem is played.
### ULTRA +
Murals painted with the power of the symphony, like those in the Faded Castle, stand near the Alta Semita on the approach. A viewpoint here, "The End of An Ancient Dream", quotes Remus's last line about a dream where mortals had free will.

## people
### STANDARD
Remus ruled here and is known by titles such as Sebastos, God King and Imperator. Boethius and Cassiodor were two of his four Harmosts. Scylla, the Prince of Vishaps, was Remus's friend and the one to whom he entrusted his final plan. The Traveler can only see Remus in memories.

## history
### LITE
Remus took Sybilla's wisdom and his own power to make Phobos, which would let humanity take charge of its own fate against the prophesied destruction. The palace was its stage. Phobos took on a will built from the worst desires of the ascended Remurians, who had put their souls into immortal golem bodies, and it led them into war on the other peoples of Fontaine. Remus tried to end it with a secret Requiem, a self-destruct order for the symphony, to be carried out with Scylla; Scylla was lured into a trap and sealed, and the Requiem sank Remuria in a flood.
### STANDARD +
Millennia later, Boethius, whose soul was bound to the shattered Phobos, awakened it and invited Cassiodor to Domus Aurea to take part in a ritual to "take on flesh", a new body for himself as a new Sebastos and a new power over the world. His plan was to gather every will scattered through the waters.
### LARGE +
Cassiodor appeared to agree, joined Phobos, and betrayed Boethius from within, trapping Boethius and the symphony in one body. Phobos then spoke for itself, claimed to be the sum of every Remurian wish, and said it had devoured Boethius. Cassiodor asked the Traveler to destroy him together with Phobos, and he faded. The Traveler then rode a lift to the podium and played the final Requiem on the harp, sending the freed souls to the sea. Scylla later left to roam the waters.

## status
### LITE
Quiet after the Requiem. Phobos is gone for good, the souls it held have left, and the people of Petrichor are back to normal.
### STANDARD +
The palace still stands. Two viewpoints, one of them titled "Domus Requiem", become available after the story, and a soundtrack that reuses the Requiem motif plays at the harp.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Gold, machinery and a slow echo of music: choral swells, humming pipes and water moving through ancient channels. The air feels like something that has only just stopped playing. Where the old music was warm, the corridors now carry a bruised, mournful note.

## entry: Golden Aqueducts and the Gates of Domus Aurea
@keys: Golden aqueduct, Golden aqueducts, Alta Semita
@version: 4.6
### STANDARD
In Remuria the will of Phobos flowed along golden aqueducts into every corner of the empire. In Gradus ad Capitolium Scylla tells the Traveler that rebuilding the broken section before the palace will open its gates. They collect lost fragments of the symphony in the area, restore the paths, release stored Ichor, and play the symphony together; the gates swing open.

## entry: Boethius's Ritual in Domus Aurea
@keys: Boethius
@keys2: Domus Aurea, Phobos, Sebastos, take on flesh
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Boethius claims the palace lets him affect the present world directly because it is so close to the heart of Phobos, and Cassiodor fears that if the ritual succeeded, Boethius could pull the whole surface into his delusions and revert Fontaine to its ancient appearance. In the fight he appears in three forms: as a statue named "The Immortal Sebastos's Second Coming", as the "Bleeding Idol", and as Phobos, "Discordant Symphony". He rejects the people of Fontaine as inferior and sees the souls he trapped as a tool for Remuria's return.

## entry: The Requiem
@keys: Requiem, Remus's Requiem, final Requiem
@keys2: Domus Aurea, Phobos, Scylla, Remus
@version: 4.6
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
Remus's Requiem was the self-destruct order of the Grand Symphony. In his original plan he would unleash it with his own life, while Scylla controlled the unleashed primordial power in the palace and turned the Remurians back into flesh-and-blood people. Scylla's seal ruined the plan. In the present, Scylla's Ichor body still holds the Requiem; the Traveler plays it on the harp and the current of freed souls rises towards the surface, led by a golden bee.
