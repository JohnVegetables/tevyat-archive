## summary
### LITE
The Debris of Panjvahe is a dry river valley and the ruins around it in the Desert of Hadramaveth, Sumeru. Panjvahe was once an irrigated district under Gurabad, where nobles sailed ornate boats on the river and flew hunting falcons. Its water system failed, the district fell into poverty and unrest, and today the valley is quiet and mostly empty, with wild vultures where falcons once flew.
### STANDARD +
Panjvahe's fate is told in notes and inscriptions: neglected maintenance of the irrigation network fed by the water system at Mt. Damavand, shrinking oases, and a Gurabad council that refused to pay for repairs. The name is read as "five oases" in the Chinese and Japanese versions.

## geography
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
A broad river valley cut into the desert, with ruins along its sides and a hidden quiet pool in the valley. It lies in the same desert as Gurabad and Mt. Damavand. The nearby Fane of Panjvahe, a domain listed under The Sands of Three Canals, is a separate place: a hermitage that Gurabad's ancestors built for the Lord of the Desert and that now shelters desert dwellers and bandits.
### LARGE +
The Debris of Panjvahe has a fishing spot with desert species such as the Sandstorm Angler and several kinds of axe marlin, according to the wiki's fishing data.

## history
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
In the viewpoint's telling, the great river valley once irrigated Panjvahe, and nobles of Gurabad sailed on it in ornate boats. Records at Mt. Damavand describe the system behind it: Jinn fragments set into machinery regulated the water supply, which was more efficient than older methods. Over time the canal system was clogged by sand. The priests wanted large-scale maintenance but the Jinni Ferigees, who guarded the nearby Orchard of Pairidaeza, refused it because it might disturb the Goddess of Flowers's tomb, and offered some of her own power instead.
### LARGE +
Under King Parvezravan, maintenance funds were cut and the filtration system failed; a note says Panjvahe's irrigation network became unusable, mud and sand clogged everywhere, and the wealthy left the farmland. A note says the king seemed bewitched by Jinn-made spectacles and blind to the ground loosening beneath him. Gurabad's Records of Gurabad report the Council of Nobles refusing a general's request for funds, citing the district's long poverty and rebellion and the rise of the Brass Mask followers.
### ULTRA +
The same notes connect the failure of Panjvahe's water to Ferigees's weakening: she grew ever less responsive and by the last note lacked even the strength for wrath, though the records are the notes of a human official and not Ferigees's own words. Panjvahe's trouble is thus part of Gurabad's slow decline before its fall to a chasm.

## status
### LITE
Today it is an empty, quiet valley of the Great Red Sand, with the remains of the old district along the dry riverbed.
### STANDARD +
As of Version 7.1 nothing found changes Panjvahe's status as an abandoned ruin.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Dry stone and cracked riverbed, red vultures overhead, and the remembered image of gilded boats and falcons. A place of lost abundance, quieter than the ruins of Gurabad itself.

### LARGE
Three stone tablets near Mt. Damavand, readable only after pipe puzzles are solved in Dune-Entombed Fecundity: Part II. They record a priest's worry over falling water output, Ferigees's refusal of large maintenance, and the king's budget cuts, giving the best picture of why Panjvahe's irrigation failed.
