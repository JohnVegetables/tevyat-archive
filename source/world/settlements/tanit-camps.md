## summary
### LITE
The Tanit Camps are the tent settlement of the Tanit, an old Eremite tribe, on the southern border of the Desert of Hadramaveth in Sumeru. They were the home of Matriarch Babel and, for a time, of Jeht, the Traveler's friend. After the events of the world quest series Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters..., the camps are empty: the tribe is gone and the Vahumana Dastur Tanjiyy is the only person living there.
### STANDARD +
The camps are the Tanit's most recent settlement. The tribe prized words and learning but refused to trust the Akasha, so its tents hold many books that were smuggled out of the Akademiya's library. Visitors who arrive after the tribe's fall find ash, abandoned tents and supplies still in place.

## geography
### STANDARD
The camps lie in the Great Red Sand, on the southern border of the Desert of Hadramaveth. They are a cluster of tents around the Matriarch's tent, with water on the western side and a bridge leading to further tents to the north. Wadi Al-Majuj, where the final quest of the Her Foes series begins, is nearby.
### LARGE +
The camp's layout, as shown by the objects left in it: Babel's tent holds the Akademiya-marked contract and, after the Akasha crisis ends, a merchant's letter; Jeht's tent stands apart and keeps its own note and a list of names; Aderfi's and Masseira's tents hold books; a tent north across the bridge holds a scholarly archaeology anthology.

## places
### LARGE
Matriarch Babel's tent is the centre of the camp. Jeht's tent and the tents of Aderfi, Masseira and others surround it. A water source lies on the west side, where a book of lunar philosophy was left. Aderfi's tent is where the Tanit read their smuggled books; Masseira's is where a joke book was found with a sour note on it. Outside Babel's tent lie the signed contract with the Akademiya and, later, the merchant's letter. Jeht's tent door carries a short cheerful note saying she is out on business, with a stick-figure drawing of herself grinning. After her story ends, the namelist in her tent changes place.

## people
### STANDARD
While the tribe lived here, the NPCs at the camp were the Matriarch Babel, Jeht (with her companion Benben), the mercenary guards Mendas and Hugeweh, the farmer Yuften, the tent-holders Aderfi and Masseira, and the Falcon Tadhla. After Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters... only Tanjiyy, a Vahumana Dastur from the Akademiya, remains. He came to give educational assistance to the Tanit under a deal that Setaria had made and found the camp deserted.
### LARGE +
Tanjiyy treats the mystery as history: he sees the Tanit's tragedy as part of the same record as the legend of the God King, and he has sent his findings to the Akademiya and applied for a new project to study them. He says the General Mahamatra, Cyno, has promised supplies and protection for researchers sent to the camp. He found a few card decks in the tents and is willing to play the Traveler.
### ULTRA +
Tanjiyy also notes that he found a large number of books smuggled out of the Akademiya, and that any remaining belief in the Goddess of Flowers among the Tanit has probably vanished for good. His idle line, wondering what really happened to the Tanit, is the camp's last echo of the tribe.

## governance
### LARGE
The Tanit had no written law; they followed what was passed down in their blood and habits. A Matriarch led the tribe together with nine Elders, and the enemies they hated most were not outsiders but traitors within the tribe. Babel held the title until her death and, by the time of the Traveler's visit, had removed the other Elders.
### ULTRA +
Babel's authority rested on the idea that the tribe is a family, so anyone she judged a traitor had betrayed that family. Jeht, a "half-outsider", was used to deal with such traitors. Babel signed contracts on the tribe's behalf, such as the agreement hiring Tanit warriors to escort an Akademiya researcher across the desert.

## culture
### STANDARD
The Tanit are matrilineal and revere the Goddess of Flowers. They value words and knowledge but distrust the Akasha, so they educate themselves on long walks across the desert and with books smuggled out of the Akademiya. They trade crocodile products, spices and essential oils to Sumeru's markets and hunt Wenut for spices.
### LARGE +
The tribe also raised "Falcons", children lost in the desert and trained as devoted hunters, a custom wiki sources trace to a perversion of the old priests' archery ceremonies. Only a few Falcons are favoured by the Elders. Books at the camp are worn from use: even a volume of Fontainian film reviews has been handled until its pages discoloured. A tribe merchant's letter shows the Tanit's worry that Akademiya researchers who show interest in Gurabad or the Eternal Oasis would bring harm.

## history
### STANDARD
The Tanit are said to be the oldest tribe of the Desert of Hadramaveth, with many branches, and the camps are their latest settlement. The camps entered the story in Version 3.4 with The Dirge of Bilqis, in which Jeht brought the Traveler and Paimon to meet Babel. Babel secretly meant to seize the Eternal Oasis and its Jinni, Liloupar, and, after the Traveler and Jeht discovered the Orchard of Pairidaeza, ordered the Traveler killed. When the Traveler worked out the truth and told Jeht, Jeht turned on the tribe; the confrontation at the camp left Babel dead and the tribe destroyed.
### LARGE +
Babel had removed rival Elders with the help of Snezhnayan supplies and technology offered by the Fatui, then turned on the Fatui. She told Jeht the Traveler had joined the Fatui, and told the Traveler the same of Jeht, to separate them. At the camp's entrance, Babel said she wanted the Oasis for the tribe so it could topple Sumeru, and wanted Jeht as her successor. Jeht answered that her true mother was Ufairah, and refused to kill her best friend. The wiki's tribe page says the survivors were only those Babel had sent to warn the other tribes and exiles who had not been silenced.
### ULTRA +
Afterwards Jeht, with Benben following, left, free to belong where she chose. The camp stayed untouched, with ash and signs Tanjiyy found disturbing. He reported it to the Akademiya, and the General Mahamatra has promised protection for the researchers. Before that, in the Akasha Pulses, the Kalpa Flame Rises quest, a letter in Babel's tent shows the tribe hearing rumours that the Grand Sage Azar had been deposed and that book restrictions were being lifted.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
An abandoned desert camp: dry heat, tents with their belongings still in them, ash and stains, quiet where a tribe's noise once was. Books, cards and keepsakes make the place feel lived in. Tanjiyy's cheerful chatter is the only warm note.

## dangers
### LARGE
While the tribe lived, the camp was guarded by Eremite warriors and Falcons, and its Matriarch was hostile to anyone she thought a threat. After the tribe's fall, the camp itself is not a stated threat; the surrounding Desert of Hadramaveth keeps its sandstorms, Eremites and ruins, and the Fatui hid camps in the area during the Jeht quests.

## travel
### LARGE
The camps sit at the southern border of the Desert of Hadramaveth. Outsiders were admitted by the Matriarch's permission, which Jeht obtained for the Traveler and Paimon before The Dirge of Bilqis.

## quests
### LARGE
The Dirge of Bilqis brings the Traveler to the camps. Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters... (three parts: Behold, the Sign Comes Like A Thief...; Make Bright the Arrows, Gather the Shields...; For Her Judgment Reaches to the Skies...) ends with Babel's death at the camp's entrance. It is gated behind the series Apocalypse Lost and Rejoice With Me, for What Was Lost Is Now Found.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the camp is abandoned. Babel is dead and the Tanit are gone; only the Vahumana Dastur Tanjiyy stays, studying what happened.
### STANDARD +
Jeht left with Benben and no longer belongs to the tribe. No later story, including Nod-Krai and Snezhnaya, returns to the camps.

@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Babel's betrayal is the tribe's last chapter: she tried to turn the Traveler's friendship with Jeht into a weapon, then ordered the Traveler's death to control the Jinni Liloupar. Jeht refused, killed Babel, and the tribe fell. Babel had already sent members to warn the other tribes of Jeht's supposed treachery.

## entry: Tanjiyy at the Camps
@keys: Tanjiyy, Vahumana Dastur
@keys2: Tanit Camps, Tanit
@version: 3.4
### STANDARD
Tanjiyy, a Vahumana Dastur, came to the camps for educational assistance and found them deserted. He is friendly, treats the Tanit's fate as history worth reconstructing, and offers the Traveler a card game with decks he found in the tents. He does not know the real cause of the tribe's fall.

## entry: Smuggled Books of the Tanit
@keys: Akademiya Library emblem, smuggled books, Lunar Philosophy, Pursina's Essays
@keys2: Tanit Camps, Tanit
### LARGE
Many books at the camp carry the Akademiya Library emblem and were smuggled out. Titles include On Language and Rhetoric (IV), Pursina's Essays: 26, 101 Painful Jokes, Lunar Philosophy, Anthology on Desert-Based Archaeology (III) and a Fontainian film volume that failed Akademiya review. Heavy wear shows the tribe read them often. The tribe merchant's later letter says restrictions on many books are being lifted.
