## summary
### LITE
Port Ormos is the largest port in Sumeru and the nation's hub for trade and travel, in the Ardravi Valley beneath two giant trees with a river running to it from Sumeru City. Parts of it are under the Akademiya's supervision, but smuggling and shady dealing are more common here than in the capital. Prices run lower than in Sumeru City, Eremite mercenaries from many factions pass through, and students come to blow off steam.
### STANDARD +
Most long-term residents are traders and laborers who grew up in the area. Some students consider the port good luck.

## geography
### STANDARD
Port Ormos lies in Ardravi Valley, in the Dharma Forest, beneath two enormous trees; the river between them runs from Sumeru City to the harbour. Docks are divided into the Northern Docks, Eastside Docks and Westside Docks, with an Upper Tier above, and the Pharos Lighthouse stands near the harbour.
### LARGE +
Kaveh is credited with designing and building the port's arch bridge. A trading company advertises that it has opened a route to Dornman Port in Mondstadt.

## places
### STANDARD
Djafar Tavern, where Eremites gather and rumours spread; Shapur Hotel in the Upper Tier, owned by Shapur and managed by Sareh; Pharos Lighthouse, guarded by Araby; the Port Master, Reza, and his deputy Masrur; and shops and stalls selling fish, spice, jewellery and goods.
### LARGE +
Notices at the Port Ormos Bulletin Board include advertisements from trading companies. Jewelry shop owner Royinjan and Jazari (after the Vimana Agama quests) are among the people tied to world quests here.

## people
### STANDARD
Port Master Reza handles tax levies and customs and complains that reform of foreign trade is slow; Masrur is his deputy; Shyam is the sheriff. Merchants include Berbere and Rama (spices), Dilawar and Gauhar (trade supervisors) and Alexandra and Przheva. Eremite mercenaries and captains, such as Feroz and Matari, are present among the docks.
### LARGE +
Farahnaz is a researcher who tells the Orghana story. Eymen tends the bar at Djafar Tavern with the hostesses Nesmil and Roshana, and Pooria is a goldsmith. Cats Sisi, Lale, Rozan and Nargis also live around the port.
### ULTRA +
By dock: the Northern Docks hold the sheriff Shyam, the fisherman Kira, the sumpter beast keeper Salm, the street performer Taraneh and the snake handler Gulabgir; the Eastside Docks the porter Sanjay, the crafts merchant Shadi and the book collector Siger; the Westside Docks the ship technician Anand, the dockworker Bita, the cashier Fariba, the merchant ship captain Xiaodong and the Mahamata Mahshid. Araby guards the Pharos Lighthouse.

## governance
### STANDARD
Reza says his office levies taxes and runs customs, and smuggling is the most common offence; he asks people to report violators and to flag down Eremites for help with fugitives. Parts of the port are supervised by the Akademiya. The Corps of Thirty garrisons Sumeru City's own small port, which a guard there says is far easier to police.
### LARGE +
Reza adds that occasional fugitives try to flee through the port, and says bystanders should leave them to the Eremites patrolling nearby.

## culture
### STANDARD
A trading culture of bargaining, fishing and rumours; Sumeru's students come here when academic life gets stressful. Official text jokes about swapping boring lectures for floral-honey wine and a voyage across seven seas.

## history
### STANDARD
Farahnaz says Port Ormos was originally Orghana, the city of the Twenty-Nine Deys, pirate captains who allied to share power fairly and keep peace. Infighting weakened them, and a powerful sea monster wiped them out. A book, The Shepherd and the Magic Bottle, instead names Orghana as the Deys' city but mentions Port Ormos separately, and its credibility is questioned.
### LARGE +
The book is set fifteen years after the port was built, when a boy from the Gravelsong Dunes finds a magic bottle with a jinni in it. It is possible Port Ormos was built on the older city and Farahnaz uses the modern name. The name Ormos is Greek for a cove or small bay; it may also nod to the Kingdom of Ormus.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
A busy harbour beneath two giant trees, with merchants and porters on the docks, Eremite mercenaries on patrol, students taking a break from lectures, and, officially, a tempting promise of wine garnished with floral honey.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Smuggling, scams and fugitives; Eremite factions with radical views; and the Fatui, who figure in a world quest here.

## travel
### STANDARD
A river runs from Sumeru City to the port. A trading company's notice on the Port Ormos Bulletin Board says it has opened a route to Dornman Port in Mondstadt for a fruit business; a reply doubts whether fruit is worth importing over such a long voyage.

## quests
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
In the Archon Quest "Lost in Prosperity" the Traveler and Paimon reach Port Ormos looking for the Akademiya's missing item, overhear students, and watch Alhaitham quarrel with the Eremite group Ayn Al-Ahmar, who set a fight for a week later at the pier before Pharos Lighthouse, then wait for Eremites at Djafar Tavern. They help Alhaitham win the merchant Dori's trust and meet her contact in a warehouse. The next quest, "Ever So Close", brings the fight with Ayn Al-Ahmar and its leader Mizri.
### LARGE +
World quest The Price (3.0) involves the merchants Alexandra and Przheva, two low-ranking Fatui who want to leave the organisation by faking their deaths with the Traveler as witness, and a person named Nika. The Kaveh hangout quest "A Refuge in the Sands" (3.7) begins here at the port.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 Port Ormos remains Sumeru's main port, busy with trade and customs. No disaster at the port is shown.
### STANDARD +
Its trade reaches abroad: a trading company advertises a route to Dornman Port in Mondstadt, and Reza says new regulations limit foreign trade to Port Ormos.

## entry: Lost in Prosperity at Port Ormos
@keys: Lost in Prosperity, Ayn Al-Ahmar, Mizri
@keys2: Port Ormos, Alhaitham, Dori
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon learn at the port that extremist Eremite groups, among them Ayn Al-Ahmar, claim the long-dead King Deshret will revive. Alhaitham from the Akademiya quarrels with Ayn Al-Ahmar, and they set a fight for a week later at the pier in front of Pharos Lighthouse. He reveals that the missing item is a Knowledge Capsule, but will talk only after the Traveler becomes a customer of Dori, a shrewd merchant who is wary of Akademiya people because most of her wares break Akademiya regulations.
### LARGE +
In Ever So Close, Dori's help reveals that Ayn Al-Ahmar's leader Mizri holds the Divine Knowledge Capsule. At the fight Mizri goes berserk after using it; Alhaitham cuts his Akasha Terminal link, the Matra take him away, and Mizri murmurs the words "World... Forget me". Alhaitham quietly keeps the capsule.

## entry: Orghana and the Twenty-Nine Deys
@keys: Orghana, Twenty-Nine Deys
@keys2: Port Ormos
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Orghana, the city of the Twenty-Nine Deys, is said to be Port Ormos' predecessor. The Deys were pirate captains who allied to divide power fairly. Infighting led to instability and a powerful sea monster wiped them out. The accounts differ on whether Port Ormos stands on the same site.

## entry: Reza, the Port Master
@keys: Reza, Port Master
@keys2: Port Ormos, customs
### STANDARD
Reza is Port Ormos' Port Master, in charge of tax levies, customs and trade. He calls the port Sumeru's key trade hub and says the most common offence is smuggling; anyone seen with suspicious cargo is probably a smuggler. He wearily admits that reform of the foreign trade sector is very hard.
