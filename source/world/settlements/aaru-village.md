## summary
### LITE
Aaru Village is the largest human settlement in Sumeru's desert, in the Land of Lower Setekh of the Great Red Sand. Its people are the followers and descendants of the desert god King Deshret, and it is also where the Akademiya sends scholars it has exiled, known as Village Keepers. Uncle Anpu is the village chief, and Candace guards it. It is the Traveler's base while the Akademiya's plot against the Dendro Archon unfolds in Chapter III.
### STANDARD +
Candace says the mountain and lake where the village sits once belonged to King Deshret's territory. The village is built in an old desert style with intricate patterned facades, and sandstorms and Eremite politics shape life around it.

## geography
### STANDARD
The village stands in the Land of Lower Setekh, on the desert side of the Wall of Samiel. Houses are stacked in tiers: an upper tier with the Village Chief's House, a middle tier of shops and homes, lower tiers by the water and outskirts beyond. Water comes from a stepwell below the village, supplied by ancient desert-kingdom infrastructure. Caravan Ribat, at the foot of the Wall to the rainforest side, is the usual road in.
### LARGE +
The stepwell once yielded a bucket at a time. Later a former Kshahrewar Village Keeper, lucid enough to help, helped build the White Lift, an elevator that lowers to the water; Kazim maintains it and checks it every morning and night, and is training his son Kazan to take over. Local specialties are Henna Berry and Scarab. Dar al-Shifa, an abandoned Eleazar hospital, lies in the desert nearby.

## places
### STANDARD
The Village Chief's House is the village's meeting place, where Uncle Anpu lives and where visitors shelter from sandstorms. Azalai's shop sells goods and plants; the stepwell and White Lift supply water.
### LARGE +
According to the wiki's reading of weapon text and Safhe Shatranj, the Village Chief's House is a surviving border fort from the era of Gurabad. It was built under a paranoid prince to guard the line between desert and rainforest civilisations, and the village grew around it. This is an inference, not stated by a character.

## people
### STANDARD
Uncle Anpu is the village chief; he says he will help with food, lodging and advice, and urges anyone caught in loose sand or strong wind to take shelter in the village. Candace describes herself as a village guardian with spear and shield, and Dehya, a mercenary, invites friends to visit her in Aaru Village. Residents include Village Keepers such as Khalil (the mad scholar the boy Isak calls "Grandpa"), guards such as Belkacem, Raef and Kazim, and ordinary villagers such as Nermin, Granny Kulsoom, Shani, Azalai and Kazan, Kazim's son.
### LARGE +
Khalil is a Village Keeper whom the boy Isak calls "Grandpa": Candace explains that Isak's real grandfather died when he was very young, and Isak attached himself to this elderly mad scholar because of the resemblance. Isak asks the Traveler and Cyno for help when his "Grandpa" goes missing. Kazim is the White Lift's keeper. Nermin tends a flower with advice from Granny Kulsoom, and in Blooming Sands the guard Raef has the Traveler collect a flower pot and seeds from Azalai's shop and bring the pot to her. Merchants such as Bonifaz (a Mondstadt merchant) and Nachtigal also trade here.

## governance
### STANDARD
Uncle Anpu is chief, with guards on watch day and night. Inscription and weapon text say the village's founders swore off the rule of kings. Candace and Dehya step in when visitors start fighting, as when Cyno and Alhaitham clash.

## culture
### STANDARD
The village keeps the faith and traditions of King Deshret's people and, according to weapon and inscription text, its founders awaited the return of Al-Ahmar. Its founders chose the name Aaru for the "ancient dream of the gods". Uncle Anpu refused the Akademiya's request to take the Village Keepers back, and the village looks after them.
### LARGE +
Village Keepers are tolerated rather than valued: by Cyno's account the villagers at first resented having to take them in, until the strongest earthquake in living memory struck one night: a mad scholar crouched with his hands on the ground, glowing a soft green, and not a single house collapsed. After that the villagers treated the mad scholars more kindly and began calling them Village Keepers. Candace says she loves the village's old-style architecture, and often describes the village as what she means to protect, not simply a place.

## history
### STANDARD
Candace says the earliest inhabitants were survivors from Akhtamun, the city King Deshret built from golden sand and crimson rock somewhere in the desert. Other text describes refugees from the warring successor cities who, tired of bloodshed, left the desert under a well-educated blind heir from Tulaytullah, swore off kings and named their new home Aaru. At some later time the Akademiya made it a place of exile for scholars.
### LARGE +
After Deshret's death the desert civilisation broke into warring city-states. The refugees chose to preserve the embers of their culture and wait for Al-Ahmar's return. The village's fate in the Archon Quest turns on the Akademiya's misinformation about Deshret's return (see Chapter III events).

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Warm, dry and tiered, with patterned facades; when sandstorms rise, the village chief urges everyone to shelter in the village. Inside a village home, Candace says a home is about atmosphere: "Warm, intimate, safe, secure, peaceful...", and that everything she seeks to protect is there.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Sandstorms (Uncle Anpu says they, and occasional earthquakes, have recently grown more severe and frequent); rifthounds that come with the storms; Eremite radicals devoted to King Deshret; and, during Chapter III, the Akademiya's agents.

## travel
### STANDARD
Reach it from Caravan Ribat, the checkpoint between forest and desert. Dar al-Shifa, the abandoned Eleazar hospital, lies in the desert nearby.

## quests
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
In the Archon Quest the Traveler arrives with Paimon and Alhaitham; Cyno attacks Alhaitham, Dehya intervenes, and when a sandstorm hits Candace brings everyone into the chief's house. Village Keepers go missing, and the Traveler follows the trail to the desert. Smaller World Quests (Blooming Sands, Desert Bloom, Lost in the Sands) show daily life and the start of the Golden Slumber archaeology line. In the limited-time 3.6 event A Parade of Providence, Tighnari, who passed out from the heat during the Interdarshan Championship's desert round, is brought back to the village to rest.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the village still stands, led by Uncle Anpu and defended by Candace. Its missing Village Keepers were recovered in 3.1.
### STANDARD +
At the end of 6.6's Welkin Moon story Candace and Dehya say they are heading back to Aaru Village to hand out supplies and help people. No later threat to the village is shown in the material checked.

## entry: Dreams, Emptiness, Deception at Aaru Village
@keys: Village Keepers, Isak, Mizri, Delavar, Rahman
@keys2: Aaru Village, Candace, Cyno
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
When the Traveler reaches Aaru Village with Alhaitham, Cyno (the General Mahamatra, in self-imposed exile) attacks Alhaitham and Dehya intervenes; and a sandstorm drives everyone into the Village Chief's House, where Candace asks them to explain their quarrel. Uncle Anpu then tells them that the Village Keepers, scholars exiled by the Akademiya, have been disappearing. A boy, Isak, asks for help finding the Village Keeper he calls "Grandpa".
### LARGE +
The trail runs through Caravan Ribat, where Dehya's drinking buddy Zaki leads her into an ambush by the radicals Delavar and Engur, who are beaten. Zaki confesses that a cloaked man with a covered face, calling himself King Deshret's envoy, had them lure Keepers out at night with incense; Uncle Anpu suspects the Akademiya, which had earlier tried to take the Keepers away. Shani, approached through Alhaitham, tells of crying heard at night, which leads to Dar al-Shifa, where the scholar Razak is found. Alhaitham reasons that the Akademiya spread the rumour of Deshret's resurrection so that the radicals would gather the Keepers and bear the blame. To win the Keepers back from Rahman, an Eremite leader, Dehya offers her right arm as proof of resolve; he stops the blow and agrees to meet them the next day.
### ULTRA +
At the exchange Rahman brings only Isak's "Grandpa"; Dehya fights his gang, an earthquake strikes, and the old scholar glows green and shields the group as the sands swallow them into ruins of Deshret's era holding a record left by his priest Kasala. It says forbidden knowledge Deshret brought into the world drove his people mad, that Rukkhadevata had priests build temples infused with the power of life to temper the disaster, that the embers of the civilisation were preserved in Aaru Village, and that Deshret finally chose to sacrifice himself. Rahman accepts the truth and returns the Keepers the next day; and the culprits, Morghi and Judar, admit the Akademiya sages planned to make a new god with Dottore, using the dream samsara and Divine Canned Knowledge taken from mad scholars. Back in the village everyone agrees to resist the sages.

## entry: The Village Keepers
@keys: Village Keeper, Village Keepers
@keys2: Aaru Village, Akademiya, exiled
### STANDARD
Village Keepers are mad scholars the Akademiya has long exiled to Aaru Village; Cyno says they arrive incoherent but invariably calm down after some time there. Most still seem eccentric or unwell, yet some lucid moments are valuable: a former Kshahrewar Keeper helped build the White Lift. After King Deshret and the Three Magi, Khalil is listed among the village's Keepers.
### LARGE +
In the Akademiya's later project, the Keepers' ramblings were exploited: the sages' agents harvested Divine Canned Knowledge from mad scholars. Uncle Anpu says the Akademiya approached him earlier to take the Keepers back, and he refused.

## entry: Uncle Anpu and the Village Chief's House
@keys: Uncle Anpu, Village Chief's House, Anpu
@keys2: Aaru Village, village chief
### STANDARD
Uncle Anpu is the village chief and lives in the Village Chief's House. He is practical and humble, offering food, lodging and warnings about sandstorms; Nahida's All Schemes to Know reveals him privately wondering whether he is fulfilling his obligations as village chief. He appears in the Chapter III Act III and IV quests.

## entry: The White Lift and the Stepwell
@keys: White Lift, stepwell, Kazim, Kazan
@keys2: Aaru Village
### STANDARD
Aaru Village's water comes from a stepwell fed by ancient infrastructure below the village. The White Lift, built long ago with the help of a Village Keeper, lowers to the water. Kazim keeps it running, checking it each dawn and dusk, and plans to pass the work to his son Kazan.

## entry: Blooming Sands and Daily Life
@keys: Blooming Sands, Desert Bloom, Nermin, Raef, Granny Kulsoom, Azalai
@keys2: Aaru Village
@version: 3.1
### STANDARD
In Blooming Sands, the guard Raef asks the Traveler to fetch a flower pot and seeds from Azalai's shop for Nermin, his girlfriend, who is in poor health and convalescing at home. Nermin then cares for the plant with tips from Granny Kulsoom, and the daily commission Desert Bloom has the Traveler fetching water for her from the distant stepwell, chasing a kitten that chews leaves, or making a drink with Zaytun Peach for Raef.

## entry: Competition on the Sands
@keys: Competition on the Sands, Parade of Providence, Interdarshan Championship
@keys2: Tighnari, Aaru Village

### STANDARD
During the limited-time event A Parade of Providence (3.6), the second round of the Interdarshan Championship takes place in the desert around Aaru Village. Tighnari runs out of water and passes out from the heat, dizzy from the disturbed ley lines.
