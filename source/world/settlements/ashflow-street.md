## summary
### LITE
Ashflow Street is a dead settlement at the foot of the Cradle of Fleeting Dreams in the ruined island of Ochkanatlan, Natlan. The in-game viewpoint calls it the Unfinished Ashen City: a place where people tried to found a new country on the ruins of a city built by a forgotten race, and where only ash remains. The alliance that gathered to build it has long since scattered.
### STANDARD +
It is a Version 5.2 location in the Ochkanatlan area. What a visitor finds are fragments: scrolls, recordings and a tower, left by priests, rangers, scholars and young mystics from different ages.
### LARGE +
Because the notes are damaged and blanked in places, treat the street as a place of half-sentences. A scene set here should never deliver a full explanation.

## geography
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
Ashflow Street sits at the base of the Cradle of Fleeting Dreams, the castle-like structure at the centre of Ochkanatlan. Vucub Caquix Tower stands in the middle of the street. The soundtrack Ashes of Presumption plays there.
### LARGE +
Ranger notes found in the tower say the terrain on the way is far more tangled than expected; the writer blames Abyssal erosion for ruining roads that ought to have been passable, and says they marked the way for the return trip.

## places
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
- **Vucub Caquix Tower:** a tower in the middle of the street, with a lower level reached by an elevator. Several scrolls and notes lie inside.
- **Cradle of Fleeting Dreams:** the castle-like structure above the street.
- **Recordkeeping Secret Source Sentinel:** an automaton used as a recorder, holding two recordings made very far apart in time.
### LARGE +
The sentinel's older recording is an apology by someone named Manqu to Sakkuk, mentioning a feather accessory left in a stone box on a table and a promise left unkept. The newer recording is a dying adventurer's voice, aware that the Flower-Feather Clan's blockade made the trip unwise, who warns a companion to run from monsters.

## history
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
The viewpoint text says the settlement was an attempt to found a new country on top of an older city built by a forgotten race, by an alliance that has since broken apart. The notes in the tower give a picture of its last days: a recording officer and a temple, craftspeople recruited to repair waterways, and a halt to the waterway work after a deep well was overrun by a dark calamity.
### LARGE +
Warriors of the alliance sealed the entrance and burned the infected with flames said to be borrowed from the Holy Sovereign. Common people blamed the plague on the breaking of a taboo about offerings to the Lord of the Night, a priest tried to talk them down, and an old woman at the temple raved about the sun and the alliance's collapse. Three areas of the southwest sector were sealed by decree, yet the infected, though decayed, still walked. The last entry ends at a barricaded gate and a stained, unreadable page. All of this comes from damaged notes, not from a witnessed scene.
### ULTRA +
The notes name the recording officer Tlamachtihquetl and the craftsman Chupicua, who offered obsidian to avoid labour and was replaced by Manqu; the priest Chichiri tried to calm the public. Kamayuq warriors dragged the raving woman out, and the text hints that the alliance's laws would punish her. The alliance as a whole, and how this settlement fits within the wider story of Ochkanatlan, are covered by their own entries.

## people
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
No living residents. The street holds the voices of the dead: the alliance-era priest and officials in the tower, and later visitors from the tribes. The visitors include a young Spiritcaller of the Masters of the Night-Wind (Liriwu), a mountain ranger and a traveling scholar, who came looking for the treasure rumoured to be aboard the Tonatiuh.
### LARGE +
The Spiritcaller's notes say her mystic arts failed here: the voices of the Wayob could not be heard and even healing had no effect, and she wondered whether even her grand master, Sanhaj, could lift the curse. The ranger regrets bringing such a young child along. The scholar thinks an ancient scroll shows that dragon artisans once made a weapon against the Abyss, but the note breaks off at the realisation that they were deceived about what the treasure truly is.

## atmosphere
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
Ash, stillness and the sense of an unfinished plan. Voices from earlier ages survive in notes and recordings, but nothing answers back. The mood is melancholy rather than action-heavy: people's last words and unkept promises.

## dangers
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
The ruins are described as cursed and Abyss-corrupted, and a dying adventurer's recording warns of monsters. A Spiritcaller's note reports that healing and mystic arts failed here. The same recording mentions the Flower-Feather Clan's blockade.

## travel
@version: 5.2
### STANDARD
The settlement lies within forbidden Ochkanatlan. An elevator links the levels of Vucub Caquix Tower. The ranger's note says entering the city proper requires finding a mechanism that leads toward the sky-vessel, the Tonatiuh.

### STANDARD +

## entry: Vucub Caquix Tower Notes
@keys: Vucub Caquix Tower, Vucub Caquix
@version: 5.2
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Tower in the middle of Ashflow Street. Its scrolls and notes contain the ranger's account, the alliance-era priest's final record, a young Spiritcaller's diary and a scholar's analysis of an old scroll about a treasure on the Tonatiuh.
### LARGE +
The notes record that the expedition hoped to reach the sky-vessel, that the Spiritcaller was a child despite her confidence, and that the scholar's claim of a weapon against the Abyss ends with a warning of deception.

## entry: Manqu and Sakkuk Recording
@keys: Manqu, Sakkuk, Chupicua, Tlamachtihquetl
@version: 5.2
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In Ashflow Street, a recording sentinel holds an old apology from Manqu to Sakkuk about an unkept promise and a feather accessory, and a much later dying adventurer's recording. A Manqu also appears in the tower's notes as the craftsman who replaced Chupicua on the waterway repair crew; the texts do not state outright that this is the same person.
