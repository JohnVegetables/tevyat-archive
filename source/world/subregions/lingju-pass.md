## summary
### LITE
Lingju Pass is a mountain pass in Lisha, Liyue, made up mostly of old ruins and puzzle mechanisms. Legend says the ruins were once a stronghold of the Geo Archon Morax where mortals and adepti fought side by side, and a sign in the pass carries an anonymous poem about a divine order. The name is usually read as "divine law".
### STANDARD +
The pass hides one of the three Nameless Treasures that tell of the sealing of the Geo dragon Azhdaha, and a one-time domain lies close by.

## geography
### STANDARD
A subarea of Lisha with ruins, a lake with a floating platform, and Geo-controlled puzzle monuments. The one-time Domain of the Wayward Path lies between Lingju Pass and Mt. Tianheng. Local specialties are Cor Lapis and Violetgrass.
### LARGE +
Elite foes listed for the area include Ruin Guards, Blazing Axe Mitachurls and Abyss mages of several elements, alongside Fatui skirmishers.

## places
### STANDARD
The ruined stronghold; a signboard bearing a poem (see history); the lake and its floating platform; the Domain of the Wayward Path nearby.
### LARGE +
The lake puzzle uses four Geo monuments, each unlocked a different way: the northern one is free, the southern needs nearby Boom Blossoms triggered, the western needs two pressure plates, and the eastern needs an Anemo pinwheel. Completing them raises a wind current that lets the player glide to a fifth monument on a floating platform and open chests.

## history
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
The wiki records a legend that Morax built the ruins as a stronghold where mortals and adepti fought together, and vague tales of Morax's divine law, lingju, which may connect to the poem on the sign. The poem reads, in summary, that a divine order governs the world, that everyone must die whether noble or wicked, rich or poor, and that the creator shows favour to none, so the faithful praise the divine rule in song.
### LARGE +
Because the puzzle at Qingxu Pool says its ruins date from the Archon War, the wiki suggests Lingju Pass probably does too; this is an inference. A Nameless Treasure found here bears a pattern of the three adepti who helped Rex Lapis seal Azhdaha; Linlang identifies two of them as Mountain Shaper and Moon Carver, and the third has been deliberately ground away.

## quests
### STANDARD
And This Treasure Goes To... (world quest: investigate a strange camp, look for a missing scholar, defeat Treasure Hoarders for a key, open a cage and dig up the buried Nameless Treasure). Zhongli's story quest Historia Antiqua Chapter, Act II, No Mere Stone, passes through while following missing miners. Tartaglia's story quest Monoceros Caeli Chapter, Act I, Mighty Cyclops' Adventure!, begins here when Ruin Guards roam the wild and the Traveler meets a boy from Snezhnaya named Teucer.
### LARGE +
The Nameless Treasure collection ("Hidden in Nameless Treasures") spans Qingxu Pool, Lingju Pass and Dunyu Ruins, and Linlang values them together. The wiki lists Alrani as the named character of And This Treasure Goes To..., in which the Traveler also looks for a missing scholar.

## dangers
### STANDARD
Hilichurls, Fatui skirmishers, mitachurls, Abyss mages and Ruin Guards.
### ULTRA +
Ruin Guards are the pass's signature threat: in Tartaglia's story quest, a single ruin is said to be the source of many Ruin Guards wandering the wild, and the Millelith ask for help from the Traveler.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
Solemn and puzzle-laden: weathered stone, mechanisms that answer to Geo, and a poem that reminds visitors the divine order is impartial.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 Lingju Pass is unchanged and open. No later story content was found that alters it.

## entry: Nameless Treasures and the Sealing of Azhdaha
@keys: Nameless Treasure, Hidden in Nameless Treasures
@keys2: Lingju Pass, Qingxu Pool, Dunyu Ruins, Azhdaha
### STANDARD
Three Nameless Treasures, one each at Lingju Pass, Qingxu Pool and Dunyu Ruins, form a set whose patterns tell how Azhdaha, a dragon born from Liyue's Geo spirits, was lured into a cavern by Rex Lapis and sealed there by three adepti. Linlang reads two of the three adepti as Mountain Shaper and Moon Carver; the third carving was erased, and the identity is not stated.
