## summary
### LITE
The Serpent's Heart is a subarea of Enkanomiya, the underground realm of Byakuyakoku. It was made with the power of Sunfire Phantasms, to guard secrets, imprison criminals and worship the great imaginary serpent Ouroboros. At its centre stands an altar regarded as the heart of that Cosmic Serpent, a sealing device for storing important objects. Its oldest name was Delphi, the Serpent's Land.
### STANDARD +
Its name predates the arrival of Orobashi, the Watatsumi Omikami, and did not change after it. The Traveler retrieved a Reins of Revival fragment from the altar during the quest The Heart of Ouroboros.

## geography
### STANDARD
A cavernous zone of Enkanomiya's underground sea. It is where space itself is said to overlap, a phenomenon the early Enkanomiyans noticed when they first found the place, and which they used to build the prison-vault.
### LARGE +
A cave in its northern cliffs leads to the ruins where the Lotus Eater quest begins. Altar Patterns, Key Sigil seals, and day-night mechanisms scattered here change with Whitenight and Evernight.

## places
### STANDARD
The Altar at the Serpent's Heart: the vault itself; patterns entered here retrieve stored objects. The Emanant Skylight: a spot with a pool and a Time Tunnel that leads to Kunado's Locus once the trials begin.
### LARGE +
Cave systems, ancient stone tablets and a hiding place in the Narrows connect with the altar's puzzles. Messages about the altar's codes were scattered around Enkanomiya.

## people
### STANDARD
No standing population. An afterimage at the altar names itself Supada no Hiko (Spartacus), a resistance leader imprisoned here; he has forgotten his own name until the Traveler restores it. The guide Aru supplied information about the fragments during The Subterranean Trials of Drake and Serpent.
### LARGE +
The afterimage claims Byakuyakoku's nobles, who guard the Sunchild, were the real rulers, and that the prison erodes souls and memories.

## history
### STANDARD
The Byakuyakoku Collection describes the site as discovered with its overlapping-space phenomenon already present. Ancestors later built the Serpent's Heart on it. Before Orobashi arrived it was known as the Heart of Ouroboros; Delphi kept its name afterwards.
### LARGE +
The altar functions through strict entry procedures: the right pattern retrieves the right object, which makes it equally useful as a prison. Aru says the codes were scattered across Enkanomiya. The site was among those that held fragments of the Reins of Revival, along with the Evernight Temple and the Narrows.
### ULTRA +
Sunfire Phantasms are a ley-line phenomenon: as the land phases between Whitenight and Evernight, energy gathers and disperses, so objects and entrances hide or appear. Aru says the Enkanomiyans later learnt to use them to conceal secrets. Before Orobashi's arrival, the people called Phantasms and Sinshades alike 'Eidolons' and did not understand them. The Serpent's Heart is one of three places, with the Evernight Temple and the Narrows, that held a Reins of Revival fragment, and Aru says the proper entry procedures must be followed in strict order for the altar to release its objects.

## quests
### STANDARD
The Subterranean Trials of Drake and Serpent (the fragment hunt), The Heart of Ouroboros (a Reins of Revival fragment is taken from the altar), and Lotus Eater (a side quest begun at the altar).

## dangers
### STANDARD
Ruin Sentinels guard parts of the zone, and Abyss and hilichurl enemies are found in Enkanomiya generally. Switching between Whitenight and Evernight is part of exploring safely.

## atmosphere
### LARGE
A hushed vault beneath the world, lit by Sunfire glow, where altar mechanisms click into place and old prisoners' voices linger as afterimages.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 the altar's prize is gone and the prison is empty save for afterimages.
### STANDARD +
The Byakuyakoku texts record the sealing of the depths and the emigration of Watatsumi's people; the Serpent's Heart is a relic of that age. No 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content revisits it.

## entry: Delphi and Ouroboros
@keys: Delphi, Ouroboros, Cosmic Serpent, great imaginary serpent, Heart of Ouroboros
### STANDARD
The Enkanomiyans revered snakes even before Orobashi became their patron. Ouroboros, also called the great imaginary serpent or Cosmic Serpent, was worshipped from the earliest times, and Delphi meant the land of snakes. After Orobashi came, they worshipped both. Ancient art depicts Ouroboros as scaleless and Orobashi as coral-adorned.

## entry: Supada no Hiko
@keys: Supada no Hiko, Spartacus
@version: 2.4
### STANDARD
In the Lotus Eater quest an afterimage in the altar says he led a resistance against Byakuyakoku's nobles and that his students, whom he calls his children, hold the nation's hope. He forgot his name under the prison's erosion; the Traveler restores it from an ancient tablet naming him as Supada no Hiko and the 'Righteous', condemned as a bandit.
