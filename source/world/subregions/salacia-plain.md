## summary
### LITE
Salacia Plain is an open, serene stretch of crystal-clear sea in the Court of Fontaine Region. It is explored with the blessing of the Transoceanic Sourcewater, which lets travellers pass here unhindered, and it is home to sea creatures such as the Angelic Sea Hares and the singing Blubberbeasts.
### STANDARD +
Official text calls it a place where many precious memories and secrets still slumber beneath the glittering surface. It belongs to the underwater world around the capital, in the same map level as "Source of All Waters: The Great Fontaine Lake".

## geography
### STANDARD
Open sea water with currents, seagrass and sea-floor features, near the Court of Fontaine and the Opera Epiclese. The surrounding sea is full of explorable underwater mechanisms.
### LARGE +
Exploration features listed there: Accelerated Aquaring, Bullet Barnacles, currents, Fontemer Seagrass, High-Pressure Water Vents, Pneumousia Blocks and Relays, Recovery Orbs, Seelies, time trial challenges and Xenochromatic Creatures. Fishing is possible at the Western Shoal.

## atmosphere
### STANDARD
Calm, bright, and clear: current-borne music, sunlit water and slow-moving sea life. The official site invites visitors to swim alongside the Sea Hares, listen to the Blubberbeasts' song and let the "music of the currents" guide them.

## dangers
### LARGE
Little is stated. As a Hydro sea area it includes underwater hazards such as strong currents and vents, and the Dagger in the Water daily commission uses Pufferfruit targets there (a gameplay challenge).

## quests
### LARGE
Dagger in the Water, Freminet: A Story and Sightseeing With Friends: A Trip to Fontaine are the quests that list Salacia Plain. Dagger in the Water has spots near the Opera Epiclese and near the Court of Fontaine, as well as in Liffey and Morte.

## travel
### STANDARD
Reached by diving from the Court of Fontaine Region; the Transoceanic Sourcewater's blessing allows passage.

## status
### LITE
As of 7.1 no change to the area is recorded.
### ULTRA +
Because the sources give almost nothing in the way of history, treat the place as scenery. Its name comes from Salacia, the Roman goddess of salt water.
