## summary
### LITE
Everyday life in Liyue, the Geo nation of contracts, runs on trade. Liyue Harbor is the busiest commercial hub in Teyvat, with merchants from every nation, and the villages around it are mostly home to the young and the old while able-bodied people go to the city for work. People drink tea, argue over cooking styles, trust written agreements, revere the adepti of the mountains, and are governed by the Liyue Qixing, a council of merchants, rather than by a god.
### STANDARD +
Liyue's mood is practical and orderly: bargains are honoured, goods and gossip travel fast, and traditions (festivals, idioms, habits around tea and furniture) are taken seriously without being stiff. The people now live under mortal governance, with the adepti guarding the land and stepping in only if humans cannot cope. Most residents do not follow high politics; life in the Harbor goes on as normal even on days of great ceremony.
### LARGE +
Rocky, mountainous terrain makes farming harder than in Mondstadt, so Liyue leans on mining, smithing, crafts, tea and sea trade. The country is also marked by its past: farmland on the Guili Plains ruined in the Archon War, ancient devices such as the Guizhong Ballista and Dunyu Ruins, and the slow reopening of the Chasm mines.

## practice
### LITE
Common routines: haggling and contract-making, tea and dim sum, visits to restaurants and teahouses, hiring escorts for goods, pilgrimages toward the adepti, festivals such as the Lantern Rite and Moonchase, and sending children to tutors or martial clans.
### STANDARD +
Settlement: many people live in villages such as Mingyun, Qingce and Qiaoying, but Liyue Harbor is the great centre. Villages that cannot support their people are abandoned; Mingyun Village emptied when its ore ran out and its people moved west for better prospects, and other abandoned settlements dot the nation.

Farming and goods: Qingce Village keeps terraced rice fields whose harvest is sold in the Harbor, sunsettia trees grow across the country, and Qiaoying Village is known for tea leaves served as drinks or made into tea cakes. Every establishment serves tea its own way, and tea bags would seem foreign.

Food: breakfast often includes dim sum, taken with tea as yum cha. Liyue has two rival culinary styles, the bold, rich Li style from mountain ingredients and the lighter Yue style that favours seafood. Their fans bicker constantly; in the Harbor's Feiyun Slope shopping district, Liuli Pavilion is the well-regarded Li-style restaurant and Xinyue Kiosk the Yue-style one. Chihu Rock, the poorer end of the city, favours street food, with newer places such as Wanmin Restaurant.

Trade: Liyue Harbor handles commerce with Mondstadt, Inazuma, Sumeru and likely Snezhnaya, while Yilong Wharf mainly handles trade with Fontaine. Liyue is known abroad for cloth and silk (cloth goes to Inazuma), tea and Chilibrew, and local merchants resell Dawn Winery drinks. Roads still need protection, so many merchants hire local security agencies; being independently run, these are hard for the Qixing to standardise, and the Sword and Strongbox Secure Transport Agency is among the better known.

Language: people use idioms constantly (for example "the walls have ears," "money belongs to the world outside your head," "food is life"), and a local script resembling seal script appears on signs.
### LARGE +
Education and clans: schooling appears to depend on family means. Wealthy households hire tutors (Xingqiu is well educated), while people of lower standing may be illiterate, as the Adventurers' Guild's Lan says of herself. Some people instead join clans to learn martial arts, such as the Guhua Clan, whose arts are Raincutter for swords, Light Piercer for polearms and Life Ender for mastery of both. Swords and polearms are the typical weapons of Liyue, and Morax is praised there as the Warrior God.

Medicine: Liyue's medicine is relatively comprehensive. Baizhu is a renowned physician and Bubu Pharmacy is known beyond Liyue, though few know he draws on Changsheng's powers. Herbs such as violetgrass are common ingredients, and Chenyu Vale keeps its own medical practices.

Home and architecture: buildings follow ancient-Chinese-inspired styles with eaved roofs and warm colours. In a study, furnishings must be placed at set positions and angles; Zhongli says there are three schools of thought and nine specialisations on this.

Industry: mining and smithing are important. Blackcliff Forge produced the Blackcliff and Prototype weapon series and doubles as a mine. The Chasm is the nation's best-known mine and employs many miners; the Qixing closed it after incidents and later let small groups of experienced miners return. The Eight Trades, eight commerce sectors under the Qixing, oversee industries.

Religion and the adepti: many people revere the adepti and some make pilgrimages to their abodes in Jueyun Karst. The Wangsheng Funeral Parlor, run by the Hu family, handles funerals.
### ULTRA +
Government in daily life: the Qixing run the nation from Yuehai Pavilion. The wiki's culture page has Ningguang (Tianquan) handling law, Keqing (Yuheng) land, building and employment, and Uncle Tian (Tianshu) policy. The Millelith double as peacekeepers, keep outposts along common roads against monsters and bandits, and are heavily present in the Harbor. Treasure Hoarders are more active in Liyue than elsewhere and prowl lonely roads and the Chasm. The Yuheng's work against banditry has made roads safer, but demand for escorts remains high.

The Rite of Descension: each year the Qixing invited Rex Lapis to give predictions for the coming year, which shaped policy and business. It was open to everyone and drew visitors, including Liyue natives living abroad, though most Harbor residents carried on as usual. A Rite of Parting, by contrast, commemorates a deceased adeptus.

Motifs: Liyue design uses geometric patterns such as meanders and diamonds, auspicious clouds, wan, thunder, ruyi and shou symbols, and scales as an emblem.

Music: Liyue's music favours classical string and wind instruments such as the erhu, guzheng and dizi.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Harbor versus village. The Harbor is cosmopolitan, loud and rich, with quarters from the wealthy Feiyun Slope to humbler Chihu Rock. Villages are quieter, older in population, and tied to one crop or craft: rice at Qingce, tea at Qiaoying, formerly ore at Mingyun.
### LARGE +
Mountain and sea. Jueyun Karst is the home of adepti and hermits and a pilgrimage destination; Yilong Wharf is the trade gate toward Fontaine, with a small, discreet Millelith unit; the Chasm region is miners' country; and Chenyu Vale has its own healing customs.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Good hooks: a merchant haggling and insisting on a written contract; a heated debate over Li versus Yue cooking; tea served in a way that matters to the host; a stranger hiring a security agency for a risky delivery; a villager moving to the city for work; a pilgrim heading toward the mountains; idioms used as polite warnings. Keep the tone businesslike, hospitable and rooted in reputation.
### LARGE +
For depth, contrast wealthy and poor, remind characters that the Qixing govern and the adepti watch, and let ancient history (Guili Plains, the Chasm) surface in ruins or stories without resolving mysteries canon leaves open.

## entry: Li and Yue Culinary Styles

### STANDARD
Liyue has two long-standing culinary styles: Li, bold and rich, built on mountain ingredients, and Yue, lighter and built on seafood. Fans of each are in a perpetual feud. In the Harbor's Feiyun Slope district, Liuli Pavilion is the notable Li-style restaurant and Xinyue Kiosk the Yue-style one.

## entry: Tea in Liyue
@keys: Liyue tea, yum cha, dim sum, Qiaoying tea
@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Tea is very popular in Liyue. Qiaoying Village is famed for its leaves, which can be served as drinks or turned into tea cakes. Each establishment serves tea in its own way, and tea bags are an unfamiliar idea. Dim sum is a common breakfast, called yum cha when taken with tea.

## entry: Moonchase Festival Origins
@keys: Moonchase Festival, Stove God Festival
@version: 2.1
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A festival of reunion and feasting, organised by the Qixing, who choose a theme each year. It honours Marchosius, the Stove God who taught the people of the Guili Assembly to make fire and cook. Rex Lapis later unified older human festivals, including what Keqing's grandfather called the Stove God Festival, under the Moonchase name.

## entry: Guhua Clan Martial Arts
@keys: Guhua Clan, Guhua arts, Raincutter, Light Piercer, Life Ender
@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A Liyue martial lineage whose three arts are Raincutter (swords), Light Piercer (polearms) and Life Ender (both). The brotherhood that preceded the clan was founded by an associate of Guhua, who himself wielded the claymore Rainslasher. Xingqiu practises Guhua sword style. Joining a clan is one path to training besides formal schooling.

## entry: Liyue Idioms
@keys: Liyue idiom, Liyue saying, Liyue proverb
@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Liyue is known for idioms and sayings used in everyday talk, such as "the walls have ears" (be careful what you say), "the hunter by day is the hunted by night" (roles reverse), "don't always call it as you see it" (be tactful), "food is life," "money belongs to the world outside your head" and "we will meet again." In English localisation some are reworded to keep the spirit.
