## summary
### LITE
Snezhnaya is the frozen northern nation, and ordinary life there is organised around staying warm. Each city is built around a Kresnik's Torch burning Kresnik Kristall, trains cross the tundra, and the Fatui, the Veche and state bureaus run daily affairs. Loyalty and keeping one's word matter deeply.
### STANDARD +
Daily routine is shaped by heat, rail travel and the state. Ordinary people are practical and often wry: notices and newspapers show them complaining about scams, queueing for theatre tickets and insuring against train delays. Nod-Krai, in the south, is warmer and looser and is treated separately.
### LARGE +
Beneath the routine runs a long squeeze on energy. Project Stuzha, a set of Veche-approved reforms limiting public energy use so power can go to the Armory Palace, has been extended again and again and has left parts of the country in poor shape; villages that depend on kristall deliveries can be cut off. The old order also lingers: Prime Ice art, once a court luxury, is now being opened to the public, and fae and humans share towns whose mixing varies from city to city.

## practice
### LITE
- **Warmth first.** Blizzards can last a month (Nadia of Liyue) and Tartaglia says you would freeze if you stopped moving outside, so people live indoors in heated towns built around Kresnik's Torches.
- **Values.** Loyalty and plain dealing are prized; a nursery rhyme threatens a pinkie-promise breaker with the ice, and betrayal or an unpaid debt feels like a personal insult.
### STANDARD +
- **Heat as infrastructure.** The Royal Energy Commission maintains the torches and manages how kristall is shipped and used. Kristall is mined mainly at Novokitezhgrad and sent out by rail. The main torch burns in Kresnik's Burning Heart in Snezhnograd.
- **Trains.** Railways carry people and goods safely across the tundra. The Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau staffs the stations; Snezhnograd's Central Station is the largest, and arrivals pass an inspection before taking the lift up into the city.
- **Who runs things.** The Fatui act as army, police and diplomatic corps, the Snezhnograd Veche (a council of interest groups) debates and sets policy, and the Druzhna guard the Tsaritsa. The Adventurers' Guild began in Snezhnaya.
- **Drink.** Fire-Water is the famous strong spirit. Downing a whole glass in one go is normal and respectful, and Tartaglia says drinking and talking together in the cold builds complete trust.
### LARGE +
- **Why the torches exist.** Before them the whole land was only frigid or subzero ground, habitable by hardy fae alone; the Belyi Tsar built the torches after he opened his realm to humans. The Fatui keep a debt-collection division.
- **Travel and commuting.** Svetloledovka's Prime Ice miners live in Snezhnograd and commute by train. Morepesok is a fishing station where the biggest catch earns respect. Sretomorozsk, in the southwest, is almost deserted and mostly used by people entering or leaving Nod-Krai. Rankova Zorya is a former temporary stop that someone still maintains.
- **News and notices.** The Snezhnaya Vestnik newspaper is read at stands inside train stations. In Snezhnograd the Veche set up a bulletin board for municipal notices and public messages, between Podvorye Restaurant and the Northland Bank.
- **Eating out.** Podvorye Restaurant serves refined versions of traditional food and good Fire-Water; its owner and staff are all Volkodlak and fae. Coastal people eat mostly seafood (Tartaglia), borscht is called an authentic dish, and rye replaces wheat because wheat grows poorly in the cold.
- **Culture and reading.** The Korolevskiy Theater is described as a holy land for singers and dancers, and tickets are so sought after that the troupe warns the public to buy only from the theatre. Torchlight Books is a Snezhnograd bookshop owned by Moes that takes reader suggestions.
- **Alchemy.** Alchemy is banned on the Snezhnayan mainland, so Snezhnograd, uniquely among the main cities, has no crafting bench.
- **Rules of the cold.** Game text sorts areas into Normal Temperature, Cold, Frigid and Subzero zones (a gameplay abstraction). Towns and train interiors are Normal Temperature Zones. At Svetloledovka a worker named Zhuravlev nearly freezes and recovers beside a portable heat source, thanking the Traveler with Glaciafruit.
- **Kristall supply.** The supply can fail. In Gunfire in the Silent Lands the engineer Shishkin says the village of Alatyr, which has no station, has had its kristall deliveries stop, so everyone could freeze unless something is done. Kristall was first found by following Kristall Bugs.
- **Energy politics.** Per Guiding Notes, Project Stuzha is an umbrella term for time-limited energy policies meant to conserve power for research at the Armory Palace; progress has been slow, costs have soared above projections, and the extensions annoy many people. Many Veche disagreements revolve around it. Arlecchino, Tartaglia, Pulcinella and Pantalone are named as involved, with Pierro behind it. A later bulletin notice says the Veche has resolved to close it in the near future.
- **Class and work.** Zolnyy, the "Ash District" of Snezhnograd, is the industrial quarter. A Vestnik article on a Prime Ice sculpture exhibition quotes a poem from the Pale Star Court era: nobles received sculptures while Zolnyy's miners could not afford a bowl of red beet soup; the Veche and the Prime Ice Art Alliance now present the art as being brought closer to the people. One anonymous poster on the board complains that blacksmiths prioritise Snezhnograd locals for apprenticeships.
- **Fae and humans.** Snowland Fae live among humans: many in Novokitezhgrad, fewer but still a fair number in Snezhnograd. Jack Frosts are common and playful but cannot speak.
- **Civic life.** The Rokot, a civilian political group whose members hide behind codenames, shows people organising outside the Veche.
- **Hazards.** Some areas contain the Shadow Realm. When one is found, the Royal Energy Commission quarantines it and calls the Druzhna; those caught inside rarely come out unchanged.
### ULTRA +
- **Newspaper life.** The Vestnik is not a sober paper. Issues have included a franchise drive for Frosta, a cold drink presented as an upgrade of Fonta, at ten million Mora; a Healthguard column attributed to Prof. Lobanov recommending walking; a piece from the Society for the Advancement of Traditional Snezhnayan Medicine claiming hot baths are harmful because people did not bathe hot before the torches; and an advert for Snezhnajoy Insurance covering train delays (ten times the ticket price when human factors cause it), melted Prime Ice tools (up to 200,000 Mora) and "audience trauma" from a theatre production. All of it is in-world advertising or opinion, not neutral fact.
- **Public notices.** Other board posts show a fan from Fontaine asking how to meet the singer Vodyanitsa while locals warn that backstage passes are scams; one poster asks why an adopted Morozhound puppy keeps rolling around, and another reader explains it is warming up for a slide and is happy; Podvorye advertises an improved borscht made from beets of several regions; and Torchlight Books replies to a reader's request for a Liyue perch stew recipe.
- **Town-level politics.** Pulcinella is Speaker of the Veche and Mayor of Snezhnograd and Glupov.
- **Celebrations.** Krsnik Noc, the "Ice Hearth Festival", is named as a Snezhnayan festival in 1.3 Lantern Rite dialogue. Canon through 7.1 gives no description of how it is celebrated.
- **Elsewhere.** Nod-Krai is warmer, more independent and exempt from Snezhnayan institutions by Edict; see its own entry. The Frostmoon Scions of Hiisi Island live simply and avoid Nasha Town.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
- **Snezhnograd.** Capital with the Central Station, the Veche State Hall, the Druzhna HQ, Glupov (the Fatui's base, named by Anastasya "the city of fools"), the Adventurers' Guild headquarters and a Statue of The Seven. Flins calls it beautiful, with brilliant night skies and endless blizzards.
- **Novokitezhgrad.** Mining and liquor town southeast of Snezhnograd, with many fae living alongside humans.
- **Svetloledovka.** Prime Ice mining village under the frozen peaks.
- **Morepesok.** Small fishing village and Tartaglia's hometown.
- **Sretomorozsk.** Border town, station and port facing Nod-Krai.
### LARGE +
- **Alatyr and small villages.** Settlements without a station depend on deliveries, which makes them vulnerable when the supply chain falters.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Open with warmth and travel: a torch glowing over a platform, the Transport Bureau inspection, frost on a window, hot soup. Let NPCs be practical and a little sardonic, quick to name a debt or a promise. Use notices, newspapers and ticket queues for texture. Do not present the Tsaritsa's Edicts or Fatui policy as something every citizen resents or loves.
### LARGE +
Cold is a constant: characters pause by heaters, check that deliveries are coming and grumble about energy limits. A Fire-Water toast is a natural way to show trust. Avoid inventing festival rituals, school systems or family customs; canon through 7.1 does not detail them.

## entry: Kresnik's Torch and Kresnik Kristall in Daily Life
@keys: Kresnik's Torch, Kresnik Torch, Kresnik Kristall, Kristall, Kresnik's Burning Heart
@keys2: heat, warmth, shipment, deliveries, fuel, cold, village
@logic: and_any
@version: 7.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Kresnik's Torches are furnaces burning Kresnik Kristall that keep Snezhnayan towns warm and protect them from snowstorms. Every city is built around one, and small civilian models are placed around the country. The most important is in Kresnik's Burning Heart in Snezhnograd. By the Snezhnayan account the Belyi Tsar built them to let humans make a world of their own in the cold. The Royal Energy Commission oversees the torches and manages how the kristall is shipped and used. Kristall is mined chiefly at Novokitezhgrad and travels by rail.
### LARGE +
Kristall is said to come from the authority released by the Cryo Dragon when it lost control about 6,000 years ago, an account from Aksinya and a potion formula in quest text. Early people allegedly found its veins by following Kristall Bugs. Villages without stations, such as Alatyr, suffer when deliveries stop.

## entry: Trains and Stations in Snezhnaya
@keys: Snezhnayan trains, Snezhnaya train, Transport Bureau, Central Station, Rankova Zorya, public train
@keys2: Snezhnaya, Snezhnograd, station, tundra
@logic: and_any
@version: 7.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Trains are the lifeline across the tundra, in the loading-screen words the veins of an iron-and-steel nation. The Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau runs them, and conductors, ticket agents and safety officers staff the stations. Snezhnograd Central Station is the largest; Svetloledovka's miners commute from the capital; Morepesok serves fisherfolk; Sretomorozsk, nearly deserted, serves those entering or leaving Nod-Krai; Rankova Zorya is a former temporary stop that someone still maintains. Vestnik newspaper stands sit inside stations.
### LARGE +
Train interiors are Normal Temperature Zones, so passengers can travel safely without being exposed to the cold. The Traveler's own train, called with the Eye of Graeae, is a separate gameplay convenience and not how ordinary citizens travel.

## entry: Snezhnograd Bulletin Board and the Vestnik
@keys: Snezhnograd Bulletin Board, Snezhnaya Vestnik, Vestnik, Snezhnajoy Insurance, Frosta, Healthguard
@version: 7.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Two sources show the tone of public life. The bulletin board in Snezhnograd lets residents read municipal notices and leave messages, and it mixes official Veche resolutions with ticket-scam warnings, pet questions and shop adverts. The Vestnik is a newspaper sold at station stands, running news, advertisements and "Tales of the Strange and Incredible" contributor stories. Its adverts and health columns are in-world opinion and often absurd.
### LARGE +
Examples include the Frosta franchise offer, the walking-based Healthguard column, a study against hot baths and Snezhnajoy Insurance. Treat these as satire-tinged colour, not facts about the world.

## entry: Project Stuzha and Everyday Energy Limits
@keys: Project Stuzha, Stuzha, energy reforms, energy limits
@keys2: Snezhnaya, Veche, Armory Palace, torch, village
@logic: and_any
@version: 7.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Project Stuzha is a package of Veche-passed energy reforms that limit public energy use so power can go to the Armory Palace. It has been extended repeatedly, cost far more than expected, and has left parts of Snezhnaya in poor condition. Many Veche arguments revolve around it, and a later Veche notice says it will be formally closed. Details of its purpose and the Harbingers behind it belong to its own entry.
### LARGE +
Quest text shows concrete effects, such as the stopped kristall deliveries to Alatyr. The later chapters reveal that Stuzha was a step toward Project Mistilteinn, a weapon for the Tsaritsa's struggle against the Heavenly Principles (spoiler-level minor to major; see the project entry).

## entry: Prime Ice, Zolnyy and Class
@keys: Zolnyy, Ash District, Prime Ice Art Alliance, Prime Ice sculpture
@keys2: Snezhnograd, miners, Veche, Pale Star Court
@logic: and_any
@version: 7.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Zolnyy is Snezhnograd's industrial "Ash District". Prime Ice mining happens at Svetloledovka, and a Vestnik article records that in the Pale Star Court era Prime Ice sculptures were a court-only art while Zolnyy's miners could hardly afford soup. The Veche and the Prime Ice Art Alliance now stage a public exhibition as part of a move to bring the art to ordinary people.
