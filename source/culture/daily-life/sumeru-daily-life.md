## summary
### LITE
Everyday life in Sumeru, the Dendro nation of wisdom, is shaped by scholarship. The Sumeru Akademiya is both Teyvat's largest known centre of learning and the body that handles much of the nation's daily government, so people value rationality, practical knowledge and academic achievement. Under the old sages many adults wore Akasha Terminals, took pride in not dreaming, and looked down on the arts; since Chapter III people can dream again and the arts are returning. The land is split between northern rainforest and southern desert, with different peoples, guards and habits in each.
### STANDARD +
The mood is earnest, hard-working and a little anxious. Degrees take many years, theses are feared, and old attitudes survive: even after the sages were removed, talking about the arts is still said to be the quickest way to end a conversation. Rainforest dwellers live under the Akademiya and Nahida; desert communities have long been self-governing and distrust the Akademiya.
### LARGE +
Most of this entry rests on the Chapter III picture of Sumeru and the wiki's culture summary. Later events, especially the burning of Irminsul in Song of the Welkin Moon Act X (6.6), change the system underneath daily life (the Aaru system now carries Irminsul's functions), but no source read here shows how ordinary routines changed.

## practice
### LITE
Common routines: studying, researching and defending theses; consulting the Akasha (formerly) or books; hiring Eremites as bodyguards; trading spices, medicine and goods at Port Ormos and Caravan Ribat; eating fragrant, spicy food with flowers, mushrooms, fruit and nuts; and, in the desert, living by the rules of one's tribe.
### STANDARD +
Learning and status: the Akademiya takes students from across Teyvat and sends travelling scholars (Driyosh) abroad to gather research. Knowledge is treated as a measure of wealth and privilege. Students call each other senior and junior by academic progress, not age (Tighnari is Abattouy's senior because he graduated first). Passing one thesis graduates a student; two make a formal researcher (Dastur); the Sages (Herbad) lead the six Darshans. Hosseini says it is not unusual to graduate after thirty, and Lisa, famed as the best student in two hundred years, finished in two. Lisa left disillusioned with the workaholic culture and the cost of "uninhibited erudition" she saw.

The Akasha era: the Akasha System, built from Greater Lord Rukkhadevata's Gnosis and expanded by the Akademiya, let nearly every adult look up information through a leaf-shaped Akasha Terminal at the ear; children did not have one. Clearance depended on identity, age and other criteria, and use was monitored by the sages. Those unsatisfied with what they were allowed to know, such as Eremites, commoners and some students, turned to illegal Knowledge Capsules sold on the black market. The Akasha was shut down after Chapter III.

Dreams: many adults in Sumeru did not dream and took pride in it, a reputation linked to scholars drawing knowledge from Irminsul and to the sages stigmatising dreams. After the Akasha's suspension, people could dream again.

Food: fragrant, spicy cooking, with edible flowers, mushrooms, fruits and nuts. "Spice" is the ubiquitous seasoning, Padisarah is a common plant seasoning, and sweeter dishes use Sumeru Rose or Ajilenakh Nut. Local ingredients include Mysterious Meat from Flying Serpents and Glabrous Beans. Details belong to the Sumeru cuisine entry.

Peoples: most residents are human. The rare Valuka Shuna (Tighnari's kind) live there, the Jinni are spirits of desert oases, the Aranara are shy forest spirits, and the Pari live in the Girdle of the Sands.

Healthcare is free, according to Alhaitham, at least in the rainforest.
### LARGE +
Economy: Port Ormos is the main port where foreigners trade. Crocodile products, spices and essential oils for beauty goods are traded; Eremite tribes sell to merchants who resell in the city. Shaggy Sumpter Beasts carry goods, especially across the desert, and so serve as a sign of wealth. Dori and the Eremites supply Knowledge Capsules, often to Akademiya researchers.

Order and guards: Sumeru has no official military. Eremites, desert-born mercenaries, are hired as bodyguards by researchers and important families. The Corps of Thirty, a large Eremite group, helps keep peace in Sumeru City, Port Ormos and Caravan Ribat. The Matra police academic misconduct and crime within the Akademiya's rules. The Akademiya's three virtues and six sins (banned lines of research) underlie its rules.

The arts: under the old sages the arts were dismissed as useless. Plays needed approval and approved themes, then public performances were prohibited altogether; even books were not openly sold. The scorn spread beyond the Akademiya to painting, street performance, dancing and architecture. Once Nahida took control, entertainment and imported light novels returned to the marketplace, and the Zubayr Theater in the Grand Bazaar could perform openly. Training centres for architecture and other careers have also appeared for those who cannot enter the Akademiya, though Kaveh's Hangout shows that some are scams the Akademiya overlooks.

Names: surnames may prefix an ancestor's name with "Pur", as in Anisa Purbiruni, from the sage Biruni of five hundred years ago. The Traveler notes this may reflect admiration for sages rather than descent.
### ULTRA +
Festivals: the Sabzeruz Festival marks the birthday of Lesser Lord Kusanali, taken as the day the sages found her, and was first conceived by the Goddess of Flowers for Rukkhadevata. Traditions include the Haft-Mewa Feast (seven foods on the table), Farris the Knight of Flowers (a figure children like) and the Dance of Sabzeruz. The Akademiya stopped funding it after Rukkhadevata's death. The Akademiya Extravaganza is another local festival.

Desert tribes: some Eremites live in tribes, self-governed under elders and possibly matriarchs, with their own rules centred on the tribe's wellbeing; breaking them can mean exile or death. The wiki names the Tanit, Afiyah, Uzza and Shimti tribes.

Motifs: Akademiya emblems appear on student clothing and the scarves of the Corps of Thirty, and each Darshan has its own emblem. King Deshret is represented by the sun and the eye, and the Eremite Crest is a sun motif above crossed scimitars. Character cards use Darshan symbols for rainforest figures and four ministers' symbols for desert ones.

Post-Chapter III: with the old sages removed, Nahida oversees selection of new sages; Alhaitham refused the Grand Sage post and served only as Acting Grand Sage. In Act X (6.6) Dottore's Irminsul tree appeared in Sumeru City and was burned by Nahida with a Pyro Gnosis; Aaru now carries Irminsul's role. Aranara were asked to show themselves to humans.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Rainforest versus desert. The rainforest centres on the Akademiya, Sumeru City and Port Ormos, with Forest Rangers at Gandharva Ville. The desert, once ruled by King Deshret and full of ruins, is the Eremites' country, where tribes are self-governed and distrust the Akademiya. Caravan Ribat is the trade post at the border and Aaru Village is a desert town.
### LARGE +
The desert is described as endless sand, scorching sun and sandstorms. Ruins from Khaenri'ah and Deshret's time, and Deshret's advanced buildings, lie beneath the sand. Originally the whole land was forest; a Celestial Nail turned much of it to desert.

## entry: Akasha Terminal Era
@keys: Akasha Terminal, Knowledge Capsule, Canned Knowledge
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
For generations the Akasha System let adults retrieve information through a leaf-shaped earpiece, within the limits of their clearance. Knowledge Capsules were illegal data cartridges that bypassed clearance and were sold on the black market. After the Chapter III conspiracy, the Akasha was shut down and people could dream again.

## entry: Akademiya Careers and Titles
@keys: Driyosh, Dastur, Herbad, traveling scholar
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Driyosh are students on leave to travel and research. One thesis passed makes a graduate, two make a Dastur (formal researcher), and the Herbad are the Sages, one per Darshan, under a Grand Sage. Students call each other senior or junior by progress, not age.

## entry: The Arts Under the Old Sages
@keys: Zubayr Theater, Sumeru arts, anti-art policy
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The old sages treated art as pointless: plays needed approval, public performance was banned, and even books were not openly sold. Contempt spread through society. After Nahida took control, performance and publishing returned, and the Zubayr Theater in the Grand Bazaar (its manager is Sheikh Zubayr) works openly.

Some surnames prefix an ancestor's name with Pur: Anisa Purbiruni takes hers from the sage Biruni, who lived five hundred years ago. The Traveler remarks it might also be admiration for a sage rather than blood descent.

## entry: Eremites as Hired Guards
@keys: Eremite bodyguard, Corps of Thirty, Eremite tribes
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Sumeru has no official army, so Eremites serve in its place. Researchers and important families hire them as bodyguards, and the Corps of Thirty helps keep order in Sumeru City, Port Ormos and Caravan Ribat. Desert tribes govern themselves and enforce their own rules.

## entry: Sabzeruz Festival Customs
@keys: Sabzeruz, Haft-Mewa, Farris, Dance of Sabzeruz
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Sabzeruz Festival celebrates Lesser Lord Kusanali's birthday. Customs include the Haft-Mewa Feast of seven foods, Farris the Knight of Flowers, and the Dance of Sabzeruz. The Akademiya long refused to fund it, treating it almost as if it did not exist.
