## summary
### LITE
Before the cataclysm, Khaenri'ah was a kingdom built underground, with no god and a great deal of human pride. Daily life ran on stone houses, a diet of meat and mushrooms (grain had to be imported), alchemy and machines, a royal court and a library with banned books. Ordinary people had jobs, friends to drink with, new restaurants to try and partners to come home to, while street performers entertained crowds with a spell that mocked surface Visions. The star-shaped eyes and symbols of its people are still recognisable today. None of this survives as a working society: the cataclysm ended it about 500 years ago.
### STANDARD +
The sources are thin and scattered. Treat anything beyond the points here as unknown unless a scene clearly marks it as invention.
### LARGE +
The kingdom's pride (Dainsleif calls it "the pride of humankind") and its daily texture sit side by side: ordinary people had no gods to pray to or blame, but lived under a king and, near the end, a rising noble clan. Plain jobs and small pleasures coexisted with state secrets such as a "King's Guard" posting that was a cover for a much more dangerous role.

## practice
### LITE
Food: mostly meat and mushrooms, with some alchemically modified or lab-grown strains; grain was imported and imports fell. Homes: stone, simple furnishings. Pastimes: drinks with friends, trying new eateries, street performances. Work: guards, research staff, alchemists, engineers. Reading: a Royal Library with banned texts.
### STANDARD +
Agriculture and food: Khaenri'ah was dark and mostly barren of natural life, which is why its alchemy, the Art of Khemia, concentrated on creating life. In Rerir's journal, grain imports keep falling and he fears they will soon be back to "meat and mushrooms only". He dislikes a newly fashionable mushroom that began as a lab-grown variety and is said to be mildly corrosive. Nefer, watching the memory, concludes (her own inference) that the lack of sunlight explains both the diet and the stone buildings.

Entertainment: the Universitas Magistrorum developed a minor spell, "Chaos Permeation" or the Seven-Shifting Serpent, that used Abyssal energy to imitate the effects of the seven Elements. It mocked the Vision holders of the surface and was a party trick at performances and parades. Ordinary street performers might spend months learning to shift a single element.

Education and books: the kingdom had a Royal Library that nobles such as Chlothar and Eide could use. It also held banned texts, among them recipes that depended on the power of the Seven, which were taboo.
### LARGE +
Home and social life: the memory shows a stone room with a table, chairs, a cabinet and neatly organised storage boxes. An unsigned note says its writer is trying a new kebab shop while Rerir works late, and mentions bringing home teacups and a pen rack from the Universitas Magistrorum. Rerir's journal describes drinks at a man named Joseph's, who has a fine collection, and a partner, Tholindis, who was busy that day. Another scene shows Rerir drinking with Dainsleif and the court prophet Vedrfolnir, and Tholindis saying her equipment is being serviced so she finished work early. Rerir's job as a member of the "King's Guard" is a cover he has not yet explained to her.

Technology: pre-cataclysm Khaenri'ahn technology produced automatons, now called Ruin Machines, built as weapons of war. Ruin Guards were given the codename "Field Tillers", from a saying that land is to be fought for with steel and blood, not tilled with farming tools. The machines can run for centuries thanks to self-repair and take the forms of surface animals. Azosite, drawn from ley line energy and formed into energy blocks, powered some systems; the factory in the Girdle of the Sands is said to be the last in the kingdom to use an Azosite-powered system. The wiki notes that sites such as the Ruins of Dahri, the Ruin Golems and the Azosite factory share a grand scale and an industrial, brass-like interior.

Intellectual fashion: alchemy and the Beastmaster Knights were mainstays of the Crimson Moon Dynasty; under the Eclipse Dynasty, interest moved to mechanical engineering and Khemia fell behind. The people were described as obsessed with mimesis, copying and improving natural forms.

National flower: the Inteyvat once bloomed across the kingdom, lasting about two weeks before wilting. Taken out of Khaenri'ah, its petals stop growing and turn hard; only on returning to home soil do they soften and turn to dust. Dainsleif calls it a symbol of a wanderer far from home.
### ULTRA +
Who was around: the Court of Khaenri'ah was the royal court where King Irmin, the last Eclipse Dynasty king, resided; the Universitas Magistrorum was the research institute. In the memory, Dainsleif, Rerir and Vedrfolnir drink together casually, showing that people of very different standing could socialise, at least in this circle. Tholindis calls herself and Rerir "nobodies", which shows how they saw their own place.

Politics around daily life (the wiki's reading): the Eclipse Dynasty's power waned near the cataclysm and the noble Alberich Clan took power with Anfortas as regent. Whether ordinary people felt the shift is not stated.

After the fall (context for scenes): the Abyss Order, founded by Chlothar Alberich, seeks to revive the kingdom. Halfdan, now a Black Serpent Knight, still follows Dainsleif's old order to protect hilichurls entering the Chasm's underground areas. In Memories of Inteyvat, an Inteyvat flower placed in an underground hilichurl camp shows how symbols of the homeland outlast it. The Rerir journal in the 6.1 quest is the main surviving record of private life, together with the memory scenes around it, touching on drinks, imports and a proposal.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Not applicable in the usual sense: Khaenri'ah was a single underground kingdom, and sources do not describe regions within it. The clearest divide is by descent: after the cataclysm pure-bloods were made immortal, while people with surface or foreign ancestry were turned into monsters.
### LARGE +
Eye shape marks lineage: ordinary pure-blooded Khaenri'ahns have star-shaped (four-pointed) pupils, descendants of the Crimson Moon Dynasty have red, X-shaped pupils within black irises, and highly diluted bloodlines may show neither (from the quest All Is Foretold, as summarised by the wiki). Dynasty also marks style: Eclipse Dynasty officials and soldiers wear distinct uniforms, and the Black Serpents' armour carries the eight-pointed star.

## symbols
### STANDARD
The four-pointed star is a recurring shape on Khaenri'ah-linked characters' clothing and designs; pure-blooded Khaenri'ahns have pupils of this shape. The eight-pointed star appears on Chlothar's and Halfdan's clothes, the Black Serpents' armour, the Gate of Zulqarnain and the "eyes" of Ruin Machines. Both names are unofficial wiki terms.

## fashion
### STANDARD
Only visuals are recorded: separate men's and women's civilian outfits, Eclipse Dynasty uniforms, and unisex royal guard armour. Star motifs appear on some outfits. No text explains clothing customs, so describe clothing sparingly.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use this for memory scenes, ruins, and conversations with Dainsleif or other Khaenri'ah-linked characters. Good details: stone rooms, plain furniture, a grumble about corrosive mushrooms, a note about a new kebab shop, drinks with friends, a street performer shifting an element, a banned book. Tone: warm and ordinary, with an uneasy awareness that it ended. Do not invent names, prices or laws.
### LARGE +
Characters who lived it, such as Dainsleif, speak of it with grief. Characters who did not (the Traveler, Paimon, Nefer) only infer from remains. Kaeya was left in Mondstadt; do not assume he has lived knowledge of this life.

## examples
### STANDARD
Examples from canon: Rerir's journal complaining about corrosive lab-grown mushrooms; the note about a kebab shop; the Seven-Shifting Serpent at a parade; Eide reading forbidden books in the Royal Library as a restless young noble; an Inteyvat flower brought to a hilichurl camp.

## entry: Khaenri'ahn Food and Agriculture

@version: 6.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Khaenri'ah was underground and mostly barren of natural life, so grain was imported and the diet leaned on meat, mushrooms and alchemically produced food. In Rerir's journal, imports are falling and a trendy lab-grown mushroom is disliked as mildly corrosive. Source: Special Operation (6.1).

## entry: Seven-Shifting Serpent
@keys: Seven-Shifting Serpent, Chaos Permeation
@version: 5.7
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A minor Universitas Magistrorum spell that used Abyssal energy to imitate elemental effects, created to mock surface Vision holders. In Khaenri'ah it was a party trick for performances and parades; street performers might spend months mastering one element. The Foul (Surtalogi) later mastered all seven at once and taught it to Skirk as a means of disguise. Source: Skirk's Character Story.

## entry: Royal Library of Khaenri'ah

### STANDARD
Nobles such as Chlothar and Eide could use the library, but banned texts, including recipes needing the power of the Seven, were taboo. Eide says she read forbidden texts in her youth out of boredom with noble life. Source: Fortune-Mocking Pedigree.

## entry: Inteyvat
@keys: Inteyvat, Khaenri'ah national flower
@version: 2.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The national flower of Khaenri'ah, once blooming across the kingdom for about two weeks before wilting. Outside the kingdom its petals harden and stop growing; they soften and turn to dust only when returned to home soil. It symbolises a wanderer far from home. Source: Memories of Inteyvat.

## entry: Four-Pointed and Eight-Pointed Stars
@keys: four-pointed star, eight-pointed star, star-shaped pupils

### STANDARD
Four-pointed stars appear on Khaenri'ah-related designs and as the pupils of pure-blooded Khaenri'ahns; eight-pointed stars mark Chlothar, Halfdan, the Black Serpents' armour, the Gate of Zulqarnain and Ruin Machine eyes. The names are unofficial.

## entry: Ruin Machines as Weapons of War
@keys: Field Tillers, Khaenri'ahn automatons
@keys2: Khaenri'ah, Ruin Guard
### LARGE
Ruin Machines were built as Khaenri'ahn weapons; Ruin Guards were codenamed "Field Tillers". They self-repair, last for centuries and, left without masters after the fall, spread across Teyvat. Source: Dainsleif, A Herald Without Adherents.
