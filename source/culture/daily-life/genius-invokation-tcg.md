## summary
### LITE
Genius Invokation TCG is a card-dueling game that is all the rage across Teyvat. Two players each field a team of character cards and spend rolled Elemental Dice on skills and action cards until one side's characters are all defeated. It began in Sumeru, spread through Inazuma's publishing trade, and is now played in taverns and cafes from Mondstadt to Sumeru City, with an official Genius Invokation TCG Society that tracks players and cards.
### STANDARD +
In Mondstadt the centre of the scene is The Cat's Tail tavern, where owner Margaret took on dedicated staff for the new crowd of players. The game is a social pastime more than a sport: strangers, friends and well-known figures all duel at tables and in taverns.
### LARGE +
The game's own promotional novel series, King of Invokations, gives it a mythic backstory that has little to do with how it is actually played, so keep the real history and the novel's fiction separate.

## practice
### STANDARD
Each side builds a deck of three character cards and thirty action cards. A duel is split into rounds. At the start of a round each player rolls eight Elemental Dice; during the action phase the players alternate, spending dice to use a character's skills, switch the active character, or play cards, and a round ends when both declare it over. Characters build Energy as they attack and can then unleash an elemental burst, and combining elements triggers reactions that mirror the world's own. The winner is whoever defeats all of the opposing characters.
### LARGE +
Action cards come as equipment (weapons, artifacts, talents, techniques), events (including food and elemental resonance) and supports (companions, items, locations). A player may hold up to four support cards and four summons at once, and can discard a card to retune a die. Playing cards and tuning dice are fast actions; using a skill, switching characters or ending the round takes the turn. These are the mechanics taught by the in-game tutorial and are real game rules rather than a roleplay adaptation.
### ULTRA +
Beyond casual duels the game has structured play. Heated Battle Mode, from Version 3.4, is a time-limited format whose special rules change each version, reached from the Cat's Tail invitation board. The Arena of Champions, from Version 3.7, is a PvP series: five victories complete a run and three defeats end it, and the deck cannot be changed between battles. Official players are registered by the Society, which also handles Lucky Coins, issues cards, and invites a player to take Ascension Challenges once it judges them ready.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
In Mondstadt the game's home is The Cat's Tail. In Sumeru City, Puspa Cafe is a common place to play. In Inazuma it arrived through the Yae Publishing House and spread from there. In Liyue it has become fashionable enough that Ningguang, according to an in-game mail, finds it harder to find a partner for her usual game of Liyue Millennial chess.
### LARGE +
Opinions differ by person. Diona, for instance, grumbles that the game draws drinkers into the Cat's Tail, which she dislikes because it sells more alcohol.

## history
### STANDARD
Canon credits the game to Garvipidam and Gulab, who devised it to pass time while Gulab was ill with Eleazar. It became popular in the hospital where he was staying, but he died before it could spread. Garvipidam fell into depression and withdrew from public life. The rights apparently passed to a group of researchers, who refined the rules and wanted to produce a new version. An unspecified Sumeru researcher then commissioned Liben to carry a prototype to the Yae Publishing House in Inazuma, from where the game spread across Teyvat.
### LARGE +
To promote the game, Sumeru Akademiya researchers asked the Inazuman author Fukumoto to write the novel series King of Invokations, illustrated by Asagiri and published through Yae Publishing House. Fukumoto struggled at first because the rules were unfinished and messages travelled slowly between Sumeru and Inazuma. The novel drove game sales, the researchers produced a new version of the rules, and a sequel followed. The Traveler supplied pose references for the second book and Cyno for the third; while writing the third volume Fukumoto grew frustrated with the researchers and invented new mechanics instead of ordinary card play. The bow Ibis Piercer was made to promote the final volume, though the publishing house had only meant to order a sample.
### ULTRA +
As a game mode, Genius Invokation TCG became permanent in Version 3.3, followed by Heated Battle Mode (3.4) and the Arena of Champions (3.7). Later versions added more cards and events; none of the pages read changes the game's in-world history.

## story_king_of_invokations
@version: 3.3
### STANDARD
The novel's hero is Abu, a young man from Sumeru who finds an ancient Casket of Tomes in his attic. Sealed inside is the Crocodile King, a duelist and viceroy of King Deshret, trapped there by his rival the Ibis King for wanting war. The Crocodile King possesses Abu and turns him into a legendary duelist; the first volume ends with Abu beating him, then wishing for a bloody war so the king would live on. This is promotional fiction: the wiki connects the Crocodile King to Sobek, and nothing read says the game itself existed in King Deshret's era.
### LARGE +
Fukumoto made the story exciting by bending the game: in the second book characters play while riding horses, in the first two the hero often wins by drawing a card that was never in his deck, and in the third the characters gather cards hanging in the air rather than playing real duels. The hero is said to favour a slow, resource-building deck.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use it as an easy social setting. A duel can break the ice between strangers, give rivals a polite arena, or let a nervous character relax. Most characters of the present era know it, though skill varies and most are casual players. Keep stakes light unless a scene gives a reason.
### LARGE +
The Cat's Tail with its staff Prince and Shuyun (Shuyun speaks on Prince's behalf), a table at Puspa Cafe, or a Society registration desk are all easy scene anchors. Do not give characters of earlier eras knowledge of the game, and treat King of Invokations as a book some characters have read.

## status
### STANDARD
As of Version 7.1 the game is a permanent, widely played pastime with an official Society. No source read says it has gone out of fashion or been banned anywhere.

## entry: Genius Invokation TCG Society
@keys: Genius Invokation TCG Society, TCG Society, Casket of Tomes
@version: 3.3
### STANDARD
The Society keeps the register of official players, manages Lucky Coins, and creates cards. It gives players a Casket of Tomes and invites qualified players to complete Ascension Challenges once it considers them ready. Its founding and membership are not described in the sources read.

## entry: King of Invokations
@keys: King of Invokations, Fukumoto, Asagiri, Ibis Piercer
@version: 3.3
### STANDARD
A promotional novel series about Abu and the Crocodile King, written by Fukumoto and illustrated by Asagiri at the Akademiya's request. It sold the game to a wide audience in Inazuma and beyond, but its plot and rules diverge from real play on purpose. The Ibis Piercer tie-in grew out of a sample-order mistake.
