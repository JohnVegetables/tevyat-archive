## summary
### LITE
The Snezhnaya Vestnik ("Herald") is the newspaper read across Snezhnaya. Copies can be picked up at newspaper stands, typically inside a town's train station. Each issue mixes sensational crime and railway news, official statements, adverts, theatre criticism, serialized fiction and stories sent in by readers. It is comic, gossipy and not always trustworthy, and it shows what ordinary Snezhnayans read while they wait for a train.
### STANDARD +
The paper is usually just called the Vestnik; the word is Russian for "herald". It is a very public medium: the Transport Bureau, the Snezhnograd Veche and private firms all use its pages, and anyone may submit a manuscript.

## practice
### STANDARD
Where it is found: newspaper stands in the train stations of Snezhnaya's towns and cities, and in a few other spots in settlements. Station life and newspaper reading go together; a traveller buys or reads an issue while waiting for the train.

What it prints: (1) news and crime reports, often in a breathless, patriotic tone with appeals to "Her Majesty"; (2) official notices, such as Transport Bureau statements on rail incidents and banned passengers; (3) paid adverts and franchise offers, some of them swindles; (4) health and lifestyle columns; (5) arts criticism, notably the "Today's On Ice" theatre column; (6) serialized fiction; and (7) reader-submitted pieces under the banners "Tales of the Strange and Incredible" and "Snowland Sensations", marked as contributor stories.

How readers take part: the Snezhnograd bulletin board carries a standing call for submissions. Authors whose work is approved are promised a free one-year subscription. Named contributors printed in the paper include Korsakov, Mamontov, Trigorin and Tripolev; Trigorin and Tripolev quarrel over a ballet production in two successive columns.
### LARGE +
Tone and conventions: crime stories use pseudonyms ("identified here by the pseudonym..."). Stories that are fiction carry a disclaimer that the paper takes no liability for their accuracy, and one long-running serial is advertised with a pointer to Torchlight Books for the full text. Editors also annotate reprints: a Fontainian travelogue claiming to describe Snezhnaya was printed with bracketed corrections marking its errors, and the editors note that its author never left Fontaine.

Mind-set of the pages: official bodies are praised, ordinary people are told to be wary of scams, and almost every column drifts toward gossip, grievance or conspiracy. The Transport Bureau uses the paper to advise citizens to buy its official delay insurance instead of private policies.
### ULTRA +
Because stands sit in different stations, different readers see different issues. The wiki lists distinct stand editions at Central Station (exit levels and platform), Morepesok, Svetloledovka, the Korolevskiy Theater, Sretomorozsk, Flamefeather Valley and Restaurant Zorya in Rankova Zorya, each carrying three articles. Treat this as a game-layout fact: in a scene, any stand may plausibly hold a mix of crime, advert and contributor material.

## regional_variation
### LARGE
The paper is printed for all of Snezhnaya, but its stories travel with the rails. Snezhnograd readers get capital crime and arts news (the ice-sculpture exhibition, the ballet column, the "Wandering Samurai" arrest). Stands in Morepesok, Flamefeather Valley and Rankova Zorya lean to rural legend, rail incidents and Okurov. Svetloledovka's stand carries insurance and theatre pieces, and an article notes that Prime Ice sculpture commissions went to designers there. Sretomorozsk carries a train derailment investigation and a further instalment of the Reed Miller serial.
### ULTRA +
Stories reach beyond Snezhnaya's borders: the paper reprints Fontainian writing, mentions Nod-Krai and its Curatorium of Secrets, retells an Inazuman merchant's flight to Snezhnograd, and carries pieces that joke about Sumeru's Akademiya and Natlan ruins. A reader in Snezhnaya therefore meets foreign places only as filtered by this paper's voice.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use the Vestnik as a prop and as a source of rumour in scenes set in Snezhnayan stations, trains or taverns. A character can overhear someone reading an item aloud, complain about a column, or recall a contributor story. Good uses: an NPC waiting at a stand with a folded issue; a bored traveller quoting a ridiculous health column; a Transport Bureau notice that explains a delay; a reader arguing about the ballet columns.

Guardrails: the paper is not an oracle. Do not let a character treat a Vestnik claim as established lore, and do not invent new articles that give Fatui or Tsaritsa secrets. Keep new items consistent in tone: bureaucratic notice, scam advert, serial fiction or reader letter. Stay with the Snezhnayan voice (formal addresses, pseudonyms, references to Her Majesty), and keep fictional crime stories non-graphic.
### LARGE +
Characters from outside Snezhnaya may find the paper charming or baffling. A Snezhnayan can plausibly treat contributor stories as folklore, adverts as warnings, and the Veche or Transport Bureau notices as the voice of authority, without any of them being fully trusted.

## examples
### LARGE
News and notices (Snezhnaya Vestnik, version 7.0):
- The Inazuman "Wandering Samurai" who terrorised Snezhnograd's Downtown District is arrested in an abandoned bakery by the Criminal Investigation Division and Druzhna patrols. The report says he was a bankrupt Ritou merchant who stole armour and a shrine blade, passed through Nod-Krai (where the Curatorium of Secrets exposed him), and Inazuman authorities refuse to recognise him as their citizen, so he awaits judgment under Snezhnayan law.
- A Transport Bureau investigation into freight train I-330, derailed on the Snezhnograd to Novokitezhgrad line, questions whether the conductor's decision to uncouple the freight cars and speed through a landslide was heroism or a breach of safety rules.
- A Transport Bureau notice says a Wild Hunt creature carried a Lightkeeper's lantern along the tundra line between Snezhnograd and Sretomorozsk, and that the Bureau and the Lightkeepers at Piramida are clearing the monsters.
- A second notice bans a grieving Snegovik, Mr. I. N. Ivanov, from the Snezhnograd to Novokitezhgrad trains because his sorrow chills the carriage and makes passengers ill.
- A Veche and Prime Ice Art Alliance exhibition of Prime Ice sculpture is announced as the tsaritsa-era promise of art for everyone, with commissions given to designers in Svetloledovka.
- The investigation of the missing jewel "Star of the Snowfield" is reported with pseudonyms taken from a ballet; the suspect confesses to murder but denies theft.
- A Fatui-funded astronomer reports seeing a crystal palace and creatures on the moon through a giant telescope; the instrument then sets fire to the observatory, and the paper promises a six-issue serial of his letter.

Adverts and columns: Snezhnajoy Insurance sells train-delay, Prime Ice melt and "audience trauma" cover, but a later article reports it refuses claims on technicalities and is under investigation. A franchise offer for "Frosta", described as an upgrade to Fonta, demands ten million Mora. A Society for Traditional Snezhnayan Medicine column insists hot baths are harmful and a woman publicly announces she has left her husband over his refusal to bathe. "Healthguards" columns offer deliberately silly health tips. A grand duke posts a boastful job advert for private guards after his fountain-repainting disaster.
### ULTRA +
Tales and serials: "Tales of the Strange and Incredible" (a duke's confession, a haunted honeymoon in Svetloledovka, the Firebird of Okurov, a witch of the Jack Frosts, a headless train attendant, Volkodlak miners and a curse, a naturalist's nightmare through time) and "Snowland Sensations" (an ode to the snowy plains, and two conspiracy essays claiming the world's ancient rulers all derive from Tsar Monomakh). The Reed Miller thief serial appears in numbered chapters. See the sub-entries for the details.

## entry: Vestnik contributor stories

@keys2: Vestnik, Snezhnaya Vestnik
### STANDARD
Reader submissions are labelled contributor stories. "Tales of the Strange and Incredible" retells ghost stories and urban legends told as "a friend of a friend": a duke recounting the fire that took his family, a honeymooning couple found dead near Svetloledovka, the Firebird of Okurov that feeds on memories, a lost adventurer saved by a Jack Frost witch who demands a Jack Frost choir, a headless railway attendant wandering the line with a lantern, Volkodlak miners whose find from the ruins of Kitezhgrad is blamed on the curse of Chernabog, and a naturalist who claims a soul-voyage into the age of lizards. "Snowland Sensations" offers a poem to the snowy plains and pseudo-historical essays. Each carries a disclaimer that it may contain fiction.
### LARGE +
Two "New Teyvat Chronicles" pieces argue that Decarabian, Al-Ahmar, Remus and Och-Kan are regional echoes of one ruler, Tsar Monomakh, and that the unified ancient ruins found across Teyvat prove his world-spanning realm. They are presented as the author's theory, are full of bad etymology and invented supporting evidence, and should be read as comic crank scholarship. The Okurov and Svetloledovka stories are legends told in the paper, not established history of those places.

## entry: Reed Miller serial
@keys: Seven Impossible Mysteries of Reed Miller, Reed Miller serial
@keys2: Vestnik, Snezhnaya Vestnik, newspaper
### LARGE
The paper prints numbered chapters of "The Seven Impossible Mysteries of Reed Miller, Thief Extraordinaire!", with the story of the Platinum Piece shown in Chapters 5 and 11. In the printed extracts a man presenting himself as Grand Duke Brahe Trubetskoy exposes a viscount who took a platinum chess piece and killed his wife; in the later chapter the real grand duke learns that the "envoy" was Reed Miller in disguise, and that Miller has a female companion who calls herself Wagtail. Only fragments of the serial appear in the paper. For the book and the character, see the Reed Miller entries.

## entry: Vestnik and the Transport Bureau
@keys: Transport Bureau notice, Transport Bureau statement, banned passengers list, freight train I-330
@keys2: Vestnik, Snezhnaya Vestnik, newspaper
### LARGE
The Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau uses the paper to issue public statements: a rail-line hazard notice naming a Wild Hunt creature and a Lightkeeper's lantern, a ruling banning a grieving Snegovik from trains, an inquiry into freight train I-330's derailment, and a warning about insurance fraud that promotes its own delay insurance. These notices show how the Bureau speaks to the public: careful, procedural and slightly defensive.
