## summary
### LITE
Everyday life in Fontaine, the Hydro nation of justice, mixes elegant city living with steam-age technology and a love of drama. People ride aquabuses along the waterways, read the newspaper The Steambird, devour mystery novels, go to the theatre as casually as drinking water, and give flowers whose colours carry fixed meanings. Machines called clockwork meka do routine work. Humans share the nation with the Melusines. Below the polished Court of Fontaine, the poorer quarter of Fleuve Cendre is run by the Spina di Rosula, and the law is notoriously fussy.
### STANDARD +
Fontaine is hospitable to outsiders but fixed on rules, performance and craft. Trials were treated like shows in the era of the former Archon Furina, and the habit of treating public life as theatre outlived her. Diving is a common pastime and even a livelihood; holders of a Vision can simply swim, while everyone else wears a large, bulky diving suit. Outside the Court, travellers meet Treasure Hoarders and various criminal groups.

For scenes, the best tone is polite, articulate and a little dramatic, with small bureaucratic or legal absurdities in the background.
### LARGE +
Under the surface calm are real divisions: the formal, decorated clothing and tidy streets of the Court of Fontaine against the plainer, more practical life in Fleuve Cendre and outlying areas. Fontainians lived for centuries under a prophecy that their people would dissolve into water; the wiki's culture page notes that once the prophecy passed, people stopped worrying and returned to ordinary routines. Technology, publishing and the arts are the main sources of civic pride.

## practice
### LITE
Common routines and pastimes: newspaper reading, theatre and cinema, mystery novels, aquabus rides, diving, gifting flowers with set meanings, and following the courts.
### STANDARD +
The Steambird is the main newspaper of the Court of Fontaine and is read across Teyvat. Its chief editor is Euphrasie, with reporters such as Charlotte, a photographer, a reviewer and columnists. Columns include "All Things Astrological" (Mona took it over from a retiring columnist) and "Tales of Humanity," a reader-submission column where locals also post commissions. In-game, that column explains the Reputation system. The paper also gives the La Verite Prize, Fontaine's top media award, presented at the Opera Epiclese.

Theatre is woven into ordinary life; a local saying equates growing up with theatre to being raised on drinking water. Most shows are held at the Opera Epiclese. Plays by Coppelius, a famed playwright of about 500 years ago, are still performed. Mystery novels such as "Great Detective Hurlock" and "The Case Files of Miss Orith" are very popular.

Aquabuses run on a dedicated aquarail. Melusine guides give commentary on the scenery, and the lines are named after Callas, Clementine and Navia. The Callas Line connects the Court to the Fontaine Research Institute, the Clementine Line connects it to Romaritime Harbor, and the Navia Line goes to Marcotte Station near Erinnyes.
### LARGE +
Technology: Alain Guillotin is credited as a pioneer after the cataclysm, with kinetic cores, the Arkhe energy system, clockwork mechanisms and the Fontaine Research Institute. Arkhe splits into Pneuma and Ousia, and combining both yields Annihilation energy; clockwork meka can do menial labour and entertainment, and be adapted for combat. The Kamera, and a film variant, originated in Fontaine. A man named Garcia is working on a calculating "Numbering Machine." The wiki records a villager, Santon, complaining that the city air is far dirtier than Sumeru's forests, which suggests industrial pollution.

Law and administration: Fontaine's legal system is famously complex. NPCs such as Patrice say tiny infractions can be harshly punished (for example, eating ketchup on its own). The Maison Gestion handles paperwork and its staff are famously overworked. Before a trial the accused may choose to face a Champion Duelist, which is rarely survivable. The Fortress of Meropide, where convicts are sent, runs under its own rules, and people who served time there are often shunned afterwards.

Economy: Lumidouce Harbor handles most imports and exports; some ships also use Romaritime Harbor, which has an aquabus line. Fontaine exports mechanical goods and lacks organic items, which are in demand.
### ULTRA +
Melusines: an all-female, amphibious people, the "daughters" of Elynas. Most live in Merusea Village, though many who work in the Court live in the city for convenience. They are diligent, have a strong sense of justice and sharper senses than humans, and make up much of the Marechaussee Phantom. Their sense of taste differs from human taste, so their cooking is often thought unfit for humans. In Merusea Village, scrap mechanical parts are valued and used as currency called components.

Aquabus history: the system was created by Callas working with the Court and the Research Institute. He thought it would improve relations with Poisson. Costs were high and led to a feud with a fellow Spina di Rosula member, Romeuf, who thought the money should improve the town. After his wife Clementine died giving birth to Navia, Callas funded construction of three lines. An explosion at the Research Institute, linked to Edwin Eastinghouse's Arkhium research (he denies responsibility), destroyed the Callas Line. After the Prophecy of Fontaine was resolved, Romeuf's faction plotted to collapse the Clementine Line's supports onto Poisson. The Traveler and Navia stopped it, and Navia then decided to rebuild the Callas Line and add more stops.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Court of Fontaine versus Fleuve Cendre. The Court has formal, decorated clothing, theatres, cafes and orderly law. Fleuve Cendre is a settlement in the sewers beneath it, mostly home to ex-convicts, criminals and the destitute, with the Spina di Rosula as de facto government. Outside the city, clothing is simpler and practical, and bandits are a real concern.
### LARGE +
Fleuve Cendre was historically a grey legal area where the Court had no jurisdiction. Under the Spina, residents report that armed fighting is punished, illegal funding is harder to find, and the group mediates disputes and handles dealings with the Court. Many residents have spent time in the Fortress of Meropide. The Fortress has its own vocabulary of "overworld" for the surface and "underworld" for itself.

Fontaine's uniforms indicate roles: Gardes (with an emblem of two back-to-back Melusines beneath scales), chefs, sailors, aquabus operators, divers, guards and Champion Duelists each have distinctive outfits. Maid outfits resemble those in Mondstadt.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Good scene hooks: boarding an aquabus with a chatty Melusine guide; a reporter chasing a story; an argument over a novel or a play; a petty regulation turning serious; a gift of flowers whose meaning someone else notices. Keep legal and social rules strict but often amusing.
### LARGE +
Language of flowers is a reliable source of social tension: the right flower says more than words. Fontaine's people are open about theatre, so characters often pose, narrate their own moves or treat arguments as performances. Do not turn Melusines into children or ornaments; they are skilled professionals with their own culture.

## examples
### STANDARD
A cafe regular folds The Steambird over a half-finished mystery novel. An aquabus guide cheerfully names every landmark. A stranger hands someone a Lumidouce Bell and is understood to be saying farewell.

## entry: The Steambird newspaper

@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Steambird is the main newspaper of the Court of Fontaine, read across Teyvat. Euphrasie is chief editor; Charlotte is a reporter. Columns include "All Things Astrological" by Mona, "Tales of Humanity" (reader submissions and commissions, behind the game's Reputation system), and the out-of-game "Teyvat Food Notes." The paper hands out the La Verite Prize at the Opera Epiclese. According to the wiki it covered the Traveler's fight against Dvalin and Kazuha's duel with the Raiden Shogun, to wide interest.
### LARGE +
Its mascot is Sir Arthur, a clockwork bird in a top hat. The paper's emblem is a gear around that bird. It treats public events as stories and gathers reader contributions, so it is both informant and gossip.

## entry: Aquabus
@keys: aquabus, aquarail, Clementine Line, Navia Line, Callas Line
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The aquabus is Fontaine's water tour and transit vehicle, running on an aquarail with Melusine guides. Lines: Clementine (Court of Fontaine to Romaritime Harbor), Navia (Court to Marcotte Station, near Erinnyes), and Callas (Court to the Research Institute, damaged after an Arkhium-related explosion). A Lumidouce Harbor line was rumoured or cancelled. The lines are named for Callas's wife Clementine, his daughter Navia, and himself.
### LARGE +
The system began as Callas's project to improve relations with Poisson and led to a feud with Romeuf inside the Spina di Rosula. After the prophecy, Romeuf's plan to bring down the Clementine Line was foiled; Navia then began restoring the Callas Line.

## entry: Language of flowers
@keys: language of flowers, Rainbow Rose, Romaritime Flower, Lumidouce Bell
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Fontaine's flowers carry meanings. A Rainbow Rose stands for passion and romantic encounters (it is a pink lily-like flower that took the name of an extinct rainbow-coloured rose). A Romaritime Flower stands for loyalty and unswerving oaths; it blooms on contact with Hydro, and folklore says it was a Hydro spirit in love with Egeria. A Lumidouce Bell stands for parting and a wish to reunite; Coppelius's play Golden Hyperborea calls it "Weeping Crystal." Some flowers have less common alternate meanings.

## entry: Fontinalia Film Festival
@keys: Fontinalia Film Festival, Furina Award
@version: 4.3
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
During the limited-time event Roses and Muskets, the Fontaine Film Association added a film festival to the Fontinalia Festival. People may submit films, and the highest-scoring entry receives the Furina Award, a name nobody changed after Furina's abdication. The first award went to The Two Musketeers (Musketeer Pictures, produced by Xavier, directed by Furina); in the limited-time event Sunny Summer Fontinalia (6.7) the film adaptation of Great Detective Hurlock won the latest one. Event-derived details; not every Fontainian is shown knowing them.

## entry: Fleuve Cendre
@keys: Fleuve Cendre
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Fleuve Cendre is a settlement in the sewers below the Court of Fontaine, home to ex-convicts, criminals and the poor. It was long a grey legal area outside the Court's jurisdiction; the Spina di Rosula is now its de facto government. Residents mention that violence is punished and basic supplies and help are available.
