## summary
### LITE
Daily life in Inazuma is an island society of samurai families, shrine and festival calendars, family-run shops and fishing and farming villages, all administered through the Tri-Commission under the Raiden Shogun. Before and as the Traveler arrived (the Vision Hunt Decree had been in force for about a year), the Sakoku and Vision Hunt decrees made it tense, poor and closed; once both ended, the nation reopened to visitors and trade.
### STANDARD +
Rice, seafood, sakura, books, fireworks and swordsmanship are the recurring textures. Pride and honour matter a great deal, especially among samurai, and the shrine, clan and commission someone answers to shape much of what an ordinary person can do. Watatsumi Island and the Shogunate islands follow different faiths and governments, though both count as Inazuma.
### LARGE +
Inazuma has only one major city, Inazuma City, where most of the population lives, and one port, Ritou; the rest of the people live in small villages and estates, some now abandoned after disasters. The decree years left scars: scarce goods, fear of the Tenryou Commission, ships lost in the storm belt, and families with Visions deciding whether to hide them.

## practice
### LITE
Everyday matters run through three commissions. Tenryou handles security, Kanjou handles borders and documents (checking foreigners and returning citizens), and Yashiro handles shrines, festivals and culture. Samurai houses send children into the Shogun's army and guard their honour, rice and seafood are the staples, and most towns have a shrine nearby.
### STANDARD +
- **Work and trade.** Printing is a main trade, led by the Yae Publishing House, known across Teyvat for its "This Novel Is Amazing!" contest. Inazuma also exports kimonos, toys and fireworks. An NPC calls Naganohara Fireworks, Yoimiya's family business, one of the oldest and best firework shops in the country. Smithing is another famed craft, with Jade Steel from Tatarasuna in its finest swords, although that production has halted since the peace between Watatsumi and the Shogunate. Jakotsu Mine closed after a "sickness" and Mikage Furnace after its core went critical, so many people hesitate to return to either.
- **Farming and food.** Rice is the staple grain, eaten steamed or pounded into cakes. Seafood is central and noodles are popular. Sakura gives dishes a flavour found nowhere else, as in sakura mochi and sakura tempura. Poor soil limits farming on some islands, including Watatsumi and around Konda Village. Watatsumi's people explain bleaching soil as "Holy Soil" and hold the Watatsumi Mitama Omatsuri against it, while a shrine maiden who studied at the Akademiya thinks salty seawater is the cause.
### LARGE +
- **Homes and buildings.** Aralia, maple, otogi and yumemiru wood are the common timbers; yumemiru is used for shrines.
- **Honour.** Samurai families are common and proud. Losing to someone, or needing an outsider's help, is felt as shame.
- **Dress.** Kimono-style garments are everyday wear. Fishers are known by their wide kasa hats, shrine maidens wear a kosode with a hakama that is red on Narukami Island and blue on Watatsumi Island, and soldiers wear ashigaru-style uniforms. Clans carry crests (mon), as the Kamisato and Kujou do.
- **Sayings.** A national motto praises the Shogun's everlasting reign. Inazumans who leave the country for training sometimes call themselves "shugenja".
- **Governance.** The Tri-Commission is led by three great clans: Hiiragi (Kanjou), Kujou (Tenryou) and Kamisato (Yashiro). Inazumans compare it to the Liyue Qixing and the Eight Trades. When a commissioner falls, their clan loses standing and subordinate clans compete for the post, causing instability. The Yashiro commissioner also directs the Shuumatsuban, a ninja organisation for intelligence and counter-operations. Watatsumi Island is governed mainly by the Divine Priestess of the Sangonomiya Clan and has its own Watatsumi Army.
- **Armies and ranks.** The Shogun's Army has recruits, captains and samurai. Non-samurai favour polearms, while samurai use swords and bows because the sword is highly regarded.
- **Spiritual neighbours.** Humans are most numerous, but tengu, oni and kitsune live in Inazuma, mostly in seclusion, as the adepti do in Liyue. Bake-danuki are known for pranking travellers by offering food and shelter that turn out to be leaves and mud.
- **Urban life.** Inazuma City, on the southern cliffs of Narukami Island, is famed for a low crime rate and many family businesses; most of the population lives there and it keeps expanding outward. Hanamizaka, north of the river, is one of its three parts alongside Tenryou and Tenshukaku.
- **Lawlessness outside the city.** Treasure Hoarders and masterless warriors such as Nobushi and Kairagi are common in the wilderness.

## eras
@version: 2.0
### LARGE
**During the decrees (Version 2.0 Archon Quest).** The Vision Hunt Decree (confiscating Visions) and the Sakoku Decree (closing the borders) were both in force. Few goods came in, and some smugglers refused to bring anything to Inazuma. An NPC describes the atmosphere as tense and dangerous. The Tenryou Commission carried out the Vision Hunt and helped enforce Sakoku, and the Kanjou Commission checked everyone entering or leaving and treated ships as a possible means of escape, so sea trade and fishing both declined. Both decrees were in reality part of a Fatui plot to destabilise Inazuma and sell Delusions. A civil war broke out between the Watatsumi Army and the Shogunate.
**After the decrees.** The Vision Hunt Decree was abolished first and corrupt officials were removed. The Sakoku Decree lingered, though restrictions on foreigners were relaxed, and ended after Ei's duel with the Shogun puppet in Transient Dreams. Visitors, trade and foreign contacts returned, and Inazumans were free to travel again. The ceasefire with Watatsumi turned into a peace.
### ULTRA +
Soon after, the Yae Publishing House revived the old Irodori Festival as an international light-novel convention. Having admitted her beliefs had been wrong, Ei decided to take a more active role in giving her people the happiness they deserved.

## regional_variation
### LARGE
- **Narukami Island:** Shogunate culture, the Grand Narukami Shrine, sakura, the capital and most family businesses; shrine maidens in red.
- **Ritou:** the outlying island port, and the only legal way in for foreigners during Sakoku. Kanjou Commission officials processed documents here, and visitors could not leave on foot without a Travel Permit, which the Traveler eventually obtained in the quest Chisato's Letter.
- **Watatsumi Island:** worships Orobashi rather than Ei, under the Sangonomiya Clan; blue shrine robes, poor soil, its own army and the Mitama Omatsuri ritual.
- **Yashiori, Kannazuka, Seirai:** Yashiori and Kannazuka are Shogunate islands. Seirai has no permanent residents after the Thunder Manifestation destroyed it, and Higi Village and a village south of it were evacuated after Tatarigami energy was released.
- **Tsurumi Island:** part of the archipelago but empty of people since its Thunderbird civilisation was destroyed by Kapatcir.

## festivals
### LARGE
Festivals shape the calendar, and the Yashiro Commission handles many of them. The Summer Festival on Narukami Island centres on the Naganohara fireworks show, and Yoimiya is sometimes called its queen. The Irodori Festival honours the Five Kasen, poet-singers of ancient Inazuma, and was revived after Sakoku. The Mikawa Flower Festival was once a youkai gathering over five hundred years old. The Goshintou Ceremony offers masterwork swords to the Grand Narukami Shrine, and the Warrior's Spirit Swordfighting Exhibition tests swordsmen, with an emissary standing in for the Shogun.
### ULTRA +
The Hyakkiyakou, the Night Parade of a Hundred Demons, grew out of the Kitsune Saiguu's story-telling gatherings and has not been held for centuries because so many youkai died in the Cataclysm; in the quest The Great Narukami Offering, Yae Miko sent off youkai memories in a farewell. Several of these festivals are known mainly through limited-time events, so not every Inazuman character will have the same picture of them. See their own entries for details.

## duels
### LARGE
Disputes can be taken to the Shogun as a Duel Before the Throne, fought at Tenshukaku and witnessed by her. The duel continues until one side cannot go on, and the loser dies, by a lethal blow or by execution with the Musou no Hitotachi. Kazuha's friend challenged the Shogun over the Vision Hunt and lost this way, and the Traveler challenged Signora in the same form. Treat it as a grave formal procedure, not a casual brawl.

## scene_use
### LITE
Open with sensory detail: sakura petals, grilled fish or sweet dango, shop signs, shrine bells, boats, or thunder over the sea. Locals are polite but proud about clan, craft and honour, and they know the commissions by name. Pick the era first, because before and after Sakoku have very different moods.
### STANDARD +
- During the decrees: tense and careful, guarded talk, few imported goods, Visions kept hidden, travellers questioned by officials.
- After: warmer and busier, with foreign goods, curious visitors, festivals and light novels in conversation.
- A Watatsumi islander should not use Narukami customs automatically, and the reverse.
- Clerks and guards speak in terms of commission, clan and permit; pride rather than hostility is the usual tone.
### LARGE +
Concrete settings include a Hanamizaka street, the Yae Publishing House shop, the Ritou customs office or a fireworks stall. Do not invent laws, prices or holidays beyond what is listed here, and keep scenes at or before Version 7.1.
### ULTRA +
Names worth knowing in conversation: Kamisato Ayaka and Ayato of the Yashiro Commission, Kujou Sara and Kujou Kamaji of the Tenryou Commission, Yae Miko at the publishing house and Grand Narukami Shrine, Yoimiya for fireworks, and Sangonomiya Kokomi and Gorou on Watatsumi. Their own entries carry the biographies.

## entry: Tri-Commission
@keys: Tri-Commission, Tenryou Commission, Kanjou Commission, Yashiro Commission
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Tri-Commission administers Inazuma for the Shogun. The Tenryou Commission, led by the Kujou Clan, handles internal and external security; its commissioner Kujou Takayuki was dismissed over the Fatui plot, Kujou Kamaji acts as proxy and Kujou Sara commands the Shogunate forces. The Kanjou Commission, led by the Hiiragi Clan, handles borders and documents; Hiiragi Shinsuke was dismissed after supplying the Fatui, and Hiiragi Chisato appears in the Travel Permit quest (Chisato's Letter). The Yashiro Commission, led by the Kamisato Clan under Kamisato Ayato, handles shrines, festivals and culture and directs the Shuumatsuban.

## entry: Ritou and the Travel Permit
@keys: Ritou, Travel Permit, Kanjou Commission Ashigaru
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Ritou is the Kanjou Commission's processing centre for non-Inazumans. During the Sakoku years foreigners could not leave Ritou on foot without a Travel Permit, though Fatui and Snezhnayan merchants were at first exempt because of diplomatic ties. The Traveler eventually received a permit from Kujou Kamaji in the quest Chisato's Letter.

## entry: Yae Publishing House
@keys: Yae Publishing House, This Novel Is Amazing, light novel
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A leading publisher in Inazuma and across Teyvat, founded by Yae Miko, with a contest called "This Novel Is Amazing!" Its notice board advertises Zhenyu's Liyue work A Legend of Sword and a search for editors who like light literature, and Albedo's artwork is said to interest the house. Yae Miko is editor-in-chief, known as Lady Yae.
