## summary
### LITE
Inazuman arts mix refined pastimes and working crafts. Flower arrangement (ikebana) is a polished accomplishment of the noble Kamisato house. Poetry is honoured through the legend of the Five Kasen, the ancient poets remembered at the Irodori Festival. Printing and popular fiction are a major trade, led by Yae Publishing House. Bladesmithing is the most famous craft, descended from five forging schools called the Raiden Gokaden, of which only the Amenoma Art still thrives.
### STANDARD +
Two of these traditions are tied to the nation's wounds. The Gokaden collapsed through a conspiracy generations ago, and the nation's reopening after the Sakoku Decree revived its public arts, including the festival and the publishing contests. Martial arts have their own entry.

## practice
### STANDARD
Ikebana is the art of arranging flowers into a composed display. The wiki's culture page lists Kamisato Ayaka and Fushizome as practitioners; Ayaka's profile counts flower arrangement among the disciplines she has mastered alongside tea ceremony, Go, music, poetry and dance, and she was preparing ikebana lessons during the 2.6 event.

Poetry and song hold a legendary place. The Five Kasen were gifted poets of ancient Inazuma who, in turns, presented a yearly collection to the Shogun, and their works are said to have helped Inazuman culture blossom. Stage plays once retold their story with each figure dressed in one colour, so audiences named them by colour.

Printing is described as one of Inazuma's main trades. Yae Publishing House, founded by Yae Miko, is known across Teyvat as a giant of the industry and periodically holds the "This Novel Is Amazing!" competition.

Bladesmithing is the nation's signature craft. Inazuman swords were famed enough to bring steady trade from Fontaine, and the best-known material is jade steel from Tatarasuna, though production halted after the peace between Watatsumi and the Shogunate.
### LARGE +
The Raiden Gokaden ("Five Traditions of Raiden") were five smithing schools, each with its own forging art and closely guarded techniques; variation is said to come from astrology, materials, furnace conditions, the smith's character and elemental changes. The five were the Amenoma, Isshin, Futsu, Hyakume and Senju Arts. Their families became important within the Yashiro Commission because swordsmithing mattered so much to Inazuma's culture.

The Goshintou Ceremony honours the Shogun's arrival and the sword-making she brought: master smiths present their finest swords, goshintou, to the Grand Narukami Shrine as an offering. This detail comes from an in-game book cited on the wiki.

Yae Publishing House's catalogue appears across Teyvat. It publishes "A Legend of Sword" (written by Xingqiu under the pen name Zhenyu and illustrated by Albedo as Calx), "Legend of the Shattered Halberd" by Mr. Nine, and a long-running serial, "The Soulblazer Saga", with spin-offs and merchandise. Its columnists include "Ms. Hina". Chang the Ninth, a Liyue author, wrote an entry for the competition, mixing Liyue and Inazuman storytelling.
### ULTRA +
Serialisation travels widely: a Natlan character remarks that a serial takes two weeks to reach Natlan and about a year to appear as a collected volume. The publishing house is said to be no more than five hundred years old. The wiki lists other works mentioned in quests, such as "Onibudou" and "Literature Club"; treat these as flavour rather than central lore.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Within the Inazuma archipelago the crafts vary by place. Narukami Island and Inazuma City hold the publishing house, noble households and the Amenoma Smithy. Tatarasuna was the centre of jade-steel forging, with the Mikage Furnace; both are now closed or halted. Watatsumi Island's shrine maidens wear blue where those of Narukami wear red, and the island has its own army and clan crest.
### LARGE +
Outside Inazuma the arts are carried by export and by foreign admirers: Fontaine buys Inazuman blades, Liyue and Natlan readers follow Yae Publishing House serials, and visiting writers and artists take part in its events.

## history
### STANDARD
The Five Kasen belong to the distant past, and much about them was lost in the cataclysm five hundred years ago. During the Sakoku Decree, trade and exports dropped sharply; after it was repealed the economy and public arts began to recover, and the Irodori Festival was revived in the limited-time Hues of the Violet Garden event (2.6).

The Raiden Gokaden were broken apart in the Case of the Eccentric: three schools vanished through "mishaps", the Isshin Art's smiths fled after failing to forge a sacred blade, and a man calling himself Kunikuzushi beat Kaedehara Yoshinori and the Kamisato head who pursued them. Only the Amenoma Art continues, led by Amenoma Tougo with Hajime as his pupil.
### LARGE +
The Isshin Art had been ordered to recreate a sacred blade from a diagram sent by the Shogun; its smiths could not forge a working sword and fled to Snezhnaya for fear of punishment. The diagram had been tampered with by Kunikuzushi. Yoshinori and the Kamisato head kept the truth secret; the Kamisato head died soon after, the Yashiro Commission and the Kamisato Clan lost standing, and Yoshinori ended the Isshin Art.

The attacker's own origin is tied to Tatarasuna. Years earlier a kabukimono befriended the smith Niwa Hisahide, who ran the Mikage Furnace. Il Dottore, disguised as the Fontaine mechanic Escher, manipulated the furnace crisis; Niwa died, and a false tale blamed Niwa's flight on cowardice. The kabukimono later, as a Fatui agent, planned the fall of the Gokaden in revenge on an unspecified bladesmith. This is the original history; the Wanderer's own story and the Irminsul revision are covered elsewhere.

Yoshinori recorded the truth in ink that appeared only on contact with water. Kamisato Ayato found it while investigating a Fatui spy, then staged a fabricated Five Kasen legend during the festival to lead Kazuha to it. This is a limited-time event (2.6).
### ULTRA +
After the character later known as the Wanderer removed himself from Irminsul, the history changed in small ways: Dottore still infiltrated the furnace, but Niwa did not die at his hand; instead Niwa and an unnamed smith died purifying it. The smith's orphaned descendants nursed a grudge, and about three hundred years later a clan head killed his way through the Futsu, Hyakume and Senju schools before the Kaedehara and Kamisato clans stopped him. The Traveler verified these altered records in "A Dance of Destruction". Tougo's account also changed to say that the Gokaden stem from the Shogun.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use Inazuman arts for quiet, cultured scenes: a flower-arranging lesson in a noble estate, a bookstall at Yae Publishing House, a poetry contest, or a smith's workshop at the Amenoma Smithy. Smiths and the Kamisato household treat the Gokaden's fall as a sensitive subject; the full story is known only to those who read Yoshinori's letter or lived the events. Writers and editors are enthusiastic and commercial rather than solemn.
### LARGE +
Do not give the Five Kasen real names or fixed works; present them as legend. Keep Ayato's fabricated poems separate from any ancient text. Do not make the Gokaden a single unified guild that still exists.

## examples
### STANDARD
Examples include Kamisato Ayaka's flower arrangement, the poems and plays of the Five Kasen, "A Legend of Sword" and "The Soulblazer Saga" from Yae Publishing House, jade-steel blades from Tatarasuna, and the sacred swords offered at the Goshintou Ceremony.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the Amenoma Art survives under Amenoma Tougo, while the other four Gokaden schools are lost or nearly lost. Yae Publishing House remains a leading publisher. The Irodori Festival is shown once in canon (2.6) and its later schedule is unstated.

## entry: Raiden Gokaden
@keys: Raiden Gokaden, Gokaden, Amenoma Art, Isshin Art, Futsu Art, Hyakume Art, Senju Art
@version: 2.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Raiden Gokaden were five Inazuman forging schools: Amenoma, Isshin, Futsu, Hyakume and Senju. Only the Amenoma Art, led by Amenoma Tougo, still trains new smiths; the Isshin Art barely survives; the rest are lost. The fall followed the Case of the Eccentric, in which a man calling himself Kunikuzushi defeated Kaedehara Yoshinori and the Kamisato head after tampering with a sacred-blade diagram.
### LARGE +
The Isshin Art was held by the Kaedehara, Niwa and Akame families, and its three masters were known as the Isshin Sansaku. The origin of the schools is disputed between a ronin teaching five disciples and the Shogun's teaching.

## entry: Yae Publishing House
@keys: Yae Publishing House, Yae Hall, This Novel Is Amazing, Soulblazer Saga
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
Yae Publishing House is Inazuma's giant of the printing trade, founded and led by Yae Miko, who also serves as editor-in-chief. It runs the "This Novel Is Amazing!" contest and publishes serials such as "A Legend of Sword" and "The Soulblazer Saga". Its authors include Xingqiu (as Zhenyu) and Mr. Nine; Albedo has illustrated under the name Calx.

## entry: The Five Kasen
@keys: Five Kasen, Suikou, Aoi no Okina, Akahito, Sumizome, Kuronushi
@version: 2.6
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
Ancient Inazuman poets whose names are colour-based stage names: Suikou (green), Aoi no Okina (blue), Akahito (red), Sumizome (white) and Kuronushi (black). Their true lives are lost. The poem series told in the 2.6 event, The Five Kasen's Splendor, was written by Kamisato Ayato to guide Kazuha to a hidden family secret.
