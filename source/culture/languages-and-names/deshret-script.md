## summary
### LITE
The Deshret Script is the community's name for the writing found on the ruins of the Great Red Sand in Sumeru's desert: on doorways, pillars, obelisks, tombs and Primal Construct inscriptions. It looks like hieroglyphs built from the Teyvat alphabet, and the letters mostly spell out names and words from real-world Egyptian, Persian and Ancient Greek. Sacred Seals and Weathered Obelisks each bear a single character of it.
### STANDARD +
The game never names or explains the script. It links the desert's old civilisation, remembered through King Deshret, with a look of its own that stands apart from the Vedanagari-style writing of the cities.

## practice
### LITE
It is written without spaces, capitals or punctuation, so fans add these when transcribing. Inscriptions are short: names, titles and single words on doors and monuments.
### STANDARD +
- King Deshret's tomb in the Mausoleum bears two lines read as an Egyptian-style title for Amon (Amon, Chief of the Dead) and a royal title read as "King Deshret, Conqueror of the Desert".
- Pillars at Khaj-Nisut, the Towers of Betrayal and the Great Hall of Truths read AHTATEN, and obelisks in and outside Khaj-Nisut and the Mausoleum read AKHTAMON, combining a word for horizon with Amon.
- Doorways across the Great Red Sand repeat names such as ASAR, SEKHEM, KHEPERA, KHEFTIU, TEM, RA, HORAKHT and AMON in random order; some text is mirrored.
### LARGE +
- Primal Construct inscriptions read POIM ANDR ES (Poimandres, a Greek title); the Algorithm of Semi-Intransient Matrix of Overseer Network shows KOSMOS, EROS and PLE ROMA.
- Lamb-Devourer Rock is read as a Persian phrase, Marg bar Parvezravan ("Death to Parvezravan"), which is the code used to enter Thutmose's hideout in the Hidden Mercenaries event.
- The Nameless Tablet at Sobek Oasis shows a stepped pattern read as a barbarous name from Greek magical papyri (Ablanathanalba).
- Outside the open world, Faruzan's idle animation shows MATHEMATIKE SYNTAXIS, Cyno's namecard Silence uses it, and a chair in The Orchard of Pairidaeza bears King Deshret's name.

## regional_variation
### LITE
It belongs to the desert of Sumeru and to the ruins of King Deshret's people. The cities of the rainforest and Sumeru City use a different script, covered under culture.sumeru-script.
### STANDARD +
Both scripts descend from the Teyvat alphabet in the wiki's analysis, but they differ in look and in source languages: Egyptian-style words in the ruins, Devanagari-style lettering in the city.

### ULTRA +
The wiki notes that the Deshret Script is based on the Teyvat alphabet but draws on Egyptian hieroglyphs for its look. Transcriptions fill in unclear letters by educated guess, so a reading such as HORAKHT may be incomplete. The repetition of names such as Asar (Osiris), Ra and Amon on doorways, and Kosmos, Eros and Pleroma on Primal Construct machinery, is the clearest sign that the ruins' lettering mixes ancient Egyptian and Greek ideas of order and creation, which is an out-of-universe observation, not lore.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use it as colour on a ruin: a door of angular glyphs, a tomb inscription of a few letters. A scholar of the Akademiya might guess at one word.
### STANDARD +
- Keep decoded text short, a name or title.
- Do not invent a translation for the full inscriptions or a grammar; canon provides neither.
- Tie inscriptions to the ruins and to King Deshret's remembered civilisation, not to the modern cities.

## examples
### STANDARD
Fans read AHTATEN as a word meaning the place where the sun rises and AKHTAMON as horizon plus Amon. These are interpretations, not in-game translations.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the script stays unnamed and untranslated in the sources read, with no change in 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content found.

## entry: Deshret Script

### STANDARD
The Deshret Script is the unofficial name for the writing on the ruins of the Great Red Sand. It resembles hieroglyphs built from the Teyvat alphabet and spells out names and words from Egyptian, Persian and Greek, as on King Deshret's tomb, pillars at Khaj-Nisut, Sacred Seals and Weathered Obelisks. The game does not name or translate it.
