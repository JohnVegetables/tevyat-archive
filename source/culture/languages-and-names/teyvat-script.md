## summary
### LITE
The Teyvat Script is the common writing seen across most of the world: on signposts, shop signs, books, notices and tutorial pages. It is the everyday way to write the common language that most people in Teyvat speak. "Teyvat Script" is an unofficial name; in the world, people simply read and write.
### STANDARD +
Visually it looks like a mirrored, broken-up Fraktur (medieval German) hand. It spells out ordinary English words, with a few Latin phrases; there is no letter case or punctuation, and spaces are optional. It appears in Mondstadt, Liyue and Natlan, so it behaves as a shared script even though some nations also have their own.
### LARGE +
It stands apart from other scripts in canon: the Ancient Teyvat Script is the old script of the Unified Civilization, used by Khaenri'ah and the Abyss Order and for Natlan's Ancient Names; Hilichurlian is a spoken language with no known writing; and Liyue, Inazuma, Sumeru, Fontaine and Snezhnaya each have local scripts documented elsewhere in this archive.
### ULTRA +
The in-world implication is that merchants, adventurers and officials from different nations can talk, trade and read the same notices without translation. Canon never dramatises how that came about, and no source gives it a date.

## practice
### LITE
Characters read signs and notices as plain words. Nobody comments on the lettering. Letters, notes and menus in scenes can be treated as ordinary written language.
### STANDARD +
Examples on the wiki's transcription pages: Mondstadt's Angel's Share pub sign (it reads "Pub"), a signpost on Dragonspine reading "Notice", the pedestal of the Anemo Archon statue reading "The Gateway of Celestia", a Good Hunter board advertising fine food and beer, and in-game tutorial images. Longer texts include the Abyss Order's ransom note in the Darknight Hero's Crisis quest, a letter to the Treasure Hoarders in Currents Deep Beneath the Lanterns, and pages from a book titled Teyvat shown in the Liyue trailer.
### LARGE +
Some in-world books carry titles in this script (the covers of the Hilichurl Cultural Customs collection and the Handy Handbook of Hilichurlian read, in it, as Hilifolk Study and Hilifolk Handbook). The transcriptions often differ slightly from the English text the player is shown, and some signs contain misspellings (Chicen for chicken, Dalight for daylight), which suggests the world has ordinary spelling mistakes.
### ULTRA +
In Natlan an ancestor statue at Ameyalco Waters and graffiti in the Stadium of the Sacred Flame show the script too, which supports the idea that it is common even in places far from Mondstadt. Natlan's Ancient Names, by contrast, use the older Ancient Teyvat Script.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Mondstadt, Liyue and Natlan show the common script in open-world signs. Liyue also has a separate local script that resembles Chinese seal script, used on signs and some skill effects; Inazuma, Sumeru, Fontaine and Snezhnaya have their own scripts, each given its own entry. Mondstadt's use of German-flavoured wording (such as Der Himmel for Venti's lyre) concerns names and titles, not a different script.
### LARGE +
Hilichurls, per the wiki's Lore section, can no longer speak or understand the common language, because of the curse that took their speech and faces. The Aranara have their own tongue. A few special scripts (the Abyss mages' chant, the Adepti's bridge script, the Ishine Script of Tsurumi Island) are separate again.

## scene_use
### LITE
Do not mention fonts or mirror-writing. A character who is literate reads the sign. Use the common tongue as the default language of scenes between people from different nations.
### STANDARD +
Do not invent a named in-world language for it. Do not invent a cipher or mirror-writing mystery for ordinary signs. If a scene turns on an ancient or magical inscription, use the Ancient Teyvat Script entry instead. If hilichurls speak, use Hilichurlian and keep them uncomprehending of the common tongue.
### LARGE +
When a character reads something odd, a misspelled sign or a clumsy letter fits canon, but a secret message hidden in the lettering of an ordinary sign does not.

## examples
### STANDARD
Mondstadt: a pub sign reading simply "Pub"; a wooden sign saying "Halt and behold!"; a Good Hunter board. Dragonspine: a signpost reading "Notice". Archon statue: "The Gateway of Celestia". Klee's treasure marker: "My Goodie".
### LARGE +
Natlan: an ancestor statue's text reads about a flowing spring longing to return to its source.

## entry: Ancient Teyvat Script
@keys: Ancient Teyvat Script, Abyss Language, Abyss script, ancient alphabet
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
The old script of the Unified Civilization, which existed about 6,000 years ago. It is found on ruin inscriptions, around locked chests and seals, on Ley Line Monoliths, and in association with gods and alchemy, and it is used by Khaenriahns, the Abyss Order, and in Natlan's Ancient Names. It is also based on Latin letters, but it has punctuation and is mostly written in Latin, with some Ancient Greek, Swahili and English. "Ancient Teyvat Script" and "Abyss Language" are unofficial names.
### LARGE +
A separate set of runic glyphs, of an Enochian look, appears above Abyss mages when they renew their shields. They have not been deciphered, and the letter sequences look random, so treat them as magical writing.

## entry: Manga scripts
@keys: manga script, manga common script, Genshin manga
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
The manga, which predates the game's global launch, uses its own unreadable filler script on documents, and a plain-English stamp reading "The Chivalry of the West Wind - Monde Library". These look like scrapped placeholders, were not carried into the game, and should be treated as noncanon curiosities.
