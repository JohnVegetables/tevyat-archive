## summary
### LITE
The Aranara language is the community's name for the vocabulary the Aranara, the small forest spirits of Sumeru, weave into their speech. They talk to travellers in ordinary Teyvat speech but use their own words for what matters to them: Nara for humans, Vana for forest, Valuka for desert, Marana for the Withering, and Sarva for the place Aranara and the dead are said to come from and return to. Their words are plant- and forest-flavoured, and many resemble Sanskrit.
### STANDARD +
The label is unofficial. Canon never presents a full grammar or lexicon; the word list is assembled from dialogue, the Aranyaka gadget's entries and quests such as the 3.0 world quest chain around the Aranara.

## practice
### LITE
Aranara drop their own words into otherwise plain speech and name outsiders in their own way. They call humans Nara, and typically prefix a human's name with it (for example Nara Varuna).
### STANDARD +
- Naming: if the human name sounds too much like the prefix, they may merge it. "Nara Rana" becomes Narana, and in For All Children Who Long for Life the human Arana remarks that this makes the name the same as hers.
- Core words (wiki glosses): Vana, forest, and by extension the forest half of Sumeru; Valuka, sand or desert, and by extension the desert half; Nara Valuka, the Eremites; Aranara, themselves; Marana, the Withering; Sarva, the Irminsul or the place of origin and return; Silapna, the stone-like Aranara-kin; Bija, seed; Utsava, festival; Varuna, "water and sky".
- Place and thing names: Vanarana for the Vanarana area and Mahavanaranapna for its dream version; Vanagni for the Pyro Whopperflower; Apshvaana for Spinocrocodiles; Vasara for the trees bearing Vasoma Fruit; Ashvattha for the Ashvattha Tree; Aranyaka for the gadget; Hilinara for hilichurls.
### LARGE +
- Ararakalari is the Aranara's word for their abilities. The wiki links it to Aranara and to kalari, an Indian martial art, as an observation.
- Plant-spirits fear fire, which explains the name for the Pyro Whopperflower in the wiki's reading.
- Terms in the Aranyaka gadget are gathered as entries, so the Traveler's collection of Aranara words grows through the quests.

## regional_variation
### LITE
Aranara speech is bound to the Aranara of Sumeru, found in the forests and, in the dream, in places like Vanarana. Other peoples do not use it, though Sumeru's humans who befriend Aranara learn some words.
### STANDARD +
The Aranara words partly echo the vocabulary of human Sumeru (Vedic or Sanskrit-like names) but form a separate layer. Sumeru's ordinary written script is the Vedanagari-like script described in culture.sumeru-script.
### LARGE +
Wall inscriptions and the leaf of a Silapna carry unreadable glyphs that fans take as a possible Aranara script; the game gives no translation.

### ULTRA +
The wiki ties the word Sarva to several lines: an Aranara talks of returning to Sarva when Greater Lord Rukkhadevata passed away; the quests The World of Aranara, For the Children of the Past and For All Children Who Long for Life speak of Aranara originating from, returning from or travelling through Sarva; and in For Fruits, Seeds, and Trees the Marana is said to invade it. After the Archon Quest Akasha Pulses, the Kalpa Flame Rises, Araja says Sarva no longer remembers Marana. The wiki reads Sarva as the Irminsul on this basis; treat it as interpretation. The Sanskrit-like roots (nara for person, apsh+shvaana for water dog) are also fan observation.

## scene_use
### LITE
Let an Aranara speak simply and warmly, then drop in one or two of its own words, explained by context. Do not give long Aranara sentences.
### STANDARD +
- Use Nara for humans, Vana and Valuka for the two halves of Sumeru, and Marana for the Withering, which they treat as a threat.
- Name the Traveler "Nara" plus a name if the scene calls for it, and let names that clash merge, as in Narana.
- Keep Sarva ambiguous: it is where the dead go and where Aranara come from, and canon does not fix its nature.
- Do not invent grammar, a full vocabulary or translations of the wall inscriptions.

## examples
### STANDARD
"Nara" is used for a person, "Marana" is the Withering, "Bija" is a seed, "Utsava" a festival. Nara Varuna and Narana show both name patterns.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 nothing read changes the picture from 3.0: the language is a scattering of words, never a documented language. The Irminsul reading of Sarva is a wiki interpretation, and no 6.x or 7.0-7.1 content read adds to it.
