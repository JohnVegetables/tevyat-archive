## summary
### LITE
The Inazuman script is the writing seen on Inazuma's shop signs, posters, book covers and charms. The game never names it. It is a stylised alphabet that mostly writes romanised Japanese, usually reduced to the first letter of each syllable, so a sign for a festival ("omatsuri") may read only OMTR. It can run top to bottom, right to left, or sideways. Tsurumi Island has a separate, canon-named Ishine Script that only priestly families knew.
### STANDARD +
Inazuman letters correspond to Latin letters, and some Inazuman texts are even in English. The script is the Inazuman counterpart of the scripts of other nations, and it sits alongside the common Teyvat script rather than replacing it.

## practice
### LITE
Signs name a shop by its trade or name in abbreviated form. Examples read by fans: KM for the Komore Teahouse, ST for a bathhouse (sentou), KJ for the Amenoma Smithy, NTK for Netsuke no Gen Crafts. Book covers use the same compressed shorthand, as with SGNMYA on the Sangonomiya Chronicles.
### STANDARD +
Reading is a matter of guessing from context. Per fan analysis, only the first letter of each syllable is usually written, so ka, ki, ku, ke and ko all share one symbol; voicing is often ignored; long vowels and the final n are often dropped; and there are no spaces or capital letters. A few letters (L, Q, V, X) turn up with inconsistent values. Many texts therefore do not match the official Japanese transcription of the same text. In a scene, an Inazuman sign is a quick label, not a place to hide a full paragraph of exact words.
### LARGE +
- Directions: vertical top to bottom and right to left, as on Mysterious Flyers or Idol Posters and in Arataki Itto's burst animation (AK ONI, taken to mean "red oni"), or horizontal, as on the Yellowed Letter.
- Longer prose is rendered as strings of consonant-heavy letters, as on the Official Tenryou Commission Document. These are hard even for fans to decode, which fits the compressed, shorthand feel of the script.
- Calligraphy: Kamisato Ayaka's calligraphy in her character teaser is written vertically in the script.
- Some items use plain alphabetic Latin in the same style: a queue sign near the Kousaka stall reads "Queue here", and during the Irodori Festival event (2.6, limited-time) the title was written in the script as a transcription of its Japanese name.
### ULTRA +
- Seirai Island: a floor mechanism in the Relics of Seirai uses the script, so it is not only for commerce and books.
- The Ancient Forging Blueprint shows the script used for a technical diagram: its parts of a blade (tip, flat face, edge, ridge) and its listed materials are labelled in it, which shows the shorthand also serves craft terms.
- Some covers are in other languages: the cover of A Legend of Sword is possibly German, and its spine shows two pen names of Xingqiu, evidence that Inazuman lettering can carry text from elsewhere.

## regional_variation
### LITE
The script is Inazuma's; nothing in canon says it is used outside it. In other nations, other scripts (the common Teyvat script, the Liyue, Sumeru, Fontaine and Snezhnayan styles) appear.
### STANDARD +
Tsurumi Island is the exception inside Inazuma: its old civilisation had the Ishine Script, written glyphs known only to priestly families, used for names of people and places. It is a different writing system from the Inazuman script and is not the same thing under another name.
### LARGE +
Canon in the 2.2 Octave of the Maushiro quest shows that the glyphs survived on carved stones and cliffs: stones must be struck in the order of the script on a cliff wall, and the stone-tiles must be matched with the same glyphs. In a hidden exploration objective called Ishine Script Deciphering (unofficial name; 2.2), seven Stone Slates are collected from Mysterious Carvings around Tsurumi Island and placed in carvings in the ruins under Chirai Shrine; a "Seven Letters" achievement is attached.
### ULTRA +
The wiki infers that the Ishine glyphs are for a language related to Ainu; the letters themselves do not resemble Ainu writing. Tsurumi's names (such as Kama) are claimed to reflect this. Treat that as an observation by fans, not canon.

## tanuki_speech
### STANDARD
The tanuki (bake-danuki) of Inazuma also have a spoken language of their own, though no script is attested. According to Ioroi, tanuki can understand human speech by nature but speak their own tongue among themselves. In the 2.0 world quest Tanuki-Bayashi in the Forest, a magical food prepared by Ioroi lets the Traveler follow some of it, though much still sounds like nonsense (the tanuki Kichiboushi is rendered as "squelch squelch lavender melon"). Ioroi explains the nonsense as the Traveler not yet being used to Kichiboushi's aura, and the wiki notes that talking to him grows clearer over the quest.
### LARGE +
This is the only description of the tanuki tongue, and it comes from one quest. Treat it as flavour, not a system: do not invent tanuki words, only describe it as garbled and aura-dependent.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use the script as background colour: a shop sign in a few angular letters, a poster or a book spine whose title is only a handful of symbols. A character who can read it simply does; an outsider from other nations may only guess at it.
### STANDARD +
- A Traveler or outlander reading Inazuman signs should have to guess, or ask, rather than read fluently; a local reads the shorthand without effort.
- Avoid inventing a full grammar, a long translated text, or "ancient secrets" in the ordinary Inazuman script. Keep any decoded sign short and plausible: a trade word or name.
- Ishine glyphs belong to Tsurumi Island and to its past; canon shows the knowledge was restricted to priestly families.
### LARGE +
- A mysterious cipher scene is better served by the Ishine glyph puzzles (stones struck in order, tiles matched) than by inventing rules for Inazuman shorthand.
- Keep the tanuki speech vague: gibberish that gradually clears as the listener adapts.

## examples
### STANDARD
Read as fans do: OMTR is "omatsuri" (festival), KJ is "kaji" (smithing), OMR is "omamori" (charm), SK for "shoukai" (trade association) on the International Trade Association sign. Each is a guess from context, not an official translation.
### LARGE +
- SGY for the Bantan Sango Detective Agency is read as "Sango, ya" (shop).
- KOUKA SUGATQ NO IDORVRX SAI is read as the Irodori Festival title during the 2.6 limited-time event, showing that Q and X can stand in for other letters.
- The line IROHANIHOETO CHIRINURUO from the event is read as the opening of the Iroha poem, an ancient Japanese poem used as a pangram (limited-time event content).

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the script appears unchanged and unexplained in the sources read: canon has never named it, taught its rules or translated it. The Ishine Script remains a Tsurumi-only matter from 2.2, and no source read ties either script to any content in 6.x or 7.0-7.1.

## entry: Inazuman Script
@keys: Inazuman Script, Inazuma script, Inazuman alphabet, Inazuman writing
### STANDARD
The Inazuman script is the community's name for the writing on signs, posters, books and charms throughout Inazuma. It looks like a stylised alphabet, mostly spelling out romanised Japanese by its first letters (KM for Komore, OMTR for "omatsuri"), written vertically or horizontally. Readings are educated guesses; canon never explains the script.
### LARGE +
Besides signs, it appears on a floor mechanism in the Relics of Seirai, the Official Tenryou Commission Document, the Yellowed Letter, a festival promotion poster and Arataki Itto's burst animation. In the 2.6 Irodori Festival event it carried the event title.

## entry: Ishine Script
@keys: Ishine Script, Ishine glyphs, Seven Letters, Ishine puzzle
@version: 2.2
### STANDARD
The Ishine Script is the written language of the old civilisation of Tsurumi Island. In the 2.2 quest Octave of the Maushiro, illusions of its people say that its knowledge was passed only through priestly families, and that it was used to name people, places and things on the island. Puzzles in the quest and a hidden objective require matching or ordering its glyphs: striking stones in the order of the script on a cliff wall, matching tiles, and placing seven Stone Slates into carvings in the ruins under Chirai Shrine. The meaning of the glyphs is never translated in-game.

## entry: Tanuki language
@keys: tanuki language, tanuki speech, Kichiboushi
@keys2: Ioroi, Chinju Forest, bake-danuki
@version: 2.0
### STANDARD
Per Ioroi in the Chinju Forest, tanuki understand human speech naturally but have their own tongue. His magical food lets the Traveler follow part of it; the rest comes out as gibberish ("squelch squelch lavender melon" from Kichiboushi), which Ioroi blames on the Traveler not yet being used to that tanuki's aura. Speech with Kichiboushi becomes clearer as the quest goes on.
