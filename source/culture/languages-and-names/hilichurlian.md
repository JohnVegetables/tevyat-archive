## summary
### LITE
Hilichurlian is the language of the hilichurls, the masked tribal creatures found across Teyvat. It is made of short, often repeated words, strongly tied to feelings, food, fighting and song. Almost nobody studies it: the scholar Ella Musk of Mondstadt and her ancestor Jacob Musk are the main authorities. Ordinary people assume hilichurls cannot communicate at all, and hilichurls attack most outsiders on sight, but a visitor who uses the right words can be answered with a dance instead of a club.
### STANDARD +
Mondstadt's scholars treat it as a real but under-researched field called Hilichurlian Linguistics. Its recorded material is mostly Mondstadt tribes: Jacob Musk's two book collections, a short handbook of phrases credited to Ella's grandfather and Ella's own field attempts. Hilichurlian is spoken, sung and shouted; whether hilichurls write is unknown. Venti and Kaeya are shown to speak some, and Abyss mages are said to speak it fluently.
### LARGE +
A core idea of the language is that meaning leans on tone and context. The handbook warns that a bare word such as gusha may mean a plant, food, or plain displeasure, depending on how the hilichurl says it. The same short words appear again and again across songs, greetings and threats, so treat any claimed translation as a best guess.
### ULTRA +
Beneath the comedy there is a darker backdrop. Canon says the hilichurls the Traveler meets in the Chasm are former citizens of fallen Khaenri'ah, and that the curse took their speech and faces. The hilichurls of the surface are never confirmed to share that origin, and no source ties Hilichurlian to any older language.

## practice
### LITE
Hilichurls speak in clipped words and repeat them for emphasis. Common examples the handbook records: da or dada (good, very), muhe (like or want), ika ya (a bad person), mita (meat, liked things), gusha (plants, disliked things), celi (hot, fire, sometimes the sun), lata (cold) and unu (one, and the word tied to their sense of the divine). Mosi mita and mosi gusha work as figures of speech for happiness and sadness.
### STANDARD +
Numbers and colours are built from simple pieces: du is two, unu du three, dudu four and mani five (also hands or physical labour); a colour is a thing plus boya, such as celi boya for red or lata boya for blue (these glosses come from Ella's handbook, which also lists times of day such as aba mosi dada for morning). The handbook adds that hilichurls do not use absolute directions and talk about places relative to either you or themselves depending on whether they like you.
Songs are the best-preserved speech. Jacob Musk compiled seven in Hilichurl Ballad Selection: a brawling battle song, a festival hymn sung around totems, a melancholy grief song, a samachurl philosophical hymn, a drunken ode to the wind sung by Anemo-worshipping tribes, a sacrificial ode and a bonfire lullaby that ends with the chief calling nunu (sleep) three times. Musk's interpretations are guesses and often wry.
### LARGE +
Language and ritual intertwine. Jacob Musk reports that hilichurls worship elemental power in the abstract rather than any Archon, that shamans lead hymns and dances, and that raw leftover meat serves as an offering. Unu, the one-word name for oneness and the divine, appears in several songs. A tribe in Dadaupa Gorge, the Eclipse tribe, worships a crude symbol of an eclipsed sun; the Traveler's own voice-over suggests a possible link to the Eclipse Dynasty of Khaenri'ah, which is speculation, not stated fact.
Musk also notes the camp scrawls are copies of ruin inscriptions, and that hilichurls like to camp near ruins, though he found no explanation.
### ULTRA +
The title Lawa is the tribal word for king or chief, and it is the root of the name Lawachurl; Jacob Musk says these huge loners do not actually lead anyone and avoid the reverence the smaller hilichurls give them. The terms Sama (as in samachurl) and Hili (as in hilichurl) appear in names but their meanings are unconfirmed.
The Abyss's use of the language shows in Natlan, where the Abyss commands hilichurls in battle. Abyss mages themselves chant in a separate magical script and not in Hilichurlian.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Nearly all known material is from Mondstadt, because both scholars lived there; Ella's collection and Musk's field notes cover tribes near Mondstadt, especially around Dadaupa Gorge. Jacob Musk describes three named tribes there with distinct cultures: the Meaty tribe (feasting, a boar pen, a combat arena), the Sleeper tribe (sleeping huts, thought the cleverest) and the Eclipse tribe (occult worship). Whether tribes in other nations speak the same tongue with local differences is not documented.
### LARGE +
Ella Musk admits that without more fieldwork she cannot be sure of several words. Jacob Musk toured tribes across the continent for his ballad book, but his published tribal detail concerns Mondstadt.

## scene_use
### LITE
Hilichurls rarely hold conversation. A short phrase, a gesture and a mood do most of the work. A visitor who speaks even a few friendly words (offering food, calling dada, saying odomu for friend) may calm a camp; one wrong word, especially an insult like ika, ends in a fight.
### STANDARD +
Use Hilichurlian in scenes as flavour, not as a cipher. Keep lines short and repeat words. Let the translation come from a character who plausibly knows it (Ella, Venti, Kaeya) and let them be unsure. Ella is enthusiastic and slightly boastful about her field; a hilichurl rebuffing her, or her mixing up a phrase and getting both of them beaten up (as happened with Mika), is in keeping with canon. Do not have ordinary characters understand hilichurls; the Adventurer Handbook-style attitude is that they cannot be talked to.
### LARGE +
When the Traveler or another character negotiates with a hilichurl, canon treats it as possible but chancy: in Venti's quest, offering a gift and singing in a friendly way persuades a hilichurl to hand over a shield, and a bad answer starts a fight. Match that: correct and friendly words bring a dance or a trade, a wrong word brings hostility at once.
Do not give hilichurls complex abstract speech unless a source supports it. Samachurls chant hymns and their songs may hold meaning, but the exact content is unknown.

## examples
### STANDARD
The handbook's own samples: Da or dada means good and very; Ika ya means a bad person (answer with a blow if you are sure of yourself, or run); In movo lata mita refers to meat in the water; Du ya zido dala asks where this thing went. Ella opens her greeting in the field with a hostile-sounding but friendly phrase meaning something like "hello, have you eaten", which she says in Hilichurlian while the Traveler flinches.
### LARGE +
Excerpts from Jacob Musk's collection show the style: the first song's lines run Mi muhe ye, Mi biat ye, and the third song, a grief song, repeats Mi muhe mita nye. His translations of these are tentative and disputed by fan readers.

## entry: Ella Musk and Hilichurlian scholarship
@keys: Ella Musk, Hilichurlian Linguistics, Handy Handbook of Hilichurlian, Hilichurlian scholar
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
Ella Musk works in the Mondstadt library as a self-styled professor of Hilichurlian Linguistics, specialising in applied grammar of the vernacular. She calls her field underdeveloped and says fieldwork is the main gap, since few dare approach hilichurls. Dr. Edith calls her her older sister, and she descends from Jacob Musk. She remarks that Sucrose also studies hilichurls, but studies bones, not the living. Before he joined the Knights as cartographer, Mika went with her on a language exchange trip that ended with both beaten up after she made a mistake.
### LARGE +
Canon is inconsistent about the family tree: the handbook calls her the granddaughter of its author and Jacob Musk is called an ancestor. Treat the exact relation as unsettled. A dialogue branch gives the Traveler volume two of the Hilichurl Ballad Selection.

## entry: Hilichurl Ballad Selection
@keys: Hilichurl Ballad Selection, Poet Laureate of Hilichurlian, hilichurl songs, hilichurl ballads
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
A two-volume collection compiled by the ecologist Jacob Musk, who toured hilichurl tribes to write down their songs; it earned him the title Poet Laureate of Hilichurlian, an honour neither he nor the hilichurls liked. Seven songs are given with his notes, including a battle song, a festival hymn, a grief song, a philosophical samachurl hymn, an ode to the Anemo Archon sung by drunk wind-worshippers, a sacrificial ode and a bonfire lullaby.
### LARGE +
Musk admits he is guessing at meanings; he compares the grief song to the best poetry of his birthplace and says it moved him despite the smell of the elderly hilichurls who sang it. He remained fascinated by whether hilichurls hold philosophical discourse.

## entry: Hilichurl Cultural Customs
@keys: Hilichurl Cultural Customs, Meaty tribe, Sleeper tribe, Eclipse tribe, hilichurl shaman customs
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Four volumes by Jacob Musk on hilichurl society: tribes work like extended families led by the oldest samachurl, or sometimes a stronger, more aggressive big hilichurl; they worship elemental power in the abstract; the Lawa are huge, lonely and respected; and the Dadaupa Gorge tribes show cultural diversity. He notes hilichurls seem to have no sense of past or future and do not honour the dead, yet cling to ruins.
### LARGE +
The Eclipse tribe's throne for its strongest shaman and its sun-eclipse symbol are the most occult of the three. The wiki records that the Traveler's voice-over links the tribe to the Khaenriahn Eclipse Dynasty; no quest confirms it.

## entry: Hilichurls as cursed Khaenriahns
@keys: hilichurls were Khaenriahns, hilichurl curse, Chasm hilichurls, curse of wilderness
@keys2: Dainsleif, Khaenri'ah, Chasm
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Dainsleif confirms that the hilichurls met in the Chasm are former citizens of Khaenri'ah, and canon links the curse to the loss of speech, memory and faces. Hilichurls wear masks because they cannot bear to see their own reflections. Other lost civilisations may also have produced hilichurls (the Gurabad story speaks of people scattered into wild creatures who lost language and faces). Treat the origin of ordinary surface hilichurls as unconfirmed.
### LARGE +
A few cases show the curse can be partly reversed by the Abyss: the Abyss-touched statue let Caribert speak again, and in Fontaine, the Narzissenkreuz gave a hilichurl rogue sentience and speech (Caterpillar). These are special cases, not a general cure.

### STANDARD
Both Venti and Kaeya can speak some Hilichurlian, but canon never says how they learned it.
