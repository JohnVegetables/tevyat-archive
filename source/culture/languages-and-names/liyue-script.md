## summary
### LITE
The Liyue Script is the angular, character-based writing seen on shop signs, banners, notice boards and scrolls in Liyue, especially around Liyue Harbor. "Liyue Script" is an unofficial name. Its look is modelled on real Chinese seal script. It is background texture for a scene, not a code that characters are shown deciphering.
### STANDARD +
The same lettering shows up in a few other places: the Jade Chamber intel wall in an animated cutscene, a poetry gala banner, some character skill effects, and the seal in the domain Beneath the Dragon-Queller.

## practice
### LITE
Merchants and notice boards in Liyue carry it. Visually it is blocky and square, built from strokes rather than an alphabet. Canon never has a character explain how it is read.
### STANDARD +
In Liyue Harbor, shop signs repeat the same small set of glyphs: stalls, a bank, a teahouse, an antique shop, a jeweller, the dockyard and a pharmacy share the same eight symbols, with only a few exceptions (the last symbol on one restaurant banner and the bookstore signs). In scene terms, the writing reads as a familiar visual style rather than as distinct, readable shop names.
### LARGE +
Skill effects use the same glyphs. Characters of the script ring around Chongyun while his blade infusion is active and appear when Zhongli's stone stele breaks. Yanfei's charged attack leaves a stamp-like mark of four characters, which the community reads as blessing, prosperity, business and seal. This fits her work as a legal adviser, but the readings are fan interpretation, not canon text.
### ULTRA +
The Serenitea Pot's Adeptal Mirror and the Neighboring Nations Congenial Poetry Gala banner (Waterborne Poetry event) also carry it. Because the glyphs repeat so much, treat any invented translation of a sign as flavour you made up for the scene, not as a canon reading.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Inside Liyue the script is a civic and commercial presence, strongest in Liyue Harbor. No canon source describes how it differs between Liyue towns, and other nations have their own scripts.
### LARGE +
A stylised or ancient-looking form appears on the ground around the seal after Azhdaha is defeated in Beneath the Dragon-Queller. Four glyphs at the seal's centre mark which element Azhdaha will be infused with, and are based on seal-script forms for Electro, Hydro, Cryo and Pyro; the Electro glyph is simplified by swapping part of it for the game's Electro symbol. Whether this ancient form is an official separate script is not stated in canon.

## deciphered_characters
@spoiler: none
### ULTRA
Community decipherment finds that some glyphs closely match real seal script, but they often do not form clean sentences. Poems and signs read as partial strings of familiar characters with gaps, for instance fragments suggesting blessing, a horse, clouds, a cotton seller or a place name. These are fan readings and many glyphs stay unread. Treat the script as an aesthetic echo of Chinese writing, not a solved language. A mask on the Unusual Hilichurl bears a Chinese character that is not in the same style.

## idioms
@keys: Liyue idiom, Liyue proverb, Liyue saying
### STANDARD
The spoken idioms of Liyue are a separate, richer layer than the script. Characters in Liyue quests, voice-overs and stories quote proverbs about caution, tact and reversal, adapted in the English localisation. Examples include the warning that the walls have ears, the advice not to always call it as you see it (be tactful), the reminder that even the most meticulous can slip, the line that you cannot walk along the river and keep your shoes dry, and the saying that food is life. Ningguang uses the meticulous-slip saying about Ganyu, Yun Jin uses the one about meeting someone in person, and Beidou's character story uses a saying that an object takes after its master.
### LARGE +
Other sayings are marked with a clear meaning in the source: the hunter becomes the hunted, a thing tossed away never returns, even upright officials struggle with family quarrels, and rare goods can be hoarded for resale. They tend to reward restraint, practicality and awareness of consequences, which suits a culture of contracts and trade. Use them only where a character would plausibly say them.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use the script as visual flavour: painted square characters over a shop door, banners, scroll labels, notice boards. Do not have a character fluently translate it or treat it as a secret.
### STANDARD +
A visitor from another nation sees square glyphs and cannot read them; a local points out the same shop by its usual name. If a magical effect shows the script, treat it as decorative, as in Chongyun's frost circle.
### LARGE +
Keep the dragon-seal use rare and mysterious. Do not invent a canon meaning for the glyphs, and do not claim the script is magic or an officially separate language.

## examples
### STANDARD
Liyue Harbor shop signs; banners and scrolls inside shops; skill effects such as Chongyun's frost infusion, Zhongli's stone stele and Yanfei's seal stamp; the Jade Chamber intel wall in the cutscene of the Zhongli's Treat quest; and the seal glyphs at Beneath the Dragon-Queller.

## entry: Liyue Script
@keys: Liyue Script, Liyue writing, Liyue seal script
### STANDARD
The Liyue Script is an unofficial name for the logographic writing found on signs, banners and notice boards in Liyue. It looks like real Chinese seal script. Its glyphs repeat heavily on signage and appear in skill effects and the Azhdaha seal.

## entry: Liyue idioms
@keys: Liyue idioms, Liyue proverbs, Liyue sayings
### STANDARD
Liyue speech is known for idioms and proverbs, often adjusted in English to preserve the sense. They concern caution, tact, consequences and trade. Use them sparingly as natural-sounding speech.
