## summary
### LITE
The Ancient Teyvat Script is the community name for the Latin-based lettering that appears on seals, Ley Line monoliths, domain walls, magic circles, ruins and the gear of the Abyss Order. The game never names it. It is separate from the common Teyvat Script that ordinary people write in, and context ties it to the Unified Civilization of 6,000 years ago and to Khaenri'ah.
### STANDARD +
Its glyphs follow the Latin alphabet but are divided into a top and a bottom half by a thin gap. The deciphered texts are mostly Latin, with some Ancient Greek, Old Norse and English. Nothing in canon states who invented it or how it relates to any spoken tongue.
### LARGE +
Because the script is identified from images and context rather than from an in-game explanation, it works best as atmosphere and mystery. Characters can recognise it as "that old lettering from ruins and the Abyss" without being able to read it.

## practice
### LITE
The script has no capital letters and, in most texts, no spaces. It mostly appears as short ritual phrases, labels or mottos rather than flowing prose, and is associated with magic seals, gods, alchemy, astrology and Ley Lines.
### STANDARD
Characteristics reported on the wiki:
- It is a letter-based alphabet like the common script, with glyphs split into upper and lower halves.
- Some texts use only one half of each glyph, or invert the top half, so the reading can depend on the form used.
- It is used by the Khaenri'ahns, including the Abyss Order, and is inscribed on seals around chests and monuments.
- It is not used for everyday correspondence; the common script appears on letters and signs.
### LARGE +
Half-glyph examples: only the bottom half in the event menu of Unreconciled Stars; only the top half (one line inverted) in the quest A Herald Without Adherents; and bottom-half reading in the secret messages of The Chasm, whose upper halves are altered. These are puzzles of presentation as much as of language.
### ULTRA +
The Chasm's hidden messages are a modest cipher layered over the script. The wiki describes the alphabet split into groups of five letters, with each group's top half swapped to mirror its far end, while the bottom half stays unchanged. The practical rule is that only the bottom half should be read.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
The script is not tied to one nation. It shows up wherever seals, ruins or Abyss activity occur. Places named on the wiki include Mondstadt (the sealed doorway, Dragonspine murals), Liyue (stone tablets, jade plates, The Chasm), Inazuma (Tsurumi murals), Sumeru (an Akademiya fountain) and Fontaine (Beastly Rifts in Elynas).
### LARGE +
In the Teyvat Chapter storyline preview, each nation's story theme is given a short motto in the script (for example one for Mondstadt about freedom and song, and one for Liyue about contracts). These mottos are promotional framing, not in-world statements by a nation.
### ULTRA +
The wiki also mentions the script in connection with Natlan's Ancient Names. This is a wiki connection only; consult the Ancient Name entry before treating it as established.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use it as texture and mystery: a seal glowing with red letters, a circle in a ruin, a puzzle the Traveler cannot read. Characters can note that the lettering looks old or Abyssal without understanding it. Do not give them a full translation unless a scene sets one up.
### LARGE +
Safe things to say: the script is old, it appears with seals and the Abyss, and it is not the common writing. Do not have a character assert that it is "the language of Khaenri'ah" as settled fact, because canon does not state this.
### ULTRA +
Scholars such as those of the Sumeru Akademiya or Fontaine's institutions could plausibly be curious about the script, but no canon scene shows them deciphering it. Keep such moments speculative.

## examples
### STANDARD
Short readings reported on the wiki, with the wiki's translations:
- Around locked chests and monuments: Ad virtutem, "to virtue".
- On Ley Line monoliths: Parmae, "shields".
- On magic circles with a heptagram: a line that translates roughly as "from the peak of light in the greatness of the elements".
- In domains: Pronoea and Fortuna, "providence" and "fortune".
### LARGE +
- Stone tablets near Jueyun Karst: a line meaning there has never been great fortune without a mix of intelligence and bravery.
- Dragonspine carvings: short commands such as "Go!" and a line about aiding faithful angels.
- The Chasm messages: names and titles linked to the Abyss Lectors Agnarr and Egill, plus "I call the living" and "I mourn for the dead".
### ULTRA +
- Tsurumi Island murals: lines adapted from a Roman poem to the Moon goddess, with the Moons substituted for the original.
- Nicole's attacks and idle animation: Latin and Greek phrases that the wiki traces to real classical sources.
- A portal conjured by Pierro during Black Feathers Scattered at Parting: a blend of Proto-Norse and Old Norse, only tentatively translated.

## entry: Abyss Language

@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
"Abyss Language" is an unofficial nickname because the lettering appears on Abyss Mages, Lectors and Order gear. The same script is also on seals and Ley Line monoliths, so it is not exclusive to the Abyss Order.

## entry: Secret Messages in The Chasm
@keys: Secret Messages in The Chasm, Chasm hidden messages
@version: 1.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Scrawled inscriptions in The Chasm: Underground Mines, in the script, mention a commander named Agnarr and a pious Egill, a road to the abysses, and phrases about calling the living and mourning the dead. Only the bottom half of each glyph should be read. The wiki connects them to the two Abyss Lectors of Perils in the Dark.
### LARGE +
One message plays on a famous comic Latin "go home" joke, reading as a call for Celestials to leave. This is a wiki interpretation.

## entry: Dragonspine Carvings
@keys: Ancient Carvings, Dragonspine carvings, Dragonspine's Glacial Secret
@version: 1.2
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Ice caves and ruins on Dragonspine carry short script texts: a command to go, a word meaning revere or fear, and a line about aiding faithful angels. Albedo's quest treats some patterns on Dragonspine as unfamiliar.
