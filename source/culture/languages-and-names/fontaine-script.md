## summary
### LITE
The Fontaine Script is the lettering used on signs, posters and shop fronts across Fontaine. It looks like a flowing, cursive version of the common script seen elsewhere in Teyvat, but it mostly spells ordinary English and French words, such as shop names and the label Banque du Nord. Characters just read it as their normal writing.
### STANDARD +
The name is the wiki's label, not an in-world term. An in-game research log from the Tower of Ipsissimus calls it the Fontainian script and says it is how most old Fontaine documents are written, from the country's early nation-building stage onward.
### LARGE +
The script is also known to have been used for German text, though that is rare. Its origin is unknown.
### ULTRA +
The log's author notes that the older Remurian texts left earlier scholars stuck for a long time, because they were treated as simple musical notes. That contrast is the main in-world reason to mention the Fontainian script at all: it is the readable layer of Fontaine's written record, sitting on top of a much older one that was not.

## practice
### LITE
Seen on storefronts, café signs and street posters. Typical examples: Bertin's House of Curiosities shows the owner's name, a boutique sign reads couture, and a café sign reads Cafe Lutece.
### STANDARD +
Other deciphered open-world signs include the shop name Leschots, Lepaute's, Debord's, Salon Caritat (caritat reads as charity), the newspaper-office sign L'oiseau de vapeur (the Steambird), and a grocery sign. After the quest When They Talk About Tonight, the Chioriya Boutique sign was redone to show the name Chiori. These readings come from fan decipherment and carry some guesswork.
### LARGE +
The Tower of Ipsissimus log explains why the script matters to scholars: texts from older Golden Troupe ruins and from collections were almost all in Fontainian script, so researchers could read them. The much older Remurian writing found in the tower was a separate problem; the log describes it as a phonetic notation the team could first only partly read, and says progress began with a copy kept at the Institute of Natural Philosophy.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
No regional variants are documented. Other nations have their own lettering and the common script is found in Mondstadt, Liyue and Natlan; the Fontaine Script is tied to Fontaine.
### LARGE +
It is described as a late script, dating from Fontaine's early nation-building era, about a century after Remuria fell. The Remurian writing that came before it is treated as a different, ancient script.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use it as set dressing: a sign or poster in neat Fontaine lettering, shop names and short French-flavoured labels.
### STANDARD +
Do not invent a grammar, alphabet or sound values for it. If a character reads an unusual inscription, keep to what canon supports: it is Fontaine's ordinary writing, and the truly old Remurian texts are what need scholars to decode.
