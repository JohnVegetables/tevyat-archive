## summary
### LITE
Mondstadt worships Barbatos, the Anemo Archon and God of Freedom. Devotion runs through the Church of Favonius and its Cathedral in Mondstadt City, the Statues of the Seven, a few ancient ruined temples and festivals such as Windblume, Ludi Harpastum and Weinlesefest. The faith is relaxed: Barbatos asked for freedom rather than obedience, so people honour him with wine, song and flowers more than strict doctrine. Most worshippers do not know that their god walks the streets as the bard Venti.
### STANDARD +
The Church of Favonius staffs the Cathedral, the highest landmark in the city, where its sisters pray. According to Jean, the Knights also manage the Church. The Church's most precious holding is the Holy Lyre der Himmel, an instrument said to have belonged to Barbatos himself. Older strata of belief lie beneath this: a faded cult of the Four Winds, and a temple where the Ruler of Time was once honoured beside the Anemo god.
### LARGE +
The overall tone is affectionate rather than awed. Mondstadters thank Barbatos for the dandelions, the mild climate and the wine, and the Church is seen more as a civic institution than a force in daily life. The Traveler meets the faith early: Paimon explains the Statues of the Seven and Anemoculi in Mondstadt.
### ULTRA +
Three layers of religion can be told apart in the sources. The oldest is the temple at Thousand Winds, built for Istaroth and later shared with Barbatos. The middle layer is the cult of the Four Winds, the guardians Barbatos appointed, which flourished after Vennessa's rebellion and then faded. The present layer is the Church of Favonius centred on Barbatos. They overlap in the word windblume, in the thousand winds and in the Sacrificial weapon series, whose ceremonial use is tied to the old temple.

## practice
@availability: limited
### LITE
- Prayer and blessing. Sisters pray at the Cathedral; Seamus Pegg greets people with May the Anemo Archon protect you on your path. Rosaria, the irreverent nun, skips prayers.
- Offerings. People with Visions offer Anemoculi at Statues of the Seven; festivals offer flowers (Windblume), wine (Ludi Harpastum, Weinlesefest).
### STANDARD +
- The Cathedral. The Favonius Cathedral is run by the Church of Favonius and is used to worship the Anemo Archon. A travel note calls it the city's highest landmark. Its basement holds the Holy Lyre der Himmel, guarded.
- Anemo Archon Statue. In the plaza in front of the Cathedral stands a statue of Barbatos as a hooded figure with two wings and cupped hands. Its pedestal bears a line in the Teyvat script rendered as The Gateway of Celestia.
- Clergy. Known figures include Seneschal Seamus Pegg (the Cardinal of Daybreak), Cardinal Calvin (who covers for him), Deacon Dahlia (Herald of Barbatos), Deaconess Barbara (the Shining Idol), Mother Maria and a number of nuns including Rosaria. The Church's symbol on headwear is a fleur-de-lis variant with wide, downward-curving wings, resembling the Anemo emblem. Sisters wear black habits, or white ones in a few cases; whether colour reflects rank is unknown.
- Everyday ministry. The Church hears confessions and says prayers but has little power over social problems such as heavy drinking; deaconesses even bless new wardrobes after children complained to Grace about drunken fathers stumbling out of them.
- Statue offerings. Anemoculi are consolidated winds that only allogenes such as the Traveler can see. Fairy tales say the statues originally had gem eyes that swallows took, and Anemoculi are offered in exchange for the Seven's blessings. The quest The Blessings of The Seven introduces the custom.
### LARGE +
- Ludi Harpastum. A fifteen-day festival of food, games and music that commemorates how Barbatos taught his people to brew wine and live freely. The champion chooses a maiden to throw the Harpastum, a ball wrapped in red cloth, at the end; whoever picks it up is said to enjoy good fortune for a year. The Holy Lyre is used in this festival, and any other use requires signed documents from the Grand Master, the Seneschal and a community representative.
- Windblume. A festival celebrating the thousand winds. People offer windblumes, a flower said to have been a symbol of freedom and resistance in Decarabian's era, to Barbatos and to those they love; love poems often go with them. A Windblume Star, chosen from those who excelled during the festival or sacrificed for Mondstadt, formally presents a flower to Barbatos at the Windblume Ceremony, and individuals may leave flowers at his large statue throughout. The identity of the real windblume has been lost and is a matter of popular debate.
- Weinlesefest. A festival celebrating the return of the Anemo Archon on the west wind each autumn. People brew fresh wine and keep it sealed until Windcoming Day, when it is uncasked as a way of inviting the god to share a drink. If he is satisfied, a gentle breeze blesses the people. The 3.1 event Of Ballads and Brews was set during it.
- Rosaria's attitude. Rosaria avoids prayers and morning mass. She says a god might have the power to protect everyone but would never choose to, and that prayer is for yourself, not for gods or others. Barbara is sure she believes in the Anemo Archon anyway.
### ULTRA +
- Faith as a job. Rosaria compares herself to hunters who will not eat pork or fishermen who will not eat fish: doing a job does not mean loving it. The picture is of a Church with room for a nun who is not devout.
- Barbara's account. Barbara says the Archon gave her the purpose of bringing people's desires together and fulfilling them. She relies on Dahlia to share Barbatos's word and prays for the safe return of Varka's expedition.
- Jean. Jean's battle lines call on the wind and ask Barbatos to guide the Knights. She admits she knows how Barbara is doing because the order also manages the Church.
- The outsider. The Traveler has no Vision, and Paimon observes that the rules of this world do not bind them; they still make offerings to the statues as any adventurer does.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Worship is concentrated in Mondstadt City, where the Cathedral gives it a formal home. Elsewhere it is mostly folk custom. The wiki's Venti page says Vennessa appointed the ancestors of Vind, a member of the Church today, to watch the eastern coast and warn Mondstadt of storms, so the Church has long had outposts. The old temples are ruins: the Temple of the Falcon at Windrise and the Temple of the Wolf near Thousand Winds are now explorable sites.
### LARGE +
The Falcon domain text calls its ruin a temple devoted to the benevolent Falcon of the West, watching over Windrise for thousands of years. The Wolf domain text says the priests are gone and monsters hold the temple, though the spirit of the Wolf is said to watch over Mondstadt's fields from elsewhere.
### ULTRA +
A Fatui diplomat is listed among the NPCs at the Cathedral, which shows that foreign visitors come and go without changing its customs. No other organised religion is attested in Mondstadt in the sources checked.

## history
@version: 1.0
### LARGE
The oldest faith is that of Arcadia. The Thousand Winds Temple was built by its people for Istaroth, the Ruler of Time. During the Archon War the Gunnhildr Clan, sheltering among the ruins from the blizzards of Decarabian and Boreas, built a small shrine to Istaroth and prayed; their prayers were answered by a small wind spirit, the future Barbatos, who also met Istaroth there and received fragments of her authority. After he became the Anemo Archon the temple was rebuilt for both of them, and the Sacrificial weapons were its ceremonial implements. Istaroth was gradually forgotten for unknown reasons and only Barbatos was worshipped. In the aristocratic period the site became an arena where slaves fought. After Vennessa's rebellion about a thousand years ago the people worshipped the Four Winds and built temples to them; after the cataclysm that worship declined and the temples were abandoned.
### ULTRA +
Barbatos left his people after founding the modern city. The legend that survives in taverns says he returned as a bard to help Vennessa defeat a drake; a travel note adds that the Holy Lyre is the instrument he used to liberate Mondstadt. Venti awoke shortly before the Prologue, took up his guise as a bard again and, per the wiki, appointed Dahlia of the Church as his herald. In the Prologue the story of the worn Holy Lyre ends with Venti handing it back to Barbara with an illusion that hides its condition.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the Church of Favonius still centres on Barbatos, and no change to Mondstadt's faith has been found in the material checked. Seneschal Seamus Pegg is back in Mondstadt after the Nod-Krai expedition (see his entry), and Dahlia is a playable character (5.7) while remaining a deacon and Barbatos's herald.

## scene_use
### LITE
Keep the faith as gentle background, warm and informal. Do not have Mondstadters preach doctrine or recognise Venti as a god.
### STANDARD +
A Cathedral scene should feel quiet and slightly formal: stone steps, the plaza statue with its cupped hands, sisters at prayer, and perhaps a visitor asking to see the Holy Lyre and meeting polite paperwork. Characters can talk about the god casually, as a generous presence rather than a stern one.
### LARGE +
Clergy meeting a stranger show friendly curiosity without pressure. A character who knows Venti's secret (Varka, Seamus Pegg, Dahlia) should be careful not to expose it. Rosaria is the standard voice of doubt; Barbara the standard voice of sincere, anxious devotion.
### ULTRA +
For theology, canon stays vague. Barbatos is a god of freedom who does not command. Do not claim prayers are answered, that Barbatos watches a particular person, or add Celestia-linked doctrine unless a quest the character has experienced establishes it.

## entry: Barbatos worship
@keys: Barbatos, Anemo Archon, God of Freedom
@keys2: Mondstadt, Mondstadter, Church of Favonius
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Barbatos is the Anemo Archon, whom Mondstadt calls the God of Freedom. He does not rule; the Knights of Favonius, not a priest-king, run the city, and the Church of Favonius is his devotional institution. Seamus Pegg's blessing and Jean's battle lines are the most common direct appeals. The public does not connect him with the bard Venti; his origin and his identity as Venti are major spoilers covered in Venti's own entries.
### LARGE +
The official map description credits Barbatos with the dandelion scent that drifts across Cider Lake to the city. Weinlesefest tradition says he returns each autumn on the west wind.

## entry: Holy Lyre der Himmel
@keys: Holy Lyre der Himmel, Holy Lyre, Der Himmel
@keys2: Venti, Cathedral, Church of Favonius
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Church keeps this lyre in the Cathedral basement under guard. Its item description calls it a treasure that once belonged to Barbatos, with its Anemo power run dry. It is used in Ludi Harpastum, and other use needs signed documents from the Grand Master, the Seneschal and a community representative. In the Prologue, Venti cannot borrow it because the Seneschal is away with the expedition, so the Traveler enters the basement at night. After the lyre is retrieved from the Fatui, Venti says it is the real thing but its power has run dry after a thousand years, with a rosewood frame carved with flowing wind. A purified Teardrop Crystal from Dvalin partly restores it. At the end of the Prologue it is returned to Barbara in a worse condition, and Venti masks this with an Anemo illusion.
### LARGE +
Venti's other lyre, which his fake Vision turns into in battle, is a separate instrument; the wiki names it Der Fruehling. He does not carry the Holy Lyre himself.

## entry: Statues of the Seven in Mondstadt
@keys: Statue of the Seven, Statue of The Seven, Anemoculus, Anemoculi
@keys2: Mondstadt, Anemo, offering
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
Mondstadt's Statues of the Seven are the god's shrines in the wild. People with Visions offer them Anemoculi, consolidated winds that only allogenes can see, in exchange for blessings. The Traveler first learns the custom in The Blessings of The Seven, when Paimon explains the offering. Fairy tales say the statues' eyes were gems taken by swallows. Long ago Mondstadt lost a statue of its own, which the Traveler later finds hanging upside down in the ruins in Call of the Abyss.
### LARGE +
The statues also revive fallen travellers, heal characters and act as waypoints, and the Traveler can resonate with a statue to change element. These are gameplay features, not liturgy.

## entry: Thousand Winds Temple and Istaroth
@keys: Thousand Winds Temple, Istaroth, winds of time
@keys2: Mondstadt, temple, Barbatos
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The ruins northeast of Mondstadt were built by the people of Arcadia for Istaroth, the Ruler of Time. The Gunnhildr Clan prayed there during the Archon War, and a wind spirit, the future Barbatos, answered. Once Barbatos was an Archon the temple was rebuilt for both, and the Sacrificial weapon series served in its rituals; later Istaroth was forgotten. The official map description gives the carved maxim that wind brings the seeds of tales and time lets them take root. Aristocrats later used the amphitheatre for slave combat.
### LARGE +
The phrase thousand winds is used across sources for Istaroth, for the personified winds that protect Mondstadt (Venti was once a single thread of them) and in one Aranara usage for erosion. The Windblume Festival is described as a celebration of the thousand winds.

## entry: Four Winds worship
@keys: Four Winds, Falcon of the West, Wolf of the North, Dragon of the East, Lion of the South
@keys2: temple, worship, Mondstadt
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
The Four Winds are the four guardians Barbatos entrusted with Mondstadt's protection before he vanished a thousand years ago. After Vennessa's rebellion Mondstadters worshipped them and built temples; after the cataclysm the worship declined, the temples were abandoned and the Four Winds were almost forgotten. The Temple of the Falcon at Windrise is a ruin devoted to the Falcon of the West, and the Temple of the Wolf is a ruin devoted to the Wolf of the North. Their four symbols still appear on Mondstadt character-card art.

## entry: Herald of Barbatos
@keys: Herald of Barbatos, Dahlia
@keys2: Church of Favonius, deacon, Venti
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Dahlia, a Church deacon, holds the title Herald of Barbatos and knows that Venti is Barbatos. According to the wiki, Venti appointed him herald when he awoke shortly before the Prologue: Dahlia relays notable events in the nation to Venti, and Venti gives his views back through him. Barbara looks up to him because he shares Barbatos's word, though he rarely stays long in the Cathedral and prefers markets and taverns. Dahlia became playable in 5.7.

## entry: Seamus Pegg and the Church leadership
@keys: Seamus Pegg, Seneschal, Cardinal of Daybreak, Cardinal Calvin
@keys2: Church of Favonius, Cathedral
@version: 1.0
### STANDARD
Seamus Pegg, the Cardinal of Daybreak, is Seneschal of the Church of Favonius and the father of Jean and Barbara. He comes from Nod-Krai. He joined Varka's expedition, so Cardinal Calvin covered his duties. His absence is why Venti cannot get the Seneschal's signature for the Holy Lyre in the Prologue. The wiki says he is aware of Venti's identity. During the 3.1 event Of Ballads and Brews he was still away with Varka's expedition; after the quest An Elegy for Faded Moonlight he left the expedition for "private matters", and in his own dialogue he says he returned to Mondstadt only as the Nod-Krai conflict drew to a close, intending to take on more of the Church's workload.
