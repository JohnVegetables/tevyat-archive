## summary
### LITE
Liyue's faith centres on Rex Lapis (Morax), the Geo Archon and god of contracts and commerce, who guided the nation for over three thousand years, and on the adepti, mountain-dwelling immortals who protect Liyue from afar. Worship is practical and ritual-minded: wishes at censers, offerings at Jueyun Karst, the yearly Rite of Descension, and funeral rites for departing adepti. In Chapter I Rex Lapis stages his death and retires; the Liyue Qixing and the adepti now carry on, and the god lives as the mortal Zhongli.
### STANDARD +
Liyue folk emphasise that their god has always been with them: unlike Mondstadt's absent Archon, Rex Lapis walked with the city through its history, and locals describe him as a father who handles everything. His authority is the root of Liyue's contract culture and of Mora, the currency named after Morax. Outsiders are expected to say Rex Lapis, not Morax, as the courteous form.
### LARGE +
The adepti are revered almost as deities: they are mostly illuminated beasts, and Liyue people make offerings and occasional pilgrimages to Jueyun Karst, the sacred mountain land where mortals do not tread without a Sigil of Permission. After Chapter I the Qixing's public account is that Rex Lapis failed a divine trial and his soul regained the celestial heights, while Madame Ping tells the Qixing that the age of contracts between gods and Liyue has passed and the age of contracts between Liyue and its people has begun. The adepti accept humans governing Liyue, though they will step in if humans cannot protect it.
### ULTRA +
Belief is layered: formal state ritual run by the Qixing, popular wish-making, folk legends of stone-statue adepti and adepti trials, and an expert tradition of funerary etiquette preserved by the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor, which keeps rites for adepti that are rarely needed.

## practice
### STANDARD
Annual state ritual: the Rite of Descension, in which the Qixing invited Rex Lapis to give predictions for the year, was held at Yujing Terrace until Chapter I. At the rite people prayed and wished at censers, believing adepti and the god blessed the day. Nationwide, the Lantern Rite and Moonchase Festival are Liyue's great festivals. Pilgrims leave offerings at the edge of Jueyun Karst. When an adeptus departs, the Rite of Parting is held.
### LARGE +
Liyue's Statues of the Seven carry Geo: the Liyue statues look different from Mondstadt's, and legend says the monumental Geo Archon statue that watches over Liyue was sculpted in the god's image. Rex Lapis's role as guarantor of Mora and of contracts gives commercial acts a religious weight (see culture.liyue-contracts). Exorcists and thaumaturges, such as Chongyun's clan, form a related spiritual tradition.
### ULTRA +
Some adepti use sacred names (the Chinese text gives Rex Lapis a higher-ranking sacred name than most adepti, and Prime of Adepti is another of his titles; Madame Ping has a sacred name, Streetward Rambler, but goes by her alias, and it is unclear whether Ganyu has one), and the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor's Zhongli oversees rituals down to costume, timing, place and audience. Legends hold that some adepti asked Morax to turn them into stone statues and that the statues move, a story recorded in the Records of Jueyun.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Liyue Harbor holds the state rituals; villages like Qingce and Mingyun are home to older and younger residents and keep quieter local customs. Jueyun Karst, in the northwest of Liyue, is the adepti's land. No organised church is shown; mortals manage public life.
### LARGE +
Chenyu Vale and its adepti (Lingyuan, the Herblord, Fujin) have their own history from the Archon War, when the vale's unnamed mistress fought Morax and, at the end, her own adepti rebelled against her to save the valley from her flood; locals there also keep their own medicine and traditions.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use incense, wishes, bells, and a keen sense of propriety. Locals speak of Rex Lapis in the respectful third person and still pray to him. After Chapter I, ordinary people believe the official story that his soul returned to the heavens; only a few know he lives as Zhongli. Adepti speak of mortals with affection but expect certain etiquette. A foreigner who says "Morax" in front of a Liyue native may be corrected.
### LARGE +
Characters differ: Madame Ping, Cloud Retainer and Zhongli view the gods' era with weary affection; Ningguang and Keqing speak in the Qixing's practical, contract-minded voice; Hu Tao treats death with irreverent ease while respecting ritual form.

## examples
### STANDARD
- Chapter I: the Rite of Descension ends in a staged death, and the Rite of Parting later marks the god's retirement.
- Childe tells the Traveler that Liyue people believe Jueyun Karst is the adepti's home and leave offerings at its borders, while he knows they really exist.

## entry: Rex Lapis as a deity
@keys: God of Contracts, Warrior God, Geo Archon worship, Prime of Adepti
@keys2: Liyue, Rex Lapis, Morax
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Rex Lapis is the Geo Archon, oldest of the Seven, called the God of Contracts, Commerce and War, the Prime of Adepti, the Lord of Geo and (outside Liyue) Morax. Mora is named for him. He led Liyue through the Archon War, resettled his people south of Mt. Tianheng after Guizhong's death and made a contract with the adepti to guard the harbor. In the main story he feigns death at the Rite of Descension, passes his Gnosis to the Tsaritsa's emissary Signora and retires; his character entry carries the detail.

## entry: The adepti and Jueyun Karst
@keys: Jueyun Karst, Sigil of Permission, adeptus, adepti
@keys2: Liyue
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Most adepti retired into seclusion at Jueyun Karst after the Archon War. Mortals who enter without a Sigil of Permission risk offending them, and Liyue people leave offerings at the borders. Some adepti remain in the harbor, Ganyu as the Qixing's secretary and Madame Ping at Yujing Terrace, while Xiao hunts demons as the last yaksha. When Rex Lapis stepped down, the adepti agreed to let humans govern but remain ready to defend Liyue.

## entry: Descension and Parting

### STANDARD
Two paired state rites. The Rite of Descension was the yearly invitation to Rex Lapis; the Rite of Parting is the funeral for a departed adeptus or god, held for him in Chapter I. Both are described in their own entries.
