## summary
### LITE
Nod-Krai has no Archon, and its one organised faith belongs to the Frostmoon Scions, a tribe descended from Hyperborea who live on Hiisi Island. They worship the Frost Moon: the dead goddess Canon (Huurrekuutar to them), and her successor the Moon Maiden Kuutar, whom the wider world knows as Columbina. The faith is led by the Moonchanter, currently Lauma.
### STANDARD +
It is a living faith with a heavy past. The Scions kept the rites of Hyperborea after its fall, prayed for centuries to a goddess who no longer answered, and passed down a prophecy that the Moon Goddess would one day return through a Sacred Heir. About 500 years ago the first Moonchanter, Aila, broke with the older cult, abolished its harshest rites and its Archpriestess office, and reshaped the creed around benevolence, faith and avoiding conflict with outsiders. Kuutar was born in that era, and the Scions took her as their goddess.
### LARGE +
Its central tension is between worship and the person worshipped. Kuutar found that her devotees wanted their wishes granted rather than her, and left them. In the Song of the Welkin Moon chapter Lauma tells the Scions that the moon they prayed to belongs to a false sky, and the goddess herself asks to be treated as a person, not a deity. How each Scion answers that is the living drama of the faith as of 7.1.
### ULTRA +
The creed has therefore been reshaped twice in its recent history: by Aila's reform, and by the truth Lauma chose to tell. The Scions are not portrayed as fools or villains. The older rites were harsh and sometimes cruel, yet the present community is gentle and hospitable, and protective of its island.

## practice
### LITE
The Scions pray to the Frost Moon and the Moon Maiden, sing hymns and Runo, treat kuuvahki as the goddess's sacred blessing, and are led by the Moonchanter. Statues of the New Moon, carved in Kuutar's image, stand across Nod-Krai.
### STANDARD +
Clergy and ranks: the Moonchanter leads, followed by Priestesses, Acolytes and Adherents (ordinary followers). Adherents train to become Acolytes and must pass an examination on hymns and blessings. A quest states that men of the tribe cannot become Acolytes or Priests. Known Priestesses include Ehrnrooth, Sarakka and Rahetar; Verna is an Acolyte who is trying to change the Scions' stance on kuuvahki devices.

Doctrine and customs: kuuvahki is seen as the Moon Goddess's blessing, so using it for personal gain, including through kuuvahki devices, is blasphemy to traditional Scions. They value benevolence and faith and avoid conflict with outsiders.

### LARGE +
Hymns: the book Hymns of the Far North, compiled by Priestess Ehrnrooth, preserves the old prayer songs and Runo, noting which were abolished or rewritten by Aila.

Statues: the Scions carved the Statues of the New Moon in the likeness of the newborn goddess, from fine Lunasoul Stone. The Fatui later caged several of them in steel.

Aila's substitutions show how daily practice changed. Where the old rite for a birth offered sea salt and chanting to a mother-of-life figure, a senior cleric now says "May the new moon illuminate your path" over the child to guard it from the taint of the sublunary sphere. For the dead, regardless of social standing, a cleric of Assistant Priestess rank or higher says "May the new moon lead you to the shore of peace," followed by a simple solemn funeral. The old funerary rite had required animal sacrifices scaled to the dead person's rank. Prayers to nature's wrath were rewritten as plain appeals to the Frost Moon, and the dawnstar prayer was reduced to a short plea for the Frost Moon's protection.

The Scions' home on Hiisi Island, the Frostmoon Enclave, once consisted of towers, fortresses and temples. Aila had them torn down, saving only the pillar raised by Xiuhcoatl, because she believed that isolation fed delusions. The Enclave of today has a Grand Library.

Moon-Prayer Night (Kuunrukous) began as a Scion observance on the night the moon comes closest, when they wore Moon-Prayer Blossoms, sang and danced for the Moon Goddess's birth and gave away food and handicrafts. Today it is mostly a local custom and business occasion across Nod-Krai; the Scions hang Moonveils on Nasha Town's buildings. See its own entry.
### ULTRA +
The old Hyperborean cult: Hymns of the Far North suggests the Scions once revered not only the Frost Moon but also the Heavenly Principles, the Four Shades, Nibelung, Koitar, the apostle Anacharsis and the figure Saarelainen, each with a prayer song and sacrifices. The first hymn, a plea to the "Lord of the Seven Calamities," was chanted indistinctly rather than redacted, so as not to provoke the god, with animal sacrifice and divination by entrails on the summer solstice. Another song tells how Saarelainen was cut into seven pieces in Pohjola. Aila's reform ended most of these rites.

Memory and secrecy: Aila removed Lovia from the record, and only clergy of Priestess rank or higher are permitted to know her name. The sealed Court of the First Edict held evidence of Lovia's misdeeds and the Sacred Heir Project.

## history
### LITE
After Hyperborea fell, the Frost Moon goddess Canon guided survivors to Hiisi Island and died. The Scions kept her faith, which Aila reformed 500 years ago, and then took the newborn Kuutar as their goddess.
### STANDARD +
Fall and refuge: after the Celestial Nail destroyed Hyperborea, Canon led the survivors to Hiisi Island in Nod-Krai and died. They settled around a pillar raised by Xiuhcoatl, later called the Sanctum of the Oathbearer, and kept their rites even though their prayers went unanswered. A prophecy arose that the Moon Goddess would return through a Sacred Heir born to the tribe.

Aila's reform: about 500 years ago the last Archpriestess, Lovia, pressed the Sacred Heir Project to extremes, and her charge Aila killed her. Aila abolished the Archpriestess office, became the first Moonchanter, ended the old rites and reshaped the hymns. She foresaw Kuutar's birth, and the Scions took Kuutar as the Frost Moon's successor.
### LARGE +
Details of the reform: Lovia's twin charges Alia and Aila were made to murder other Scions and drink their blood to raise their purity. Aila sided with the hero Solovei and killed Lovia; Alia died stopping Rotwang's attempt to open a gate to the Abyss. Aila gave Solovei the Arrow of Terpikeraunas, which he used to found the Lightkeepers, and offered the newborn Kuutar the first drop of silver blood. The Scions carved Statues of the New Moon across Nod-Krai in her likeness.

Kuutar soon saw that the Scions cared more about having their desires granted than about her, and when she could no longer answer prayers she left; she later joined the Fatui as Columbina, the Damselette, Third of the Harbingers. The Fatui in turn valued only her power. A few months before the Song of the Welkin Moon events she left them and returned to Hiisi Island, where Lauma apologised for her ancestors and hid her in Silvermoon Hall.

Lauma had realised that the moon in the sky was not the real Frost Moon. Lauma sensed that the remnants of the real Frost Moon lay beyond the False Sky. During the Song of the Welkin Moon chapter (In the Shadows of Night) she told her people the whole truth after long consideration. Some left the Scions for good; those who remained met Columbina and agreed to respect her wish to be treated as herself, not as a goddess.
### ULTRA +
After Dottore was stopped, Sandrone invited Lauma, apologised on behalf of the Fatui for the harm done to the Scions, and the two sides agreed to take steps to reconcile. As of 7.1 Columbina has chosen to live among the Frostmoon Scions on Hiisi Island again, and Lauma has invited her to a feast. The Palestar Edict, the Fatui order to recover her, was revoked by Anastasya after Dottore abused it to seize her power.

Earlier, the Fatui had been threatening the Scions to claim the ancient moon remnants on Hiisi Island and to force them to reveal Columbina's location, and had caged Statues of the New Moon, which is why Scions held the Fatui in resentment.

## regional_variation
### LITE
The faith is centred on Hiisi Island. Elsewhere in Nod-Krai most people are not Scions, and Nasha Town treats Moon-Prayer Night mostly as a business occasion.
### STANDARD +
Nod-Krai is a borderland of many peoples, none of whom share the Scions' creed. The Lightkeepers, the Voynich Guild and the Curatorium of Secrets are not religious bodies, and the Fatui treated the moons as a source of power rather than an object of worship. Statues of the New Moon are respected landmarks, and the in-game text says travellers pray for protection when they come across one.
### LARGE +
The sacred bloodline traits are tribal rather than doctrinal: silver blood, kuuvahki sensitivity, antler crowns (said to have been gifted to the Hyperboreans by the Frost Moon at the end of her life) and the ability to communicate with living things. They run strongest in rare individuals such as Lauma, whose unusual sensitivity is said to come once in a century.
### ULTRA +
The Sacred Heir Project was an attempt to protect that bloodline through strict control of family trees, a eugenic method used after Hyperborea's genetic arts became impossible. It was meant to be phased out from the seventh generation, but successive Priestesses emphasised purity more and more. Aila's reform ended it, and no source shows the present Scions pursuing it.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use the Scions as gentle, hospitable and quietly wary of outsiders. Mention hymns, moonlight and kuuvahki; do not make them cultists.
### STANDARD +
Typical scene beats: a cleric recites a blessing for a child or the dead; Lauma answers a question with a formal blessing; a resident hangs a Moonveil; a Scion hesitates before using a kuuvahki device; someone prays at a Statue of the New Moon. Treat Columbina as a person who lives among them, not as a distant idol, and let the Scions struggle with that.
### LARGE +
Distinguish the three audiences: Scions on Hiisi Island who still believe; Nasha Town residents who treat the festival as custom or business; and Fatui who see the moons as resources. Keep the old cult's cruelty in the past: the present Scions condemn Lovia, and her name is known only to higher clergy.
### ULTRA +
Do not invent doctrine beyond what the hymns, notes and quests state. In particular, do not invent holy days, holy books, commandments or catchphrases for the Scions, and do not claim that the Moon Goddess grants miracles.

### STANDARD
The Frostmoon Scions are led by the Moonchanter, a title created by Aila about 500 years ago when she abolished the older office of Archpriestess. Below the Moonchanter are Priestesses, Acolytes and Adherents. Adherents train to become Acolytes and must pass an exam on hymns and blessings. A quest states that men of the tribe cannot become Acolytes or Priests. Lauma is the current Moonchanter; the Priestess Sarakka is the sibling of a previous Moonchanter.
### LARGE +
The wiki lists the Priestesses Ehrnrooth, Sarakka and Rahetar; the Acolytes Verna, Zirnytsia and Eveliina; and Adherents including Krodo, Ossian, Reetta, Lyylikki, Kukkia and Kantor. Former members include Vilemina, who cut off her antlers on defecting to the Fatui, and Ryksa. Historical clergy named in the Sanctum texts include Saredne, an Archpriestess in the thirteenth generation after Hyperborea's fall, and Lovia, the last Archpriestess.

## entry: Aila's Reforms
@keys: Aila, first Moonchanter, Aila's Notes
@keys2: Moonchanter, Frostmoon Scions, Lovia
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Aila became the first Moonchanter after she killed Lovia and abolished the Archpriestess office. She ended the ancient Hyperborean rites, rewrote many hymns and tore down the Scions' walls, towers and temples, believing that isolation bred delusion. The faith was to rest on reverence for nature, kindness toward outsiders and benevolence. She erased Lovia from the record and sealed the Court of the First Edict with evidence of Lovia's deeds. Her private notes admit she lied to Solovei when he asked whether she felt hatred.
### LARGE +
She also foresaw Kuutar's birth and offered her the first drop of silver blood with Moonweaver's Dawn, and she gave the Arrow of Terpikeraunas to Solovei to found the Lightkeepers. Her own later fate is not stated in the sources read.

## entry: Sacred Heir Project
@keys: Sacred Heir, Sacred Heir Project, sacred bloodline, Avrelian's mother
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A long-term plan to keep the Hyperborean bloodline pure so that the prophesied Sacred Heir could revive Hyperborea. It was modelled on Hyperborea's Seed of Life Project but used eugenics and strict family-tree control, because the old genetic arts were lost. It was meant to be phased out from the seventh generation, but later Priestesses stressed it more. Around 500 years ago a maiden of unusually pure blood, chosen to bear the Heir, eloped with a Snezhnayan officer and bore Avrelian; Lovia had Rotwang retrieve her, and the officer died and she took her own life. The project was then abandoned. The Scions did not regard Avrelian as the Sacred Heir.
### LARGE +
Lovia turned instead to twin girls from a lesser branch, Alia and Aila, and forced them to murder other Scions and drink their blood to raise their purity. The Sacred Heir idea persists in the prophecy that the Moon Goddess will return, but no source names a present Sacred Heir.

## entry: Hymns of the Far North
@keys: Hymns of the Far North, Runo, Prayer Song, Ehrnrooth
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A three-volume book of hymns and rites passed down among the Scions and compiled by Priestess Ehrnrooth, found at the Frostmoon Enclave. Most of its contents and rites have long fallen into disuse. Each prayer song is preceded by ritual instructions, and Ehrnrooth's notes record how Aila abolished or simplified it. The book mentions sacrifices on the solstices and at harvest. One note observes that the prayer to the Mother of Time describes the Verdant Crest, a tree that appeared at the Moon Maiden's birth five centuries ago, in a rite supposedly older than the tree, and treats it as a scribal error. The fifth song is missing.
### LARGE +
Runo are the hymns that structured Hyperborean magic; the first incantations are lost but described as still able to stir change. The book opens with a traveller's lament for a lost homeland, urging Hyperborea's heirs to endure through hymn and Runo.

## entry: Statues of the New Moon
@keys: Statue of the New Moon, Statues of the New Moon, Idol of the New Moon
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Giant statues of the newborn Moon Goddess Kuutar, carved by the Frostmoon Scions and found across Nod-Krai. The in-game descriptions say travellers pray to them for protection. During the Fatui's presence, steel cages were built over some of them to harness their power or display Fatui authority. They also serve as the region's worship statues in gameplay.
### LARGE +
The gameplay function (offerings and blessings) is a mechanic; in the story they are objects of local reverence and a symbol of who controls Nod-Krai.

## entry: Kuutar and the Scions
@keys: Kuutar, Moon Maiden, Huurrekuutar
@keys2: Frostmoon Scions, Nod-Krai
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Kuutar is the Scions' name for the Moon Goddess, born about 500 years ago and recognised as the Frost Moon's successor after Canon (Huurrekuutar). The wider world knows her as Columbina, once the Third Fatui Harbinger. She left the Scions because they wanted wishes granted more than they cared for her. After the Song of the Welkin Moon events she goes by Columbina Hyposelenia, asks to be treated as a person, and lives with the Scions on Hiisi Island. Her own biography is in her character entry.
### LARGE +
Her presence is divisive: some Scions left the faith when told the truth, while those who remained agreed to respect her wishes. This should be played as a community adjusting, not as unified adoration.

## entry: Hiisi Island and the Frostmoon Enclave
@keys: Hiisi Island, Frostmoon Enclave
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Hiisi Island, in Nod-Krai's Southern Isles, is the Scions' home and the centre of their faith. Much of it is untouched wilderness under their care. The Frostmoon Enclave is the Scions' home area and holds a Grand Library; the Sanctum of the Oathbearer is the surviving pillar from Xiuhcoatl; Silvermoon Hall is a hidden cavern where Lauma hid Columbina to rest.
