## summary
### LITE
Unu is a Hilichurlian word. The Handy Handbook of Hilichurlian calls it a holy word that embodies the hilichurls' idea of gods and the origin of life. The same word also means "one." Canon gives no name, shape or personality for any single god behind it, so portray it as a reverent word, not a character.
### STANDARD +
Hilichurls drop it into odes sung at sacrifices and into everyday compounds. Human scholars in Mondstadt, chiefly the Musk family, have recorded it but admit they understand little of what hilichurls mean by it.

## practice
### LITE
Unu appears in hilichurl song. Jacob Musk's Hilichurl Ballad Selection records a pious ode, built on repeated cries of "unu, unu," that hilichurls sing only during sacrifices. Hilichurls seem to beat the weakest tribe members with planks as percussion during it.
### STANDARD +
Another recorded song, sung by Samachurls (hilichurl shamans), ends on the word Unu. The chief's reaction convinced Musk that it holds a special philosophical meaning for the tribe, though he doubts mainstream scholars would accept hilichurls as having philosophy. Hilichurl worship in general is song, dance and offerings led by the shaman; Musk saw raw leftover meat placed on altars as sacrifice.
### LARGE +
The word also shows up in ordinary vocabulary. Per the handbook, "unu" is the number one, "unu du" is three (literally one-two) and "unu boya" is the colour yellow. Both "kucha gusha" and "unu gusha" mean seed; the author finds it striking that the holy word can name a seed, since hilichurls hoard seeds and sometimes bury them when in a good mood. "Kucha" means small, with a mildly contemptuous tone.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
Hilichurl belief differs by tribe. Musk reports that the Mondstadt tribes worship elemental power itself, such as Anemo, without recognising the Anemo Archon, and that members of one tribe may follow different elements. The Eclipse tribe of Dadaupa Gorge instead worships an occult symbol that looks like an eclipsed sun. How unu fits into any of these practices is not stated in canon.
### LARGE +
Almost all human research on hilichurls comes from Mondstadt scholars (Jacob and Ella Musk), so what is known is limited to the tribes around Mondstadt. No Nod-Krai or Snezhnaya material through 7.1 adds to it.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use unu sparingly and from a distance. A Samachurl may chant it over an offering, and a character who knows some Hilichurlian (Ella Musk, or a scholar) might remark that it means both "one" and something holy. Do not give Unu a voice, an appearance or a will, do not have hilichurls explain it, and do not claim a translation canon does not give. Hilichurl lines should stay short and repeated, as in the songs.
### LARGE +
Most Teyvat residents think hilichurls have lost their spirituality along with their history and intellect. A scholar could therefore treat unu as a curiosity, while the hilichurls themselves treat it as sacred. Keep both views.

### ULTRA +
Source cautions: nearly everything on unu comes from one handbook note by Ella Musk's grandfather, plus Jacob Musk's song notes. The common English reading of unu as simply "god" is a community gloss. Humans who can speak some Hilichurlian (Venti and Kaeya are shown using it) are never shown discussing unu, so no character should authoritatively explain it. Musk's Eclipse tribe notes are a separate, occult cult; the Traveler's voice-over only hints at a link to the Eclipse Dynasty, so do not attach unu to it.

## examples
### STANDARD
Canon examples: the sacrificial ode of repeated "unu" cries; the Samachurl song ending with Unu; the number words unu and unu du; the colour word unu boya; and "unu gusha," a seed.

### STANDARD
Unu is the hilichurls' holy word for the divine and the origin of life, and also their word for "one." It features in a sacrificial ode and a Samachurl song recorded by Jacob Musk, and in compounds such as unu boya (yellow) and unu gusha (seed). Canon names no single god and gives no further detail.
