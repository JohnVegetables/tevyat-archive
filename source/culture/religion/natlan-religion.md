## summary
### LITE
Natlan's faith is lived rather than preached. Each of the six tribes honours a Wayob, an ancestral spirit in the Night Kingdom where the dead go. A sacred fire, the Sacred Flame, burns in the Stadium of the Sacred Flame. Warriors carry Ancient Names, and the Pyro Archon, a mortal who wins the throne in battle, tends the Flame.
### STANDARD +
Four things anchor belief. The Wayob give protection and, through the chief's dreams, guidance. The Night Kingdom is both the afterlife and, in practice, Natlan's ley lines. The Sacred Flame is a channel to that realm and is fuelled by the Contending Fire of warriors' battles. Ancient Names preserve the deeds of heroes. Natlan's gods are not remote: the Pyro Archon is a human champion, and the first one, Xbalanque, set the rules the nation still follows.
### LARGE +
For five centuries the faith was also a wartime arrangement. The Wayob guarded the Night Kingdom against the Abyss, the Pilgrimage chose warriors for the Night Warden Wars, and the Ode of Resurrection let the Archon bring fallen Name bearers back. That war ended in the 5.x Archon Quest, so the practices below are described in both their wartime form and as they stand after it.
### ULTRA +
The belief system is layered. Some of it is old tribal ancestor worship, some was shaped by the dragon Kukulkan teaching the tribes about Phlogiston, and some rests on arrangements with higher powers (the Lord of the Night, and the Ruler of Death Ronova) that most Natlanese do not see. Canon gives no formal creed, scripture or priesthood hierarchy. Do not invent doctrine; present belief as customs, rites and stories.

## practice
### LITE
Chiefs receive the will of their tribe's Wayob in dreams. People who die go to the Night Kingdom. Warriors draw Nightsoul power from it. The Pyro Archon enters the Sacred Flame to fetch fallen heroes in the Ode of Resurrection.
### STANDARD +
Wayob and tribe: each tribe has its own Wayob, called on for protection and blessings, and the tribe's chief is understood to hear its will in dreams. Villages keep an obsidian totem or black stone through which a Wayob can manifest as a Wayob Manifestation, though no one knows exactly why a Manifestation appears; it is believed to be sent to solve a problem. The Wayob choose which tribe members inherit Ancient Names.

The dead: when a Natlanese person dies they enter the Night Kingdom, where they gradually lose their sense of self and join the sea of souls. These souls are called Nightsouls, and warriors draw on them for Nightsoul's Blessing. The Night Kingdom accepted only Natlanese souls and kept only Natlanese memories; since Capitano's sacrifice (see below) it accepts people from other nations.

Ancient Names: an Ancient Name is an honorific linked to a quality such as Resilience (Uthabiti) or Unity (Umoja), with an obsidian feather-shaped stone on which glowing characters spell it. It carries the deeds of all earlier bearers, so passing it on keeps a tribe's history alive. Names normally grant no special power; the bearer earns their place. Six Names (Baraka, Malipo, Umoja, Vuka, Bidii, Uwezo) belong to the Heroes of Natlan.

The Sacred Flame: a flame that burns without stopping in the Stadium of the Sacred Flame. It is a channel to the Night Kingdom and a way to speak with the Wayob. When a Pyro Archon ascends, it gives them the knowledge and memory of the land. It is fed by Contending Fire, produced only when Natlanese warriors fight each other, which is why the Pilgrimage exists.
### LARGE +
The Pilgrimage of the Return of the Sacred Flame: a tournament held at the Stadium. Before the war ended it picked the strongest warriors, fuelled the Flame, and doubled as the Natlanese way of testing a future Pyro Archon in battle. Outlanders could not take part because Contending Fire can only come from Natlanese fighters. Now it is a purely athletic competition; Kinich won the first of these.

Ode of Resurrection: a solemn rite at the Stadium. An officiant speaks a eulogy, the Archon steps into the Flame to find the fallen warrior and their Ancient Name in the Night Kingdom, the crowd bows and sings a song in Swahili, and the Archon walks out with the resurrected person. It worked only for Ancient Name bearers whose team had at least one member return victorious; a team that fell entirely was not revived, and its Names were forgotten, hence the saying that victors burn bright and losers turn to ash. It was discontinued when the Night Warden Wars ended.

Speaker's Chamber: beside the Stadium, presided over by the Pyro Archon. By tradition the tribal chiefs gather there by the flame at set intervals to negotiate. It also seems to act as Natlan's main judicial body, though that is only a suggestion on the wiki.

Obsidian: only the Children of Echoes produce it, and it is used for ritual objects, Ancient Name stones and communion with the Night Kingdom. The Masters of the Night-Wind are the tribe closest to the spirit realm and offer divination, blessing of tools and weapons, and similar services.

Turnfire Night: the Scions of the Canopy mark it each year, using a small flame split from the Sacred Flame. It remembers Yupanqui, who stole the Turnfire from the tyrant Och-Kan and fell into the Night Kingdom.
### ULTRA +
Name Engravers: a skilled smith can forge a new Ancient Name, usually on the Archon's commission. It costs part of their lifespan. Xilonen says the technique is simple but the research is not: the engraver must gather the subject's life story accurately, because falsehoods make a flawed Name that can cause cognitive problems for its bearer. Names can also be forged to record a dead person's life and later given by a Wayob to a worthy recipient; the wiki says it is unclear how often this is the origin of Names.

A tribe that fell from favour: the Muratans, an Anemo tribe, were exiled over a civil war more than 1,000 years ago; some settled in Mondstadt and the rest lived at Tenochtzitoc, where the Abyss struck at the Cataclysm. Tenoch lost his Ancient Name while still alive, which Mavuika says was likely due to his status as an exile.

The Traveler's Name, Tumaini (Hope): normally only Natlanese memories can form a Name. After the Traveler rescued Kachina and helped repel the Abyss, Mavuika promised them one. The Lord of the Night gave Citlali and Xilonen a Pilgrim's Chronicle to record the Traveler's deeds in place of the Night Kingdom, and later spoke the Name's incantation to unlock the Traveler's Nightsoul's Blessing.

Absence of rules: canon never lists commandments, holy days beyond those named, or a priestly class. When writing scenes, rely on the specific rites above.

## regional_variation
### LITE
All six tribes share the Night Kingdom, the Sacred Flame and Ancient Names but each has its own Wayob, its own Saurian companion and its own rites. The Masters of the Night-Wind are the most spiritual tribe.
### STANDARD +
Children of Echoes (Nanatzcayan): miners and obsidian-workers who forge Ancient Names; chief Pacal. Scions of the Canopy (Huitztlan): the Turnfire Night tribe, birthplace of Mavuika; chief Wayna. People of the Springs (Meztli): hot-spring keepers and guides; chief Amina. Flower-Feather Clan (Tlalocan): sky patrols and Qucusaur riders; chief Mutota. Masters of the Night-Wind (Mictlan): shamans who see souls and monitor ley lines and Phlogiston; chief Biram. Collective of Plenty (Teteocan): farmers and wrestlers, some of whom wear masks in a wrestling tradition; chief Acatl.
### LARGE +
Saurians: the dragons of Natlan, descended from the ancient dragons after the Heavenly Principles' conquest, who lost their intelligence over time. Each tribe bonds with one kind of Saurian (Tepetlisaurs, Yumkasaurs, Koholasaurs, Qucusaurs, Iktomisaurs, Tatankasaurs), and Natlanese warriors often dress in imitation of their companions. Saurians choose their human partner and refuse anyone they do not acknowledge. They have their own language that humans learn to read only through long association. A rare red mark known as the Flamelord's Blessing is regarded as dangerous, and Saurians carrying it were traditionally sent back to the Night Kingdom unless the companion attempted a ritual at the Great Volcano of Tollan with the fallen dragon Xiuhcoatl; the wiki says that ritual was considered outdated because it almost never worked. Later quests reveal that Kukulkan deliberately controlled the expression of this mark.

Mictlan scrolls: the Masters of the Night-Wind weave scrolls that record important events in Natlan, appointed by the Iktomisaur Elder Tlapo.
### ULTRA +
Attitudes to dragons changed over time. In Och-Kan's era, Saurians were subjugated and one man was condemned to death for protecting his tribe's Saurians. The overthrow of Och-Kan returned authority to the tribes, restored their link to the Night Kingdom, and repaired relations with Saurians. Today there are still poachers, but the tribes live in harmony with their companions.

Outside views: Neuvillette likens war in Natlan to an undying flame. Foreign visitors such as Adel in Bayda Harbor describe the Natlanese as people whose souls are forged for battle from birth but who are not barbarians. These are outsider impressions, not doctrine.

## scene_use
### LITE
Show faith through objects and habits: obsidian stones, a village totem, the Flame glowing above the Stadium, names carried like heirlooms. Natlanese characters talk about the dead and their ancestors plainly, without hushed awe.
### STANDARD +
Natlanese characters speak of "the Night Kingdom" as a real place their ancestors went to, and of the Wayob as caring, sometimes overworked, ancestors. They use the Ancient Names of heroes as inspiration. Do not turn the Pyro Archon into a distant deity: Mavuika is a person who won the title, and the Natlanese treat Archons as champions, not untouchable gods. Visitors from other nations may find the openness about death and war surprising.
### LARGE +
Before the war ended, leaving Natlan needed a chief's approval through the Wayob because their protection did not reach beyond the border; the wiki says the Wayob's absence could cause memory loss, stress and bad luck. This restriction has lapsed, and asking the chief is now a fading formality. Resurrection scenes are over: the Ode is retired, so a death in a present-day scene should not be reversed by the Archon unless canon supports it.
### ULTRA +
Respect the ambiguity about the gods behind the system. A character who knows about the Lord of the Night or Ronova should be someone who was present for it (the Traveler, Mavuika, Citlali, the Heroes of Natlan, and so on). An ordinary tribesperson knows the Wayob and the Flame, not the arrangements with the Shades.

## entry: Wayob
@keys: Wayob, Great Spirit, ancestral spirit, Natlan ancestors
@keys2: Natlan, tribe, Night Kingdom
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Wayob are entities in the Night Kingdom that the six tribes venerate as the embodied will of their ancestors. They can see past and future and are described as will without form or thought. Each tribe calls on its own Wayob for protection and blessings, and its chief receives the Wayob's will in dreams. The Wayob choose who inherits an Ancient Name. They cannot extend their protection beyond Natlan without spending strength needed against the Abyss, so Natlanese who left risked memory loss, stress and bad luck. After the Abyss invaded the Night Kingdom, the Wayob were infected with Abyssal energy and their link to the people weakened. After the Night Kingdom was cleansed they resumed their duties, and told Citlali that Xbalanque was coming back.
### LARGE +
Known Wayob include Chaac and Angakuk (tribes unnamed), a "Great Spirit of This Land" tied to the Children of Echoes, and and "Maghan's Master" (Masters of the Night-Wind). The Lord of the Night's page credits a human named Chaac with first contacting the Night Kingdom, leading to the creation of the Wayob; a Wayob also named Chaac is listed, and the pages read do not say whether they are the same. The wiki notes the word comes from the Mayan "way."

## entry: Night Kingdom
@keys: Night Kingdom, realm of the Wayob, kingdom of the Lord of the Night, Nightsoul, Nightsouls
@keys2: Natlan, soul, dead, afterlife
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A realm that the Wayob and the souls of the dead inhabit, described as a space between body and mind, life and death. It lies deep underground and works as Natlan's ley lines, since the original lines were wrecked in the War of Funerary Flame. The Lord of the Night built it from fragments of those lines on the advice of Ronova, and Xbalanque later strengthened it. Natlanese souls there are Nightsouls, which warriors draw on. Memories gathered there did not match those of the outside world, so the Night Kingdom could not record deeds done outside Natlan. At the Cataclysm the Abyss overran it, and the Wayob fought it for five centuries.
### LARGE +
The Weaver of Secrets, a Dragonlord, merged her consciousness into the Night Kingdom to make a dream labyrinth where dead dragons and humans find peace, and keeps a pact of protection with the Masters of the Night-Wind. The Abyss was purged when the Traveler and Mavuika defeated Gosoythoth. Mavuika planned to give her life to restore the realm, but Capitano gave his life instead, merging his life force with the Lord of the Night and rewriting the lines' rules, so non-Natlanese are now accepted there.

## entry: Sacred Flame
@keys: Sacred Flame, K'ahk', Contending Fire, Contending Sparks
@keys2: Natlan, Stadium, Pyro Archon
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A fire that burns continually in the Stadium of the Sacred Flame, called K'ahk' in the ancient tongue. It works as a conduit to the Night Kingdom and a way to communicate with the Wayob, and was the foundation of resistance against the Abyss. A new Pyro Archon receives the knowledge and memories of Natlan from it. It is fed by Contending Fire from battles between Natlanese warriors, and objects imbued with it can be burned to sustain it. It has several outlets: above the arena, in the Speaker's Chamber's fire pit, and in the Night Kingdom.
### LARGE +
Five hundred years ago Mavuika placed her life in the Flame so that she could return when the tribes were ready. When the Pilgrimage was suspended and Contending Fire ran short, she sacrificed her own Archon power to keep it lit. Gosoythoth's manifestation briefly put it out, and Mavuika used the Pyro Gnosis to relight it. From the Flame in the Night Kingdom the two drew A Thousand Blazing Suns against Gosoythoth.

## entry: Pyro Archon succession
@keys: Pyro Archon, God of War, Haborym, Kiongozi
@keys2: Natlan, Archon, throne
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Pyro Archons are born human and win the position through battle, and they age and die like other mortals; Natlan has had many. The first, Xbalanque (divine name Haborym, Ancient Name Kiongozi), defeated the Pyro Dragon Xiuhcoatl about 2,000 years ago and set up the rules that let ordinary people ascend. The Pilgrimage is how a new Archon is chosen. Mavuika, of the Scions of the Canopy, is the current Archon and was also the Archon at the Cataclysm. Named earlier Archons include a scarlet-eyed youth of the Flower-Feather Clan who succeeded Xbalanque, Cochanina, Gloharry, Shulevitz and Isaja.
### LARGE +
Xbalanque borrowed power from Ronova, the Ruler of Death, to establish the Ode and Pilgrimage and made a bet with her: if humanity won against the Abyss, he could see the new Natlan for one day. Those who use her power are fated to die; Mavuika used it in the final battle and Ronova demanded her life, but Capitano's death was substituted. According to the Belyi Tsar, the Heavenly Principles had instead anointed Och-Kan as Sacred Lord and Xbalanque's succession defied Fate. In the present day Xbalanque returned for a day in Mausau's body, fought Mavuika in a spirit-level duel and lost narrowly, then left.

## entry: Lord of the Night
@keys: Lord of the Night, Yohualtecuhtin, Lord of the Crossroads
@keys2: Natlan, Night Kingdom, Wayob
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Yohualtecuhtin is the ruler and god of the Night Kingdom, once an Angel of the same kind Seelies descended from. She made the Night Kingdom with Ronova's guidance and, in ancient times, guided the tribes. Abyssal corrosion left her weakened and asleep until Capitano woke her with the Secret Source mechanism. She agreed to sacrifice herself for Natlan, but Mavuika refused, and she gave Citlali and Xilonen the Pilgrim's Chronicle for the Traveler. When Capitano gave his life instead, she absorbed his life force and became essentially immortal. She named the Traveler's Ancient Name, Tumaini.
### LARGE +
In ancient times the tribes reached her with obsidian and with live human sacrifices, often prisoners of war, and she told them to spread the Essence of Sacrifice on sterile ground to make it habitable for humans, a practice the wiki compares to Watatsumi's Goryou Matsuri. When Och-Kan banned worship of the Wayob and the Lord of the Night, a Nameless Priest believed her anger had cursed the people of Ochkanatlan with a horrific transformation. That was one man's belief, not confirmed.

## entry: Och-Kan and the banned faith
@keys: Och-Kan, Sacred Lord, Ochkanatlan, Grand Alliance
@keys2: Natlan, tribe, worship, Wayob
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Och-Kan, a half-dragon, half-human tyrant, took power after Xbalanque entered the Sacred Flame and proclaimed himself Sacred Lord. He banned live sacrifice and forbade people to worship the Night Kingdom, so as to consolidate power and keep humanity from being "strung up" by the Lord of the Night. The tribes gave their authority to a Grand Alliance and the dragons and Saurians were persecuted. After the Scarlet-Eyed Youth and his party overthrew him, sovereignty returned to the six tribes, who restored their link to the Night Kingdom and made peace with the Saurians.
### LARGE +
Och-Kan's mind and body were later separated: the body became the Dragon of the City of Flowing Ash, and the mind the spirit Cocouik, who helps the Traveler in Ochkanatlan. Before dying, Och-Kan passed the Golden Entreaty to the Saurian Little One. His spirit returned to the Night Kingdom and helped the Traveler there. See his own entry for his history.

### STANDARD
A fire from the Grand Alliance era used by Och-Kan to kill and oppress. Its victims feel burning from behind and are burned if they look back. Yupanqui stole it and used it on Och-Kan's army, then glanced back and was consumed, burning for a hundred days before falling through the ley lines to the Night Kingdom, where legend says it still burns. Malipo became an Ancient Name; its bearers include Burkina and Kinich. The Scions of the Canopy hold a Turnfire Night each year in memory of their ancestors.

## entry: Ode of Resurrection
@keys: Ode of Resurrection, Rite of Resurrection, Ode
@keys2: Natlan, Pyro Archon, Ancient Name
@version: 5.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A rite performed in the Stadium of the Sacred Flame to bring Ancient Name bearers back from the Night Kingdom after they died in the Night Warden Wars. It needed at least one member of the bearer's team to return victorious. Kachina's Ode failed because the Abyss was disrupting the Night Kingdom, which led Mavuika to suspend the Pilgrimage. During the final invasion, Mavuika called on the Lord of the Night's power to extend the Ode's protection to all Natlanese warriors, and Kachina later led an Ode for Mavuika and the Traveler that restored the Flame. After the Night Warden Wars ended the rite was discontinued.

## entry: Ronova's Fall and Natlan
@keys: Ronova, Ruler of Death, Arbiter of the Heavenly Principles
@keys2: Natlan, Ode, Xbalanque, Pyro Archon
@version: 7.1
@availability: permanent
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Ronova, a Shade created by the Heavenly Principles, guided the Lord of the Night in building the Night Kingdom about 6,000 years ago and, 2,000 years ago, let Xbalanque borrow her power to set Natlan's rules; the Heavenly Principles considered this overreach. After Mavuika used her power against Gosoythoth, Ronova claimed Mavuika's life, but Capitano's death stood in for it. In the 7.1 Snezhnaya Archon Quest, Ronova was defeated after exceeding her authority and died. No text read says how that affects the rules she granted Natlan.

### STANDARD
Each tribe pairs one Saurian kind with one Wayob manifestation type: Tepetlisaurs and the Rock-Cavernous Wayob (Children of Echoes), Yumkasaurs and the Foliar-Swift Wayob (Scions of the Canopy), Koholasaurs and the Flow-Inverted Wayob (People of the Springs), Qucusaurs and the Burning-Aflame Wayob (Flower-Feather Clan), Iktomisaurs and the Biting-Cold Wayob (Masters of the Night-Wind), Tatankasaurs and the Thundering Wayob (Collective of Plenty).
