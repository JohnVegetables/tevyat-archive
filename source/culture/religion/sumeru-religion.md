## summary
### LITE
Sumeru's faith has three strands. In the rainforest people worship the God of Wisdom, the Dendro Archon: first Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, who died in the Cataclysm 500 years ago, then her successor Lesser Lord Kusanali (Nahida). In the desert, many Eremites revere the ancient god-king King Deshret and some hope for his return. The Sumeru Akademiya, which governs the nation, prizes reason and counts "revering gods without acts of devotion" among its sins.
### STANDARD +
For centuries the people's affection went to the dead goddess while the living one sat confined in the Sanctuary of Surasthana, unseen. After Chapter III (3.0) Lesser Lord Kusanali walks the city openly, the Sabzeruz Festival marks her birthday, and the Akademiya no longer starves it of support. Faith here is mostly stories, festivals and personal feeling rather than temples and clergy.
### LARGE +
Sumeru's religion is also a story about memory and rivalry. The Akademiya spread a distorted version of the Rukkhadevata and Deshret legends that set forest folk against desert folk, and devotees of Deshret came to see Rukkhadevata as a traitor. When the truth surfaced in 3.0, several Eremite groups dropped their hostility. Smaller threads run alongside: the Aranara's love for their "queen", Pari devotion to Egeria's remnant, and matriarchal desert tribes who revered the Goddess of Flowers.
### ULTRA +
Chapter III also rewrote history: Rukkhadevata was erased from Irminsul and everyone but the Traveler now thinks Nahida was always the Dendro Archon. Careful speakers should keep three layers apart: what people believed before 3.0, what happened in Chapter III, and what Sumerians remember afterwards. In 6.6 Nahida burned the old Irminsul, and Aaru, the unfinished afterlife begun by Deshret, took over its function as a repository of Ley Line information. That is infrastructure, not doctrine.

## practice
### LITE
Worship in Sumeru is mostly small and personal. People tell stories of the old goddess, keep the Sabzeruz Festival and trust the God of Wisdom to protect the land. Eremite devotees of Deshret keep alive dances and customs that descend from ancient worship rituals.
### STANDARD +
- Stories and affection. Rukkhadevata is praised in household tales across Sumeru. "Lesser Lord Kusanali" is a term of endearment for the current Archon, who is the god Sumeru places its faith in (Yae Miko says so in the Inazuma quest Wishes).
- Sabzeruz. A birthday festival with a Haft-Mewa table of seven foods, the Knight of Flowers, Farris, who is a favourite with children, and the Dance of Sabzeruz, its central event.
- Desert devotion. Deshret's symbol is the sun and the eye. Eremite dance traditions come from their ancestors' worship rituals.
### LARGE +
- Statues of the Seven. The Sumeru statue shows the Dendro Archon as a small figure seated on a large leaf, in a cloak and draped clothing like the other Archon statues.
- Eremite insignia. Mid- and high-tier Eremite fighters wear the sun motif on their eye coverings, and low-tier Eremites wear the Eremite crest of the sun above crossed scimitars.
- Priestly dress. Priests in the desert kingdoms that worshipped Deshret made imitations of his gold-inlaid headband, according to an artifact's lore, and Nabu Malikata's priests are said to have worn an amethyst crown as traditional headgear.
- Oral memory. The Eremites hand down their ancestors' history by mouth, and their stories are gradually fading or being distorted.
### ULTRA +
- Prayer and the throne. A mechanism at Khaj-Nisut was said to let anyone enter the "golden slumber" by reciting a certain prayer and sitting on Deshret's throne. It belongs to the ancient civilisation, not to everyday practice.
- Tribal rules. Eremite tribes are self-governed and have their own rules about the tribe's wellbeing, with exile or death as punishment for violations. These are tribal custom, not a codified religion.

## regional_variation
@version: 3.0
### LARGE
- Rainforest and Sumeru City. The God of Wisdom, Rukkhadevata then Kusanali, is the centre of belief. The Grand Bazaar is noted as a place where Nahida had a few followers who celebrated her birthday with the Sabzeruz Festival.
- Akademiya. Reason, research and discipline come first; reverence is acceptable only if paired with action (see the six sins).
- Desert. Eremite groups follow Deshret to varying degrees. Ayn Al-Ahmar, Thutmose and Rahman's faction are described as his believers, and some wish to bring him back. Aaru Village shelters the followers and descendants of Deshret.
- Matriarchal tribes. The Tanit, Shimti and Uzza tribes had matriarchs and revered the Goddess of Flowers. The matriarchs called themselves her daughters. The Tanit were destroyed in the Desert of Hadramaveth.
- Girdle of the Sands. The Pari live in the Vourukasha Oasis, descend from the Khvarena of the divine bird Simurgh, and regard Egeria, the former Hydro Archon, as their God.
- Vanarana. The Aranara live in the forest and trust few adults.
### ULTRA +
The Temple of Silence has its own tradition. It was founded by Hermanubis and his followers after Deshret's death to guard knowledge from Deshret's age. Without Hermanubis it declined in faith and strength. Its present head, Sethos, works with the Akademiya.

## history
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Long ago the desert god-king Deshret, the Goddess of Flowers Nabu Malikata and the forest goddess Rukkhadevata were allies, the Three Eternal Companion-Gods. Rukkhadevata parted from them when they planned to defy the Heavenly Principles and built her own rainforest. When Deshret's pursuit of forbidden knowledge brought disaster, she helped his people and he sacrificed himself. She died in the Cataclysm 500 years ago, after making Lesser Lord Kusanali from the purest branch of Irminsul to be her next incarnation.
### LARGE +
Rukkhadevata's most devoted followers founded the Akademiya. 
The sages found the infant Kusanali and at first the people celebrated her, moving the Sabzeruz Festival to her birthday. Disappointed that she was no wiser than an ordinary child, they confined her in the Sanctuary of Surasthana and lauded Rukkhadevata's legacy instead. Over time the real history of Rukkhadevata and Deshret was blurred. Some believe a battle between them created the desert, and devout followers of Deshret see Rukkhadevata as a traitor who took his place.
### ULTRA +
In the Archon Quest Jnagarbha Day and the acts that follow, Nahida was freed and, with the Traveler, entered Irminsul where Rukkhadevata's last remnant waited. Rukkhadevata had left a trail of clues asking the world to forget her, and Nahida, unwillingly, purged her from Irminsul to remove forbidden knowledge for good. The Withering, the crazed whispers and the Eleazar disease ended, and all references to Rukkhadevata became the generic "Lord of Verdure". Only the Traveler still remembers her clearly, though Nahida has said she feels someone came before her.

## akademiya_stance
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
The Akademiya's culture favours rational thought and practical results. Its rules include three virtues (admonition, ingenuity, praxis) and six sins. One sin is "revering gods without acts of devotion", so empty reverence is discouraged.
### LARGE +
Many adults in Sumeru were proud that they did not dream, and the Akademiya's contempt for the arts spread through society. The other five sins forbid interfering with human evolution, tampering with life and death, delving beyond the universe, investigating the origin of words and attempting the forbidden and fearing none. Under the old sages the Akademiya was authoritarian. It marginalised the Sabzeruz Festival, required plays to be approved and restricted what could be published. Four of the six sages worked with the Fatui Harbinger Dottore to build an artificial god and harvested the people's dreams through the Akasha. Naphis and Baladhuri opposed this and were imprisoned. After the Traveler and Nahida stopped the plan, the corrupt sages were removed, Naphis and Baladhuri were freed, and new sages were chosen under Nahida's oversight.
### ULTRA +
The sages' misinformation about Deshret's revival and the distorted Rukkhadevata-Deshret legends helped keep forest dwellers and Eremites at odds, which made both easier to manipulate. Nahida's own view, from her voice-overs, is that the gods gave humans barely enough knowledge to fill their stomachs and that humans then built tools, cities and a path toward the stars and the abyss, which is why she keeps watching them.

## sabzeruz
@version: 3.0
### STANDARD
The Sabzeruz Festival is the nearest thing Sumeru has to a feast of its god. Legend says it began as a banquet for Rukkhadevata's birthday. After Rukkhadevata's death the festival honoured Kusanali's birthday, the day the sages found her. The Akademiya's leadership stopped organising it, and in Chapter III it was ready to shut down the Dance of Sabzeruz altogether.
### LARGE +
In the legend the gods at the banquet got drunk, Deshret played music, Rukkhadevata sang and the Goddess of Flowers danced so that padisarahs bloomed where she stepped. After Chapter III the festival regained its prestige and the Akademiya became willing to hold a grand birthday for the goddess. A second legend says Deshret and the Goddess of Flowers planned the first Sabzeruz as a secret party for Rukkhadevata. Her gifts were a Khvarena from the Goddess and the Wedjat Eye from Deshret. Both are recorded as legend only.

## sabzeruz_event
@version: 5.1
@availability: limited
### LARGE
During the limited-time 5.1 event Chromatic Ode of Candies and Roses, the Pari Bloomguard Sorush presented the Dance of Sabzeruz to Lesser Lord Kusanali, a tradition he described as passed down over centuries, and the people of Sumeru prepared a surprise for her birthday. Nahida was delighted, and asked the Aranara to help her return the favour. This is event content and not everyone in Sumeru would know it as shared history.

## scene_use
### LITE
Treat Sumeru's faith as mostly affection and story. A character may praise Lesser Lord Kusanali fondly, mention the Sabzeruz Festival, scoff at empty piety like an Akademiya scholar, or speak of King Deshret as an ancestor or a danger depending on their background.
### STANDARD +
- Rainforest locals tend to speak of the God of Wisdom warmly. Akademiya scholars are more guarded about worship. Eremites differ by group.
- Before 3.0 people were disillusioned with the "new" deity, because the sages hid her. After 3.0 they see her in public and attitudes have softened.
### LARGE +
Keep what a person knows consistent with their position. A Grand Bazaar trader, an Akademiya scholar, an Eremite of Ayn Al-Ahmar and a Pari will not share one view of the gods. Aranara and children often see Nahida more simply than adults do.
### ULTRA +
Never put Chapter III or Truth Amongst the Pages of Purana revelations (Rukkhadevata's erasure, Dottore's plan, Irminsul's burning) in the mouth of someone who was not told. A character who "remembers" Rukkhadevata after the end of Chapter III is mistaken, or is the Traveler.

## examples
### LARGE
- A scholar of the Akademiya dismisses a fellow student's gushing piety: reverence is fine, but only if it leads somewhere.
- An Eremite elder at a camp teaches the young a dance whose steps come from ancient rites, without claiming to know all of their meaning.
- A child in Sumeru City insists a friendly stranger left a gift at night because "Lesser Lord Kusanali" would do such a thing; the parents do not believe it.
- A member of Rahman's faction recalls how his group once despised Rukkhadevata and the forest, then changed its mind after learning what she did for Deshret's people.

## entry: Worship of Lesser Lord Kusanali
@keys: Lesser Lord Kusanali, Kusanali, Dendro Archon, God of Wisdom
@keys2: worship, faith, believers, devotion, pray, prayer, statue, followers
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Lesser Lord Kusanali, also known as Nahida, is the current Dendro Archon and God of Wisdom. "Lesser Lord Kusanali" is the people's term of endearment, and she is the god Sumeru has its faith in. For 500 years she was kept in the Sanctuary of Surasthana by the sages and almost no one outside the Akademiya saw her. People concluded they should no longer expect help from above, and some said the God of Wisdom would never come back. A few followers, particularly in the Grand Bazaar, kept her birthday as the Sabzeruz Festival.
### LARGE +
Through the Akasha, Nahida observed their devotion to the old goddess and their disillusionment with her, and made no argument against it. After Chapter III she is free, walks around the city and meets people as equals.

## entry: Greater Lord Rukkhadevata veneration
@keys: Rukkhadevata, Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, Queen Aranyani, Lord of Verdure
@keys2: worship, praised, devotion, memory, legacy, followers
@version: 3.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Rukkhadevata was the first Dendro Archon. She created the rainforest and, through the Akademiya, gave wisdom to Sumeru. Her name was praised in household stories across the land, and her most devoted researchers founded the Akademiya. The Akademiya idolised the wisdom she stood for and promoted her over her living successor.

### STANDARD
The Akademiya's rules rest on three virtues (admonition, ingenuity, praxis) and six sins. One of the sins is revering gods without acts of devotion.
