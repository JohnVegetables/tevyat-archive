## summary
### LITE
The Rite of Descension is Liyue's yearly ceremony in which the Liyue Qixing invite the Geo Archon, Rex Lapis (Morax), down to the mortal world to give his predictions for the coming year. The Qixing use them to steer Liyue's policy and trade. It is held on Yujing Terrace in Liyue Harbor. In the opening Liyue Archon Quest the Archon appears to be murdered mid-rite; Zhongli later reveals that he staged his own death to retire from godhood.
### STANDARD +
The rite is open to all and draws crowds, including Liyue natives who travel home from abroad and tourists, though most of the harbor's residents carry on with their day. Merchants watch it closely because the predictions move markets: the year before the Traveler's visit Rex Lapis gave seventeen of them. In that earlier year the Yuheng, Keqing, had the honor of asking for them; in the year the Traveler attends, the Tianquan, Ningguang, presides. Onlookers pray and make wishes at censers on the terrace, believing the day brings the blessing of both the adepti and Rex Lapis.
### LARGE +
Because the Archon is the oldest of the Seven and the god of contracts and commerce, locals treat this as one of the nation's most important business events. Liyue folk contrast their god, who is "forever with us", with Mondstadt's absent one; one resident calls Rex Lapis like a father who takes care of everything. Others are indifferent: a local says what happens at the rite makes no difference to commoners like him, and the gentry De'an mutters that it is good news for some and gloom for others.
### ULTRA +
Zhongli later admits that the rite was the stage for his test of whether Liyue could manage without him. Signora, the Tsaritsa's envoy, kept the secret at his request, and he held a backup plan using his Gnosis in case Liyue failed. The Qixing then recast what happened as a divine trial, and the contract-based age of Rex Lapis was closed by a Rite of Parting, not by another Rite of Descension.

## practice
### STANDARD
Once a year, on a set day, the Qixing invite Rex Lapis down to Liyue Harbor. The Tianquan presides: in Chapter I Ningguang calls the Archon with Geo while her assistants and Millelith keep the altar clear. The Archon delivers his predictions about Liyue's economic course, which the Qixing and the merchants then act on. The public gathers on Yujing Terrace, the highest ground in the harbor, and prays at censers. Visitors are told to call the god Rex Lapis, as Liyue people do, since outsiders using the name Morax is considered impolite.
### LARGE +
Attendance mixes devotion and business. Merchants such as Bolai follow the predictions as market intelligence, some shopkeepers send a spouse to watch while they mind the store, and many locals skip it. The rite is also a tourist attraction. Wishing is customary: visitors pray for wealth, for family, or for leads, and a stranger sharing a censer may wish the Traveler luck.
### ULTRA +
Before the rite in Chapter I, locals discuss rumors, such as that Ningguang was quietly working to exempt rocks made by Geo Vision-holders from tax. Ivanovich says only those who control Liyue may preside, that most adepti take beast forms, and that he has heard Rex Lapis himself appears at formal occasions as a dragon-and-qilin hybrid.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
It belongs to Liyue Harbor, not the whole nation, and is run by the mortal rulers (the Qixing), not by a priesthood. It is distinct from the Lantern Rite, which is a nationwide festival, and from the Rite of Parting, a funerary ceremony for a departing adeptus or god.
### LARGE +
In the Chapter I opening, Liyue locals set the rite against Mondstadt, whose god is "nowhere to be seen", and use the contrast to explain what they see as their own advantage. The quest does not describe any comparable ceremony in Mondstadt.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Play it as a solemn but commercial public event: incense smoke, a cordoned altar, Millelith in the crowd, merchants murmuring about next year's prices. A character who attended before Chapter I remembers it as the yearly norm that ended in an apparent assassination. In a scene set after Chapter I, treat the rite as a memory and do not assume it continues.
### LARGE +
Only the Traveler and Paimon, Childe, Signora, and later the Qixing and the adepti know the death was staged. Ordinary townsfolk believe the official line of a divine trial, or still whisper that the god was murdered.

## examples
### STANDARD
- Chapter I opening: the Traveler and Paimon reach Yujing Terrace for the rite; Rex Lapis falls lifeless on the offering table, Ningguang orders the exits sealed, and Childe helps them escape the Millelith.
- Afterwards Childe sends the Traveler to the adepti with a Sigil of Permission to clear their name.

## entry: Rex Lapis's death at the rite
@keys: Rex Lapis was killed, assassination of Rex Lapis, murder of Rex Lapis
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
At the rite, Ningguang summons Rex Lapis, dark clouds swirl, and the lifeless body falls onto the offering table. She announces he has been killed and orders the exits sealed. The Millelith detain people near the terrace, and suspicion falls on the Traveler. Later Zhongli reveals he feigned death; the Qixing had taken his vessel, the Exuvia, to the Golden House.
### LARGE +
The Qixing's later public line is that Rex Lapis's soul returned to the heavens after a divine trial, and the people should not spread rumors. Zhongli says the only surprise was the Qixing's conduct: they used the power vacuum to take full control of Liyue, and he counts it the best parting gift.

## entry: Zhongli's reason for staging his death
@keys: Zhongli's test, feigned death, Contract to End All Contracts
@version: 1.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Zhongli says he has lived over six thousand years and founded Liyue with the adepti about 3,700 years ago. After overhearing a merchant tell a worker that his duties were done, he wondered whether his own were. He feigned death to test whether Liyue was ready for its next age, gathering Childe, the adepti and the Qixing as the cast. His Gnosis went to the Tsaritsa through Signora under their contract, though he had kept the option to intervene if Liyue failed.
