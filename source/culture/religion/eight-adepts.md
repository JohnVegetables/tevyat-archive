## summary
### LITE
The Eight Adepts are a Liyue legend about eight gifted figures from the Archon War era who purified the tree-god Tao Dou, whose body stood before the realm of the dead. They were not all true adepti: three hermits of Guizang (Wind Reader, Yunlai Angler, Wuwang Kid), two sages of Lianshan (Sage Hu, Sage Jian), and three non-human Beasts of Tao Dou (Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame, Jade-Plumed Silverwing, Gold-Eyed Celadon Mare). One of them gave her life to make the working succeed. The story was introduced during the 5.3 Lantern Rite event, a limited-time event.
### STANDARD +
Keqing describes them as legendary people said to have had adeptus-like abilities, which earned the name rather than any formal rank. The tale is told by storytellers (the opera "The Sanctification of Tao Dou" is performed at the Lantern Rite), passed on through clan genealogies such as the Lan clan's, and kept alive in practice by the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor.
### LARGE +
In 6.3 the Lantern Rite story A Lanternlit Ode to the Silver Moon connected the Celadon Mare to Zibai, who is now a living figure in Liyue, so part of the legend is also anchored in present-day events.

## practice
### STANDARD
The legend runs as follows. During the Archon War a giant tree-god, Tao Dou, stood on a divine mountain, and Liyue believed the realm of the dead lay behind it; those who committed grave sins could not pass beyond. Dragged into the fighting, the god disturbed the realm and the line between life and death blurred. The Eight rose to deal with it and laid down a great array, the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method, which uses eight nodes of Liyue's ley lines with a person at each. One gate is chosen to be obfuscated, erasing whoever guards it from the ley lines to seal the gate and keep evil from escaping. The Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame held that gate and vanished from the world. Afterwards Tao Dou's remains sank into the ley lines and became the border between the living and the dead.
### LARGE +
The members, as the legend names them:
- Three Hermits of Guizang: Wind Reader (Mad Lan), founder of the Qimen arts, from the Twelve Tribes of Chenyu Vale; the Yunlai Angler (Fish Creel), whose rod technique is the root of Yunlai Swordsmanship; and Wuwang Kid (Old Man Wuwang, Red Runt), always presented as a child, expert in Qihuang sigils to catch demons, heal illness and protect life from death.
- Two Sages of Lianshan: Sage Hu (gourd crafts) and Sage Jian (bronze mirrors). Little survives about them beyond their secret Lianshan art and a hidden palace, traditionally believed to lie in the mountains south of Stone Gate, where they cultivated.
- Three Beasts of Tao Dou, also called the Three Immortals: the Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame (Art of Homa), the Jade-Plumed Silverwing (a swallow) and the Gold-Eyed Celadon Mare (a white horse), all auspicious beasts able to take human form.

After the working, legend says Silverwing and Wind Reader went wandering together while the white horse retreated into seclusion in the mountains. Wuwang Kid, who admired the Lone Butterfly, took up her mantle and taught her methods to the founders of the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor.
### ULTRA +
Zibai's voice-over lines, as the wiki summarises them, say Wind Reader and Silverwing met once each autumn after the Tao Dou affair, and that the Eight came together because they frequented the same tea shop. Some used nicknames among themselves: Mad Lan, White Foal (the Celadon Mare), Red Runt, Fish Creel. Wind Reader and Wuwang Kid are also described as swordsmen. These details come from the wiki's summary of Zibai's profile material and were not traced to the lines themselves.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
The legend belongs to Liyue. Different places keep different threads: Chenyu Vale's Lan clan claims descent from Wind Reader's brothers and keeps the Qimen arts and a silver sparrow as a family symbol; the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor carries Wuwang Kid's and the Lone Butterfly's tradition; Yunlai Swordsmanship (the style Keqing trains in) descends from the Angler. Ordinary Liyue folk know it mainly as a story told in opera and by storytellers.
### LARGE +
The three Hidden Palaces named after ancient divination texts (Guizang, Lianshan and Zhou) echo the legend's groups: the Zhou palace is the one a book says was enchanted with the Seven-and-Eight-Gates method to suppress evils, the most revered being Tao Dou.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use it as shared folk history, retold with flourishes and some doubt. Liyue residents tell it around the Lantern Rite, characters tied to the Lan clan or the funeral parlour take quiet pride in it, and Keqing will correct embellishments. Do not present members as certainly dead or alive: it is a legend with a few confirmed anchors.
### LARGE +
Characters who know more (Zhongli, Zibai) speak with the ease of eyewitnesses and do not need to prove the story. Characters without that knowledge should treat the Eight as figures from a storyteller's tale, and should not give every member the same abilities.

## examples
### STANDARD
- In the 5.3 quest, Lan Yan's rattan figures, eight movable dolls modelled on the legend, prompt Keqing to explain the Eight, and Yun Jin later retells the Lone Butterfly's sacrifice before her performance of the Tao Dou opera.
- In the same event the Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method is revived by Ningguang, Hu Tao and the Ministry of Civil Affairs: Hu Tao is chosen to be obfuscated at the Gate of Death, and other gates are watched by Keqing, Qiqi with Baizhu, Ganyu, Ningguang with Lan Yan, Xiao, Beidou with the Crux, and Yelan. Lan Yan's rattan dolls of Hu Tao link the overseers to share the burden of fetor.
### LARGE +
Lan Yan's silver hairpiece is modelled on Silverwing; Hu Tao recognises it as the Jade-Plumed Silverwing, one of the Three Immortals of Tao Dou.

## entry: Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method
@keys: Seven-and-Eight-Gates Method, Seven-and-Eight-Gates Array, Seven-and-Eight-Gates
@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A secret Liyue art that suppresses evil spirits, even gods. It places a person at each of eight key nodes of Liyue's ley lines (the gates) and obfuscates one, erasing its guardian from the lines to seal evil in. The seven in its name is the number of gates left once it is working. The gates and their latest positions, per the wiki: Life at Yilong Wharf, Death at Wuwang Hill, Repose at Qingce Village, Concealment at Mt. Aocang, Initiation at Liyue Harbor, Terrain at Dihua Marsh, Fright at Guyun Stone Forest and Injury at the Chasm. The nodes move as human activity changes the ley lines. A Descender, who is not recorded on the ley lines, would not vanish if obfuscated. The knowledge was lost over time and no record remains in Yuehai Pavilion; the 5.3 event revives it with help from an anonymous letter from the adepti.
### LARGE +
The gate names derive from the Chinese divination Qimen Dunjia. Of the eight, Life, Repose and Initiation are auspicious, Death, Fright and Injury inauspicious, and Concealment and Terrain neutral.

## entry: Lone Butterfly of the Crimson Flame

### STANDARD
One of the Three Beasts of Tao Dou, an adeptus of the Art of Homa. In the legend she was in charge of the gate that was chosen to be obfuscated, so she disappeared from the world; she was the only one of the eight to sacrifice herself. Wuwang Kid admired her, took up her mantle and passed the work of guarding the line between life and death to the founders of the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor.

## entry: Wuwang Kid

### STANDARD
One of the Three Hermits of Guizang, said to have always presented himself in child form. His speciality was Qihuang sigils for catching demons, healing illness and protecting lives from death. The Wangsheng Funeral Parlor's founders are said to have studied under him, and he took up the Lone Butterfly's legacy. The wiki presumes him dead; canon does not say so.

## entry: Wind Reader

### STANDARD
One of the Three Hermits of Guizang and the founder of the Qimen arts, said to come from the Twelve Tribes of Chenyu Vale. The Lan clan, including Lan Yan, claims descent from his two brothers. After Tao Dou he travelled with Silverwing. Zibai recalls that he never sought longevity and must long since have died.

## entry: Jade-Plumed Silverwing
@keys: Jade-Plumed Silverwing, Silverwing
@keys2: Eight Adepts, Lan Yan, Wind Reader, Tao Dou
@version: 5.3
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
One of the Three Beasts of Tao Dou, a swallow and, per the wiki, the one member who is truly an adeptus. Lan Yan's silver hairpiece is a family heirloom modelled after her, and the Lan clan treats her as a symbol. Zhongli compares her to the Ship of Theseus: as long as swallows fly in Liyue she remains, though her memories are replaced as swallows come and go.

### STANDARD
A white horse, one of the Three Beasts of Tao Dou, identified by the 6.3 Lantern Rite story with Zibai's Third Deadly Self Ziqiao, whom Zhongli (Morax) freed from the Zhanzhu painting during the Archon War. After Tao Dou her powers waned and she retreated into the Qingce mountains. In the 6.3 event she was restored as Zibai and now stays at Baiju Guesthouse by arrangement with the Wangsheng Funeral Parlor. Her own entry carries the details.
