## summary
### LITE
The Pilgrimage of the Return of the Sacred Flame ("the Pilgrimage") is Natlan's great combat tournament at the Stadium of the Sacred Flame. Fights between Natlanese produce Contending Fire, which fuels the Sacred Flame against the Abyss. Until the Night Warden Wars ended (5.3), the top-ranked Ancient Name bearers were sent to fight the Abyss in the Night Kingdom. Since then Mavuika has run it as a purely athletic competition.
### STANDARD +
Placing high brings instant fame, and in wartime winning was the highest honour in Natlan. Natlanese also say the Pilgrimage is how the Pyro Archon is chosen: anyone can take the title by proving themselves the mightiest warrior of their era.

## practice
### LITE
In its wartime form there were two stages. Teams, usually captained by an Ancient Name bearer, fought each other for Contending Fire. The winning teams' members then fought one-on-one matches, and the top scorers won.
### STANDARD +
Contestants register at the Stadium, and on the eve the Weary Inn feeds and lodges them free at the Archon's expense. After the Archon's opening speech, teams are dropped by hot-air balloon at random spots. They win Fire at Fire Towers and must carry enough back before time runs out. Losing a fight, leaving the grounds or running out of time all mean elimination. In the individual stage, results decide each contestant's next opponent, so closely ranked rivals end up meeting.
### LARGE +
- Registration: the pre-registration form has an optional page of hobbies and personal details, meant for the promotional team. Captains must register teams before the evening of the day before. Every Ancient Name bearer is expected to take part, as a duty.
- The eve: the Weary Inn is packed with revellers, and Mavuika can often be found there playing cards or singing.
- Punctuality: a late or oversleeping contestant gets the whole team disqualified.
- Team stage: monsters are placed on the grounds on purpose. Teams hand in their Fire by lighting the Sacred Flame at the Stadium. Teams rarely include more than one Ancient Name bearer, and doing so can look cowardly. Because the captain usually decides the team stage, weak bearers struggle to find teammates. The team stage is meant to teach warriors to fight together.
- Individual stage: lots set the first pairings. Commentators call the action from the stands and from the balloons.
- Close: the victors are announced on stage and receive the Archon's charge to go and defeat the Abyss.
- Conduct: contestants may only use the weapons and items recorded at registration. Losers must accept the result. Staff warn anyone who harasses contestants still in the running, and repeat offenders can be barred. Organisers say a few sore losers turn up every year.
- The Stadium is closed for maintenance after a Pilgrimage, and unannounced sparring or rehearsals in the main arena are forbidden.

## purposes
### STANDARD
The wartime Pilgrimage did three things: it made Contending Fire for the Sacred Flame, it chose warriors for the Night Warden Wars, and by tradition it is how a Pyro Archon proves their claim. Contending Fire comes only from battles between people of Natlan, so outlanders were banned. Only victors with Ancient Names went to war, because the Ode of Resurrection works only on Ancient Name bearers, and only if at least one member of the team returns victorious.
### LARGE +
Natlanese hold that battle is the most complete test of a future Archon, and that a clear victory is hard to dispute. Kachina told the Traveler that the current Archon won overwhelmingly. Mavuika has said the Pilgrimage and the war "have nearly always been linked": without a chance to fight the Abyss, rankings lose prestige, fewer people enter, and less Fire is made.

## history
@spoiler: major
### LARGE
The Pilgrimage is old. Mavuika's father won one and went to war against the Abyss when she was a small child, before she became Archon. The most famous bearer of Kachina's Ancient Name fought in twenty-seven Night Warden Wars and still achieved top rankings in the Pilgrimage when he was over sixty. According to the NPC Karpa, it was held whenever the Abyss threatened, and more often as incursions worsened in recent years.
- 5.0: the tournament the Traveler watched. Five victors went to war: Chasca, Iansan, Kaiulani, Kibangu and Kachina. Their side won, but Mavuika's Ode of Resurrection failed to bring Kachina back, and Mavuika suspended the Pilgrimage "until this matter is resolved". Kachina was later rescued from the Night Kingdom.
- 5.3: after Gosoythoth was defeated and the Night Warden Wars ended, Mavuika announced the Pilgrimage's return as a purely athletic competition, to help Natlan move past the war.
### ULTRA +
Mavuika's character story shows what the old rite cost. Her father's team won after three days and nights of fighting but lost three of seven warriors, her father among them. He was revived at the Rite of Rekindling. The Ode reverses only the fatal wound, though, not injuries built up over a long battle or illnesses he already had, and he came back too broken to stand.

## status
@version: 5.3
@spoiler: major
### LITE
As of 7.1 the Pilgrimage is a sports contest, no longer a prelude to war, and outsiders may now enter. Kinich was the first champion of the new format.
### STANDARD +
Mavuika says it remains a trial to find the strongest and a stage for each contestant to show their growth. No later Pilgrimage appears in 6.x (Nod-Krai) or 7.0-7.1 (Snezhnaya) content.

## regional_variation
### LARGE
The custom is unique to Natlan, and all six tribes take part: the Children of Echoes, People of the Springs, Scions of the Canopy, Flower-Feather Clan, Collective of Plenty and Masters of the Night-Wind. Contestants are introduced by tribe, and each tribe helped prepare the 5.3 tournament. Chasca of the Flower-Feather Clan was announced as a victor "with her sixth consecutive win". Mualani had been to three Night Warden Wars, won each time, and never needed reviving.
### ULTRA +
Individuals relate to it differently. Years of losses brought Kachina ridicule from her playmates and doubts about whether she deserved her Ancient Name. Varesa is said to be knocked out quickly because she cannot bring herself to fight fellow Natlanese seriously. Kaiulani made a comeback after ten years without a win.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Good scenes: the crowded Weary Inn on the eve; the Archon's speech under the Sacred Flame; balloon drops; tense waits for the match draw; friends drawn against each other in a final round. Check the era. Before the 5.0 suspension, victory meant war, and a team wiped out meant its members' Ancient Names were lost for good. From 5.3 on, it is a sports festival open to outlanders.
### LARGE +
Between Kachina's failed Ode and the end of the war, the Pilgrimage was suspended, and the suspension frightened and divided people. Do not let an outlander compete in a wartime Pilgrimage, and do not treat the Ode as unlimited.
### ULTRA +
The Traveler and Paimon served as special supervisors at the 5.3 Pilgrimage, so the Traveler did not compete. A {{user}} from outside Natlan could plausibly enter a later athletic Pilgrimage, but that is an extrapolation; canon does not show one.

## examples
### LARGE
- Kachina's teammates left her on registration night for stronger captains. Kinich took over Mualani's team so that Mualani and Kachina could enter as a pair. In the final round, Kachina beat Mualani and took the last of the five victors' places.
- Tepexpan and Tamuin, both eliminated, tried to force a rematch on Kachina in the contestants' lounge and were warned off by staff. When her Ode failed, they led the doubts about her victory.
- At the 5.3 Pilgrimage, Kinich beat Nlteego in the final. Mavuika then picked out the reborn first Pyro Archon, Xbalanque, among the contestants: a boy the crowd took for Mausau, Tawto's grandson. By his own account he did not even reach the final 32.
### ULTRA +
- Citlali entered the 5.3 Pilgrimage, and Kinich locked his companion Ajaw in a chest so he could win on his own merit.

## entry: Contending Fire
@keys: Contending Fire, Contending Spark, Fire Tower
@version: 5.0
### STANDARD
Contending Fire is the energy produced when people of Natlan fight one another, and it is the fuel of the Sacred Flame. During the Pilgrimage's team stage, contestants win it at Fire Towers and carry it back as Contending Sparks, small sparks "born from conflict" in the item text. They light the Sacred Flame with them. Objects carried in battle by Natlanese warriors also absorb some Contending Fire. In Past and Future, Mavuika burned such objects to release it. Fights involving outlanders reduce how much Fire is made, which is why outlanders were barred from the wartime Pilgrimage.

## entry: Suspension of the Pilgrimage
@keys: Pilgrimage suspended, suspend the Pilgrimage
@keys2: Pilgrimage, Mavuika, Kachina
@version: 5.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
After the 5.0 Night Warden War, Mavuika's Ode of Resurrection could not find Kachina in the Night Kingdom. Sore losers in the crowd claimed she had not really won. Mavuika declared the victory and Kachina's part in it beyond doubt, apologised for the failed ceremony, and suspended the Pilgrimage to prevent more deaths. She rejected Chasca's idea of holding it without the war, because without the war the event would lose prestige and produce less Fire.
### LARGE +
In private with Kinich and Iansan, Mavuika admitted the real reason. The Abyss's corrosion had already caused a massive Pyro shortage, and the Pilgrimage could not produce enough Contending Fire to make up for it. She hid this from the public to avoid panic and kept the Sacred Flame burning with her own power until the heroes were ready.

## entry: The new Pilgrimage
@keys: A New Pilgrimage, new Pilgrimage
@keys2: Pilgrimage, Mavuika, Iansan, Kinich
@version: 5.3
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
After the war ended, Mavuika remade the Pilgrimage as a pure athletic competition promoting fair play and personal growth: "competition in place of ceaseless battle." The tribes shared the preparations: supplies, cleaning and remodelling the venues, recruiting staff and even forecasting the weather. Iansan designed the events. The Traveler and Paimon were invited as special supervisors, which kept them out of the competition, and they followed the contestants around the course to check that it was run fairly.
### LARGE +
The knockout round was a pursuit course: contestants chased a winged, fiery ball across varied terrain. It had a vertical section that Mavuika worried might make people dizzy. Other events included a ball-launcher challenge, since ball games are an old Natlanese tradition. The eight contestants with the highest total scores went on to final one-on-one battles, and only one champion was named. Kinich won by beating Nlteego in the final. Mavuika then picked Xbalanque out of the crowd. Onlookers recognised him as the boy Mausau, and he admitted he had not even reached the final 32.
