## summary
### LITE
The Hyakkiyakou (the "night parade of a hundred demons") was a gathering of Inazuma's youkai in an age when the land was thick with them. It grew out of parties organised by the Kitsune Saiguu, where guests drank and told stories into the small hours. It has not been held for centuries. In Version 2.5 Yae Miko staged one last, ritual version as a farewell for youkai memories.
### STANDARD +
The name works on two levels: hyakumonogatari daikai ("great meeting of a hundred tales") for the storytelling party, hyakkiyakou for what it turned into once the guests were drunk. The tradition is bound up with Yae Miko's own youth and her old friends among the kitsune.

## practice
### STANDARD
Yae Miko explains that the Kitsune Saiguu would host gatherings where everyone drank and told stories late into the night, and that this became known as the hyakumonogatari daikai. Once the guests were properly drunk, they competed to cover the night sky with their powers, and the event came to be called the hyakkiyakou. Youkai were so numerous then that their silhouettes could block out the moon.
### LARGE +
Miko describes it simply as a gathering of youkai, not something sinister, though she admits the name sounds ominous. Youkai normally rose into the sky under their own power; the guests were competing, so spectacle and drink were the point.

## history
### STANDARD
The Hyakkiyakou belongs to the era when Inazuma was full of youkai and the Kitsune Saiguu presided over its gatherings. Many youkai died in the Cataclysm and their bloodlines thinned, so the gathering lapsed and had not been held for centuries by the present day.
### LARGE +
The old festival culture is shared with others of the period, such as the Mikawa Flower Festival, which youkai also held in those days.

### ULTRA +
According to the wiki's account of New Chronicles of the Six Kitsune, Urakusai had been banished by the Kitsune Saiguu about five hundred years ago over a "mistake" whose nature is unknown, so his place in the tradition's story is partly unresolved.

## farewell_2_5
@version: 2.5
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
In Yae Miko's Story Quest (The Great Narukami Offering, Divina Vulpes Chapter Act I), a novel with a real summoning incantation circulates in Inazuma City. It was written by Urakusai, a kitsune and old friend of Miko, who had died and whose memories, along with those of other youkai, had leaked from the Ley Lines after rifthounds damaged the Sacred Sakura's roots in Transient Dreams. Urakusai possessed a struggling novelist to write it so that lingering youkai spirits could enjoy a last taste of life and leave without regret. When Miko meets him through the Traveler he asks for one more Hyakkiyakou for himself and the others, and she promises it.
### LARGE +
Because the youkai memories are weak and can no longer fly, Miko performs a convocation ritual at the Grand Narukami Shrine, with the shrine maidens evacuated to avoid anyone being possessed and the Traveler clearing monsters stirred up on the way to Mt. Yougou. Miko, the Traveler and Paimon recite the incantation together as the youkai ascend, and readers of the novel across Inazuma City join in. The sky above the shrine fills with youkai memories, blocking the moon as in the old days, and they depart peacefully. No wishes are granted by the incantation, and the craze for novels containing incantations fades afterwards.

### ULTRA +
Miko, hearing that the farewell is in the Traveler's hands too, treats the whole matter lightly and then slips away to eat a large meal at a restaurant while the Traveler and Paimon look for her, a typical trick of hers. The quest frames the 2.5 ritual as the practical use of the novel's "moonless night" incantation, which Miko had planted in the team's own novel beforehand.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use it as nostalgic background for Yae Miko, kitsune or old youkai: a rowdy, affectionate night of sake, stories and showing off. For a modern scene it can only be a memory or a ritual remembrance, since living youkai in numbers are gone.
### LARGE +
Keep the tone bittersweet. Do not invent rules, dates or a formal programme for the original event, and do not claim that wishes can be granted by it.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the Hyakkiyakou is not held as a regular festival. The one revival in canon is the 2.5 farewell ritual; Miko and Urakusai treated it as a final one.

## entry: Urakusai
@keys: Urakusai
@version: 2.5
### STANDARD
Urakusai was a cultured kitsune known for his literary works, once an associate of the Kitsune Saiguu and a grandfather figure to the young Yae Miko. His memories lingered in the Ley Lines after his death. He wrote the summoning novel in 2.5 so spirits could leave with a farewell, and asked Miko for a last Hyakkiyakou.
