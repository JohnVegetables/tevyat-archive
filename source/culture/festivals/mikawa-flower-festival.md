## summary
### LITE
The Mikawa Flower Festival was a lively festival held by Inazuma's youkai, an occasion to drink, laugh and play music, in the days when the Hyakkiyakou still wandered the night. Its highlight was Akitsu Haneasobi, a game played with hagoita (decorated wooden paddles). The tradition dates back more than five hundred years, and Ei, her sister Makoto, Kitsune Saiguu and Yae Miko are among those said to have attended. It has since been revived twice in modern Inazuma: first as a small festival by three young youkai (Version 3.3), then as a larger festival for humans and youkai alike organised by Yae Publishing House (Version 5.4).
### STANDARD +
The festival is remembered above all as a place where youkai and humans could simply have fun together. Its legend of a samurai and a youkai who became lifelong friends over a game is the heart of how it is retold today.

## practice
### STANDARD
In the old festival, youkai gathered to eat, drink, make music and play games. Akitsu Haneasobi, played with hagoita to hit a ball toward a target, was its centrepiece, and the game had long been lost by the present day. Ei remembers it as once quite popular at festivals, and Makoto enjoyed playing it. The modern versions replace it with invented games: Akitsu Yuugei (3.3), a flipper-and-brick game on the beach, and, in 5.4, games made by attendees themselves, including "Akitsu Harpastum".
### LARGE +
The festival's meaning in tales retold today is friendship between humans and youkai and gratitude between them. Youko, the youkai who retells the legend, calls Akitsu Haneasobi "a symbol of friendship" and says the festival is meant to bring happiness to all who attend. Food stalls are central to the modern editions, and in 5.4 the proposed proceeds split gave forty percent to Yae Publishing House and ten percent to the festival representative (Raiden Shogun's account).
### ULTRA +
The wooden hagoita actually used by Kamai and the samurai in their game survived into the present day as a spirit, Hanyuuda Chizuru, who gradually gained sentience from long exposure to youkai power but has no human form of her own at the time of the early festivals. Her power has dwindled over the centuries, and the hagoita themselves have begun to rot.

## history
### STANDARD
More than five hundred years ago youkai held the Mikawa Flower Festival, and prominent youkai, Ei, Makoto, Kitsune Saiguu and Yae Miko were among the guests. One year a human samurai, Yanagibashi Takuto, stumbled into the festival. The youkai debated how to make him leave, but a youkai named Kamai Kenji declared that the point of the festival was to have fun, offered him sake and played Akitsu Haneasobi with him, ending in a draw. They agreed to meet for a duel every ten years, and their friendship shaped the sword style Takuto later founded, the Souran Isshin Art.
### LARGE +
According to Hanyuuda Chizuru, Takuto was about twenty-five when they met; they met again ten years later, and when Kamai next saw him Takuto was sixty-five and too weak to finish a last game of Akitsu Haneasobi. Five hundred years ago, Kamai fought beside Tominaga Masanari, third-generation heir of the Souran Isshin Art, until their final moments, wielding a sword handed down from Yanagibashi, resolving the regret Kamai had once felt. Miko followed Kitsune Saiguu around the festival one year and saw the hagoita pair then.

## modern_revivals
@availability: limited
@version: 3.3
### STANDARD
In 3.3 Kamisato Ayato set up the Test of Courage on Narukami Island after learning that three young youkai, Youko, Kappa and Hitotsume Kozou, wanted to hold their own Mikawa Flower Festival. They lacked the youkai power for a full festival, so they spent most of what they had on Akitsu Yuugei, their reconstruction based on the legend, and set up a few stalls in the forest. Strange hauntings during the event traced back to Hanyuuda Chizuru, the spirit of an old hagoita pair, whose slumber the three youkai's game had woken and who had used her fading youkai power to scare off a man trying to steal decorations from Akitsu Yuugei. At the end Arataki Itto took over organising a proper festival, which was held with the Traveler's friends in attendance.
### LARGE +
Ei, who visited the Test of Courage, recalled the old festival and Makoto's fondness for it. The three youkai said they wanted to thank the humans who had driven off monsters and allowed youkai to live in peace.

## festival_2025
@availability: limited
@version: 5.4
### STANDARD
In 5.4 Yae Miko, with the Raiden Shogun's approval, appointed the Traveler as festival representative for a larger Mikawa Flower Festival open to all humans and youkai, run by Yae Publishing House and aiming to combine human ingenuity with youkai arts. The core of the festival was a two-day game-making contest. Rumours of doppelgangers at the venue, stories of a threat from a dream-linked creature called a Nightmare Fragment, and a plan by Miko, the Kamisato siblings, Ei and Yumemizuki Mizuki to use dream against nightmare, made up the story event; the festival then went ahead, and an organiser reported three times the usual number of visitors.
### LARGE +
The bake-danuki, including Kichiboushi, Shousei and Gyoubu Koban, took part in the festival after Miko's personal invitation, despite the grumbling of Ioroi in Chinju Forest. The games made for it included the Akitsu Harpastum (Thoma's blend of Akitsu Yuugei and Mondstadt's Ludi Harpastum), and a "Tanuki Game Award" went to the winning booth. Shikanoin Heizou says the Tenryou Commission sent soldiers to stay undercover on the festival grounds. The event is limited-time content, so it is optional knowledge for characters.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use it as a nostalgic setting for older characters (Ei, Miko) or for a warm, slightly mischievous youkai scene. Games, stalls and sake are always on offer; a human newcomer may be met with suspicion that melts when the host stresses that the festival is for fun. It is also a natural place to bring up the old days of the Hyakkiyakou.
### LARGE +
The tone is best kept light and friendly, with a vein of wistfulness: friends who die and friendships that outlive them. Do not claim a fixed date or location, as each canon edition used a different site (the beach on Narukami Island in 3.3, a venue by Chinju Forest and Mt. Yougou in 5.4).

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the festival has been revived twice (3.3 and 5.4). No later edition or regular calendar is established.

## entry: Kamai Kenji and Yanagibashi Takuto
@keys: Kamai Kenji, Yanagibashi Takuto, Akitsu Haneasobi, Souran Isshin Art
@version: 3.3
### STANDARD
A youkai (Kamai Kenji) and a human samurai (Yanagibashi Takuto) became friends after playing Akitsu Haneasobi at the Mikawa Flower Festival and agreed to duel every ten years. Their duels fed into the Souran Isshin Art, which Takuto founded. Kamai later befriended Tominaga Masanari, third-generation heir of the style, and died fighting at his side five hundred years ago.

## entry: Hanyuuda Chizuru
@keys: Hanyuuda Chizuru
@version: 3.3
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
Hanyuuda Chizuru is the spirit of the hagoita pair Kamai and Takuto played with, born of long exposure to great youkai power. She felt proud to have left a mark on their story, but her rotting body left her hiding from humans and using scare tactics. Yae Miko first saw her at the festival and sensed her youkai power on the beach before the 3.3 event.
