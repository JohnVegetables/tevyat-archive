## summary
### LITE
The Irodori Festival is an Inazuman arts festival that honours the Five Kasen, five legendary poets of ancient Inazuma who were gifted in song and verse and whose collections were once presented to the Shogun. It faded from memory over the centuries. After the Sakoku Decree was lifted, Yae Miko and the Yashiro Commission revived it in Ritou as an "international cultural festival" built around light novels, with guests invited from other nations.
### STANDARD +
Its one confirmed modern edition is the "Magnificent Irodori Festival" of Version 2.6. Nothing in canon says it is a regular yearly fixture.

## practice
### STANDARD
The festival exists to show appreciation for the Five Kasen, whose works are said to have sparked cultured discussion and made Inazuma's culture blossom. In legend, one of the five presented a new poetry collection to the Shogun at Tenshukaku each year, and stories about the group were long popular, often as stage plays in which each Kasen wore one colour: green, blue, red, white and black. The revived festival swaps poetry for the modern equivalent, light novels, showcased with official and fan-made works, giveaways, prizes and new-release parties.
### LARGE +
In the 2.6 edition the main exhibition space surrounded the Outlander Affairs Agency in Ritou, with stalls selling light-novel merchandise. The small plaza in front of the International Trade Association became "The Five Kasen Plaza", where five blank boards waited for portraits of the Kasen painted by an artist invited from Mondstadt. The Yashiro Commission lodged invited guests at the International Trade Association and wanted guides who knew both Inazuma and the other nations. Other event activities centred on writing poetry, floral arrangement, staged mechanical theatre and martial contests, all offered as festival pastimes.
### ULTRA +
Stories of the Five Kasen traditionally ran as four successive poems, each telling a fragment of the tale from one Kasen's point of view; no known chapter was told from Kuronushi's perspective. The Five Kasen Summary preserves the common traits ascribed to them: Suikou a sake-loving free spirit, Aoi no Okina an old man accomplished at chess who also left a few novels, Akahito a talented swordsman, probably from a martial family (some say his name comes from marking works with a scarlet seal), Sumizome a former shrine maiden skilled in floral arts and dance who became the Shogun's personal attendant, and Kuronushi an unknown whose identity and personality vary between stories.

## history
### STANDARD
The Five Kasen brought a cultural flowering to ancient Inazuma, and the Irodori Festival grew out of the people's gratitude for their works. As their tales were gradually forgotten, so was the festival. Yae Miko revived it once the Sakoku Decree was abolished, in the Hues of the Violet Garden event (2.6, April 2022); Ayato calls it the first major Inazuman festival since the decree was lifted. She admitted that her fondness for light novels shaped the choice of format, while also arguing that a living culture needs a modern medium.
### LARGE +
Information about the Five Kasen is scarce, and the Five Kasen Summary blames the great calamity five hundred years ago for much of the loss. The festival's name echoes the real kabuki piece Rokkasen Sugata no Irodori, according to the wiki; this is an out-of-universe etymology.

## story_2_6
@availability: limited
@version: 2.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
During the Hues of the Violet Garden event the Traveler and Paimon were commissioned by Yae Miko to act as guides for foreign guests arriving at Ritou's docks. The guests included Venti, Albedo (painting the Kasen portraits), Xingqiu (author of the novel "A Legend of Sword") and Kaedehara Kazuha. According to the wiki, Albedo's portraits were modelled mainly on the invited party, with the blank-looking Kuronushi portrait based on Scaramouche (now the Wanderer). Copies of Xingqiu's latest volume went missing, and a series of poems telling a new version of the Kasen legend appeared around town.
### LARGE +
Kamisato Ayato revealed that he wrote the poems himself and used the legend to guide Kazuha toward a secret left by Kaedehara Yoshinori; the clues, including ink that appears only with water and the hidden Kuronushi portrait, led Kazuha to the truth about his family. Miko had originally asked Ayato only to tease the editor and Xingqiu after seeing them smuggle books out at night. This is an event-only story, so characters outside it would not necessarily know of it.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use the Irodori Festival as a cultured, slightly eccentric setting: poets and novelists on holiday, serious scholars mixing with enthusiastic fans, Yae Miko pursuing her publishing interests under cover of tradition. It is a good scene for introducing a foreigner to Inazuman arts, or for a character who is nervous about being in public. Keep it tied to Ritou and the post-Sakoku opening of Inazuma.
### LARGE +
Reasonable hooks include a missing manuscript, a rival guest arriving by ship, or an odd poem that seems to point toward a buried family secret. Do not treat the festival as a fixed annual event, and do not assign the original Kasen real names or works.

## status
### STANDARD
As of 7.1 the revived festival has been held once in canon (2.6). The Five Kasen remain legendary, with no confirmed identities.

## entry: The Five Kasen
@keys: Five Kasen, Suikou, Aoi no Okina, Akahito, Sumizome, Kuronushi
@version: 2.6
### STANDARD
The Five Kasen were gifted poets and singers of ancient Inazuma whose works stimulated its culture. Their names are colour-based stage names from plays: Suikou (green), Aoi no Okina (blue), Akahito (red), Sumizome (white) and Kuronushi (black). Their true names and exact lives are lost, and accounts vary, so treat any detail as legend. The wiki notes the group parallels the real-world Rokkasen, six famous Japanese poets of the ninth century.
### LARGE +
In the Hues of the Violet Garden event the version told to the party is Ayato's invention, and Albedo painted portraits based on the party. Kuronushi's portrait had to be splashed with water to reveal its secret.
