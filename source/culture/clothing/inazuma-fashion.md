## summary
### LITE
Inazuman dress is built on kimono-style clothing: kosode and hakama for many ordinary and ceremonial roles, full kimono for formal or fashionable wear, and wide kasa hats for fishers. The military and the shrines wear uniforms with colours that tell you whose service someone is in. Cloth and dye were hit hard by the Sakoku Decree, which cut off Liyue silk and ruined the Ritou dye houses.
### STANDARD
Dress in Inazuma is recognisably kimono-based and varies by age, work and allegiance more than by region. Ordinary townsfolk wear everyday attire in a handful of patterns for men, women, children and the elderly; kimono proper marks the better-dressed. Fishers have no special costume but are picked out by their kasa. Soldiers of the Shogun's Army and the Watatsumi Army wear foot-soldier uniforms in their own colours with their masters' crests, and shrine maidens wear a standard kosode-and-hakama outfit, red on Narukami Island and blue on Watatsumi Island. Fabric trade runs through shops such as Ogura Textiles & Kimonos in Inazuma City, whose supply of imported silk and local dye was disrupted by the Sakoku Decree.
### LARGE +
Fashion also shows up as a living trade. The Ogura shop is a century-old family business serving samurai families, and its owner is trying to find new patterns and dyes to keep customers. Inazuman tailoring is also where the Fontaine designer Chiori started out, which gives the craft a link to the wider world.

## practice
### LITE
- Everyday wear is kimono-style: simple layered garments for work, nicer kimono for formal or fashionable occasions.
- Fishers wear kasa hats; soldiers wear army uniforms; shrine maidens wear a fixed outfit.
- Uniforms carry clan or Shogunate crests and distinguishing colours.
### STANDARD
**Ordinary dress.** NPC models show separate patterns for children, men, women and the elderly, with plainer everyday clothes and fuller kimono for the well dressed. Wiki observers note that women often have bangs, younger women a short bob and older women their hair tied up in a bun; this is a pattern in the models, not stated in game text.

**Fishers.** Fishers do not have a distinct costume; the wide kasa hat is what marks them.

**Samurai and fighters.** Samurai-style clothing and armour appear on enemy groups such as the Kairagi and the Nobushi, who are told apart mainly by colour palette and by the kind of kasa they wear (the Jintouban wear a conical Nirayama-style hat).

**Shogun's Army.** Rank-and-file soldiers are modelled on feudal ashigaru: a shirt (shitagi), loose trousers (kobakama), white socks and straw sandals (waraji), plus eggplant-coloured armour bearing the Shogunate crest, and a cross-shaped spear (juumonji yari) with a lavender shaft. Officers have fuller sleeve armour and sometimes a jingasa hat with the crest. Higher samurai such as yoriki carry a katana on the left hip and wear a helmet and shoulder armour. Community study reads rank from colour: low ranks in purple-grey and grey, officers with lavender trousers and pale-gold trim, yoriki and above in lavender and indigo with dark-gold trim.

**Watatsumi Army.** A single uniform design serves the whole army: a shirt in a red-to-white gradient with a red-violet left sleeve, grey trousers, sandals without socks, and red-and-black armour with gold accents. The Sangonomiya crest is printed on the back of the shirt and on the right shoulder guard and under the spearhead of the red-and-white spear.

**Shrine maidens.** The standard outfit is a kosode and hakama with white socks and okobo sandals. Maidens of the Grand Narukami Shrine on Narukami Island wear red hakama and red accents; maidens of the Sangonomiya Shrine on Watatsumi Island wear blue.
### LARGE +
**Cloth, silk and dye.** The shop Ogura Textiles & Kimonos says that before the Sakoku Decree it could buy high-grade Liyue silk, including silk woven from Silk Flowers by the Feiyun Commerce Guild. With foreign trade shut, its owner says merchants lost their businesses, the dye houses of Ritou closed, and dyes grown in Konda Village no longer sold for good prices. Liyue silk became scarce and expensive, and the shop worked through old stock. After the story of Transient Dreams, she says contacts with suppliers have yet to be re-established, so stock is still limited.

**Clothing as social signal.** The shop notes that upper-class samurai are regular customers, and the owner presents an Inazuma-style outfit as a natural thing to offer a well-travelled outlander. Foreign silk is described in-world as a glimpse of how people live elsewhere.
### ULTRA +
**Mirrored colours.** Community observers note that elderly women's clothing on Watatsumi Island uses the same colours as on Narukami Island but inverted, and suggest it reflects the islands' separate worship of Orobashi and the Raiden Shogun. This is an interpretation, not stated in canon.

**Army contrast.** The Shogun's Army has three uniform designs (infantry, officer, yoriki-level) whereas the Watatsumi Army has one, so rank is less readable at a glance in the latter according to the same community analysis.

## regional_variation
### LITE
Narukami Island is associated with red and the Shogun; Watatsumi Island with blue and the Sangonomiya clan.
### STANDARD
The clearest regional split is institutional rather than geographic: shrine maidens and soldiers dress in the colours of the power they serve. On Narukami Island, shrine maidens wear red hakama and the Shogun's Army wears eggplant armour with the Shogunate crest. On Watatsumi Island, maidens wear blue and soldiers a red-white gradient with the Sangonomiya crest. Everyday dress in the rest of the islands follows the same kimono-based vocabulary, with fishers distinguished by kasa.
### LARGE +
Konda Village and Ritou matter to fashion through dye rather than costume: Konda grew dyes and Ritou had dye houses, both damaged by the closed-country years according to the Ogura shop's owner.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use clothing to place a character quickly: red hakama means a Narukami shrine maiden, a crest on eggplant armour means the Shogun's Army, a kasa means a fisher or traveller of modest work.
### STANDARD
- A tailor's shop is a natural place for small talk about the Sakoku years, scarce silk and what the customers are asking for.
- Describe soldiers by uniform colours and crest rather than by name; the crest tells the player whose authority stands behind them.
- A foreign visitor in outlander clothes stands out, and shopkeepers may offer Inazuma-style outfits as a friendly gesture.
- Keep garment names to the ones above; avoid inventing named clan patterns or ranks that canon does not give.
### LARGE +
For a fashion-minded scene, the commission with Ogura Mio gives an in-world hook: she wants new dyes and novel kimono patterns. Other outlanders she asks suggest books and tea sets as designs, and she is receptive to unusual dye materials.

## entry: Ogura Textiles & Kimonos
@keys: Ogura Textiles & Kimonos, Ogura Mio, Ogura Textiles
@version: 2.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
A family-run shop in Inazuma City, said to have existed for about a century, selling fabrics and the latest kimonos; upper-class samurai are regular customers. Ogura Mio runs it alone because her brother Yuu was called into the Shogun's Army and is in Kannazuka. The shop's notice says imported Silk Flowers are out of stock because of the Sakoku Decree and invites advance orders, with work to begin when silk returns. In the daily commission Crash Course on Inazuman Fashion, Mio asks the Traveler for ideas for new dyes or patterns. According to the Ogura Mio wiki page, which cites Chiori's Vision story, Mio studied tailoring alongside Chiori and invited her to work at the shop; Chiori declined but was moved by the offer.
