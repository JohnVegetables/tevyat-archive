## summary
### LITE
A duel before the throne is Inazuma's formal way to settle a grievance. The challenger brings the dispute to Tenshukaku, the Raiden Shogun's residence in Inazuma City, and fights the other party there with the Shogun as witness. By the account of the wiki's culture page, the duel ends when one combatant cannot fight on, and the loser dies: struck down, or executed by the Shogun's Musou no Hitotachi. Samurai also settle matters of pride with informal sword duels.
### STANDARD +
Canon shows the formal duel twice. Kazuha's friend, an Electro Vision bearer, challenged the enforcers of the Vision Hunt Decree and lost to Kujou Sara, who accepted on the Shogun's behalf. In the Archon Quest Duel Before the Throne, the Traveler invoked the same law against the Fatui Harbinger Signora and won. Anyone who calls the duel takes on a fight to the death.

## practice
### LITE
Someone who believes they have been wronged challenges the offender, and both go to Tenshukaku and state their case to the Raiden Shogun. If she permits it, they fight until one cannot continue. The loser does not walk away.
### STANDARD +
The Shogun's permission matters: in the Archon Quest scene she simply says "Proceed" and the fight begins. The victor may leave Tenshukaku alive, and the Shogun states that she acknowledges the winner's honour. Before the duel, Signora reminds the Traveler that the loser must die and asks whether they are sure; the Traveler does not retreat.
### LARGE +
The loser is either killed outright in the fight or, if they survive, executed by the Shogun with the Musou no Hitotachi, the sword technique the people of Inazuma regard as unanswerable. In the Archon Quest the defeated Signora threatens consequences from Snezhnaya; the Shogun disregards this and carries out the sentence anyway, calling Signora an enemy of eternity while recognising the victor's honour. The setting adds weight: Tenshukaku was built during the Archon War and also houses the Tenryou Commission's headquarters, so the duel takes place at the seat of power itself.
### ULTRA +
Authority is explicit. When Signora has knocked Kujou Sara unconscious in Tenshukaku, the Raiden Shogun states that the right to punish Inazumans for their crimes is hers alone. The duel before the throne lets a challenger place a dispute directly under that authority, and because it depends on the Shogun's consent it can be turned against the powerful: Signora remarks that the Traveler has learned to make the law of the land work for them, and treats the fight as being on equal footing.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
The formal duel is specific to Inazuma and to the court at Tenshukaku. The more ordinary form is the samurai's duel of pride. Samurai families have a strong sense of honour, and being beaten in a fight or needing an outsider's help is typically treated as shameful. The world quest Dreams of Sword Art shows a nameless samurai who demands to fight the Traveler repeatedly while searching for something he has forgotten.
### LARGE +
No equivalent formal duel before a ruler is described for Mondstadt, Liyue, Sumeru or Natlan in the sources read here. Fontaine has its own legal duelists, covered in a separate entry on the Champion Duelist, and they should not be merged with the Inazuman custom.

## scene_use
### LITE
Use it as a high-stakes, solemn law: a character invoking it is deadly serious, onlookers go quiet, and the challenged party must answer. Do not treat it as a casual spar.
### STANDARD +
A challenge needs a grievance and a venue. Inazuman characters who know of the custom see it as binding and fearsome, and Kaedehara Kazuha has particular reason to grieve it. Keep the Shogun's permission and the lethal outcome in view, and let characters weigh them.
### LARGE +
For ordinary samurai quarrels, a sword duel can be a matter of face rather than law, and the stakes can be anything from a lost argument to wounded pride. Mark the difference between the two: one is judged by the Shogun, the other is not.

## examples
### LITE
Kazuha's friend lost a duel before the throne against Kujou Sara and was executed. The Traveler defeated Signora in a duel before the throne.
### STANDARD +
Kazuha's friend had asked Kazuha about the Musou no Hitotachi, learned it was the Shogun's pinnacle technique, and made it his ambition to overcome it. His challenge to the Vision Hunt enforcers led to the duel, which Kujou Sara accepted on the Shogun's behalf. Kazuha arrived after it was over and took his friend's dying Vision.
### LARGE +
In the Archon Quest the Traveler follows Sara to Tenshukaku, where Signora has already knocked her unconscious. The Traveler's challenge is accepted, a hard fight follows, and Signora, after emerging in a second form, is beaten. The Shogun then executes her with the Musou no Hitotachi and says the Traveler may leave alive. Soon after, in the same quest line, the Shogun strikes at the Traveler anyway, and Kazuha blocks the blow using his own Anemo Vision and his dead friend's Electro Vision.
### ULTRA +
Kazuha's friend's story ties the custom to the Vision Hunt Decree, whose execution fell to the Tenryou Commission. After his death Kazuha tried to find someone to resonate with the Masterless Vision, even asking Beidou to offer it as the prize of the Crux Clash, but no one could, the Traveler included.

## entry: Duel Before the Throne
@keys: Duel Before the Throne, duel before the throne, Gozenjiai
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
Inazuma's formal duel, fought in Tenshukaku before the Raiden Shogun. A challenger presents a grievance, the Shogun grants permission, and the fight continues until one side cannot go on. The loser dies, killed in the duel or executed by the Musou no Hitotachi. The victor may leave alive.
### LARGE +
In the Archon Quest of the same name, the Traveler challenges Signora in this way and defeats her. The Shogun executes Signora and honours the winner's right to leave.
