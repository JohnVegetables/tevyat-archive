## summary
### LITE
Education in Sumeru means the Sumeru Akademiya, the most prestigious institute of learning in Teyvat and also Sumeru's governing body. Students pass hard entrance exams, join one of the Six Darshans, and advance by passing theses through a review panel. Degrees take a very long time, and many scholars travel the world as Driyosh to gather research. The Matra punish academic misconduct.
### STANDARD +
Rank follows the thesis, not age: one passed thesis graduates a scholar, two make a Dastur, and the leaders of the Darshans are Herbads, with a Grand Sage above them. The Akademiya also bans certain research on the grounds of six named sins, and until Chapter III it ran the Akasha System that shaped what ordinary people could know. Those who cannot enter learn from books, independent study or private training centres.
### LARGE +
The system has two faces. It produces real expertise and supports public services such as the Bimarstan hospital, yet it is stressful, slow and sometimes arrogant: under the old sages it looked down on the arts, banned unapproved information and pushed that attitude on the rest of Sumeru. The old sages who backed Azar have been removed, but the Akademiya remains the centre of Sumeru's learning.

## practice
### LITE
Admission is by entrance exam, and only those who pass are accepted. Study is organised into the Six Darshans, each with a specialty. Students call each other seniors and juniors, and graduation depends on getting a thesis past the review panel.
### STANDARD +
Children can enrol once they reach schooling age if they pass the exams. The wiki lists Alhaitham, who enrolled at seven and was homeschooled for a time, Sachin, who enrolled at nine, and Kaveh, who enrolled when young, as examples; Arina and Armin are current child students. Taking the exams is not compulsory, and people who cannot attend rely on the Akasha System (while it ran), independent study or training centres.

Rank ladder, as the wiki summarises it: a student who passes one thesis through the review process graduates and may be considered a formal researcher; a Dastur has passed two theses; a Herbad is a researcher of high prestige who leads one of the Six Great Schools as its sage and sits on advisory councils; the Grand Sage leads the whole Akademiya and is also one of the six sages. Driyosh, or traveling scholars, are students granted leave to wander and learn as part of their research.
### LARGE +
Seniority depends on academic progress rather than age: Tighnari is younger than Abattouy yet is his senior, because he graduated first.

Degrees are notoriously long and stressful. Gregor, a Mondstadt student chosen by a sage, worries on a message board that his parents will be old before he graduates, and Hosseini says graduating after 30 is not unusual and that his own hair has gone nearly grey from research. Thesis defences are the hardest step; Driyosh met in other nations talk about changing topics after rejection. Alrani says Soraya, a top student, has piles of rejected manuscripts. There seems to be a limit to how long a student may stay without graduating, and Hosseini notes that becoming a Dastur would let him remain at the Akademiya rather than travel for research.

Lisa is the cited exception: she graduated in two years through advanced study and is called the best student in two hundred years. She was disillusioned by the cost of unrestrained erudition and the workaholic culture, and returned to Mondstadt as a librarian.

Beyond degrees, the Akademiya awards a yearly Pir Kavikavus Prize to a scholar under 40 whose submission is judged groundbreaking and creative; past winners have all gone on to great renown. Travelling scholars gather material and do preliminary work for their supervisors' projects.
### ULTRA +
Research leans toward ancient history, the arcane, elemental energy and the foundations of the world, with more practical science alongside. Spantamad, with its ley line research, has the most Driyosh and the highest share of Interdarshan Championship winners, according to its wiki page. Students may take elective courses from Darshans other than their own, though how often is not stated. Tsuyuko, who studied at the Akademiya, offers a rational explanation for the Holy Soil of Watatsumi Island that later proves incomplete.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
The Akademiya accepts students from all over Teyvat, and Gregor of Mondstadt and the Driyosh met abroad show how far its reach extends. Rainforest and desert people reach it differently: while Azar was in power, desertfolk who were accepted, such as Nefer, were automatically assigned to Vahumana instead of sitting the exam that decides which Darshans a student can join. Staff such as Cyrus disliked this, but Azar's supporters suppressed the concern. The wiki reports that Vahumana, previously declining, has grown more popular since Azar's exile.
### LARGE +
Cyno, born in the desert, was raised within the Akademiya. Most desert and Eremite communities stand outside the system and are better described in the culture entry for Sumeru's daily life; Eremites, for example, sell Knowledge Capsules to researchers.

## people
### LARGE
Naphis, the Amurta sage, is now Grand Sage. Alhaitham, the Scribe, held the Acting Grand Sage post briefly and resigned. Kaveh is an Akademiya alumnus in architecture. Tighnari is an Amurta graduate. Cyno is General Mahamatra of the Matra. Faruzan, a Haravatat scholar, is a teacher whose work underlies Kshahrewar's mechanical research. Layla is an Rtawahist student. Each has their own entry.

## governance
### STANDARD
Academic life is led by the sages (one per Darshan, six in all, one of whom is also Grand Sage), with the Matra as disciplinary officers and the Mahamata as civil officials who spread the Akademiya's information and decrees. See the faction entries for the full structure.
### LARGE +
Greater Lord Rukkhadevata set up the Akademiya and its Six Great Schools, each representing a type of wisdom and led by a Great Sage. Amurta was the first, created by researchers who helped her cultivate the rainforest, and it is the largest. Other Darshans have appeared over the years, but only the six schools lasted.

After Chapter III the sages who supported Azar's plan were removed, while Naphis and Baladhuri, who had opposed it and been imprisoned, kept their posts. Training centres across Sumeru now prepare people who cannot enrol, such as for architecture, but some, like Alkami's, are scams. In Kaveh's Hangout Event the Akademiya is unaware of them, and Kaveh doubts it would act because they hold valid permits.

## rules_and_sins
### STANDARD
The Akademiya names three virtues, found in Sumeru's talent books: Admonition (the branches of the nation of wisdom), Ingenuity (its leaf-veins) and Praxis (its roots). It also names six sins, banned by the sages as the sources of all crime: interfering with human evolution, tampering with life and death, delving beyond the universe, investigating the origin of words, revering gods without acts of devotion, and attempting the forbidden without fear. Researching topics linked to these sins can lead to expulsion or imprisonment, and the sages have added rules and amendments over time.
### LARGE +
Research on mechanical lifeforms is banned and students caught doing it risk expulsion. It was tolerated and popular in Spantamad until overzealous researchers carried out unregulated vivisection and cruel experiments on animals, after which it was banned. Kshahrewar has been underfunded since Pir Kavikavus' research was lost in an accident, and Dori's long-term funding keeps it going in exchange for technology and craftsmanship, the wiki says.

The Matra enforce academic integrity. They discipline researchers for serious offences such as plagiarism or misappropriation of funds, visit offenders in person, which makes them feared, and are the authority for Knowledge Capsule controls, placing a seal on goods that pass inspection. General Mahamatra Cyno leads them; unlike the Mahamata, it is unclear whether a Matra must be an alumnus.

## scene_use
### STANDARD
Use the Akademiya as a pressure system: deadlines, supervisors, review panels and rivalry between schools. Let scholars talk about theses, grants and graduating late rather than abstract theory. Students refer to each other as senior and junior; Driyosh are away from home and anxious about their topics; the Matra are a source of dread rather than comedy. Do not make every Sumeru character a scholar: desert tribes, Eremites and craftspeople stand outside the system.
### LARGE +
Hooks: a Driyosh whose thesis was rejected and who needs material; a student drawn to a topic that brushes against a banned sin; a Matra visit about missing funds; rival schools quarrelling over how to read an ancient text (Vahumana calls Haravatat's method tunnel-visioning, Haravatat says Vahumana puts the cart before the horse); or someone who cannot afford the exams and is drawn to a dubious training centre. Show the old attitude that the arts are useless as a fading stereotype, not a settled fact.

## examples
### LARGE
Lisa graduated in two years yet found the culture hollow and left. Alhaitham enrolled as a child, became the Scribe and stepped back from the Grand Sage post. Tighnari graduated from Amurta but avoids Sumeru City and the Akademiya, while keeping close ties with researchers at Pardis Dhyai. Hosseini jokes about his greying hair at under 30.

## entry: The Six Darshans
@keys: Six Darshans, Six Great Schools, Darshan, Darshans
@keys2: Akademiya, Sumeru
@version: 3.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Akademiya's six schools, each with a specialty and uniform colour. Amurta is biology, ecology and medicine (green; sage Naphis, also Grand Sage). Rtawahist is illuminationism, the study of the stars (blue; sage Shakir). Spantamad is elementalism, ley lines and alchemy (red; sage Alkindi). Haravatat is semiotics, linguistics and ancient scripts (black; sage Rushd), and has the lowest enrolment. Vahumana is aetiology, history and social science (yellow; sage Baladhuri). Kshahrewar is technology, architecture and puzzle-solving (white; sage Firnas). Students can take electives from other Darshans.
### LARGE +
Haravatat and Vahumana overlap on ancient history and criticise each other's methods. Rtawahist students believe the stars write destiny, and only academics may study astrology in Sumeru. Spantamad has the most Driyosh. Kshahrewar's designs shaped Sumeru City's buildings and textile looms, but the school declined after Pir Kavikavus' research was lost.

## entry: Driyosh, Dastur and Herbad

### STANDARD
Academic ranks at the Akademiya. A Driyosh is a student on leave to travel for research. A passed thesis graduates a scholar, two passed theses make a Dastur, and Herbads are high-prestige researchers who lead the schools and sit on advisory councils. The Grand Sage leads the whole Akademiya. The thesis review panel is the decisive hurdle; rejection forces many to change topic.

## entry: Matra Discipline

@keys2: Akademiya, researcher, Cyno
@version: 3.0

### STANDARD
The Matra are disciplinary officers who enforce academic integrity. They deal with plagiarism and misuse of funds, visit offenders in person, and seal goods that pass Knowledge Capsule inspection. Researchers dread them, and meetings with them usually go badly. Cyno is General Mahamatra.

### STANDARD
Only people who pass the entrance exams are admitted, so many cannot study at the Akademiya. Children can enrol at schooling age, but taking the exam is not compulsory. Those excluded learn through independent study, the former Akasha System, or private training centres, some of which, such as Alkami's, are scams (Kaveh's Hangout Event, Act I).
