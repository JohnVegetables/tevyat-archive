## summary
### LITE
In Fontaine a criminal trial is a public show. Cases are heard at the Opera Epiclese, an opera house that doubles as the nation's main courthouse, with an audience watching as attorneys, witnesses and a judge argue it out. Neuvillette presides as Chief Justice (Iudex). Before a trial an accused person may instead demand a duel with an official Champion Duelist to defend their honour: win and they are acquitted, lose and they stand trial. For about five hundred years the final verdict came from a machine, the Oratrice, which stopped working after Furina's trial in 4.2.
### STANDARD +
The building is a grand multi-level opera house with raised seating and a private box for Furina, and it also hosts other performances, such as Lyney's magic shows. Canon presents Fontainians as feeling the justice of a verdict as a shared emotion, which the Oratrice turned into energy.
### LARGE +
The theatre of the court was not only entertainment. By canon's later revelation it was part of Focalors's plan: public belief in justice, gathered through trials, fed the Oratrice for roughly five centuries. Treat the show and the machinery as two sides of one design.
### ULTRA +
To the people inside it, the audience, the reversals, the machine's verdict and the Archon in her box look like pure theatre. The hidden purpose is known only to those who lived the Archon Quest through 4.2. Characters who did not are right to treat it as entertainment with real stakes.

## practice
### LITE
A trial takes place on the stage of the Opera Epiclese with a judge, attorneys, witnesses and the accused before an audience that reacts openly. Until 4.2 the Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale delivered the verdict, which the presiding judge had to honour even when he disagreed.
### STANDARD +
Duel option: before going to court, an accused person has one chance to defend their honour by requesting a duel with an official Champion Duelist. Childe describes it as a no-holds-barred fight with no fixed stopping point; a win means acquittal and a loss means standing trial. The worst case is being killed if one refuses to yield. Winning is described as exceedingly rare, so those who choose it are people gravely wronged by an accusation or who prize their honour highly. Childe suggests Fontaine keeps the system to show it respects its citizens' honour.

Attorneys: in the trial of Marcel, Neuvillette asks the accused whether he needs an attorney, and the Gardes are asked to bring a suspect to stand trial.

Presiding judges: Neuvillette is the best-known judge, but other judges from the Palais Mermonia can be assigned to cases; in the limited-time event quest An Overnight Fantasyland (Whirling Waltz, 5.6) Charlotte explains that the Iudex did not preside over one trial because it was assigned to another judge from the start.
### LARGE +
The Oratrice: a giant mechanical scale on the stage below the judge's seat, said to convert people's belief in justice into a power source called Indemnitium, which supplied lamps and transport in the Court of Fontaine and Erinnyes. It listened to the proceedings, shifted its scales after hearing arguments and objections, and delivered the verdict. Canon notes it appeared to have a consciousness of its own. At Lyney and Lynette's trial it found them not guilty once the real culprit was identified. At Childe's trial it pronounced him guilty although he had been cleared of the disappearance charges, the first time it disagreed with the Chief Justice's view; Neuvillette investigated personally.

Furina's seat: while the Archon role was active, Furina watched from a private box.

Interruptions and reversals: trials in canon turn on sudden evidence. Navia interrupts Childe's trial to accuse Marcel, citing evidence from her father's case, and a separate trial then examines him.
### ULTRA +
Honour and the duel: Callas, president of the Spina di Rosula, accused of murdering his friend Jacques, chose to duel Clorinde rather than stand trial, refused to yield and died. Later evidence indicated he did so to avoid testifying against the people behind the Sinthe affair, which would have endangered his daughter Navia. His case shows that the duel can be a decision about what testimony to give, not only a test of honour.

Death penalty: Childe says it is rumoured to exist but has never been applied. Furina's trial in 4.2 ended with the Oratrice sentencing "the Hydro Archon" to death, which the audience took as unprecedented. The sentence was carried out on the divine throne and on Focalors, not on a person on the stand.

Law and prison: the Court of Fontaine's official description calls the Palais Mermonia the highest authority in Fontaine and law the cornerstone of equity and justice. Convicts, including Childe after his 4.0 trial and Wriothesley in his past, are sent to the Fortress of Meropide beneath the sea.

## regional_variation
### STANDARD
This custom belongs to Fontaine, centred on the Court of Fontaine and the island of Erinnyes. Other nations have their own legal customs; this one is built on public spectacle and the nation's love of drama. A companion line in the Traveler's Fontaine voice-overs calls it the "City of the Arts".
### LARGE +
Duelling reaches beyond the official court: in the Fortress of Meropide, Wriothesley rose to run the place by declaring a duel on the previous administrator (Fontaine/History, via his second character story).

## scene_use
### LITE
Use the trial as a public show. Put characters in the stalls, on the stand or in the gallery, with a lively audience, a formal judge and dramatic turns. Keep the duel as an option for someone who values their honour.
### STANDARD +
- Before 4.2 the verdict is the Oratrice's, and the judge may be uneasy about it. After 4.2 the Oratrice is inactive and should not give verdicts.
- A duel is lethal in principle. Do not treat it as a light sparring match or have a character casually accept or refuse it.
- Neuvillette is formal and serious in court.
- Hold characters to what they know. Someone who did not witness 4.2 does not know the Oratrice's hidden purpose.
### LARGE +
Fontainian characters can plausibly treat attending a case as an outing, and talk about it afterwards the way people discuss a play. Visitors may find the atmosphere odd.

## examples
### STANDARD
Canon cases show the range: Lyney and Lynette's trial and the Oratrice's not-guilty verdict (4.0), Callas's fatal duel with Clorinde (years before the story), Childe's trial for the serial disappearances (4.0), Navia's trial of Marcel (4.0) and Furina's trial (4.2).

## entry: Opera Epiclese
@keys: Opera Epiclese, Opéra Épiclèse
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Opera Epiclese is an opera house on the island of Erinnyes in the Court of Fontaine Region. It is the main courthouse and the symbol of trials and judgment. The building has a lobby, a multi-level auditorium with raised seating, a backroom and a private box for Furina. The wiki's trivia count puts the auditorium's capacity at 169 seats including hers. Behind the building a lift descends to the River Talionis and the Fortress of Meropide (unlocked during A Tea Party Most Thorny). The Oratrice sits on the stage below Neuvillette's seat.
### LARGE +
Its official description calls it the place where the true and the fantastical, the comedic and the tragic, find a stage equally. The name comes from epiclesis, an invocation or epithet. Luzerne works there as receptionist.

## entry: Champion Duelists
@keys: Champion Duelist, Champion Duelists, Duel Representative, trial by duel
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Champion Duelists are official fighters chosen from among Fontaine's best, available to anyone who wants to answer a charge by duel instead of trial. Clorinde is the strongest. Other current members named in canon include Savinien and Guillaume Marais; Ismenor is a former member. Marfisa is the earliest known member, over five hundred years ago, and lost to the swordsman Emanuel Guillotin.
### LARGE +
Vitruvia, who once wanted to join, implies most Champion Duelists use swords. Clorinde uses both a sword and a pistol. Callas's death in a duel against her is the best-known recent case; she recognised that he let himself be killed, but his reasons became clear to the public only years later.

## entry: Oratrice and Indemnitium
@keys: Oratrice, Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale, Indemnitium
@version: 4.0
@availability: permanent
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale is the huge scale that rendered verdicts at the Opera Epiclese and, officially, turned public belief in justice into Indemnitium, an energy source for the Court of Fontaine and Erinnyes. Lyney suspected it was tied to the prophecy. Its hidden role, revealed in 4.2, was to hold Focalors and store the energy needed to execute her and destroy the Hydro Archon's divine throne. Afterward it stopped working.

## entry: Furina's Trial
@keys: Furina's trial, trial of Furina, Masquerade of the Guilty
@version: 4.2
@spoiler: major
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In the 4.2 Archon Quest the Traveler and Neuvillette stage a trial to expose Furina as not being the true Archon. The Oratrice agrees with Neuvillette's judgment but sentences "the Hydro Archon" to death, shocking the audience as the first death sentence they had seen. Focalors then reveals her plan to Neuvillette and executes herself through the Oratrice, destroying the divine throne and returning the Hydro authority to Neuvillette. The Oratrice is afterwards inactive.
### LARGE +
Neuvillette concluded that the sentence referred neither to Furina nor to Focalors as persons but to the Hydro Archon's divine throne.

## entry: Childe's Trial
@keys: Tartaglia's trial, Childe's trial, serial disappearances trial
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
In Chapter IV Childe (Tartaglia) is tried at the Opera Epiclese over Fontaine's serial disappearances. Navia interrupts to accuse Marcel. The Oratrice nevertheless pronounced Childe guilty, the first time it did not agree with the Chief Justice, and he was sent to the Fortress of Meropide. Neuvillette investigated the machine and the case himself.
### LARGE +
Childe said he would accept the charge if it meant another duel with Clorinde, whom he had sparred with. Neuvillette's inquiry found that Childe had fallen into the Abyss when young and unknowingly woken the All-Devouring Narwhal, but doubted this was why the machine judged him guilty, and considered that Focalors might have been using Childe to hold the Narwhal off.

## entry: Callas's Duel
@keys: Callas, Callas's duel
@keys2: duel, Clorinde, Navia, Spina di Rosula
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
Three years before the story, Callas, president of the Spina di Rosula, was accused of murdering his friend Jacques. Rather than stand trial he chose a duel against Clorinde, refused to yield and died. His daughter Navia has since sought the truth. Chapter IV points to the people behind Sinthe, whom he did not testify against, apparently to keep Navia safe. Clorinde recognised that he let himself die and accepted his last request to look after Navia.
