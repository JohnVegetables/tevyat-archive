## summary
### LITE
A four-part Sumeru world quest series from Version 3.0. The Traveler and Paimon meet a Forest Watcher named Rana, whose village's children keep vanishing and returning with no memory. After Rana is sickened by the Withering, the pair enter the dream world of the Aranara, forest spirits, and help them hold a festival and destroy an avatar of the Withering so that a Bija, a life-giving seed, can be made.
### STANDARD +
The series is a gentle story about memory, friendship and sacrifice. It introduces the Aranara, the Withering as the Aranara see it ("Marana"), Vanarana and the Fane of Ashvattha. It ends bittersweetly: the Aranara Arana gives up its memories of the Traveler and of Rana to save her, and Rana receives a Dendro Vision. No Archon Quest is required to start it.
### LARGE +
The series' title echoes the Sanskrit Aranyaka ("Book of the Forest"), texts that revolve around ritual and sacrifice, which fits a story built around a festival that ends in a sacrifice.

## plot
### LITE
Leaving Gandharva Ville, the Traveler and Paimon rescue a Forest Watcher named Rana from fungi and go with her to Vimara Village. The village's children have been vanishing and returning with no memory. After Rana is struck down by the Withering, an Aranara named Arana says she needs a Bija and sends the Traveler to Vanarana, the Aranara's dream-world village. There they win the Aranara's trust, help hold Festival Utsava, and finally destroy Marana's Avatar in Old Vanarana so a new Ashvattha tree can bear a Bija. The Bija wakes Rana.
### STANDARD +
Part I, Woodland Encounter: Rana guides the Traveler to Vimara Village, where the villagers Amadhiah and Alphonso explain the children's disappearances. The Traveler clears Withering Zones on patrol with Rana and helps thin a Spinocrocodile population, since a Forest Watcher also keeps the ecosystem in balance. The next day a boy named Iotham is chased by Eremites and then trapped in a Withering Zone in a cave. Rana rushes in despite having no Vision, falls gravely ill and vanishes, leaving traces of energy. Arana explains the cause and points the Traveler to Vanarana.

Part II, Dream Nursery: To enter Vanarana the Traveler must play the Rhythm of the Great Dream on a Vintage Lyre (obtained from Amadhiah). Arama, a Golden Nara, sets small tasks so the other Aranara lose their fear of humans, and the elder Araja explains that a Bija needs Festival Utsava, for which scattered Aranara must be gathered. The Traveler helps various Aranara in side stories (Agnihotra Sutra, An Unwavering Culinary Dream, Varuna Gatha), and Aranakula returns to tend the Vanarana nursery with Arakara. Back with Rana, Arana admits it had put the village children to sleep and removed part of their memories before returning them.

Part III, Nursery of Lost Dreams: After the festival, the Traveler and Arama take Vasoma Fruits to the weakened Fane of Ashvattha. The Ashvattha tree tells Arama to make the ultimate sacrifice, and since it is too weakened by the Withering to bear a Bija, a new tree must be planted. First Marana's Avatar, in the heart of Old Vanarana, must be destroyed.

Part IV, In the End, the Forest Will Remember: Arana, its memories wiped, takes the Bija to Rana, who wakes and invites it to travel with her.
### LARGE +
The festival: the Traveler and Paimon visit many Aranara and each gives a flower as a token of friendship; all gather to play the Rhythm of the Great Dream together, and Arama weaves the flowers into a garland. Araja reveals he cannot leave Vanarana's dream because he is the "Tree of Dreams".

In Old Vanarana Arama shields the group from the Withering and the Traveler breaks three seals: reading three Inscriptions of Remembrance, burning seven Stone Pillar Seals, and defeating a Ruin Grader that Arama must bind. A hidden space below shows no Withering, and an Aranara named Aramuhukunda, whom the Traveler had met at the festival, unlocks the final seal. Marana's Avatar repeatedly moves its soul into new Withering Zones, which the Traveler clears before destroying the core. Arana is then brought to Old Vanarana and becomes the new Ashvattha sapling while Arama dives into the ground, and a Bija fruit forms once the Rhythm of the Great Dream is played again. The Withering is not entirely gone, only kept from forming significantly for the foreseeable future.

In Part IV (the quest titled "Hello," "Thank You," and the Final "Goodbye") Arana wakes with its memories wiped, correctly deduces what happened from the gaps, and is persuaded to bring the Bija to Rana in Vimara Village even though it cannot recall her. Rana invites it to journey with her to make new precious memories and fill the "void". A light appears in her chest and she receives a Dendro Vision. She says she will tell Tighnari and hopes to meet the Traveler again, stronger.

### ULTRA +
Smaller beats. On the night in the reserve camp the Traveler and Paimon gather firewood while Rana cooks curry, and she mentions that the Aranara were once visited by another gold-haired human, which interests the Traveler. At the start of Part II Paimon worries that playing the Rhythm of the Great Dream outdoors would attract Whopperflowers, so the Traveler fetches the lyre first. Early on, the Traveler tries to coax information from the village children with stories and learns nothing. After Varuna Gatha, Aranakula plants a Vasmrti in the Vanarana nursery to grow Viparyas, and says twelve more Vasmrti sit in nurseries outside the dream world. In Part III, Aramuhukunda sees that the Traveler means to destroy Marana's Avatar and unlocks the last seal so they can face it. The Withering Zones in Old Vanarana cannot be cleared by the usual branches, so Arama destroys one whole zone directly.

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The missing children were put to sleep by Arana, who removed part of their memories before returning them; the Aranara expect children to forget them when they grow up. The Withering ("Marana") harms Rana because she lacks a Vision. The cure, a Bija, can only come from a sacrifice: the Ashvattha tree must be replaced, and the Aranara who becomes the new tree loses its memories.
### LARGE +
The Aranara fought invaders in the cataclysm, and Old Vanarana still holds ruin machines and riftwolves from that time. The identity of the "child of snow" and of the gold-haired human who once visited the Aranara is left open.

## characters
### LITE
The Traveler, Paimon, Rana, and the Aranara Arana, Arama and Araja.
### STANDARD +
Rana is a Forest Watcher without a Vision, devoted to her duty and the village. Arana is the Aranara who first helps Rana and eventually sacrifices its memories; Arama, a Golden Nara, guides the Traveler through Vanarana and Old Vanarana; Araja is the elder and the "Tree of Dreams". Amadhiah, Alphonso and the boy Iotham are villagers. Tighnari is mentioned as a fellow Forest Watcher and is told of Rana's plans. Eremites and Fatui appear briefly as enemies. Other Aranara named include Aranakula, Arakara and Aramuhukunda.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Rana is cured, receives a Dendro Vision and leaves on a journey with Arana, who has lost its memories of her. A new Ashvattha tree grows from Arama's sacrifice, and the Withering at Old Vanarana is cleared, though traces remain. The series opens the wider Aranara questline, including Varuna Gatha and the Vanarana nursery.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in Version 3.0 with Sumeru. Despite references to Archon Quest events through Chapter II and the Traveler's existing knowledge of the Fatui, no Archon Quest is required to start it.

## entry: Rana and Arana's Bija

### STANDARD
In the Aranyaka series Rana, a Forest Watcher without a Vision, is struck down by the Withering, which the Aranara call Marana. Only a Bija can cure her. The Aranara Arana gives up its memories to produce the Bija; when it wakes it no longer recognises the Traveler and Paimon, but is persuaded to carry the Bija to Rana and then travels with her.
