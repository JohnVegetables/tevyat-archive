## summary
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
Ann of the Narzissenkreuz is a three-act World Quest series in Fontaine, added in version 4.0 and shown in the Fontaine Reputation menu as "Ann's Story: Prologue". A tiny Oceanid called Ann, who says she belongs to the Narzissenkreuz Adventure Team, asks the Traveler to be her "knight" in a fairy-tale rescue of a "Princess Lyris" from the "fell dragon Narcissus". Along the way the setting stops making sense, and the series ends by showing that the underwater kingdom of Annapausis is a dream-world kept by someone else.
### STANDARD +
The acts are "The Narzissenkreuz Adventure" (two quests), "Kingdom Through the Looking-Glass" (Ann's Story) and "If She No Longer Dreams of You..." (Mary-Ann's Story). It requires The Outlander Who Caught the Wind and is followed by Initial Facts and In the Wake of Narcissus.
### LARGE +
The series is deliberately lighthearted at first and slowly turns melancholic: the adventure team's names, roles and shapes are all "storybook" assignments, and the Traveler's role in the story is to be an unplanned variable. It is the entry point to the Narzissenkreuz plotline that In the Wake of Narcissus resolves.

## plot
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Act I. Near the Court of Fontaine the Traveler and Paimon are pulled into an underwater place, Annapausis, by an Oceanid named Ann who greets the Traveler as a "good knight". She explains a story: the kingdom was conquered by the fell dragon Narcissus, Princess Lyris sleeps in a high tower, and the Narzissenkreuz Adventure Team plans a "Reconquista". The Traveler helps team members Petit Chou (a finch-shaped Oceanid) and Al (a mallard-shaped one) and meets Jak, Colonel Kate and the water-hound Mori, all of whom behave according to the story however odd their shapes look. Narcissus's "evil minions" are tainted water phantasms that the team cannot hurt, so the Traveler fights them. The team recovers the Princess's lost treasure, and then offers to give up their forms and merge into a great bubble to carry the Traveler and Ann to the top of the tower.

Act II. Beyond the tower's portal lies a library and a corridor that seems to repeat. After three loops the Traveler obtains the Holy Blade of Narzissenkreuz, which turns out to be a toy wooden sword. A "Narcissus" fight follows against an Oceanid of Loch, but the Princess is not there. Ann panics. Investigating old notes and records nearby, the Traveler and Paimon find a picture book that begins "a long time ago... there was a Princess named Lyris", and reading it transports them to "the real Annapausis".

Act III. In a sunlit garden they meet Mary-Ann, who calls Ann by name, feeds a polite talking "dog" that is not Mori, and tells Ann that there is no Princess here. The Traveler reasons that the kingdom and adventure were "a story". Mary-Ann explains that she dreamed of a small, brave Oceanid and asks Ann to live for herself and see the real world, before the room floods and the Traveler and Ann are sent away. Ann, still wanting to rescue Lyris, remembers the Narzissenkreuz Ordo and sets off for it with the Traveler and Paimon.
### LARGE +
Details of the early quests. The Narzissenkreuz Adventure begins at an underwater waypoint north-west of the Court of Fontaine. In the first quest the team's enemies are "Evil Fangs of Narcissus" and the "Four Divine Kings of Narcissus"; the last step has the Traveler fight Mori, the water-hound, after a misunderstanding (Mori calls them bad people before being calmed by Ann). Jak, who is introduced as a team member, objects when the others say "Kingdom" and mutters "Republic". In the second quest, inside Annapausis, the Traveler breaks "treasure" seals in the sky and enters the tower. A "magical fountain" is guarded by Mori, who mistakes a statue of the Princess for the real one; the real Princess is said to be in the tower.

When the tower seals are broken, Al and the others decide to give up their shapes to boost the Traveler to the top. They treat it as a return to the water and not a death: pure water can take other forms again.
### ULTRA +
In Ann's Story the corridor changes each loop (more enemies and stairs on each pass) and a hidden door to the sword room appears once triggered. The "Narcissus" fight differs from the standard boss: it uses a different moveset and can be attacked directly.

In the room of old documents the Traveler can read fragments that name Karl Ingold, Emanuel Guillotin, Basil Elton, the Institute of Natural Philosophy and Petrichor, a ship named Sponsian, and a Director who is an Oceanid. The documents point to an earlier orphanage, but this series gives no explanation.

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
- The Narzissenkreuz Kingdom, Adventure Team and "Narcissus" were all story roles. The Traveler concludes there is no Narzissenkreuz Kingdom among the seven nations.
- The Holy Blade of Narzissenkreuz in this series is a wooden toy sword. Wielding it, the young heroes could defeat all foes in their tale.
- The "real Annapausis" is a sunlit garden kept by Mary-Ann, who says the Annapausis is "our tomb" and asks Ann to live for herself.
- Ann is told her birth was "a beautiful accident"; she is a being born into the story.
- Al, Petit Chou, Mori, Jak and Colonel Kate are Oceanid-born shapes, and Mori is not the same as the "dog" Mary-Ann keeps.
### LARGE +
- Paimon realises the sealed tower still had enemies and Bubble Formations, which the Traveler reads as elements that had not been "written yet".
- The Narzissenkreuz Ordo is the next lead; Ann says she recalls it.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler, Paimon, Ann, Petit Chou, Al, Jak, Colonel Kate, Mori (the water-hound), the "Narcissus" Oceanid, and Mary-Ann. Lyris is spoken of but appears only as a statue and as the story's Princess.

## consequences
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Ann leaves Annapausis with the Traveler and Paimon. The series opens the way into Initial Facts and then In the Wake of Narcissus, where the Narzissenkreuz Ordo, Jakob and Mary-Ann are explained. In the Fontaine Reputation list the series completes the prologue of Ann's story, and its achievements include "Hardships Experienced...", "And After That..." and "Ann in Wonderland".

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in 4.0 with Fontaine. It requires The Outlander Who Caught the Wind. After Mary-Ann's Story the wiki lists Initial Facts next, which leads to In the Wake of Narcissus. Ancient Colors (Seymour's Story: Prologue) is a parallel prologue that joins in at Initial Facts.

## entry: Annapausis storybook roles
@keys: Narzissenkreuz Adventure Team, Narzissenkreuz Kingdom, Reconquista, Petit Chou
@keys2: Ann, Annapausis, Fontaine
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In the series Ann's friends act out a fairy tale. Petit Chou is a small finch-shaped Oceanid, Al a mallard-shaped one who says purpose defines essence, Jak a blue frog who mutters "Republic" when the others say "Kingdom", Colonel Kate a squirrel-like soldier, and Mori a water-hound who guards a statue of the Princess. They fight "villains" in Narcissus's name, but they are Oceanid-born and the series later shows the whole kingdom is a remembered story.

## entry: Holy Blade of Narzissenkreuz (toy sword)
@keys: Holy Blade of Narzissenkreuz, Holy Blade
@keys2: Ann, Annapausis, wooden sword
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The Holy Blade that Ann says can slay Narcissus is a wooden toy sword that children of the adventure team once played with. The Traveler obtains it in Ann's Story after the corridor loops three times.

## entry: The picture book of Lyris
@keys: picture book, Rather Aged Diary
@keys2: Ann, Lyris, Annapausis
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In Ann's Story, a picture book near the old records starts a tale of a beautiful Princess Lyris and the dragon Narcissus. Reading it aloud, Paimon and Ann trade lines; Ann interjects remarks as if she were the Princess in the story ("I don't want this name") and then the surroundings change into "the real Annapausis".
