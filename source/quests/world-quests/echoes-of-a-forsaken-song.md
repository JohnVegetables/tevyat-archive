## summary
### LITE
Echoes of a Forsaken Song is a world quest at Amsvartnir, a lake in Wavechaser Plain, Nod-Krai, added in Version 6.3. After the Lunar Conflux Barrier at the lakeshore is melted, a lyre appears; when the Traveler plays it, only the player character hears music and a sorrowful song in an unknown language that the Traveler somehow understands. Seelie lead the Traveler and Paimon up through floating islands to a hidden ruin, the Royal Court of the Seelie, where a mural completes the song and a reward chest holds a handwritten note about a woman who befriended a northern wolf named Boreas.
### STANDARD +
The quest pays 350 Adventure EXP, 40 Primogems, 30,000 Mora, 4 Hero's Wit and 4 Mystic Enhancement Ore, unlocks a Teleport Waypoint and the Palaces of Times Bygone viewpoint, and grants the achievement Farewell Hope, With Hope Farewell Fear. Only the Traveler and Paimon appear; every other voice is the song and the note.

## plot
### STANDARD
Near the centre of Amsvartnir, Paimon spots many Seelie circling above the lake and assumes treasure. Once the barrier is melted, a lyre can be played. Paimon hears nothing when she strums it and wonders if it is broken, yet the Traveler hears a desolate tune and a gentle song, and understands every word despite never having heard the language. When Paimon plucks the strings herself, the Traveler hears it again while she hears only silence.

The Seelie guide the pair upward. The Traveler rides a Moonlane to a floating island, reconnects Lakeside Prisms to break its seals, and a new verse of the song is heard at each stage. In the quest's wording, the verses move from asking why one should mourn what is lost, to a usurper on a throne of cruelty, to a golden city and the fall of the world's grandest glory, to a long decline afflicting the singer's unfortunate brethren.
### LARGE +
After the last island, the Royal Court of the Seelie appears with the Seelie from the lake gathered there. A second lyre sits around a corner and is as silent as the first. In the ruins, the Traveler reconnects a last prism and touches a mural. The final verses speak of autumn insects and a chorus of exiles, of melodies and memories lingering in a foreign land, of the last singers playing a soft final tune, and of the first Seelie wandering a hall of angels. The Seelie then enter the mural and the quest ends, revealing many chests. Paimon credits the Seelie for knowing where the best goodies hide.

The wiki's step list is: follow the Seelie upward (riding the Moonlane), break the seals on the floating island (counted 1/4 to 3/4 across two islands), investigate the nearby area, break the seal on the ruins, break the seal, and examine the mural.
### ULTRA +
Gameplay details: following the Seelie to the next island unlocks a Teleport Waypoint, and completion gives two Precious Chests and one Luxurious Chest. The wiki lists the lyre music as the soundtrack Forsaken Hymn, whose Ancient Greek lyrics (English wiki translation) speak of a song like an autumn cicada, grief and memory remaining alone in exile, and a last singer seated in her sacred place. Those themes match the quest's final verses.

## revelations
### STANDARD
- The Royal Court of the Seelie is hidden from mortal sight, and a song only the Traveler hears is the way in.
- The Luxurious Chest drops a readable note titled Ancient Handwriting. Its writer says she has sat alone in a temple that passers-by cannot see, grieving the afflictions of the land with only tiny spirits for company.
### LARGE +
The note tells how brambles sprouted and a wild beast came from the north bearing ice and snow. It spoke no human words, but gazed at her as at heavenly light and listened to her singing without anger, even when she called it a stupid dog. She named it Boreas, meaning North Wind, and let it and its kin share her dwelling. After many days it began to speak, called her Mother, and said it would go south to make poor people's cities prosperous. She let it go and called its name one last time; the note trails off with "I remember...".

The wiki treats the writer as Koitar, whose memories were wiped by the Heavenly Principles as punishment for her rebellion, and the beast as Boreas, who became the Wolf of the North in Mondstadt's Four Winds. Neither identification is spelled out in the quest text itself.
### ULTRA +
Supporting context from the wiki: Koitar's page describes her as a former Archangel of Celestia who ruled Hyperborea and regained her power and wisdom through a chance meeting with Boreas. The Royal Court page cites weapon text (Etherlight Spindlelute) describing a lake beyond ancient palace ruins, a sacred forest, and a court of the Seelie hidden from mortals by day. Amsvartnir is also described there as the lake of that court.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon are the only characters present. The singer and the writer of the note are unnamed in the quest; Boreas (Andrius) is named in the note. Seelie act as guides throughout.

## consequences
### STANDARD
The Royal Court of the Seelie becomes explorable and holds further Ancient Handwriting notes (the wiki's location page lists three). The quest unlocks a waypoint and the Palaces of Times Bygone viewpoint; nothing in Nod-Krai's main plot changes.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in Version 6.3 as a standalone world quest at Amsvartnir, with no prerequisite quest listed beyond melting the Lunar Conflux Barrier at the start location. It has no fixed place in the main story; the memory it describes belongs to the distant past; its exact date is not given.
