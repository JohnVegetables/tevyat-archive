## summary
### LITE
A Version 6.0 world quest on Paha Isle and Lempo Isle in Nod-Krai. If the active character is a Lightkeeper (Flins or Illuga), the Traveler can read the eight "Oath Lanterns" that lie abandoned around the islands. Each lantern has a line of the Lightkeepers' oath carved around its base, and each brings up the ghost of its former bearer. At the end, Flins explains what the oath is and what a masterless lantern means.
### STANDARD +
The quest has no fight and almost no dialogue until its last scene. Its worth is thematic: it shows how the Lightkeepers treat their oath and their lanterns, and how seriously Flins takes the memory of those who fell. Without a Lightkeeper active, a lantern is only "a dim lantern", described as the signature equipment of a Lightkeeper.
### LARGE +
The quest is a good way to bring the full oath into a scene. Its lines are the same ones the Ratniki recite at the oath-taking in the Nightingale's Song story, so the quest gives an early, quieter look at words that later become a rallying cry.

## plot
### LITE
With Flins or Illuga in the party, the Traveler touches an Oath Lantern and sees a line of the oath carved around its base. A ghost of a Lightkeeper appears. After the fourth lantern the rest are marked on the map. When all eight are read, Paimon suggests asking someone who knows the Lightkeepers, and they go to the Final Night Cemetery to find Flins.
### STANDARD +
Each lantern's inscription is one line of the oath. Together they run roughly: a vision of waves on the shore and a night sky devoured by silence; a declaration that whoever keeps the flame burning need not descend into that darkness; the Lightkeepers as those who shine the eternal light of hope into the endless snowy plains; a vow to use their bones and blood as fuel and make life greater than death; and lines about the winds awakening and leaves whirling, pale cheeks and burning eyes, freeing the heart from dreams of the living, and parting the snow-curtain of the sky to blast winter winds across the wasteland. The first three lanterns bring up female Lightkeeper ghosts and the later five male ghosts.

The third lantern has a hidden compartment with a yellowed note, written by a self-described humble Ratnik. The writer expects soon to be unable to speak of the lamp and asks whoever finds it to return it to the Lightkeepers.

At the cemetery, Flins greets the Traveler warmly "amidst the graves". The Traveler describes the lantern and its inscription. Flins identifies it as the oath everyone who joins the Lightkeepers swears. Each Lightkeeper normally carries a lantern of their own and gives up its care only when there is no other choice, so a masterless lantern marks a tragedy and fills him with sorrow. They knew this fate from the moment they swore. The one thing the living can still do is remember their oaths, and that was the dead's dearest wish: to bear and defend lanterns alight with eternal hope.
### LARGE +
Gameplay notes: after Flins or Illuga touches a lantern, a papakha hat, a small knife and scattered blue petals appear around it. Some lanterns sit beside Tideseal Stones. The order in which the lanterns are visited does not matter; the quest begins once four have been inspected, and the remaining four are then shown on the map. Interacting with a lantern without a Lightkeeper active gives only the generic line. Rewards: Adventure EXP, 20,000 Mora and a small amount of Adventurer's Experience.
### ULTRA +
Step list: collect the eight Lightkeepers' Oaths (eight locations, any order), then talk to Flins at the Final Night Cemetery. In the Piramida story the Ratniki recite the first three lines of the oath and Illuga recites the vow about bones, blood and fuel to open the operation, which is how the quest text connects to Wind-Stirred Ripples.

## revelations
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The quest reveals the content of the Lightkeepers' oath and that every Lightkeeper carries a lantern that stays with them for life. Flins explains the oath in his own words and shows that the order remembers its dead through their oaths.
### LARGE +
It also shows that the islands hold the lanterns of Lightkeepers who died away from home. The note in the third lantern comes from a Ratnik who expected not to return the lamp himself, which fits Flins' remark that lanterns are abandoned only when there is no choice.

## characters
### LITE
The Traveler and Paimon, with Flins as the one who explains. Illuga can be the active character instead of Flins. The lantern ghosts are unnamed.
### STANDARD +
Flins speaks in his usual courteous, slightly formal manner, but is solemn rather than playful. He treats the lanterns as memorials even though he names none of the dead.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Completing the quest gives the rewards and changes nothing else in the world. It is characterisation and world texture rather than a plot step.

## placement
### STANDARD
Introduced in Version 6.0 (Luna I), at the start of the Nod-Krai series. The wiki lists no prerequisite quest, only access to Nod-Krai and a Lightkeeper character as the active character, which means Flins or Illuga. The oath is quoted again in the Nightingale's Song world quests (6.3), so the quest can be read before or after them without conflict.

## entry: The Lightkeepers' oath
@keys: Lightkeepers' oath, Lightkeepers' Oath, Oath Lantern, Oath Lanterns
@version: 6.0
@availability: permanent
### STANDARD
The Lightkeepers' oath is a set of lines every Lightkeeper swears on joining. In the Lightkeepers' Oath quest eight abandoned lanterns each carry a line, and Flins identifies "The winds awaken, the leaves whirl round" as part of the oath. Lightkeepers normally carry their own lantern and give up its care only when forced to. A masterless lantern marks a misfortune, and remembering the oaths of those who fell is all that can still be done for them.
