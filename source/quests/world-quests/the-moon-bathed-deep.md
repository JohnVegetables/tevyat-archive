## summary
### LITE
The Moon-Bathed Deep is a Watatsumi Island world quest series from Version 2.1. Shrine maiden Tsuyuko asks the Traveler and Paimon to break the five Goshou Rock seals (Eye, Fang, Fin, Tail, Heart) that separate Watatsumi Island from Enkanomiya. The reward is the Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep, needed later to open the way down.
### STANDARD
The series has six quests: the opening The Moon-Bathed Deep and then "Eye of Watatsumi", "Fang of Watatsumi", "Fin of Watatsumi", "Tail of Watatsumi" and "Heart of Watatsumi". Tsuyuko of the Sangonomiya Shrine wants the barrier to Enkanomiya opened so that a Jade Coral Branch can be fetched for a ritual meant to halt the Holy Soil bleaching of the island. The Traveler gathers a Spirit Pearl from each of the first four seals, then opens the Heart with Tsuyuko and obtains the key. The series ends with the descent to Enkanomiya still waiting on the Divine Priestess's approval and the right spirit-vein flow, and leads into The Still Water's Flow.
### LARGE +
Tone: Tsuyuko is cheerful, self-aware about asking outsiders for help and openly sceptical of folklore, so the series mixes shrine tradition with a practical worry about Watatsumi's land. "The Moon-Bathed Deep" is also the name of the pool under the Sangonomiya Shrine that serves as the entrance to Enkanomiya, which is unlocked during The Still Water's Flow.

## plot
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
Tsuyuko at Mouun Shrine. During their travels in Inazuma the Traveler and Paimon reach Watatsumi Island. Tsuyuko, a Sangonomiya Shrine maiden, immediately asks for help, saying a divination promised that help would arrive whenever she was in trouble. The Traveler and Paimon are unimpressed by the manner of asking but hear her out.

The problem. She explains that the deep pool opposite the shrine leads to Enkanomiya, and that Watatsumi tradition holds the people's ancestors once lived there. Because the island's spirit is said to be drawn toward the depths, the soil is bleaching into Holy Soil and may one day turn to a pale desert. The remedy is a ritual using a Jade Coral Branch left by the Omikami, which means breaking the barrier and diving to Enkanomiya. Taking such a branch could weaken the suppression of the Dragonheir of the Depths, so the matter is risky.

The commission. For now Tsuyuko asks only that the Traveler visit the Goshou Rocks, five barrier nodes set by the founding clergy and the Omikami: the Eye on the island to the west, the Fang on the northwest coast, the Fin on the north coast and the Tail on the eastern shoal. The Heart is opened with the four Spirit Pearls. She admits the rite for breaking the seals is mostly forgotten, that the Divine Priestess has no time for a grand ceremony, and that an outlander is a convenient person to take the risk.

The four seals. These quests can be done in any order. Each has a nearby stone tablet whose weathered text hints at the solution.
- Eye: the tablet speaks of the Omikami's envoy with three eyes. The Traveler uses Elemental Sight to find three elemental orbs and dispels them; specters and slimes are drawn by the released energy.
- Fang: the tablet speaks of provoking battle and taking the spoils. The Traveler defends a Ley Line Monolith against five waves of hilichurls, Nobushi and Treasure Hoarders.
- Fin: the tablet speaks of two retainers greeting each other like scale and fin. The Traveler turns fish statues on discs to face each other and activates a Mysterious Pillar.
- Tail: the tablet demands slow, quiet, pious movement along the lights. The Traveler lights six Electric Lamps with an Electrogranum, then Fatui skirmishers ambush them.

The Heart. Back at the Sangonomiya Shrine, Tsuyuko joins the Traveler and Paimon. Using the four Spirit Pearls they open the seal to the Heart, a water cave where, Tsuyuko says, the Omikami once stayed and where now only the sound of water remains. The fish-statue puzzle appears on several platforms, now with Hydro Mimics emerging after each alignment and three more waves after the last. When it is solved, water gathers into the Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep.

Afterwards. Tsuyuko, briefly lost in thought, says the key is needed to open the entrance to Enkanomiya and that legend claims it was forged from the Omikami's spilled blood. They still need the Divine Priestess's approval and the right spirit-vein flow before descending. She thanks them with the lasting friendship of the shrine and says she expects to meet them again when it is time.
### LARGE +
Tsuyuko's explanations to the Traveler (extra dialogue at the shrine). She says the Goshou Rocks are named for the skeleton of an envoy of the Omikami, over which the clergy placed a seal; on the island's map the western bulge is the Eye and the south-eastern extension is the Tail, and she admits she finds the explanation hard to accept too. She also gives her own view of Holy Soil, drawn from knowledge gained at the Sumeru Akademiya: seawater encroaching on land leaves salts that leach nutrients away, while the Jade Coral Branches replenish the missing elements. Tradition says the shrine's suppression and the Omikami's power inherited by the Divine Priestess keep the Holy Soil from spreading across the rest of the island.
### ULTRA +
Gameplay details, labelled as such. The Eye, Fang, Fin and Tail are independent of each other. The Fang can be done in Co-Op mode and its monolith timer shortens with each kill. The Heart has three platforms done in any order, then a fourth with three waves of Hydro Mimics. Players note a Latin inscription, Ad Virtutem, on the seals. Completing the series grants the achievement The Same Moonlight and rewards of Primogems, Mora and Hero's Wit; the Heart also gives Mystic Enhancement Ore, Inazuma reputation and the key itself. A Precious Chest near the Heart contains furnishing blueprints.

## revelations
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
- The deep pool opposite the Sangonomiya Shrine is the entrance to Enkanomiya, and the Watatsumi people claim descent from those who lived there, in Byakuyakoku.
- The island's soil bleaching (Holy Soil) is tied in tradition to Enkanomiya drawing on the island's spirit; Tsuyuko offers a sober alternative explanation.
- The Goshou Rocks are five seal nodes, four of which yield Spirit Pearls that open the fifth.
### LARGE +
- Tsuyuko's account: Byakuyakoku survived on the faint light of the Dainichi Mikoshi against the Dragonheir of the Depths, until the Watatsumi Omikami defeated it a few thousand years ago and led the people to dry land, where Sangonomiya was founded; the underwater land was then renamed Enkanomiya.
- According to records Tsuyuko cites but does not trust, the Spirit Pearls answer someone pure and the key comes together only for a person whose heart is as clear as water.

## characters
### STANDARD
Tsuyuko, the Traveler and Paimon. The Divine Priestess (Sangonomiya Kokomi) is discussed but does not appear.
### LARGE +
Tsuyuko is courteous and rueful about imposing, with a taste for tours, folklore and digressions. Enemies met are Fatui skirmishers at the Tail and, elsewhere, hilichurls, Nobushi, Treasure Hoarders, specters, slimes and Hydro Mimics.

## consequences
### STANDARD
The Traveler holds the Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep and has earned the goodwill of the Sangonomiya Shrine. The barrier to Enkanomiya stays in place until the Divine Priestess approves and the spirit veins flow correctly. The next quest in the chapter is The Still Water's Flow.
### LARGE +
The Jade Coral Branch ritual is still pending at the end of the series.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in Version 2.1 as part of the Watatsumi Island content. It requires completing the Archon Quest A New Star Approaches and, to reach the starting location, Ritou Escape Plan. It is followed by The Still Water's Flow, which opens the way into Enkanomiya.

## entry: Goshou Rocks
@keys: Goshou Rocks, Goshou Rock, Eye of Watatsumi, Fang of Watatsumi, Fin of Watatsumi, Tail of Watatsumi, Heart of Watatsumi
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
The Goshou Rocks are five barrier nodes called the Eye, Fang, Fin, Tail and Heart, set up by the founding Sangonomiya clergy with the Omikami to separate Watatsumi Island from Enkanomiya. Four hold Spirit Pearls and the fifth opens only when all four are given. Tsuyuko says the ritual for breaking them is mostly forgotten, and the stone tablets beside each are barely legible.
### LARGE +
Per Tsuyuko the seals were placed on the skeleton of an envoy of the Omikami, which explains the body-part names. The Eye is on the island to the west, the Fang on the northwest coast, the Fin on the north coast and the Tail on the eastern shoal.

## entry: Tsuyuko
@keys: Tsuyuko
@keys2: Watatsumi Island, Sangonomiya Shrine, Enkanomiya, shrine maiden
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
A Sangonomiya Shrine maiden who used to give guided tours to visitors of Watatsumi Island. She asks the Traveler for help, is sceptical of folk legend, and mentions knowledge gained at the Sumeru Akademiya. She trusts her fortune slips and says she is sure the Traveler will return when it is time to descend to Enkanomiya. She has no separate entry in this archive; here she is the series' quest giver.

## entry: Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep
@keys: Key of the Moon-Bathed Deep, Key of the Deep, Spirit Pearl, Spirit Pearls
@keys2: Enkanomiya, Tsuyuko, Watatsumi
@version: 2.1
### STANDARD
The key forms from water in the Heart of Watatsumi after the final seal is broken, and is needed to open the entrance to Enkanomiya. Tsuyuko says legend has it forged from the Omikami's spilled blood, though she does not usually believe such records. The Spirit Pearls are the four items from the Eye, Fang, Fin and Tail seals that open the Heart.
