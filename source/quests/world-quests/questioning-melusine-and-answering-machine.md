## summary
@spoiler: none
### LITE
Questioning Melusine and Answering Machine is a three-act world quest series in Fontaine, added in version 4.2. The steambird mascot Sir Arthur has vanished from the office of the newspaper The Steambird. The Traveler and Paimon help the Melusine detective Talochard, of the Marechaussee Phantom, and her investigation mek Curve to find him.
### STANDARD +
The series is a mystery with a comic start and a bittersweet ending. Its acts are "Vagrants and Scamps", "The Red and the Black" and "Questions and Answers". It requires the story quest "A Fontainian Message" to be complete.
### LARGE +
The missing bird leads to the editors of The Steambird, a gang of con artists, a Fatui intelligence officer and the legacy of the scientist Mosso Lombroso. The main emotional thread is the bond between Talochard and Curve.

## plot
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Outside The Steambird's office the Traveler and Paimon settle an argument between the Melusine agent Talochard and the editor Jenck. Sir Arthur, the steambird, is missing. Euphrasie, the editor-in-chief, arrives with Curve, a Suppression Specialist Mek built by the scientist Mosso Lombroso, which the Marechaussee Phantom lets Talochard use for investigations. Clues lead from a drunk named Rocher to a boy, Kevin, and to a con-artist gang, the Elegant Tableware Confrerie, then to a Fatui officer who was hunting for Mosso's old research base and a threatening letter demanding Mosso's name be cleared.

The case turns out to hinge on a thirty-year-old scandal. Melo Lombroso, Mosso's granddaughter, reveals that Mosso staged his own disgrace to save Curve. A map hidden in a locket leads to Mosso's underwater base, where Jenck, who allied with the Fatui to take Euphrasie's post, triggers its self-destruction. Curve tells his first lie to get the others out and stays behind.
### LARGE +
**Act I - Vagrants and Scamps.** Outside the offices of The Steambird the Traveler and Paimon settle an argument between Talochard, a Melusine of the Marechaussee Phantom, and the editor Jenck. Sir Arthur, the steambird, has gone missing. The editor-in-chief Euphrasie arrives with Curve, a Suppression Specialist Mek built by the scientist Mosso Lombroso, which belongs to the Marechaussee Phantom and which Talochard uses for special investigations. From clues at the scene the group draws up four suspects, with the drinker Rocher the strongest lead. They defeat the debt collectors who chase him, and find he did fall asleep by the office entrance but only saw a small figure running away. That figure was a troublesome boy, Kevin, who took a knife left at Sir Arthur's pedestal when a drunk man startled him. The knife is traced to the Elegant Tableware Confrerie, a group of con artists.

**Act II - The Red and the Black.** The group tracks the Confrerie and catches them by feeding them Talochard's unusual Poissonchant Pie. Their member "Intrepid Valberry" admits he left the knife as a warning after The Steambird exposed the Confrerie, and he sent for a locksmith at Fleuve Cendre, where he met a Fatui officer, Baulard, who wanted the same thing. Baulard is a peripheral intelligence officer tasked with finding Mosso Lombroso's research base. He had fed the paper intelligence on the Confrerie, and Jenck would not repay him with Steambird information about Mosso, so he tried to break in. At the office he found Sir Arthur gone and a letter signed "Crow or Blackbird?" demanding that The Steambird clear Mosso of his crimes. After dinner at Hotel Debord the group reconstructs the order of the visitors that night. The story behind the letter is an old one: thirty years ago The Steambird reported that Mosso had faked evidence for his lie-detector technology, and he was arrested and died in a duel rather than stand trial.

**Act III - Questions and Answers.** A second letter says Sir Arthur has been dismembered and gives three locations for the parts, saying they will be buried with the treasure in Mosso's base. Euphrasie suspects Mosso's granddaughter Melo Lombroso. The Traveler recovers the parts from three sites. At the last, Talochard, Curve and Kevin's mother Madeleine are fighting the Fatui. Madeleine and Kevin's father are both Fatui, and the father is Baulard, whose real name is Baulande. The group persuades him to hand over Sir Arthur's head, and he leaves with his family to escape the Fatui. Rocher reveals he is Melo's husband and denies she is behind the letters.

Melo has an alibi and denies knowing where Mosso's base is, but she reveals that the sensational articles about Mosso were submitted by Mosso himself. When Fontainians rioted over lie-detector technology being developed in Fontaine, he drew all attention to himself to save Curve, whom he saw as the culmination of his work. The Marechaussee Phantom took Curve in as evidence and left him in storage for thirty years until Talochard found him. A locket with a childhood photo of Melo reveals a map when held to the sunlight, leading the Traveler, Paimon, Talochard and Curve to Mosso's underwater research base and a letter from Mosso to the former editor-in-chief.

There Jenck and the Fatui arrive. Jenck is the mastermind: he allied with the Fatui to displace Euphrasie as editor-in-chief. He triggers the base's self-destruct. Curve says he can stop it if the Traveler removes his core and places it in the control panel, but doing so only opens the doors to the emergency room, and he stays behind. The Traveler, Paimon and Talochard escape as the base is destroyed. Curve had told his first lie, knowingly sacrificing himself.

**Clues and suspects (Act I).** The Traveler arrives as Talochard and Jenck argue outside the office, and Euphrasie turns up with Curve afterwards. The clues at the scene yield four suspects; the most reliable lead is Rocher, whose used wine bottle was left near the entrance, and the leads that follow include the boy Kevin and the Confrerie. Rocher's dog, called Lombroso, identifies his master's bottle. Kevin's mother Madeleine says she saw Kevin carrying a knife after he came back from the office, matching the knife found at Sir Arthur's pedestal. Kevin explains that his father's absence has made him act out.

**The reconstruction (Act II).** At Hotel Debord, after Talochard has tried to give up, the group acts out the scene in order. Rocher fell asleep first. Baulard came second, found the letter and the missing bird, and took the letter with him, which is why nobody at The Steambird knew about the demands. "Intrepid Valberry" came third and left the knife as a warning. Kevin came last and woke Rocher, grabbing the knife in panic. The group also learns that a "Boss" and "Intrepid Valberry" fight over the Confrerie's money.

**The case behind the case (Act III).** Kevin has turned over a new leaf by now and helps find Sir Arthur's wing with a dog, Baulande, who looks like Rocher's dog. Rocher discovers that the dog he has been calling Lombroso actually belongs to Kevin. At the second site "Intrepid Valberry" and "Boss" quarrel over treasure while Rocher begs for the part in the chest, and they agree to hand Sir Arthur's body over to the Traveler.

**Mosso's base and aftermath.** Jenck is found a few days later kneeling on the stage of the Opera Epiclese, apparently insane and with his tongue cut out, with a note apologising to the Traveler, Paimon and Talochard. The Traveler wonders if the Fatui Harbinger responsible is interested in machine intelligence. Talochard is anguished by Curve's loss and seeks revenge on Jenck, but the three cannot find him. Euphrasie adds a recorded commendation to the rebuilt Sir Arthur's voice library: Curve's analysis that Talochard would be the best agent in the Marechaussee Phantom.
### ULTRA +
**Talochard's character in the case.** Talochard is a Melusine agent who works for the Marechaussee Phantom on her own initiative on this case and blames herself for failing to identify a suspect. After the Confrerie episode she suddenly wants to give up and offers to take the Traveler and Paimon to dinner at Hotel Debord. She had rediscovered Curve in storage and been using him for special investigations.

**Mosso Lombroso's plan.** Melo says Mosso sacrificed his name to protect his work, and Curve was kept alive as the proof of it. The tale of 'falsified evidence' was therefore his own doing, published by The Steambird at his request. The letter from Mosso to the former editor-in-chief, found at the base, sets out the truth about the incident.

**Gameplay note (labelled as such).** The investigation involves gathering clues, a scene reconstruction at the end of Act II, and fights against the Confrerie's debt collectors and the Fatui. These do not change the lore.

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
- Sir Arthur's disappearance was not a random prank but connected to an old scandal: the thirty-year-old story that Mosso Lombroso had faked evidence for his lie-detector technology.
- That scandal was staged by Mosso himself. He had The Steambird publish the articles to draw public anger away from Curve and died in a duel instead of standing trial.
- Curve, the Marechaussee Phantom's investigation mek, sat in storage for thirty years before Talochard found him. His first lie lets the Traveler, Paimon and Talochard escape.
### LARGE +
- The Fatui officer Baulard, whose real name is Baulande, was hunting for Mosso's research base on behalf of a Harbinger, and his wife Madeleine, also a Fatui member, and son Kevin were living in Fontaine.
- Jenck, an editor of The Steambird, allied with the Fatui to take Euphrasie's post as editor-in-chief and was the real mastermind. He is later found mentally broken at the Opera Epiclese.
- Rocher is Melo Lombroso's husband.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon; Talochard, a Melusine of the Marechaussee Phantom; Curve, a Suppression Specialist Mek; Euphrasie, the editor-in-chief of The Steambird, and Jenck, an editor; the drinker Rocher and his dog; the boy Kevin and his mother Madeleine; the con artists "Intrepid Valberry" and "Boss"; the Fatui Baulard/Baulande; and Melo Lombroso, Mosso's granddaughter. Mosso Lombroso appears through letters and recollection. Sir Arthur is the missing steambird.
### LARGE +
Talochard blames herself when the case stalls and is grief-stricken at the end. Curve's single lie, described by the quest as his first, is the key moment of the series. Jenck's motive is to replace Euphrasie as editor-in-chief. Baulande leaves with his family to escape the Fatui.

## consequences
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Sir Arthur is rebuilt, with Curve's recorded commendation of Talochard added to its voice library. Curve is lost with the research base. Jenck is found ruined. Baulande and his family leave to escape the Fatui.
### LARGE +
The truth about Mosso's staged downfall is learned by the Traveler, Talochard, Euphrasie and Melo. The wider public story is not shown changing.

## placement
### STANDARD
Requires "A Fontainian Message". Released in version 4.2. Acts I to III are played in order over several in-game days.

### STANDARD
Talochard is a Melusine agent attached to the Marechaussee Phantom who takes up the case of the missing steambird on her own account. She works with the investigation mek Curve. After Curve's sacrifice she is grief-stricken and wants revenge on Jenck. Euphrasie later shows her a recording of Curve naming her the best agent in the Marechaussee Phantom.

### STANDARD
Curve is a Suppression Specialist Mek built by the scientist Mosso Lombroso. Mosso's research was on lie-detector technology.
