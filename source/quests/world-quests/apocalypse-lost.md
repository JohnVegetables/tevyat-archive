## summary
@spoiler: major
### LITE
Apocalypse Lost is a world quest series in the Desert of Hadramaveth, Sumeru, released in version 3.4. It has a single quest, Memories of Gurabad. Once the Traveler has placed all five chess pieces on the chessboard at Safhe Shatranj, the jinni Liloupar leads them through a vision of ancient Gurabad. She tells her own story of ruining her husband's line, and then goes into the city, leaving the Traveler her bottle.
### STANDARD +
The series is the end of the arc that began with Liloupar becoming the Traveler's jinni in The Dirge of Bilqis. The quest is an act of farewell: Liloupar says she wants the Traveler to witness her past and what she learned, and Paimon senses that it is a goodbye. It is followed in the desert by Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters....
### LARGE +
Much of the quest's text is shaped by the Book of Nahum, which speaks of the fall of a tyrannical city. Quotations from it appear in the vision of Gurabad; this is a literary echo, not a plot element.

## plot
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
After The Dirge of Bilqis and The Fallen Falcon, the Traveler finds the five chess pieces hidden around the desert and places them on the chessboard at Safhe Shatranj. Liloupar warns of tremors under the ground, then calls them a drill, and leads the Traveler and Paimon into a training field where primal constructs attack. She then asks the Traveler to hold her bottle against a chess piece; a vision of a great, cold city swallowed by the earth follows, and she says she remembers Gurabad.

The chess piece carries them to a valley, where Liloupar tears down a wall of the cave to reveal Gurabad. After they fight more constructs, a shard of her soul joins the bottle and the Traveler sees how Gurabad fell. Liloupar explains, in her own telling, that she once loved Ormazd, a shepherd who became a king, and then punished him by ruining his line across three generations. She says she inspired her son Parvezravan to build Jinn-driven machines, which brought wealth but ruined workers and farmers, and that his ambition led him to force his sister-queen Shirin to create mindless Jinn. Shiruyeh, the brass-masked Khorramdin, led the poor and slaves in revolt and was, she says, a charlatan. The city fell into a chasm.

She tells the Traveler that darkness is rising again in Gurabad, that she must face what she began, and that she is leaving. She gives the Traveler a last gift, whispering ancient verses into their ear, and her bottle goes quiet. Paimon wonders if they will see her again.
### LARGE
**Preparation.** The wiki lists five chess pieces: King Deshret's Pillar, Border Fort, Soulferry, Khaj-Nisut and Temple of Gurabad. Four are obtained during The Dirge of Bilqis, and the fifth, the Temple of Gurabad piece, while progressing through "The Falcon's Hunt" (as the wiki records). They must be put in the glowing platforms at Safhe Shatranj, in the Hidden Exploration Objective Chessboard of Safhe Shatranj, to begin Memories of Gurabad.

**Tremors and the first vision.** Liloupar senses tremors from deep underground, jokes that it was only a drill, and Paimon is not amused. After the first fight with primal constructs she says Gurabad is near. She jokes that a slot exists on her bottle for the chess piece, then admits she invented it and tells her master to simply hold her to it. A vision follows of a great cold city whose gate sits in the mountain wall. The Traveler's head hurts and Liloupar says "That's Gurabad over there. I remember it now." She promises Paimon food and Mora as thanks and says the Traveler will be given back.

**Why Gurabad.** She says she does not fully know why she must go, but wants the Traveler to witness her past and the lessons she learned. She describes the place as holding the memories of ghosts, broken dreams and the aftertaste of revenge.

**At Gurabad.** The chess piece teleports the party, a little off-target, to the first valley. Liloupar reveals Gurabad and asks the Traveler to clear new "tenants", the primal constructs. When the last wave is down, the shard of her soul merges with the bottle. The Traveler sees the brass-masked man speak, quoting the Book of Nahum, and then the city overwhelmed. Pomegranates burst and trampled, a red flood, soldiers' weapons and machines spilling Jinn without minds, the brass-masked king fleeing into Shirin's arms while she looks old and dim, and King Kisra turned into a dark monster. Liloupar says they are all her flesh and blood.

**Her account.** Ormazd was once her lover, and through her love he rose from shepherd to king. She believed he was more than mortal, but vanity and selfishness took him in the end. She punished him by handing down ruin from father to son across three generations. Her son Parvezravan wanted unlimited power, so she designed him machines run by the remains of Jinn spirits and inspired in a dream a colossal machine that swallowed and spat out manufactured goods. They brought wealth but robbed laborers of their lives and farmers of their property. The rich and lords wept in joy at the end of slavery, but she says this began an even crueller servitude. Poverty bred hatred and ambition; riches bred greed and ambition. Parvezravan forced Shirin, his sister and queen, to create misshapen Jinn without minds, and Liloupar says her suffering pleased and empowered her. Through this she fostered the flesh and blood of Shiruyeh, the brass-masked Khorramdin, whom the poor saw as an emissary of her mistress and a savior. He led them to justice and to death, and the earth swallowed city and followers alike. She says she did nothing wrong, but her children Shirin and Shiruyeh remain there, lonely.

**Farewell.** The power of darkness is growing in Gurabad and, she says, only a mother's power can face it. She thought she could use the dark abyss to punish what she hated, but it will now be her fate to go in and face retribution. She tells the Traveler a jinni chooses her master, and that their bond will not be easily severed. She whispers into the bottle, the Traveler comes to understand the ancient verses she recites, and then the bottle dims and falls silent.

### ULTRA +
Gameplay and labelled mechanics: after the quest, the viewpoint at Safhe Shatranj opens, the previously unreadable Nameless Tablets in the desert ruins become readable, and the jinni's bottle still gives the same gadget benefits though Liloupar has left it, according to the wiki. These are game notes.

Details in the vision: the brass-masked man's words are taken from the Book of Nahum and a flood of pomegranate juice is described turning the city's reservoir into a red sea that drowns the slaves first and then their masters. The text calls the juice the "joy of life and the joy of vengeance".

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
- Liloupar, in her own telling, was the lover of Ormazd, mother of Parvezravan and Shirin, and the force behind the fall of his line.
- Gurabad's Jinn-driven machines were her gift to Parvezravan; the Jinn were created through Shirin's suffering, and Shiruyeh, the brass-masked king, led a revolt that ended with the city falling into a chasm.
- Liloupar's reason for returning to Gurabad is to face the darkness she says she once set on the city and to see her children.
### LARGE +
- Gurabad was a city of slave labour that, in her account, became industrial through Jinn machines, a change she says worsened the poor's condition.
- Liloupar says she can be bound only by her own choice: the jinni chooses her master.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler, Paimon and Liloupar. Historical figures discussed: Ormazd, Parvezravan (called Kisra in the vision), Shirin of Gurabad, Shiruyeh (Khorramdin) and Nabu Malikata (her mistress).

## consequences
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Liloupar leaves her bottle and goes into Gurabad. The Traveler learns her account of the city's fall. The desert tablets become readable and Safhe Shatranj becomes a viewpoint.
### LARGE +
The wiki's Liloupar article records later events in other quests, including her emerging again from a dormancy. Those events are outside this quest and are not summarised here.

## placement
### STANDARD
Requires The Dirge of Bilqis and The Fallen Falcon. The wiki lists the series after The Dirge of Bilqis and before Her Foes Rage Like Great Waters.... Released in 3.4. Despite being displayed as a chapter, it contains one quest.

## entry: Gurabad
@keys: Gurabad
@keys2: Liloupar, Parvezravan, Shirin, Shiruyeh, Memories of Gurabad
@version: 3.4
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In Liloupar's telling Gurabad was a great city of slave labour that became a city of Jinn-driven machines. It fell when the earth opened beneath it after a revolt led by Shiruyeh and after the darkness she had unleashed. The quest shows it as a ruined, cold city swallowed by a chasm that lies behind a wall in a desert cave. The vision and Liloupar's account do not agree with any other source on all points.
