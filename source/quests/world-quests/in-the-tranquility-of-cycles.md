## summary
### LITE
In the Tranquility of Cycles is a short 7.0 world quest in Snezhnaya's far north. It ends the story of Chulkaturin, an ancient fae who lives in a wooden cabin built on a floe of ice and refuses to set foot on Snezhnayan land. When the Traveler and Paimon return, the ice is melting and the cabin is about to sink. He declines to come ashore, thanks them, asks to be left alone, and the house sinks as the ice melts.
### STANDARD +
The quest is the third part of a chain begun in Ever Beneath the Waves and continued in The Sunken Ship Sailed Past, Ringing No Bells. Completing it grants the achievement "The Cherry Orchard Has Been Sold" (per the wiki).

## plot
### LITE
The Traveler finds Chulkaturin's cabin tilted toward the sea. Paimon calls to him; he answers from inside and agrees that the ice is going, but will not leave. Rather than argue, the Traveler can try to persuade him, offer to pull him ashore or suggest rebuilding on ice, and he rejects each. He asks them to stop breaking the ice, stop towing the house and let him go, then asks for a last goodbye. The house makes no further answer.
### STANDARD +
In the earlier quests the Traveler cleared brash ice and towed the cabin toward shore in Ever Beneath the Waves, and in The Sunken Ship Sailed Past, Ringing No Bells helped him reach crates of materials so he could expand and reinforce it. Chulkaturin says he overloaded the floe himself in a wish to restore a measure of his people's former glory and dignity.

The cabin, he says, bears all his history and is his last place. Leaving would cut him from his roots; a new house would not be built from the snow poplars he grew up alongside, and new ice would not bear the weight of the past. He fears that on land, where the future is unpredictable, few of his choices would still be his own. He asks the Traveler for a last farewell instead of rescue.
### LARGE +
At the close, the house gives no more answer and sinks quietly. For a long while the Traveler hears only a strange song hummed in an old, complex tongue, perhaps about snow poplars not yet felled and glories untarnished; it fades like ice melting into foam. The text ends: the ice melts away.

## revelations
### STANDARD
Chulkaturin's earlier statements stand: he calls himself the last scion of old snowfield-dwelling nobility, a relic of an era that "still belonged to the fae", uprooted from the land over three hundred years ago, whose snow poplars were all cut down. This quest adds that he sees the past as inseparable from his life and that he would need to atone for it before he could start a new one. What he must atone for is not explained.

## characters
### LITE
Chulkaturin, an old fae speaking from inside the cabin; the Traveler and Paimon. Chulkaturin addresses them as "shorebound travelers" and finally as friends.
### STANDARD +
His voice is formal, archaic and gentle. He thanks them repeatedly and never argues angrily. Paimon is the one who panics, pleads and offers practical alternatives.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Chulkaturin and his house are gone from the ice. The Traveler's choice here is limited to how to say goodbye (the options in the wiki text are to plead, offer to help, or accept) and does not change the outcome.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in 7.0 as an ordinary open-world quest in northern Snezhnaya, available after completing The Sunken Ship Sailed Past, Ringing No Bells. It has no link to the Archon Quest.
### ULTRA +
For roleplay: Chulkaturin's refusal is a considered, calm choice, not despair spoken in anger; it is a quiet counterpart to other Snezhnaya stories about the weight of the past. Do not invent a name for the nobles' lost kingdom or a cause for its fall, because none is given.

### STANDARD
Chulkaturin is an old fae who has lived 321 years in a wooden cabin on a floe, built from felled snow poplars, never setting foot on Snezhnayan land. He calls himself the last scion of old snowfield-dwelling nobility. He speaks in a courtly, archaic manner and addresses the Traveler and Paimon as "travelers by the shore". He asks them not to save him in the end and sinks with his home.
