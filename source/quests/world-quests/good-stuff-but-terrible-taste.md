## summary
### LITE
Good Stuff, but Terrible Taste is a comic Fontaine world quest series added in 4.0. Whenever the Traveler and Paimon solve certain odd puzzles in the wild, a child named Salsa appears and insists the objects are traps of an "evil organization" (the Adventurers' Guild) that spoil the view. She directs them to rearrange the pieces so they fit the landscape. A sixth quest reveals the culprit: an adventurer and self-styled installation artist named Ubu.
### STANDARD
The series is five regional quests (Belleau, Court of Fontaine, Central Beryl, Southeast Beryl and West Beryl) that can be done in any order, plus a follow-up, Good Stuff, but Terrible Taste - Continued, that unlocks after all five. Each regional quest starts by opening a chest after a nearby Water Volume Detection Crystal puzzle. The Traveler then moves one object under Salsa's direction to a spot that follows the natural lines of the scenery. The tone is gentle comedy about taste, art and a child's earnest, slightly muddled view of the adult world. Each quest gives Adventure EXP, 20 Primogems and Mora, and the regional ones also open a scenic viewpoint.
### LARGE +
Across the five meetings Salsa gradually reveals herself: a painter-in-training whose parents work at the Fontaine Research Institute and are rarely home, who pretends to study for them, who forgets homework, and who treats her sketching trips as serious work. She names the Traveler and Paimon the first two members of an alliance she founds, and in the Continued quest she confronts Ubu over whether his work counts as art.
### ULTRA +
The wiki gives the requirement for every quest in the series as the first Fontaine Archon Quest, The Outlander Who Caught the Wind, so it can be started early in the region. The series name is a wiki label rather than an official umbrella title.

## plot
@version: 4.0
### STANDARD
Opening. In the first meeting, Paimon wonders why a puzzle looks so strange and decides the Adventurers' Guild uses such puzzles to train rookies. A girl named Salsa interrupts, introduces herself as the future of Fontaine's art scene, and, hearing that the Traveler and Paimon are adventurers, fears they belong to the "evil organization" in green that has spoiled the scenery. When they offer help she recruits them to rearrange the objects.

The task. The camera moves to a fixed overhead view. Salsa explains that nature has its own flow of lines, for example the pattern of the flower bushes, and that the ugly objects look wrong because they break it. The Traveler lifts the object, tries a few spots (Salsa notes when a placement is no better than before) and puts it on a natural line. Salsa is delighted and speaks about points, lines and planes being in harmony.

Salsa across the meetings. She leaves hurriedly to get home before her parents return and says she pretends to study when they are back. In a later meeting she frets that she has a compulsion disorder she read about in a book, and Paimon and the Traveler suggest a doctor. In another she admits she had forgotten her homework and the interruption spared her a scolding. In the last she asks whether the battle will ever end, then founds the "Alliance of Artists to Overthrow the Evil Organization and Protect the Future of Fontaine", naming herself number zero and the Traveler and Paimon members one and two. In one meeting she also says she complained to Katheryne at the Guild and got only the stock phrase "ad astra abyssosque".
### LARGE +
Continued. After all five are finished, a Hydro Elemental Monument sunk in the ground north of Echoes of the Deep Tides becomes a puzzle. Paimon, now fluent in Salsa's rules, has the Traveler lift it. The owner appears and accuses them of robbery: he is Ubu, a Guild adventurer and installation artist who made the objects and is angry that his "masterpieces" were rearranged. Salsa arrives and declares, as founder of her alliance, that she does not recognise them as art. Ubu argues that the artist defines what art is, and Salsa replies that an artist needs works people recognise. Paimon and the Traveler side with Salsa, noting that if no one understands his work it counts as a failure. Ubu leaves in distress, vowing not to give up. Salsa stays on, saying she finished her homework and will linger before going home. She remains in the area until the next daily reset, and the quest awards an achievement (Aesthetics of Ugliness).
### ULTRA +
Viewpoints. The five regional quests open, respectively, View From Mont Automnequi (Belleau), "Memories of Mont Esus" (Court of Fontaine), Fontaine Hot Springs (Central Beryl), The Seaside Village (Southeast Beryl) and Morning in the Beryl Mountains, Clear Weather (West Beryl). Which of the five meeting scripts plays depends on how many have already been completed, not on the region.

## revelations
@version: 4.0
### STANDARD
- The strange objects are not traps or Guild training aids: Ubu, an adventurer, made them as installation art.
- Salsa's parents work at the Fontaine Research Institute and are often away; Salsa says that is why she can slip out to sketch.
### LARGE +
- Salsa regards the Guild as an "evil organization" and its uniformed members as "evil green guys"; she is a child and the framing is comic. The Traveler is a Guild member but is exempted as a friend who "abandoned evil ways".
- Ubu insists the boundaries of art are set by the artist, a position the Traveler, Paimon and Salsa all reject.

## characters
### STANDARD
Salsa is a quest-only child NPC, a would-be painter who describes herself as a great artist and insists she is not a small child; she calls Paimon "Lil' Miss". The wiki describes her as light-skinned with long blonde hair and purple eyes. Ubu is a quest-only adventurer in the Adventurers' Guild who describes himself as an installation artist and is touchy about criticism. Paimon and the Traveler appear throughout; Katheryne is mentioned only.
### LARGE +
Salsa's parents are never seen. She quotes her father, who uses sayings such as that there is no trash that cannot be saved, only resources in the wrong place, and says he once forgot paperwork and was embarrassed for a week.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Each quest gives Adventure EXP, Primogems and Mora, and the five regional ones unlock viewpoints. The Continued quest ends the series with the Hydro Monument raised and the argument between Salsa and Ubu unresolved. Neither Ubu's vow nor Salsa's alliance is followed up in the material read.
### LARGE +
Salsa remains near the Hydro Monument until the next daily reset after the finale and can be spoken to, mentioning prospective alliance members she will vet.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in 4.0 with the Fontaine launch as optional world-quest content. The wiki gives the Archon Quest The Outlander Who Caught the Wind as the requirement, and the Continued quest follows the five regional ones. It has no bearing on the main plot.
