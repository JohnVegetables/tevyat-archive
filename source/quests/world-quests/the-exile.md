## summary
@spoiler: none
### LITE
The Exile is a short two-part world quest series in the Great Red Sand, Sumeru, added in version 3.1. In Aaru Village a lonely woman named Sabbah is trying to grow Sumeru Roses in sand where they should not thrive. The Traveler feeds her plants with Dendro until they sprout and then bloom.
### STANDARD +
The two quests are The Exile: Sprouting and The Exile: Blooming. They are a gentle character piece rather than an adventure: most of the content is conversation, and Sabbah gradually reveals that she is a former researcher exiled from the Akademiya. It leads directly into the longer A Gifted Rose series, which lists it as a prerequisite.
### LARGE +
The wiki's own series summary is empty, so the account here is drawn from the two quests' dialogue. No fighting is involved; the quests turn on patience (Blooming has to wait for the next in-game day) and on Sabbah's slowly growing trust in the visitors.

## plot
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Sprouting. In Aaru Village the Traveler and Paimon hear a boy, Ziyad, singing a local folk song about a Golden Rose that blooms from stone, before he runs off. A woman named Sabbah welcomes them, mistakes them for foreign scholars, and explains the song: in her view the wish-granting flower reflects how people in a harsh desert prize plants that store water. She admits that Sumeru Roses planted in the village sand never grow, and asks the Traveler, as an adventurer, for highly concentrated Dendro, since she has no Vision to make fertilizer herself. The Traveler applies Dendro and the seeds sprout leaves but do not flower. Sabbah says that is normal and asks them to come back.

Blooming. On a later day Sabbah offers to share Butter Chicken she has made. Her sprouts are growing slowly, so the Traveler uses Dendro on them again. The sprouts open into Sumeru Roses. Sabbah gives one to the Traveler and Paimon, explains that she studied how geography shaped the evolution of roses, and asks them to visit and bring Sumeru Roses when they can.
### LARGE +
Details worth keeping in a scene. In Sprouting, Sabbah guesses the party are scholars studying abroad and laughs warmly (not mockingly) at Paimon's claim to be a "world-trotting" duo; she says such cheerful talk is a surprise to someone as cut off as she is. She talks about plants and oral songs at length, and Paimon admits she lost the thread somewhere around "symbiotic relationship".

After the sprouting she lets slip that she is not from the village. If asked, she says she could once have been called a researcher but no longer can, that she said something beyond her station and learned something she should not have, that she broke the Akademiya's taboos and was removed from their ranks, and that curiosity is the impetus for wisdom but also the cause of sin, a lesson she took only after being exiled. For safety she will not say more. She still wants to see roses the way she did in her former home, where she grew many.

In Blooming she says she keeps the soil moist and that the sprouts may be dormant because the soil is not fertile enough. Optional dialogue has her remark that the Traveler has no Vision yet controls Dendro, which she says some people can do but that such people are of ill repute, and she tells the Traveler not to stray onto the wrong path. Using Nahida's All Schemes to Know on her reveals the thought that she once planted flowers at the Akademiya with other scholars and wonders how they are. She says she feels at ease because the Traveler never dismissed her talk as nonsense.
### ULTRA +
Her research interest, as stated in Blooming, was how changes in the environment changed the form of roses over time; she imagines roses that climb walls like ivy, grow in clusters, or come in white, red and gold, and notes that few people discover or develop new species any more. She says she does not think a researcher's interest should be cultivated for research alone; she grows roses because she loves the world and wants to understand how plants live. She promises small gifts later.

Gameplay note, labelled as such: each quest asks the player to use Dendro on the plants, and Blooming only becomes available after the daily reset.

## revelations
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
- Sabbah is an exile from the Akademiya who lives in Aaru Village and speaks in a refined way, which Paimon compares to the researchers she has met.
- She claims she was removed from the Akademiya's ranks for breaking its taboos, but gives no details.
- She believes Sumeru Roses are poorly suited to desert sand, yet they grow there when fed with strong Dendro.
### LARGE +
- The Golden Rose in the folk song, which Sabbah thinks of as an imagined plant, is picked up again in A Gifted Rose, where it is tied to a real earlier exile.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon; Sabbah, a former researcher living alone in Aaru Village; and Ziyad, a boy who appears at the start and sings the Singing Rose folk song. Nahida's All Schemes to Know can be used on Sabbah for her private thoughts.

## consequences
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Sumeru Roses bloom in Aaru Village sand, Sabbah has a first friend in the Traveler, and the Traveler is given a Sumeru Rose. The series leads into A Gifted Rose, in which Sabbah and the Traveler meet Affan.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in 3.1 in the Great Red Sand, Sumeru; the first quest has no stated prerequisite. A Gifted Rose lists The Exile as its prerequisite. The two parts must be done on separate days.

## entry: Sabbah
@keys: Sabbah
@keys2: Aaru Village, Sumeru Rose, exile, exiled
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Sabbah is a quest NPC in Aaru Village, an exile from the Akademiya who loves roses and plants. She is polite and a little formal, apologises easily, and starts talking at length about plants, history and folk songs when a listener lets her. She says she has few friends in the village. She gets a longer role in A Gifted Rose.
### LARGE +
She says she broke Akademiya taboos and refuses to explain. She still wants to see the roses she once grew, and she values being listened to.

## entry: Singing Rose folk song
@keys: Singing Rose, Golden Rose song, rose folk song
@keys2: Aaru Village, Ziyad, Sabbah
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
A folk song of Aaru Village heard at the start of The Exile: Sprouting, about a rose that grants wishes and a Golden Rose that sings from stone. Paimon names it the "Singing Rose". Sabbah says the village has no great library and passes its songs on orally, and she reads the song as the collective imagination of ancestors who prized water-storing plants.
