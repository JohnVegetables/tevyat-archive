## summary
@spoiler: none
### LITE
Pale Fire is a world quest series in the Girdle of the Sands, Sumeru, added in version 3.6. Strange circular trees called Fravashi Trees hold the lingering remains of Pari who could not return home. With the Pari Sorush's help the Traveler awakens ten of them, collects the Plumes of Purifying Light they leave, and has the elder Zurvan explain what they are.
### STANDARD +
The series has a main quest, Pale Fire, and ten separate quests, one per tree, all named Awaken the Residual Pari in the Fravashi Trees, which can be done in any order. Doing any one of them unlocks Pale Fire, and completing all ten finishes it. The Traveler is formally addressed as Sorush's Yasnapati, and Zurvan asks them to help the Pari that have not yet returned.
### LARGE +
The wiki's series summary is empty, so the account below comes from the Pale Fire quest and a sample Fravashi Tree quest. The Fravashi Trees quests share their dialogue structure, so what is described for one is a reasonable guide to the rest, though that is an inference rather than something checked tree by tree.

## plot
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
At each Fravashi Tree, a ring-shaped tree that Paimon at first takes for a door, Sorush speaks with scorn for the sight of her own kind clinging on. She explains that these trees are Pari who fought to the end against the defilement corrupting the land and, with their last strength, turned into trees; being tainted, they were rejected by the Vourukasha Oasis. Paimon insists the Traveler cannot leave them to suffer alone. Sorush wakes the residual Pari with her power. It lashes out at hidden enemies and the Traveler must defeat the monsters that appear (two waves in the example read, hilichurls and Abyss mages with mitachurl support). A Plume of Purifying Light floats out of the tree, the obsession within cleared away.

After the first tree, Pale Fire starts. If the Traveler has not yet done The Splendorous Sky That Day, a pop-up advises learning more about the Pari. The Traveler brings news to Zurvan, who explains what a Fravashi Tree and the Plume are and that offering the Plume to the Amrita Pool lets the Pari return to the Vourukasha Oasis. She names the ritual and the Yasnapati who performs it, and asks the Traveler to bring back every Plume. When all ten trees are awakened, Zurvan thanks the Traveler and Sorush and tells Paimon a story about visiting Oceanids.
### LARGE +
Sorush's reaction varies depending on whether the Traveler has started As the Khvarena's Light Shows: before it, she calls the trees pathetic and says Pari should hope to spend their strength in a final stand; after it, her words are gentler and she says she can hardly bear the sight, and allows it might be their wish to guard the land. In both cases she says only a battle with no end is true eternity, and that a kind heart is one of Simurgh's noble qualities but not enough to survive in the desert. Paimon calls the trees' state lonely, and says that she would want to go home to everyone if trapped.

Zurvan's explanation is as follows. The being in the tree is a fragmented memory, like those in a Plume, which cannot return to the earth. If Heart of Amrita has been completed, Zurvan adds that Plumes of Purifying Light come from Simurgh's Khvarena and that Pari arose when Khvarena met Amrita, which is why they are called descendants of Khvarena. If the Traveler has completed In the End, the Forest Will Remember, she contrasts Pari, who are born from the Vourukasha Oasis and will return to it, with Aranara, who are born of the land. The god guarding the Oasis, she says, is rejected by the time flowing through the land, and so are the Pari; this is what they wish, because they exist to keep defilement from flowing into the land with them, and the Amrita Pool washes it away. She tells Paimon that humans who walked with Pari in ancient times purified scattered Pari and brought them to the Oasis, and that this bond gave rise to the title Yasnapati.
### ULTRA +
Final conversation. After all ten, Zurvan says the scattered Pari seem to have all returned and Sorush says she takes the praise as her due. If Khvarena of Good and Evil has not been done, Paimon says the giant hole will be fixed soon; if it has, she says the hole in the sky is already fixed. Paimon jokes that Pari, born from water, should be called "Hydri"; Sorush objects to the nickname. Zurvan is reminded of a distant past in which many Oceanids from foreign lands crossed the scorching, monster-ridden desert looking for their deceased god; finding no sign of one, and after seeing the Harvisptokhm, they dispersed. Their journey became a legend, and many outsiders have since followed the route; over time it came to be read as sacrificing oneself in pursuit of pure innocence. These pilgrimages continued for a long time after the Oasis was sealed. Zurvan muses that the journey may matter more than the destination, and that striving may mean more than a single sacrifice, which sets Sorush off ("Why does it always come back to me?").

Smaller points: the Fravashi Tree residual Pari speak in battle-boasting language ("Come forth and perish!") when woken. Gameplay: completing Pale Fire grants the achievement Homeward-Bound Spirits.

## revelations
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
- Fravashi Trees are Pari who exhausted their strength against the defilement of the land and were rejected by the Oasis as tainted.
- Plumes of Purifying Light let these Pari return to the Vourukasha Oasis when offered to the Amrita Pool.
- Humans who walked beside Pari once purified them in the desert; the Traveler as Sorush's Yasnapati takes on that role.
### LARGE +
- Zurvan says the beings in the trees are best called fragmented memories, and that Plumes of Purifying Light come from Simurgh's Khvarena.
- The Order of Skeptics once did the purification rituals; according to Sorush, no one in the Order can do so now and the best they can manage is to keep the trees alive.
- Oceanids from foreign lands once crossed the desert seeking their dead god, which created the pilgrimage legend.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon; Sorush, the Pari who calls the Traveler her Yasnapati and Paimon "Pale Floater"; Zurvan, the Pari elder at the Vourukasha Oasis; and a Residual Pari of the Forest in each tree.

## consequences
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Ten Fravashi Trees are cleared and their Pari are able to return to the Vourukasha Oasis; the Oasis looks livelier. The Traveler is recognised as a Yasnapati by Zurvan.

## placement
### STANDARD
Released in 3.6, located in the Girdle of the Sands, Sumeru (Gavireh Lajavard, with the Oasis as the hub). The trees are scattered in spots including Tunigi Hollow, Asipattravana Swamp, the Temir Mountains and Madinat al-Nuhas.

## entry: Fravashi Tree
@keys: Fravashi Tree, Fravashi Trees
@keys2: Pari, Sorush, Zurvan, Plume of Purifying Light
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
A Fravashi Tree is a ring-shaped tree in the Girdle of the Sands that a Pari transformed into after exhausting its strength against defilement. Sorush says the Pari inside is stuck on obsessions of the past and has been rejected by the Oasis as tainted. Waking it triggers a short fight with spectral enemies and leaves a Plume of Purifying Light.
### LARGE +
Zurvan says the contents are more truly fragmented memories than whole Pari. Ten of them are woken in the series, in places the wiki lists as North Tunigi Hollow, Northwest Tunigi Hollow, Asipattravana Swamp, East Tunigi Hollow, Hangeh Afrasiyab, Madinat al-Nuhas and the West Temir Mountains.

## entry: Plume of Purifying Light
@keys: Plume of Purifying Light
@keys2: Pari, Zurvan, Amrita Pool, Fravashi Tree
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The Plume is a glittering object that rises from a Fravashi Tree once its residual Pari is awakened. Zurvan says it carries memories that cannot return to the earth and comes from Simurgh's Khvarena; offered to the Amrita Pool, it lets the Pari return to the Vourukasha Oasis.

## entry: Yasnapati
@keys: Yasnapati
@keys2: Sorush, Pari, Zurvan, Traveler
@version: 3.6
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Yasnapati is a title Sorush uses for the Traveler. Zurvan says that in ancient times human warriors who walked with Pari held rituals to purify Pari scattered in the desert, and those with a special bond to Pari have been called Yasnapati ever since. Sorush instead credits the Order of Skeptics with the rituals.
