## summary
@spoiler: none
### LITE
Old Notes and New Friends is a world quest series in the Great Red Sand, Sumeru, added in version 3.1 as a follow-up to Golden Slumber. In Aaru Village the Traveler and Paimon meet two Vahumana researchers, Kalantari and Soheil. Soheil dismisses the Traveler's desert finds until the Traveler enters King Deshret's Mausoleum and brings back proof, then sends them to check the ruins in his old notes.
### STANDARD +
The series has two main quests, Dual Evidence and Soheil's Wish, and Soheil's Wish branches into three exploration quests: Invisible Barrier, Hidden Mercenaries and Desert's Remembrance. It is light in tone: the slates the Traveler brings back turn out to hold leave requests, a love poem and a fable, and the story pokes gentle fun at Akademiya paper-writing culture while also showing real pressure on its researchers.
### LARGE +
The series follows on from Golden Slumber, where the Traveler helped the young researcher Tirzad on a desert expedition. Completing Dual Evidence permanently turns off the cyclone over the Mausoleum of King Deshret and opens Aaru's Shut for good.

## plot
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Dual Evidence. Back in Aaru Village, the Traveler and Paimon are approached by Kalantari, a student of the Akademiya who has read Tirzad's new paper on the late period of Al-Ahmar's civilization, in which the two are thanked as a gift from the desert. Kalantari hopes to get inspiration for his own stalled paper and offers to hire them as guides. Before they can begin, the older researcher Soheil of the Usaibia family arrives with a Corps of Thirty escort, Hulwani, to send young scholars back: the sage fears they will alert the matra or damage the ruins, and Soheil says forty-six leave applications arrived after Tirzad's paper appeared. Soheil sends Kalantari home but stays himself, and tells the Traveler and Paimon that he suspects Tirzad invented his account. Stung, they find the stone slate Tirzad left glowing and head into the Mausoleum. They light the three ritual sites, activate the great device in the Great Hall of Truths, and defeat a Primal Construct at the top of the Mausoleum. They return with a new slate. Soheil's attitude flips, and he apologises on behalf of himself and his family.

Soheil's Wish. Soheil keeps the slate and, because the sage forbids researchers to enter the desert but has said nothing against gifts, hands the Traveler his old archaeology notes as a "present". The notes point to three places, and the Traveler brings back the slates found at each.
### LARGE +
Dual Evidence in more detail. Kalantari is a student from a Port Ormos merchant family, who likes to say he is unlike researchers who cannot talk to people. Soheil calls the youngsters impetuous and doubts Tirzad's account for specific reasons: he has visited the desert in his youth and never found the Khemenu Temple or Khaj-Nisut named in the paper, and the sandstorm above the Mausoleum looks as it always did. The Traveler works through the slate's guidance: the new inscription says that to ascend to Aaru one must "peck three organs" and cross the underworld river as a falcon, so the Traveler clears the Place of Rebirth, the Place of Breath and the Place of Swallowing. These three ruins unlock the Great Hall of Truths, where four plinths open the way to a device and the slate asks for three embers of the sun to be burned. Lighting the three Primal Torches carries the Traveler to the roof, where a Primal Construct is defeated and a chest yields the Record of Aaru's Shut.

The three exploration quests. Invisible Barrier: the Traveler enters four transparent ruins marked in the notes, which Soheil had never been able to enter, and finds three slates. Soheil reads them as leave requests whose excuses resemble those of modern students, and plans a paper on the perception of days off in different societies. Hidden Mercenaries: near Lamb-Devourer Rock the notes describe Eremites of Thutmose appearing and vanishing. The Traveler follows a patrolling Eremite, overhears the password to a hidden door (Marg bar parvezravan), and reaches the secret base; the slate there is a love poem, which Soheil would send to the scholars of Haravatat for a paper on rhetoric and civilization. Desert's Remembrance: the Tomb of Carouses is a smaller version of the Mausoleum, and its slate is an animal fable that Soheil bends into a short paper on the retreat of sheep and environmental change.

Ending. After all three are done, Soheil explains why he wants to enter the Mausoleum himself: he wants to be a candidate to become a sage, who hold power over researchers. When Paimon quotes the line from his old notes about mockery being easier to bear than having no progress in knowledge, Soheil, who has forgotten writing it, asks to take his notes back, saying it is a pleasant reminder of when he was younger.
### ULTRA +
Soheil's Old Notes (an item given in Soheil's Wish) cover about forty-six days. The writer records a month recording everything outside the Mausoleum without entering, the legend that spring water would burst from a long-dried well and shatter the barrier, the discovery of four transparent ruins that repelled approach, and the cone-shaped Tomb of Carouses. On the 46th day an archaeology team was attacked by Eremites calling themselves Thutmose, led by two young men; one wanted to kill the party, the other stopped him. He decided to return to the Akademiya to face ridicule, hoped the second Eremite's axe would stay sharp, noted that sheep vanish near Lamb-Devourer Rock, and wrote that being mocked was not as painful as having no improvement in knowledge. The note also suggests Thutmose may hide in rock formations or a hidden cave. In the Thutmose base, Soheil remarks that Thutmose managed to rob him despite his precautions.

Smaller beats. Paimon wonders after Jeht and Tirzad when she arrives. Using Nahida's All Schemes to Know gives short private thoughts for Soheil and Hulwani at various points; Hulwani privately wants to return to Sumeru City. Soheil's change of attitude is abrupt enough that Paimon compares him to a Liyue storyteller. Soheil cannot go into the desert himself because of the sage's ban, and Hulwani eventually allows the Traveler to go because the ban applies to researchers.

Gameplay notes, labelled as such: Dual Evidence includes a boss fight (Field Annihilation Array) and a puzzle chain in each of three ruins. Invisible Barrier and Desert's Remembrance may be partly completed before the quest begins and the dialogue plays out as if new.

## revelations
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
- The Mausoleum of King Deshret can be entered and cleared by following a stone slate left from Golden Slumber; its inner structure includes the Great Hall of Truths and a roof reached by an elevator.
- The late-period records of Al-Ahmar's civilization have mostly been lost, so a complete slate is an unprecedented find for Vahumana, according to Soheil.
- Soheil's real motive is ambition: he wants a sage's seat, and he admits the gap between researcher and sage is like that between a hilichurl and a slime.
### LARGE +
- Eremites of Thutmose are a group of Deshret believers who operate near Lamb-Devourer Rock and had a hidden base behind a password-locked door.
- The transparent ruins, the Thutmose base and the Tomb of Carouses each contain a stone slate whose text is mundane (leave requests, a love poem, a fable). Soheil does not draw any larger conclusion from them.
- A slate mentions Aaru in a construction-log style text, but no one confirms how this relates to Aaru Village.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon; Kalantari, a student and Vahumana researcher (a quest-only NPC); Soheil Usaibia, an older Vahumana researcher; Hulwani of the Corps of Thirty; and mentions of Tirzad, Jeht and the sage. Thutmose Eremites appear in Hidden Mercenaries.

## consequences
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
The cyclone above the Mausoleum is permanently turned off and Aaru's Shut becomes permanently accessible. Soheil changes from scornful to grateful and gives the Traveler repeated gifts of friendship.

## placement
### STANDARD
Requires Golden Slumber to be finished and the next day's reset. Released in 3.1. Dual Evidence comes first, then Soheil's Wish, which has three exploration quests that can be done in any order and may be done in advance.

## entry: Soheil
@keys: Soheil, Soheil Usaibia
@keys2: Vahumana, Aaru Village, researcher, Kalantari
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Soheil Usaibia is an older Vahumana researcher of the Usaibia family. He was sent to Aaru Village to stop young scholars going into the desert, is dismissive of Tirzad's paper at first, then becomes warm once shown evidence. He covets a sage's position and openly says why. He is conscious of his age and the time left to him.
### LARGE +
He keeps his dignity by relying on forms: he cannot enter the desert, but he can accept a "gift". His rediscovered old notes remind him of why he took up research.

## entry: Kalantari
@keys: Kalantari
@keys2: Tirzad, Aaru Village, Vahumana
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
Kalantari is a student of the Akademiya from a family of merchants in Port Ormos. He has read Tirzad's paper and wants help getting out of a creative block. He is friendly, talkative and gets sent back to Sumeru City by Soheil in Dual Evidence. He is a quest-only NPC.

## entry: Thutmose
@keys: Thutmose
@keys2: Eremites, Lamb-Devourer Rock, Soheil
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: none
### STANDARD
Thutmose is a group of Eremites in the desert who describe themselves as fanatical believers in Deshret in Soheil's old notes. The notes record two young leaders, one aggressive and one merciful. In Hidden Mercenaries their base is a hidden location near Lamb-Devourer Rock behind a door that opens to a spoken password.

## entry: Mausoleum slates

@keys2: Soheil, Mausoleum, Deshret
@version: 3.1
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Stone slates are the quest items of the series. The Scarlet Sand Slate gives instructions in stages for the Mausoleum. The slates in the transparent ruins, in the Thutmose base and in the Tomb of Carouses hold, respectively, leave requests, a love poem and a fable, according to Soheil.
