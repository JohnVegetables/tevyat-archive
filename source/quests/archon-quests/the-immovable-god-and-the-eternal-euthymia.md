## summary
### LITE
The first act of Inazuma's Archon Quest. The Traveler and Paimon arrive at the port of Ritou, where the Sakoku Decree traps outlanders, hoping to meet the Raiden Shogun. Thoma and Kamisato Ayaka tell them it is not that simple: the Vision Hunt Decree is stripping people of their Visions. After meeting three Visionless people, the Traveler agrees to help the resistance and frees the craftsman Masakatsu from the Tenryou Commission.
### STANDARD +
The act has eight quests: Setting Sail, Confessions of an Outlander, Ritou Escape Plan, Three Wishes, The Meaning of Meaningless Waiting, To Treat the Well-Meaning Well, A Swordmaster's Path Is Paved With Broken Blades, and A Flower Blooms in a Prison. It introduces Inazuma's closed borders and the human cost of the Shogun's decree before the story moves on to open conflict.
### LARGE +
The act is deliberately local: most of it is small errands that show the Traveler what the decrees do to ordinary people, and the Traveler's initial refusal to take sides is overturned by what they see, not by argument.

## plot
### STANDARD
Beidou's ship brings the Traveler and Paimon to Ritou. Katheryne says she will wait for them on Narukami Island. Beidou leaves them with her business partner Thoma, since Kazuha, still a fugitive, is aboard. Thoma gets them through the processing center, explaining that under the Sakoku Decree outlanders cannot leave and some inspectors inflate fees; stranded foreigners have formed the International Trade Association to cope. He offers to help them reach the Raiden Shogun if they help others first.

The association's president, Kurisu, says the Kanjou Commission has been bleeding them with taxes that suddenly switched from Mora to Crystal Marrow, whose price rises whenever they hunt for it. The Traveler traces the scheme to a Mondstadt merchant, Werner, and to a corrupt officer named Keijirou, who is exposed. When a passing officer, Katsuie, orders the merchants arrested, Thoma shows a Yashiro Commission card and a distinctive fan, and the incident is dropped. He then reveals he serves the Kamisato Clan and the clan's eldest daughter Ayaka, who hopes the Traveler can change the tides against the Vision Hunt Decree. The Traveler is not interested, but Thoma agrees to help anyway.

Still lacking a travel permit, the Traveler meets Hiiragi Shinsuke, the Kanjou commissioner, who offers help only in return for an unbearable request. His daughter Chisato secretly contacts the Traveler, asks them to carry a letter to Kujou Kamaji of the Tenryou Commission, and arranges for the Traveler to join a supply convoy as her bodyguard. After fighting off bandits, the Traveler goes on with the letter.

Thoma then brings them into the Kamisato circle. Ayaka sends them to help three people who lost their Visions, and after seeing the harm first-hand the Traveler agrees to help the resistance by freeing the craftsman Masakatsu from the Tenryou Commission.
### LARGE +
At Komore Teahouse Thoma jokingly poses as the dog Taroumaru and explains that the earlier tests were needed to see whether they could "change the lightning". He takes them to the Statue of the Omnipresent God, where the seized Visions are set into the structure. When the Traveler touches it they hear the wishes of the Visions' owners. At the Kamisato estate Ayaka, speaking from behind a screen, asks them to join the resistance. They again refuse, so she compromises: visit three people whose Visions were taken, and she will arrange a meeting with the Shogun.

The three visits take up the middle of the act. In Konda Village a Visionless samurai, Tejima, has forgotten why he stayed there; the Traveler helps him recover the memory that he was waiting for a lover, and he decides to keep waiting. In Inazuma City, a Tenryou Commission samurai, Kurosawa Kyounosuke, has forgotten the monthly emergency rations he gave to neighbours and is accused of withholding them; the Traveler finds that he had run up debts to a grocer, Aoi, to feed people, and he is left unsure whether he is good or bad. The third is Domon, a master of the Meikyou Shisui Art who seems possessed after losing his Vision. Lady Yae of the Grand Narukami Shrine finds he is not possessed: the Vision's aspirations had been covering the memory of what he had destroyed. Anzai, the senior disciple of Domon's old master whom Domon surpassed, has given up his own ambitions and urges Domon to pass his on to his students.

Back with Ayaka, who now meets them openly, the Traveler agrees to help. Ayaka explains the resistance's work, including making fake Visions to hand over in place of real ones. Their craftsman, Masakatsu, has been arrested. The Traveler meets Yoimiya at Naganohara Fireworks, who has been fooling the confiscators with a fake, and goes with her to the holding cells. Yoimiya sets off a firework "gift" to distract the guards, Kujou Sara arrives and reprimands the guards for torturing Masakatsu, then spots the rescuers and lets them go because he needs urgent care, while warning she will bring him back when he is recovered. The act ends with a group hot pot game at the teahouse, after Thoma mentions the Tenryou Commission has been tasked with a "ceremony".
Details of the middle quests. The Crystal Marrow scam works as a cycle: Keijirou's group buys all the Marrow and passes it to Werner, who sells the excess back to the association at a profit while being paid very little. Werner is afraid to confront the president, and tells the Traveler to deliver a ledger that Keijirou was burying. Harrison, an association member, demands more of Keijirou and names the commissioner aloud, which draws Katsuie into the scene.

Kurosawa's IOU box is the key evidence: the Treasure Hoarders who had been robbing him found only IOUs addressed to Aoi of Tsukumomono Groceries. Aoi says she would have accepted his prized sword as payment but he refused. He had threatened the men with that sword while feeling unable to follow through. Domon's disciple Junya is on high alert and hired Grand Narukami shrine maidens, one of whom, Inagi Hotomi, found no demon.
### ULTRA +
The act's three Vision stories show three different effects of confiscation: lost purpose (Tejima), lost sense of right and wrong (Kurosawa) and a return of suppressed guilt (Domon). The Traveler resolves none of them by force, only by helping someone remember or reframe. Kurosawa's account that the Raiden Shogun was disappointed by his "slacking" is told by him, not shown, so why he in particular lost his Vision is known only second-hand.

The act title is rendered by the wiki as roughly "The Immovable Narukami and the Eternal Paradise" in Chinese and Japanese, with Narukami as the Electro Archon's title.

## revelations
@version: 2.0
@spoiler: minor
### STANDARD
Thoma is a Kamisato Clan attendant, not just Beidou's business partner. Ayaka and the Kamisato resistance run fake-Vision operations against the Vision Hunt Decree. The Visions set into the Statue of the Omnipresent God are seized Visions, and the Traveler can hear their owners' aspirations. Lady Yae explains that a Vision gives aspirations and that losing it can bring back what those aspirations hid.
### LARGE +
Kujou Sara, an enforcer of the decree, reprimands the guards for torturing Masakatsu and lets his rescuers go, though she says he will be brought back. Chisato reports that an arrogant-sounding woman visited and conspired with her father to keep the Traveler in Ritou.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon are guests of Thoma and Ayaka. Beidou delivers them to Ritou. Thoma guides and tests them. Ayaka backs the resistance from behind the scenes and at first asks for help from behind a screen. Hiiragi Shinsuke and Chisato represent the port's authorities and the quiet dissent inside them. Lady Yae and Kujou Sara appear briefly, on different sides of the decree. Yoimiya is a firework maker who helps with the rescue.
### LARGE +
Minor figures with their own small arcs: Kurisu (association president), Werner and Keijirou (the Marrow scam), Tejima, Kurosawa Kyounosuke, Aoi, Domon, his disciple Junya and his former fellow disciple Anzai, and Masakatsu.

## consequences
### STANDARD
The Traveler stops refusing and commits to the resistance. Masakatsu is freed but Sara has said she will bring him back. The Tenryou Commission is tasked with a "ceremony", which the next act (Stillness, the Sublimation of Shadow) picks up.

## placement
### STANDARD
Chapter II Act I, added in version 2.0. It follows Chapter I's final act (Autumn Winds, Scarlet Leaves) and precedes Stillness, the Sublimation of Shadow. Story order equals release order.

### STANDARD
In Confessions of an Outlander the Kanjou Commission switches the International Trade Association's tax from Mora to Crystal Marrow, which nearly none of them knew. Scarcity pushes up both the price and the taxes. The Traveler traces the cycle to a corrupt officer, Keijirou, and a pressured merchant, Werner.
