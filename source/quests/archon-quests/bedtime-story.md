## summary
@spoiler: minor
### LITE
Bedtime Story is the sixth and final act of Chapter IV, the Traveler's Archon Quest chapter (version 4.7). A commission from Katheryne in Sumeru City asks for help finding a missing man from Vimara Village whom no one can name. Investigating, the Traveler and Paimon meet Dainsleif, who tells them about the Five Sinners of Khaenri'ah, and finally meet Caribert, Chlothar Alberich's son, whose residual consciousness is tied to the Loom of Fate.
### STANDARD +
The act has three quests: Cold Case Commission, Memories That Should Not Exist and World-Order Narration. It requires Adventure Rank 40. It resolves the missing-person mystery, explains what the Loom of Fate is, and gives the Traveler a conversation with their sibling that they will not remember.
### LARGE +
The sibling appears inside Caribert's memory world and cannot touch the Traveler. The act closes Chapter IV on the sibling's loneliness, and the Traveler leaves with only a photograph as evidence.

## plot
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Katheryne passes the Traveler a joint commission from Vimara Village: find a missing person. A dozen attempts have failed and nobody can give his name. Villagers describe a kind young man in his early twenties who kept the elderly company and played with orphans. Atossa takes the Traveler to a tree where they talked for hours each dusk, though the sun never seemed to move. A Pyro Abyss Mage attacks them, and the hilichurls near the village are agitated.

Dainsleif, who is investigating the Abyss Order, reveals that the voice heard earlier is Vedrfolnir, his older brother and one of the Five Sinners, together with Rhinedottir, Surtalogi, Rerir and Hroptatyr. He blames them for not defending their nation in the cataclysm. He also notes that the Loom of Fate operation continues without the eye of the first Field Tiller. A memory that does not belong to him appears, in which he gives that eye to the missing man. He realises the man may implant memories and that the Abyss Order is tailing him, and sends the Traveler and Paimon back to Vimara Village. The sibling then approaches Dainsleif alone, and Dainsleif says he went along with the trap knowing it was the only way to meet them face to face.

Back in the village the hilichurls are calm and nobody remembers the commission. The Traveler finds Atossa with a stranger, Caribert, who leads them into his consciousness. There he explains that the Loom of Fate in its primitive form creates and implants memories, and when fully unlocked creates Ley Lines and can change the world. The village life was his own doing, a chance at the life he was denied. He reveals the Loom is complete: Dainsleif had kept the eye inside his own body, planning to lure the Abyss Order to a false location, confront the sibling and pass the eye to the Traveler, but Caribert implanted a false memory of Dainsleif already handing it over, so the Abyss Order obtained it. His real goal was to tell the hilichurls a calming bedtime story through implanted memory, as his father once did for him; with it finished, he fades peacefully. The sibling appears but cannot be touched; they tell the Traveler they are racing to unlock the Loom's full potential before the Heavenly Principles awaken, say they miss the Traveler, and admit they struggle to face themselves as their war goes on. Caribert's memory collapses. The Traveler wakes in the village remembering nothing of the talk, holding a photograph of themselves, the sibling and Caribert.
### LARGE
**Cold Case Commission.** Katheryne in Sumeru City explains that Vimara Village jointly issued the commission and that the villagers can give only his general appearance. Bahram calls him very kind and always willing to help; Amadhiah says he accompanied the elderly and played with the orphans. The Traveler suspects he exists only in memory. Atossa describes him as the family she never had and notes that it was always dusk when they met.

**Memories That Should Not Exist.** Dainsleif lists the Five Sinners and says that if they had joined him they could have stopped the Vinster King; the Traveler reminds him of his promise to explain the voice. He believes the Loom of Fate is closely related to the Ley Lines. The next day he is agitated by the foreign memory. At the eye's location a new memory distracts him; sensing a disturbance in the Ley Lines he sends the Traveler away. The sibling tells Dainsleif that sending an army when the Twilight Sword was ready would have spelled its own demise, so they came alone; if he wants to talk, he must beat them first.

**World-Order Narration.** Caribert planned to say farewell to Atossa and leaves her a message instead. He says his father, Chlothar Alberich, told him he would become the Loom of Fate, but he was only used for its construction, died when it was set in motion at the age of eight, and is now residual consciousness within it; his adult form is imagined from his father's appearance. After he fades, the sibling shares a short conversation with the Traveler. The villagers found the sleeping Traveler at the outskirts and carried them to the village; Paimon says Dainsleif came to check on them, seemingly injured and with a lot on his mind. Caribert's last implanted memory has the villagers recall seeing him sleeping under the tree at dusk before his parents came and they all left together; they now remember his name, and Atossa hopes he will return one day. In the photograph Caribert urges the two to get along whatever happens.

### ULTRA +
Further details. A dozen earlier attempts at the commission had ended in failure. Dainsleif says the Sinners' failure to defend Khaenri'ah is something he will never forgive and always remember despite the erosion of his memories. The sibling explains that neither twin belongs in Caribert's space, which is why they are intangible, and that with Caribert gone neither will remember the reunion. They say Dainsleif hesitated to raise his sword against them, without which they might still be no match for him, and that at the end of their journey they reached the Sea of Flowers at the End. The Heavenly Principles have shown no activity in the five hundred years since the cataclysm.

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
- The Sinner's voice belongs to Vedrfolnir, "the Visionary", Dainsleif's older brother and one of the Five Sinners of Khaenri'ah, alongside Rhinedottir, Surtalogi, Rerir and Hroptatyr.
- Dainsleif holds the other Sinners responsible for not defending Khaenri'ah when the cataclysm came.
- The Loom of Fate is a device whose primitive form creates and implants memories and whose full form creates Ley Lines and changes the world.
- Caribert, son of Chlothar Alberich, was used to construct the Loom and survives only as residual consciousness tied to it; he built the Vimara Village life for himself and calmed the hilichurls with a bedtime story.
- Dainsleif had carried the first Field Tiller's eye inside his body; a false memory implanted by Caribert let the Abyss Order obtain it.
- The sibling is rushing to finish the Loom before the Heavenly Principles awaken, which did not happen when the Divine Throne of Hydro was destroyed.
### LARGE +
- The sibling misses the Traveler but struggles to face themselves as their war continues; the Traveler will not remember the talk.

## characters
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon, Dainsleif, the sibling, Caribert, Katheryne, Atossa, Bahram, Amadhiah and a Pyro Abyss Mage. The Five Sinners are named; Vedrfolnir is a voice rather than a speaker.

## consequences
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Caribert says the Loom of Fate is completed. The Traveler's meeting with the sibling is erased from their memory but a photograph remains. Dainsleif, having lost the eye, is left injured and preoccupied after facing the sibling. Chapter V follows in Natlan.
### LARGE +
Villagers recall Caribert as someone who left with his parents, and Atossa hopes he will return.

## placement
### STANDARD
Chapter IV, Act VI (final act), released in 4.7. It follows Masquerade of the Guilty and precedes Flowers Resplendent on the Sun-Scorched Sojourn (Chapter V Act I). The Caribert act (3.5) showed Caribert as Chlothar Alberich's son in the sibling's memories; here he refers to that memory himself.

## entry: Caribert and the Loom of Fate
@keys: Caribert, Loom of Fate
@keys2: Vimara Village, Dainsleif, sibling
@version: 4.7
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Caribert, Chlothar Alberich's son, was used to construct the sibling's Loom of Fate and survives as residual consciousness tied to it. Through the half-finished Loom he created a false life in Vimara Village with memories implanted in the villagers, and gave the hilichurls a calming bedtime story. He explains that a fully unlocked Loom weaves Ley Lines and can change the world, and he fades away peacefully as the sibling arrives.
