## summary
### LITE
The second act of Sumeru's Archon Quest. During the Sabzeruz Festival in Sumeru City, the Traveler and Paimon find the same day repeating. A mysterious girl named Nahida restores their memories, tells them it is a "samsara" and that the real Dunyarzad, a gravely ill festival-lover, weakens with each cycle, and asks them to break it. The loop turns out to be a collective dream built by the Akademiya's Akasha, and it ends only when the dreamer at its centre, Nilou, realises she is dreaming and performs the Dance of Sabzeruz.
### STANDARD +
The act has eight quests: The Coming, The Arrival, The Continuation, The Samsara, The Causality, The Empty Illusions and The End of the Sabzeruz Festival, plus the denouement Dawn. It is the point at which the story shifts from a puzzle about a missing god to a direct clash with the Akademiya, and where Nahida is first revealed to be Lesser Lord Kusanali, the Dendro Archon.
### LARGE +
The act is structured like a time-loop mystery: each quest is one more repeated day, and the Traveler's progress is made by testing a theory per day and carrying the result forward through recorded messages, since memories reset.

## plot
### STANDARD
During the Sabzeruz Festival in Sumeru City, the Traveler and Paimon help the ailing festival-lover Dunyarzad and the bodyguard Dehya. Grand Sage Azar and his secretary Setaria shut the festival down just before Nilou's Dance of Sabzeruz, and the morning then repeats. The Traveler follows a strange girl to the Bimarstan and meets Nahida, who restores their memories, explains the "samsara" and shows that the real Dunyarzad is fading with every loop. With Dehya's help they test theories day by day (terminal removal, memory erasure, leaving the city) and use recorded messages to learn that the Akasha harvests users' dreams and that the samsara is a collective festival dream.

The real Dunyarzad's consciousness vanishes and Nahida leaves to save it. The Traveler identifies Nilou as the dream's host; she realises she is dreaming, dances the Dance of Sabzeruz as the dream dissolves, and everyone wakes. In the denouement Nahida, speaking through Katheryne's puppet, is revealed as Lesser Lord Kusanali, shut in the Sanctuary of Surasthana for five hundred years, and says the words "World... forget me" came from Rukkhadevata.
### LARGE +
In detail.

The Traveler and Paimon return to the Grand Bazaar and tell Dunyarzad about their trouble at Port Ormos. She lets them rest, then guides them around the festival venues (including Vihar's stall, where visitors pick a mystery box of Yalda Candy flavours). Eremite mercenaries try to seize Dunyarzad for ransom, and Dehya holds them off alone, ending up hurt because she is not yet used to a new greatsword, having sold her old one to fund the festival. Resting, Dunyarzad admits she was born with Eleazar, that it is now terminal, and that she is giving everything to the festival so she will have no regrets. They hurry back for the Dance of Sabzeruz, but Grand Sage Azar and his secretary Setaria shut the festival down in front of Nilou, citing legal violations and banning public art performances from the next Jnagarbha Day. Elsewhere, a beeping sound is heard, and Khajeh says the project is entering its most critical phase.

The morning then repeats. The Traveler feels déjà vu, finds an unfamiliar-looking snack tastes familiar, sees Dunyarzad seem a little weaker than before, and watches Azar and Setaria close the festival again. The project's "Jnana Energy" output is noted to be dropping.

On the third day the Traveler has memorised the mystery boxes, abandons the festival to chase a strange girl and ends up in the Bimarstan, where a second Dunyarzad lies in bed. The girl, Nahida, restores their memories of the loops and explains that only they, thanks to their Dendro-linked constitution, can perceive her. The bedridden Dunyarzad is the real one, fading with each cycle while Nahida slows her illness. Nahida cannot simply tell them the cause without, as she puts it, literally blowing their minds, so they must find it themselves. They recruit Dehya, who cannot see Nahida but senses Dunyarzad's aura and believes them, and she correctly deduces that the sages are using the Akasha to cause the samsara, suspecting they are extracting knowledge from its users to make one massive collective brain. Taking their terminals off does not help: they hear the beep and lose consciousness. Khajeh warns that casualties are likely, and Azar decides to continue.

The next days eliminate theories. Removing the terminal fails; so does the idea that memories are simply erased, because Dehya's training dummy shows details the sages could not recreate for every loop. The pair conclude they are not in the real world and test leaving the city. Their memories of leaving vanish each time, so Nahida modifies the Traveler's Akasha Terminal to record messages. The recordings reveal that the Akasha gathers its wisdom by taking its users' dreams, which arise in a phase of sleep with heavy brain activity, and that the sages' claim that dreams are harmful is a cover. The samsara is a shared dream of the festival in which users keep "waking" to a new day while their emptied dreams are harvested. The Traveler also works out that Nahida is Lesser Lord Kusanali.

The real Dunyarzad's consciousness can no longer withstand the harvesting and vanishes; the one in the festival is a puppet. Nahida leaves the city to find a way to rescue Dunyarzad. The Traveler's first guess at the dream's host is wrong, but a book of folk legends the puppet Dunyarzad compiled for them leads to the remembered Dance of Sabzeruz. When the Traveler interrupts Azar and Setaria confronting Nilou, they stop responding, as they are only puppets. True purple padisarah flowers confirm that Nilou is the host. Linking it to the tale of the First Sage, Nilou realises she is dreaming, begins to wake, and dances the Dance of Sabzeruz while the dreamscape dissolves and Dunyarzad's spirit appears in the crowd. The Traveler and Paimon wake up free, remove their terminals, and find Dunyarzad alive at the Bimarstan with Katheryne.

In the denouement, that "Katheryne" is Nahida, speaking through the Snezhnayan puppet's Akasha Terminal because she has been unable to leave the Sanctuary of Surasthana for five hundred years. She explains how she saved Dunyarzad's consciousness and answers some of the Traveler's questions as thanks. She says the words "World... forget me" were left by Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, whose consciousness was contaminated by something dangerous, and that the Traveler encountering them without going mad gives her hope of curing Irminsul. The sages removed their terminals to prevent leaks; she means to look into how and punish them if necessary. Exhausted, she leaves Katheryne's body, and the real Katheryne wakes confused away from her post.
### ULTRA +
Details that matter in play: Dehya's left arm is bandaged after the first mercenary fight and the injury is gone in the Samsara quest, which is how the Traveler tells that a loop has changed; the injury is one of few in-game model changes. In the fourth quest Dehya forgets the previous loop but finds her new greatsword oddly familiar, raising false hopes. The wiki notes that a later quest gives the total as 168 cycles, roughly five and a half months of days. The quests' Chinese titles are, per the wiki, likely references to chapter titles of the visual novel Steins;Gate, and the act title echoes a quatrain of Omar Khayyam's Rubaiyat.

## revelations
### STANDARD
- The Akademiya's Akasha runs on harvested dreams; the sages' public line that dreams are harmful is a falsehood that hides this.
- The Sabzeruz samsara is a shared dream built from the minds of Akasha users in Sumeru City. Its host, the person who must realise they are dreaming for it to break, is Nilou.
- Nahida is Lesser Lord Kusanali, the Dendro Archon, imprisoned in the Sanctuary of Surasthana for five hundred years and able to act only through an Akasha Terminal.
- The words "World... forget me" were left by Greater Lord Rukkhadevata, and forbidden knowledge contaminated her consciousness.
### LARGE +
Dunyarzad was born with Eleazar, and its terminal stage motivates her devotion to the festival. The Akademiya's top figures (Azar, Setaria, Khajeh) are shown to know about the project and to accept casualties; Azar calls the sacrifices trivial relative to the goal.

## characters
### STANDARD
Traveler and Paimon drive the investigation. Nahida appears as a girl only they can see, and later possesses Katheryne's body. Dunyarzad, a young woman with a terminal illness, is the emotional centre; a real and a puppet version both appear. Dehya is the Traveler's reluctant ally and bodyguard to Dunyarzad. Nilou is the festival's dancer and the unwitting dream host. Azar (Grand Sage) and Setaria (his secretary) shut the festival down; Khajeh is heard discussing the project's progress with Azar. Vihar runs a stall, and Eremite mercenaries attack the group.
### LARGE +
Azar and Setaria are puppets inside the dream, not the real sages. The real Setaria, a desert-born prodigy, is developed in later acts.

## consequences
### STANDARD
In Act III (Dreams, Emptiness, Deception), Nahida, again speaking through Katheryne, says she wants to resolve matters with the sages before turning to Irminsul and The Withering. Dunyarzad recovers in the real world but remembers nothing of the loop except Nilou's dance.
### LARGE +
Labelled as later consequences: in Act V, Nahida removes the contaminated remnants of Rukkhadevata from Irminsul and shuts the Akasha down; with the forbidden knowledge gone, Eleazar disappears and Dunyarzad is cured, and she attends the victory feast with Dehya. Earlier, in Act III, Setaria is pushed to reveal that the story of King Deshret's resurrection was an Akademiya misinformation campaign, and she agrees to work against the sages.

## placement
### STANDARD
Chapter III, Act II. Released in Version 3.0 (the Sumeru launch). The previous act is Through Mists of Smoke and Forests Dark; the next is Dreams, Emptiness, Deception, then King Deshret and the Three Magi and Akasha Pulses, the Kalpa Flame Rises. Story order and release order match.

## entry: Dunyarzad and the real and puppet selves
@keys: Dunyarzad, Dunyarzad's puppet
@keys2: Sabzeruz, samsara
### STANDARD
Dunyarzad is a festival enthusiast who donated heavily to revive the festival and says she wants no regrets when her terminal Eleazar claims her. In the samsara there are two of her: a bedridden real one at the Bimarstan whose consciousness is eroded each cycle, and a puppet who repeats the festival day and keeps the loop going. The puppet gives the Traveler a book of folk legends and echoes a line the real Dunyarzad once said, which points to the dance she most wanted to see. After the samsara the real Dunyarzad is found alive, remembering only the dance.

## entry: Dance of Sabzeruz and Nilou
@keys: Dance of Sabzeruz, Nilou's dance
@keys2: Sabzeruz, samsara, dream
### STANDARD
The Dance of Sabzeruz is the festival's central performance, which Nilou is forbidden from giving by the Grand Sage. In the dream, Nilou is the host; once she connects a tale of the First Sage to her situation, she realises she is dreaming, and her performance of the dance as the dream dissolves ends the loop. The purple padisarah flowers present in the dream are the clue that she is the host.

## entry: Dream harvesting
@keys: dream harvesting, Jnana Energy, harvested dreams
@keys2: Akasha, Akademiya
### STANDARD
The Traveler's recorded messages show that the Akasha works by taking users' dreams during the active phase of sleep, and that the sages' talk of dreams being harmful is a lie. Jnana Energy output is tracked by the project and dips as the loops continue. Taking the terminal off does not stop the samsara. The sages, per Nahida, later remove their own terminals so nothing leaks.

### STANDARD
Nahida first appears as a small girl only the Traveler and Paimon can see, because of their Dendro connection. She deliberately withholds answers to protect their minds. In the denouement she is revealed to be Kusanali, imprisoned in the Sanctuary of Surasthana for five hundred years; she speaks through Katheryne's puppet body.
