## summary
@spoiler: major
### LITE
Chapter IV is the Archon Quest chapter set in Fontaine, the Hydro nation of justice and trials. Acts I to V follow the Traveler and Paimon through courtroom dramas, the Spina di Rosula's hunt for a drug ring and a missing-women case, an undercover stay in the Fortress of Meropide, and finally the fulfilment and defeat of a prophecy that Fontaine's people would dissolve in rising water, leaving the Hydro Archon weeping alone. Act V is titled "Masquerade of the Guilty", the name the travel log gives the whole block. Act VI, "Bedtime Story", is a separate Traveler chapter set in Sumeru.
### STANDARD
Chapter IV is the Fontaine chapter of the Archon Quest. The travel log presents Acts I to V under the title "Masquerade of the Guilty" (Act V carries the same name) and Act VI, "Bedtime Story", as a Traveler chapter. All acts require Adventure Rank 40.
### LARGE +
The Traveler and Paimon arrive in Fontaine, are drawn into trials at the Opera Epiclese, help the investigators of the Spina di Rosula break a Primordial-Seawater drug ring tied to a serial-disappearance case, and spend 45 days undercover in the Fortress of Meropide at Chief Justice Neuvillette's request. The prison's sluice gate fails as the old prophecy begins to come true. In Act V the Traveler and their allies put the Hydro Archon Furina on trial; the real Archon, Focalors, is revealed to have hidden her plan for five centuries, and her self-execution restores the Hydro authority to Neuvillette, the Hydro Dragon Sovereign, which spares Fontaine's people.

Structurally the chapter is a run of mystery-and-trial plots (Acts I, II) that slowly reveal a larger problem (Acts III, IV) and then turn the courtroom device itself into the vehicle for the climax (Act V). Recurring motifs are trials and verdicts, rain and the Primordial Sea, performance and masks, and the idea (voiced by Mage "N") that even the gods' gaze has blind spots. Fatui-aligned characters (the Knave Arlecchino, her House of the Hearth "children" and Childe) are present as uneasy allies rather than enemies.

## plot
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
**Act I - Prelude of Blancheur and Noirceur (4.0).** In Fontaine the Traveler and Paimon meet Furina and the magicians Lyney and Lynette, and defend Lyney in a trial after a performer dies and an audience member vanishes during his show. Lyney and Lynette are exposed as Fatui of the House of the Hearth; their accuser Vaughn dissolves into water before naming his "Boss".

**Act II - As Light Rain Falls Without Reason (4.0).** The Traveler helps Navia of the Spina di Rosula investigate the disappearances and her father Callas' death, uncovering a Primordial-Seawater drug called Sinthe and its maker Marcel, who is Vacher. Childe is arrested and the Oratrice declares him guilty against Neuvillette's own finding.

**Act III - To the Stars Shining in the Depths (4.1).** Jailed under false charges for 45 days in the Fortress of Meropide, the Traveler and Paimon look for the vanished Childe and learn the prison's "hidden rules".

**Act IV - Cataclysm's Quickening (4.1).** A sluice gate holding back the Primordial Sea fails and Neuvillette reseals it. Arlecchino meets Furina at a tea party, and Neuvillette is recognised as the Hydro Sovereign. Childe drifts in the Primordial Sea beneath a giant narwhal-like creature.

**Act V - Masquerade of the Guilty (4.2).** See quest.masquerade-of-the-guilty: the Poisson disaster, the stone slates, Furina's trial, Focalors' revelation and sacrifice, and the defeat of the All-Devouring Narwhal.

**Act VI - Bedtime Story (4.7).** Set in Sumeru: a cold-case commission at Vimara Village leads to Caribert and the Abyss Order's Loom of Fate.
### LARGE +
How the acts unfold in more detail. Act I: after leaving Sumeru the Traveler and Paimon reach Romaritime Harbor, learn of the rising waters, and are challenged by Furina to a legal "duel". The twins' show ends with the dead performer Cowell and the missing woman Liliane, a thief; Navia helps the defence. Childe also appears early, his Hydro weapons malfunctioning. Further Act I details: Furina, aware of the Traveler's disruptions in other nations, welcomes them as the nation's Archon, then declares a courtroom "duel" and charges them with releasing a flying object on a restricted day (a reference to Paimon); Lyney's trick with a red cord of energy makes Paimon legally a balloon. Lyney hands out Magic Pockets so waterside residents can move belongings if the water rises. At the harbor the Traveler and Lyney chase a pickpocket, and Charlotte, the Steambird reporter, joins their aquabus. Childe gives the Traveler his Vision for safekeeping before a planned spar with Clorinde. In the trial that follows the show, Furina exposes the twins as Fatui; the defence unravels the "magic box" trick, and Navia's team produce the missing woman. Afterwards Lyney explains that the twins, orphans, followed Arlecchino because she rescued them, and that she believed the prophecy and would try to use the Hydro Gnosis to save Fontaine before taking it to Snezhnaya.

Act II: the Traveler joins Navia and the trail leads to an underwater Sinthe facility. Further details: Paimon accidentally drinks Navia's Fonta, which had been spiked with Primordial Seawater, and an Oceanid in the Fountain of Lucine speaks to the Traveler, asking after "Vacher". Navia, Silver and Melus fight illegal unserialised meka, and Clorinde helps. Navia's father Callas had banned Sinthe from his group; he cultivated Jacques, a seller, as informant, and Jacques was found shot at a banquet where Callas was the assumed killer. Callas, who Melus says had less than five years to live, chose to duel rather than stand trial and Clorinde judged that he wished to die. In court Navia accuses Marcel, who is exposed as Vacher; the notebook of his lover Vigneire shows where he took the name from, a child they never had. Marcel swallows Primordial Seawater to show it does not harm non-Fontainians, and is later "drowned" by the Oceanids, including Vigneire's merged consciousness, in the fountain's dream realm; doctors call it a panic attack. Neuvillette tells the Traveler he let Callas' duel proceed because he did not think anyone would die for justice, and Callas proved him wrong. He also says he had foreseen that the Oratrice might one day disagree with him.

Act III: further details: Neuvillette tells the Traveler that the Knave asked for custody of Childe and that he proposed a third party investigate on her behalf. The Traveler and Paimon are charged, in jest, with eating cake meant for Furina. Inside the fortress the currency is Credit Coupons; the "hidden rules" are rumours that turn out to be partly a cover to protect Childe, who had won the other prisoners' respect and left after hearing the "voice" again. Lyney, Lynette and Freminet got themselves jailed (through a staged theft) to investigate the same prison for Arlecchino.

Act IV: further details: The "strange meat" turns out to be Sigewinne's secret cooking for tired workers and a red drink is a Fonta recipe market-tested on prisoners. The gate's gauge had crept into the red over the past year. Wriothesley explains that the fortress began when Egeria let repentant exiles guard her secret beneath the waves, and says he has been building a huge ship inspired by Remurian legend. Arlecchino tells the Traveler she once went to Furina at night to take the Gnosis, sensed a curse on her, and found her terrified and without it; she doubts Neuvillette is the Archon either. Neuvillette says the First Usurper took part of the Seven Sovereigns' power to make the Archons' Authorities, and that without the Hydro Archon returning his Authority he can do little against the prophecy.

### ULTRA +
Act VI details: the missing man is close to Atossa, a villager of Vimara, who still hopes he will return one day. Dainsleif, investigating the Abyss Order and the Loom of Fate, tells the Traveler that the Sinner Vedrfolnir is his older brother and one of the five Sinners of Khaenri'ah with Rhinedottir, Surtalogi, Rerir and Hroptatyr. False memories show Dainsleif handing the first Field Tiller's eye to a stranger; this is a trap by the Abyss Order, and the Traveler's sibling confronts Dainsleif alone. Caribert explains that the Loom of Fate in its first form could create and implant memories, and in its fully unlocked form creates Ley Lines of its own and can "change" the world. He fades away as the sibling arrives, and the Traveler later finds a photograph of themselves, the sibling and Caribert.

## revelations
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
- Fontaine's prophecy (everyone is born with sin, the waters will rise, the Hydro Archon will be left weeping alone) is real, was set by the Heavenly Principles, and is tied to the Primordial Sea.
- Fontaine's people were once Oceanids, turned human by Egeria using Primordial Sea water poured into their blood vessels, which is why contact with Primordial Seawater dissolves them. This was Egeria's "sin".
- Furina and Focalors are two beings: Focalors, the real Archon, split off her divinity and left her human body and spirit as Furina, who was "cursed" to be unable to die while the divinity existed.
- Neuvillette is the Hydro Dragon Sovereign in human form, and his authority was held in the Hydro Archon's throne until Focalors returned it.
### LARGE +
- Skirk says the Hydro Gnosis is the remains of the third Descender, and the Traveler tells Neuvillette they are the fourth.
- Act VI: the Abyss Order's Loom of Fate was completed; the Traveler's sibling says they are working against the Heavenly Principles, which they say remain dormant even though the Hydro throne is destroyed.
- Paimon remarks that the Fontaine crisis is the first time she and the Traveler have allied with the Fatui (the Fatui also helped the rebuilding of Poisson).

## characters
### LARGE
Furina (and Focalors), Neuvillette, Navia with Silver and Melus, Lyney, Lynette and Freminet of the House of the Hearth, Arlecchino, Clorinde, Wriothesley, Sigewinne, Charlotte, Sedene, Childe (Tartaglia), Skirk, and Paimon. Mona and Mage "N" appear in Act V. In Act VI the cast is Dainsleif, Caribert, Atossa and the Traveler's sibling.

## consequences
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Furina steps down from the role of Hydro Archon, leaving the work, though not the title, to Neuvillette. Fontaine's people become immune to the Primordial Sea's dissolving effect. Neuvillette passes the Hydro Gnosis to Arlecchino, who is to deliver it to the Tsaritsa; Childe returns to Snezhnaya to recover. Neuvillette suggests the Traveler's next stop will be Natlan, where Il Capitano has entered the endless war.
### LARGE +
Furina's later life is recorded in her own entry. The Traveler's discovery that they are the fourth Descender is a thread that later chapters pick up.

## placement
### STANDARD
Follows Chapter III (Sumeru) and precedes Chapter V (Natlan). Act VI, a Traveler chapter, was released much later (4.7) but follows Act V in the chapter. The travel log shows Acts I-V as one block and Act VI as separate.

## entry: Act VI - Bedtime Story
@keys: Bedtime Story, Vimara Village commission, Loom of Fate
@version: 4.7
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Act VI of Chapter IV is the Traveler chapter "Bedtime Story", set in Sumeru's Vimara Village. A joint commission asks the Traveler to find a missing villager whom nobody can name. Abyss Order meddling has altered memories. Dainsleif tells them the Sinner "Visionary" Vedrfolnir is his elder brother. The missing man is Caribert, who explains that the Loom of Fate was completed; the Traveler's sibling meets the Traveler intangibly in his memory, says they are in a hurry to unlock the Loom before the Heavenly Principles awaken, and that the Traveler will not remember the conversation.
