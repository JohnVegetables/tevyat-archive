## summary
### LITE
The second act of Fontaine's Archon Quest. The Traveler and Paimon join Navia of Spina di Rosula in tracing the serial disappearances of young women, which prove to be people dissolved into Primordial Seawater and tied to the death of Navia's father Callas. The case ends in a trial that exposes the culprit, Marcel, after which the Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale returns a "Guilty" verdict against the Fatui Harbinger Tartaglia (Childe).
### STANDARD +
The act has three quests: Like the Faint Moonlight of Yesteryear, The Truth, Lost With the Rain, and When All Return to the Waters. It is a detective story set in the Court of Fontaine and Poisson, and it is where the Traveler learns that Primordial Seawater dissolves Fontainians and that an Oceanid can hold a dissolved girl's memories.
### LARGE +
Its last beats set up the rest of Chapter IV: a machine verdict against Tartaglia that Neuvillette does not accept as truth, and Furina's visible agitation at it.

## plot
### STANDARD
After the trial of Act I, the Traveler and Paimon dine at Hotel Debord with Navia, who distrusts the Garde and wants a fresh angle on the disappearances. That evening at the Fountain of Lucine the Traveler is drawn into a dream-like link with an Oceanid, a former girl who has lost most of her memory and asks for someone called Vacher. She asks that Vacher be told to forget her. Back in the real world the Traveler finds Navia, Silver and Melus fighting a swarm of unregistered clockwork meka, and Clorinde helps them. Navia works out that her Fonta at the hotel had been spiked with Primordial Seawater (it tasted salty, and Paimon drank it by mistake), and the Traveler and Paimon fully join her investigation.

Navia explains that three years earlier her father Callas, head of Spina di Rosula, was accused of killing his friend Jacques and chose to duel rather than stand trial, dying at Clorinde's hand. She believes the murder and the disappearances are linked. Vacher has no public records, and Neuvillette's case archives have nothing on him either. Rain on the day of the conviction and on the night of the murder suggests to the Traveler that the real killer may have dissolved into water. Melus reveals that Callas knew he was dying and had been fighting the Sinthe drink trade, which Navia now concludes was made with Primordial Seawater. Melus names three suspects (Florent, Thierry, Marcel). After talking to them and to Jacques' family, the Traveler concludes that Marcel, the only one wealthy enough to afford private meka, is the likely culprit.

When the authorities accuse Tartaglia of the disappearances, the Traveler infiltrates the underwater Sinthe production facility while Navia accuses Marcel in court. His arguments nearly succeed, until Navia names Vacher and he reacts. In the facility the Traveler finds proof that Vacher ran the experiments, plus a notebook of Vacher's lover Vigneire containing the name Marcel, chosen for a child they never had. Marcel is exposed as Vacher; he blames a system that laughed off his reports of people dissolving.
### LARGE +
Details of the investigation. The meka bore no serial numbers, and Clorinde infers a rich or powerful private owner, since Gardemeks can only be operated by the Maison Gardiennage. Callas had banned Sinthe from his group, angering the sellers, and had turned one of them, Jacques, into an informant. Clothes were found at the scene of Jacques' death, suggesting a third party. Jacques' wife believes his superiors noticed his betrayal and forced him to kill Callas against his will. Callas had left his gun inside the banquet hall when he stepped out for air, which rules out a second gun. Thierry says the meka would be nearly impossible to modify in secret. Melus warns Navia not to storm the enemy headquarters, as that would amount to war.

At the opera house Tartaglia, charged with the disappearances, is willing to accept it if he can duel Clorinde again. Navia asks Clorinde to testify, and Clorinde confirms that as a champion duelist she could tell Callas wanted to die. Navia explains that Callas would not defend himself because that would have made her and Spina di Rosula the next targets. Marcel counters that he moved to Fontaine after the disappearances began.
### ULTRA +
To show that Primordial Seawater harms only Fontainians, Marcel drinks a large quantity before the court, noting that this denies him a way to join Vigneire in death. Neuvillette declares Tartaglia innocent of the disappearances, reviews the Traveler's evidence and feeds it to the Oratrice, which returns "Guilty" for Marcel. Then, to clear Tartaglia formally of the original charge, he runs that too, and the machine returns "Guilty". Tartaglia tries to escape with his Electro Delusion and the Foul Legacy Transformation, but Neuvillette leaps from his seat and knocks him out with a Hydro power. He apologises and promises to investigate personally. Furina says the verdict is right, shows agitation and leaves.

Afterwards Marcel asks to visit the Fountain of Lucine. He is pulled into the dream realm and meets an Oceanid with Vigneire's voice that is really the merged consciousness of the women he dissolved; it drowns him, and doctors record a panic attack. A few days later Navia, having held a belated memorial for Callas and made amends with Clorinde, visits his grave with the Traveler. Neuvillette is there and explains that he let the duel go ahead because he believed no innocent human would die for justice. He adds that he had foreseen the Oratrice might one day disagree with him.

## revelations
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The disappearing girls were dissolved into Primordial Seawater, which turns Fontainians into water, and the Oceanids keep fragments of the people they were. Sinthe was made with Primordial Seawater. Marcel was really Vacher, who experimented on the victims, and Callas's duel was a deliberate sacrifice. Rain is repeatedly tied to the dissolving.
### LARGE +
The Oratrice's "Guilty" on Tartaglia is the first time it differed from Neuvillette's own judgment. Neuvillette says Furina took the Prophecy seriously.

## characters
### STANDARD
Navia leads the investigation, driven by grief for Callas. Melus, who had withheld some of Callas's secrets, and Silver fight alongside her. Clorinde, the Champion Duelist who killed Callas in the duel he chose and whom he asked to keep Navia safe, protects Navia. Neuvillette presides in court and holds to court rules despite doubts. Furina sits in the trial; Sedene lets the group into Neuvillette's office at Palais Mermonia. Marcel is the culprit. Tartaglia is accused and then found guilty by the machine.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Callas's honour is restored and Navia reconciles with Clorinde. Tartaglia is apprehended by the Gardemeks; later acts of Chapter IV follow what happens to him. Neuvillette promises to look into the verdict himself.

## placement
### STANDARD
Chapter IV Act II, added in version 4.0. It follows Prelude of Blancheur and Noirceur and precedes To the Stars Shining in the Depths. Story order equals release order.

## entry: Marcel and Vacher
@keys: Vacher, Vigneire, Marcel Vacher
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
In As Light Rain Falls Without Reason, Marcel, a wealthy man who moved to Fontaine after the disappearances began, is revealed to be Vacher, the man behind the dissolving of the girls. Marcel was the name his lover Vigneire picked for the child they never had. The Oceanid at the Fountain of Lucine that he finally meets is the merged consciousness of the women he dissolved together with Vigneire, and it drowns him.

## entry: Sinthe
@keys: Sinthe
@version: 4.0
### STANDARD
A recreational drink popular in Fontaine around the time of Callas's trial. Navia concludes it was made with Primordial Seawater, and long-term drinkers showed signs of drug dependence that hurt their families. Callas banned it from his group.

## entry: Callas's duel
@keys: Callas, Callas's duel
@version: 4.0
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Three years before the act, Callas, head of Spina di Rosula, was accused of killing his friend Jacques and chose a duel over a trial. He kept fighting after he was hurt enough to lose, and Clorinde killed him. She says he asked her to keep Navia safe and that she could tell he wanted to die. Neuvillette later admits he let the duel proceed because he thought an innocent man would not die for justice.
