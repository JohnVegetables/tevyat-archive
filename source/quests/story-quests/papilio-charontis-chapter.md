## summary
### LITE
The Papilio Charontis Chapter is Hu Tao's Story Quest (Version 1.3), set in Liyue. In its one act, Yet the Butterfly Flutters Away, the Traveler meets the 77th Director of Wangsheng Funeral Parlor and helps her intern Meng find out whether his childhood friend, who died young, has become a vengeful spirit. The answer is a gentle one, and the act ends with a funeral at the border between life and death.
### STANDARD +
The act has four quests: The Mysterious Wangsheng Parlor, In Pursuit of a Phantom, Friendships, Wishes, and Regrets, and Perfect Send-Off. It requires Adventure Rank 40 and the Archon Quest Farewell, Archaic Lord. The Chinese chapter name means roughly "Chapter of the Guiding Butterfly".
### LARGE +
The chapter is the standard introduction to how Hu Tao works: showy and irreverent on the surface, but careful, practical and compassionate about death. It also supplies the parlor's own account of its origins and of the "border" it works at. It is a prerequisite for Baizhu's Lagenaria Chapter, in which Hu Tao reappears.
### ULTRA +
Its tone mixes comedy (failed marketing, a staged exorcism, a Ley Line Monolith used as a monster lure) with a quiet ending about regret and the living friends left behind. The Traveler's own search for their sibling is mentioned only briefly, in the closing conversation.

## plot
### LITE
Travelling near Wuwang Hill, the Traveler and Paimon frighten a nervous young undertaker named Meng, who takes them for ghosts. His boss is Hu Tao, director of Wangsheng Funeral Parlor. Meng fears that a dead friend, nicknamed Big G, has turned into a vengeful spirit. Hu Tao stages an exorcism, Big G's gentle spirit turns up, and the group collects keepsakes from Big G's living friends and holds a farewell for him.
### STANDARD
Meng, a nervous new intern at Wangsheng Funeral Parlor, takes the Traveler and Paimon for ghosts on Wuwang Hill. His boss Hu Tao, the 77th Director, tries and fails to recruit them for a deal with the Adventurers' Guild. Meng fears his late childhood friend, nicknamed Big G, has become a vengeful spirit. Hu Tao stages a mock exorcism of a panicking man, Luocheng, at a Ley Line Monolith, and Big G's harmless spirit turns up, lost in the city. After the Traveler gathers keepsakes from Big G's living friends Little Wu, Mumu and Congzi, Hu Tao leads the group into the border between life and death for a farewell. She also promises to keep looking for the Traveler's sibling.
### LARGE
**The Mysterious Wangsheng Parlor.** Meng, a new intern, flees from the Traveler and Paimon on Wuwang Hill. His boss, a young woman, tells him the "ghosts" are future clients. Paimon recalls that Zhongli works at the parlor as a consultant, which surprises the girl; she introduces herself as Hu Tao, the 77th Director, and tries a sales pitch that fails. She and Meng had come to the hill to hold a funeral after reports of strange activity. Taking the pair for adventurers, she asks them to act as intermediaries in a business deal with the Adventurers' Guild branch in Liyue Harbor, but the manager, Lan, is put off by her cheerful talk of death. Back at the parlor she plans a new marketing push and then, reminded by Meng of "Big G", takes the Traveler to meet three of Meng's friends: Little Wu, Mumu and Congzi. She later admits she had abandoned the marketing plan once the Guild deal failed; the visit was to see whether Big G's spirit was lingering near them, and she sensed nothing.

**In Pursuit of a Phantom.** Hu Tao explains that Big G was Meng's childhood friend and died in an accident, and that all three of the friends they met knew him. Meng fears Big G has turned vengeful, though Hu Tao says she rarely meets such spirits. Another man, Luocheng, bursts in convinced he is cursed by a demon. Hu Tao stages a mock exorcism at a Ley Line Monolith while the Traveler fights off hilichurls drawn to it. She then tells Luocheng the monolith merely attracts monsters, that he was pranked, and that he probably had an ordinary illness or was scared sick; she advises him to believe less in superstition. Big G then appears as a small spirit, in the form he had when he died.

**Friendships, Wishes, and Regrets.** At the parlor, Big G explains that he only wanted to check on his old friends. He had followed Luocheng back from Wuwang Hill, which explained Luocheng's nightmares, and had become lost in the unfamiliar city because of a poor sense of direction and a fear of heights. Hu Tao says a proper funeral needs keepsakes from his friends, and sends the Traveler to collect them without revealing why: extra-spicy Jueyun Chili Chicken from Little Wu, a toy box from Mumu, and Plaustrite Shards from Congzi for a Xiao Lantern. The friends say all five grew up on Wuwang Hill, lost their homes to a disaster, and moved to Liyue Harbor to stay near one another.

**Perfect Send-Off.** Hu Tao takes the group into the "border" between life and death, a place where Ley Lines run wild, and warns them to follow her instructions or she will have to erase their memories. While Meng and Big G say goodbye, she tells the Traveler how the parlor began as something like a doctor's office, how the Archon War tipped the balance toward death, and how the balance was repaired over many years. Big G leaves peacefully, reassured that his friends are well. Meng reveals that the two had wished to build careers, raise families and spend a Lantern Rite together, and Hu Tao gives him unpaid leave. She tells the Traveler she guessed from their reaction that they had not found what they sought, that she had asked the dead for news and found nothing worth following, and that she will keep looking. She adds that she has never seen any previous director in the border, so she knows they all died without regret.

Details worth keeping. Hu Tao's explanation of why she keeps panicky clients "in the dark" is that someone who is truly afraid needs something believable to bind the fear to, such as a ritual, so a trick is sometimes kinder than the plain truth. Her account of the parlor's history says that Yaksha karma gathered during the fighting against vengeful gods formed an ancient plague, that hysterical people read it as a curse from the gods, and that the founders of the parlor were those who realised that purifying the air and burning the diseased dead would halt it. After many years the plague was eradicated and the parlor has watched over the balance of life and death since. In the border she notes that the dead there are those who still envy the living or cannot let go.

## revelations
### STANDARD
Minor lore: Wangsheng Funeral Parlor claims a founding role in ending an ancient plague born of accumulated Yaksha karma, and its directors tend the balance between life and death. The "border" between the living and the dead is a real, Ley-Line-disordered place that Hu Tao can enter with others. Spirits keep the form they had when they died.
### LARGE +
Hu Tao says that no earlier director has been seen in the border, which she takes to mean they died without regrets. The Traveler's search for their sibling is mentioned, and the dead there offered nothing useful.

## characters
### LITE
Hu Tao, the Traveler and Paimon. Meng, Big G, Luocheng, Little Wu, Mumu, Congzi, Lan and the Ferrylady appear. Zhongli is mentioned.
### STANDARD +
Hu Tao is playful and theatrical but conscientious, and she hides real care behind pranks. Meng is anxious, earnest and grieving. Big G is a gentle spirit with a poor sense of direction and a fear of heights. Zhongli is referred to as the parlor's consultant but does not appear in person.

## consequences
### STANDARD
The Traveler and Paimon gain a friendly acquaintance with Hu Tao, who promises to keep looking for the Traveler's sibling. The chapter is also a prerequisite for Baizhu's Lagenaria Chapter, along with the Alatus Chapter's Butterfly's Dream.

## placement
### STANDARD
Release order: 1.3. Story order: after Chapter I of the Archon Quests (Farewell, Archaic Lord). Hu Tao reappears in the Lagenaria Chapter (3.6).
