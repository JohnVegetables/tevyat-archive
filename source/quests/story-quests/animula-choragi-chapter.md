## summary
### LITE
Furina's Story Quest, set in Fontaine after the Archon Quest fallout of the Masquerade of the Guilty. The Traveler and Paimon take a Guild commission from a small amateur troupe whose director died and whose lead actress is ill. They coax Furina, who has sworn off acting, into helping. The troupe finishes the director's last play, The Little Oceanid, and stages it at the Opera Epiclese.
### STANDARD +
The chapter has one act, "The Little Oceanid", made of three quests: To Yesterday, Whither Should the Water Flow? and This Life, Just Like a Light Trickle of Song. It was released in version 4.2. It is a quiet, character-driven story about grief, guilt and what a person does with freedom after a long performance ends.
### LARGE +
The troupe's loss and Furina's own are mirrored: the director vanished in the disaster Furina could not stop, and Furina has to decide whether she can stand on a stage again. The act ends with her choosing to return to acting on her own terms, now holding a Vision.

## plot
### LITE
Lauwick, an amateur troupe member, asks the Guild for help: their director is dead and their lead actress Dulphy is ill. The Traveler suggests Furina, who refuses at first and then relents. The group finds the troupe's writer and lead actor, learns the director's story, and stages her unfinished play at the Opera Epiclese, with Furina directing, stepping in on stage, and receiving a Vision.
### STANDARD +
To Yesterday: at the Fontaine Adventurers' Guild, Katheryne points the Traveler and Paimon to Lauwick, who needs a replacement for his ill lead actress for the troupe's last show. The Traveler suggests Furina, now living in a modest home and mostly cooking macaroni. She declines, saying she has no interest in acting after playing Focalors for so long, but changes her mind and apologises to Paimon. She learns the troupe's director, Aurelie, was a victim of Marcel's plots. At their quarters the group meets more members and learns the troupe cannot finish Aurelie's script, The Little Oceanid, without its writer Pauleau and its lead actor Vilmant.

Whither Should the Water Flow?: the group finds Pauleau in Poisson, where the disaster struck. Navia arrives and tells Furina that the town is coping, though some still resent her. Furina admits she is still haunted by the rising water and by people dissolving, and does not know how to enjoy her new freedom. Pauleau finishes his ending, and the group goes in search of Vilmant.

This Life, Just Like a Light Trickle of Song: after a slime fight, Vilmant reveals why Aurelie sent the troupe out of the city: a merchant had offered sponsorship in return for selling drinks that proved to be Sinthe, and Aurelie refused it. Pauleau and Vilmant argue, and Furina points out they are using the play to vent. The Traveler collects Aurelie's diary from the Palais Mermonia with Sedene's help. Furina asks Neuvillette for the Opera Epiclese, and the performance goes ahead.
### LARGE +
To Yesterday: Katheryne has Furina's new address. Dulphy, who insists she is able to perform, is seen arguing with Lauwick. Furina overhears and is dragged in. The troupe members Elain and Buraud are surprised to see her. Lauwick explains Vilmant took Aurelie's death hard and Pauleau is busy writing the ending.

Whither Should the Water Flow?: Navia says some in Poisson are still angry at Furina for failing during the prophecy; Furina had tried to claim the visitor in her place was an actor dressed as her. Furina takes a walk, then rests aboard Spina di Rosula's ship. Pauleau reveals Dulphy was abandoned by her parents and Lauwick was fostered, and that he loved Aurelie, who treated everyone as family and rejected him. Dulphy wants an ending that mirrors the director's life, but Pauleau argues it would be meaningless.

This Life, Just Like a Light Trickle of Song: Furina cannot fight without a Vision, so the Traveler faces the slimes alone. Vilmant explains he accepted the merchant's sponsorship without telling anyone, informed Aurelie after learning the drink was Sinthe, and kept quiet about it afterwards, which enrages Pauleau. Furina realises Aurelie wanted to build a shelter for them from the outside world and says the ending should be devoted to her. The diary shows what Aurelie met in her last moments and that she cared for them. Dulphy suggests billing the play under Aurelie's name instead of her own. Furina insists on the larger stage, and Neuvillette, glad to see Furina recovering, agrees; he asks if she would perform again, and she declines.
### ULTRA +
The performance: after rehearsals under Furina's direction with help from the Traveler and Paimon, Dulphy's illness flares before the finale. Furina takes her role for the final scene, which she finds ironic. Dulphy asks beforehand what makes the heroine Clio shine; Furina answers that it is her pure heart and her unbroken belief in the beauty of the world. At the curtain call Pauleau credits Furina by name as consultant, though she had asked not to be, and the troupe had known the plan. Backstage he tells her everyone's work is acknowledged; she says they should have asked her first. Paimon was meant to lower a prop Vision from the ceiling for the scene, but a real one appeared for Furina, who wears it in place of the gem at her waist. They check on Dulphy, who is delighted that it succeeded. Furina, still annoyed at the thanks, says she will return to the stage with new perceptions.

## revelations
### LITE
Furina receives a Vision at the end of the play.
### STANDARD +
Furina is no longer the actress who played Focalors; she is unsure how to live free of the role but ends the act willing to act again. The director Aurelie, a victim of Marcel's plots, had tried to protect the troupe from a sponsor whose drink turned out to be Sinthe.
### LARGE +
The chapter confirms the Poisson disaster remains a wound for survivors and that Furina is still partly resented there. Neuvillette tells Furina he is glad she is recovering and does not press her to return to a leading role.

## characters
### LITE
Furina, the Traveler, Paimon and the troupe led by Lauwick.
### STANDARD +
Katheryne, Navia, Sedene and Neuvillette appear in supporting roles. Troupe members: Lauwick, Dulphy, Elain, Buraud, Vilmant, Pauleau and the late director Aurelie (prose only).
### LARGE +
The merchant who offered the sponsorship is described but not shown. Marcel is referenced as the author of the plots that Aurelie fell victim to; how she died is not shown.

## consequences
### STANDARD
Furina decides to return to acting at her own pace. She now has a Vision, which she keeps on her waist. The troupe finishes the director's play and honours her.
### LARGE +
Furina's guilt and fear of the disaster are not resolved by the chapter, only softened by finishing something for others.
