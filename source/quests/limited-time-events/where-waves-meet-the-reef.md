## summary
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### LITE
Where Waves Meet the Reef was a limited-time Version 6.5 flagship event (27 April to 14 May 2026) set in Nasha Town, Nod-Krai. Nefer of the Curatorium of Secrets commissions the Traveler and Paimon to help the Voynich Guild's manager Jahoda run the Nod-Krai Trade Expo, a yearly market where almost anything may be sold. Under the trade, Yelan of Liyue and Chevreuse of Fontaine are using the expo as bait for an international arms-dealing ring, and a guild member named Ashvapati and the three orphaned children in his care turn out to be tied to it.
### STANDARD
The expo's one firm rule is that there must be no violence. The Traveler registers three headline exhibitors (Beidou, Navia with Clorinde, and Aino with Ineffa), then brokers a deal in which Yelan and Chevreuse may run their sting provided they do nothing that wrecks the expo and the Traveler takes part in every operation. The sting catches a ring of agents, but it also exposes Ashvapati, a Sumeru-born former Akademiya researcher who, by his own account, once designed weapons for a desert Eremite band that were turned against that band. He had lured the investigators to Nod-Krai to reach the man he blames, Sennedjem.
### LARGE +
The story ends without bloodshed and with a public trial for the arms dealers. A cutscene shows the Alcor-class No. 2 shooting down Ashvapati's rockets as a firework display, and Dori sells the children a gemstone said to grow an oasis. Players who earned enough Joint Exhibition Popularity could invite Anemo Jahoda ("Windthreading Shadow") to their party. The event also included three minigames and a large set of optional open-world "Tales of Many" character interactions.

## plot
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
Part I: Nefer hires the Traveler and Paimon to help Jahoda coordinate the Nod-Krai Trade Expo, whose one rule is no violence. They bring in Beidou (selling an upgraded Guizhong Ballista), Navia with Clorinde (a Fontaine pneuma and ousia relay device) and Aino with Ineffa (blueprints for the Thwack 'n Smack Defense Bots). Yelan and Chevreuse reveal that their goods are bait for the same international arms ring; Nefer lets them proceed so long as the expo's protected deals are not disturbed and the Traveler joins every operation.
Part II: Yelan, Chevreuse and their allies trap the ring's buyer aboard the Flagship while the other agents are rounded up. The Traveler subdues the buyer and Chevreuse corrals a runaway agent.
Part III: Beidou's prototype ballistas vanish. The trail leads to Ashvapati, the guild member guarding three orphaned children, whose letter confesses a past as an Akademiya exile who designed weapons for a desert Eremite band that were turned against it.
Epilogue: Ashvapati lures Sennedjem, the man he blames, to a meeting and plans to die with him under a salvo of rockets. Beidou's ship turns the rockets into fireworks, Sennedjem is arrested, Ashvapati is spared charges but expelled from the guild, and Dori sells the children a gemstone that grows an oasis.
### LARGE +
Full account by part.
Part I, A Commission From the Curatorium of Secrets: A letter from Nefer brings the Traveler and Paimon to the Curatorium of Secrets in Nasha Town. She explains that Nasha Town grew out of a lawless black market and that the annual Nod-Krai Trade Expo is meant to be the largest "legal" black market in Teyvat. Jahoda, organising for the Voynich Guild, is overwhelmed, so the Traveler is hired for participant coordination. Nefer's only demand is that no violence happen at the expo. Three guests must be fetched: Beidou, captain of the Crux fleet, selling an upgraded Guizhong Ballista from her second ship, the Alcor-class No. 2 (Yelan has hitched a ride on that ship); Navia of the Spina di Rosula, selling a Fontaine Research Institute relay device that releases pneuma and ousia energy, with Clorinde as bodyguard and Chevreuse also in town; and Aino with Ineffa, selling blueprints for the Thwack 'n Smack Defense Bots. At the venue a bully pushes the children Ati, Neb and Raya aside over their booth, Jahoda faces him down, and the children's guardian Ashvapati, a guild member, turns up. After the expo opens, Yelan and Chevreuse separately question Aino and then reveal to the Traveler that they are both hunting the same arms ring, and that their goods are bait. The Traveler takes them to Nefer, who suspected as much. Yelan reports that bandits on Liyue's border have recently got a mysterious weapon, and Chevreuse that gangs in Fontaine are being armed. Nefer agrees to let them work as long as anything illegal happens outside the expo's protected deals and the Traveler is part of every step.
Part II, Encirclement of Bullets and Silk: Two days later a ring agent approaches Beidou and Navia's stalls and, after haggling, proposes a private deal away from the guild. Yelan, Chevreuse, Beidou, Navia and Clorinde plan a coordinated takedown: Yelan, with the Traveler as hidden backup, meets the buyer aboard the Flagship and stalls him while Chevreuse and Navia's local Spina members round up the other agents. The Traveler subdues the buyer; Chevreuse shoots obstacles to corral a fleeing agent and traps him by breaking a guild signboard; all twenty agents are accounted for. Tarno then summons the Traveler back to the venue.
Part III, Toys for the Children: Jahoda reports that Beidou's prototype ballistas have been stolen from storage. The children say they cannot reach Ashvapati and that he had steered them to Beidou's booth. At the Curatorium, Nefer recalls a band of Eremites who gained advanced weapons about a decade ago, sold them to the highest bidder, and were destroyed by their own creations; the children come from such a band. After Yelan locates Ashvapati's hideout, the group finds a cargo manifest of the same kind that had set both Liyue and Fontaine on the trail, a display ballista, and a letter addressed to them. In the letter Ashvapati confesses. He says he was a Kshahrewar researcher expelled from the Akademiya for taking forbidden texts, was saved from dying in the desert by an Eremite chief, Khaemwaset, and made toys for the oasis children. A newcomer named Sennedjem persuaded him to build a lure that turns Ruin Drakes into weapons, then sold the designs to the oasis's enemies. When the camp burned Ashvapati saved only three children and has raised them since while hunting Sennedjem.
Epilogue, Return Home, Children of the Desert: Ashvapati poses as Sennedjem's contact, using the old meeting signal, to lure him to the rendezvous while the authorities hold his agents, and raises a barrier around them intending to die with him under a salvo of rockets. Yelan argues that weapons made for peace-keeping can later become toys, as fireworks did, and that people are the ones who do evil. Beidou's ship fires on the rockets and turns them into fireworks. Sennedjem and his men are taken into Maison Gardiennage custody. Chevreuse declines to charge Ashvapati in exchange for Nefer dropping any claim over the broken signboard; Nefer ejects Ashvapati from the Voynich Guild for the rules he broke but urges him to take the children back to Sumeru. Dori sells them a gemstone made by the mage Alice that grows an oasis, priced for "whatever keeps the kids happy", which she sets as what the children earned at the expo.
Details of Part I: Jahoda is delighted to learn that the Traveler will cover participant coordination, and promises Nod-Krai Hot Dogs as thanks. She warns that Beidou and Navia must not be offended. Beidou says her ballista can be guided by remote control from far away and jokes that it is antique compared to what Liyue's Millelith use. Navia, represented as the Spina's president, jokes about being called top dog of the criminal underworld, and Clorinde remarks that Nod-Krai is chaotic. Chevreuse, on official business for the Special Security and Surveillance Patrol, notes that she must bend rules in a lawless place. Ineffa explains the craftshop's dessert budget is separate from research funds because overspending on desserts had caused research shortages in at least 47 cases. When Yelan and Chevreuse realise they are using the expo as bait for the same target, Chevreuse says she has looked into the Curatorium head and does not want her to know their plans, while Yelan guesses that Nefer arranged the Traveler's role precisely so they would come to her.
Details of Part II: Beidou fields the children's curious questions about her ballista, promises them a look at it in action after the expo, and Ashvapati remarks that he is envious that Liyue children can treat a warship as a playground. The ring agent suggests moving the deal somewhere the Voynich Guild cannot take its cut; Beidou, who is registered with the expo, sends a proxy. Yelan deduces from the buyer's wish for disassembly and discreet crates that the ring resells at ports such as Yilong Wharf and Lumidouce Harbor. Clorinde reveals that Navia has quietly won over Nod-Krai gangs who owed the Spina favours, and Navia laughs at being called boss. The Maison Gardiennage's reputation for firm interrogation is joked about by both investigators.
Details of Part III: Navia tells the children their uncle cannot be judged by what he made but only after asking why, a process Fontaine's courts are built for, and promises they will have a chance to ask him. Chevreuse and Yelan agree that the cargo manifest, the one slip in an otherwise untraceable operation, being in Ashvapati's hideout shows he laid the trail himself, and that he was acting alone. His letter praises them for removing more than half the gang within a day. In the flashback Khaemwaset refuses Ashvapati's offer to upgrade weapons because his scimitar is enough and the oasis is safely remote; he suggests toys for the children instead. Sennedjem's group had earlier abandoned the oasis after a sandstorm and returned when no band would take them. When Ashvapati left the arms deal after learning how they were being used, Sennedjem's men sold to the oasis's old enemies, in his words as an excuse to raise the price.
### ULTRA +
Further details: Jahoda announces over the loudspeaker that registration is complete and the expo is open. When the Traveler brings Yelan and Chevreuse to Nefer, she greets them by origin (Miss Yelan of Liyue and Miss Chevreuse of Fontaine) and asks only that she hear the real story afterwards. The sting stops short of the leader because the ring's head is not at the expo; the epilogue shows the person collecting the goods to be Sennedjem, who in his own words lives in Fontaine's Vasari Passage as a respected benefactor. The children's own expo earnings are counted as payment for the gemstone, a detail Dori calls a way for them to say they bought back their home through their own work. In the finale cutscene the bully who harassed the children returns, is startled by one of Aino's bots and sent off by Jahoda, Aino and Ineffa. The wiki lists a trivia note that Liben is shown with a short beard in the firework cutscene although he has only a moustache.

## revelations
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
The mastermind of the arms ring is Sennedjem, a former Eremite who sold weapons to the very band that had sheltered him, and who now poses as a Fontaine philanthropist. Ashvapati, the children's guardian and a Voynich Guild member, is a former Kshahrewar researcher who designed those weapons and tracked Sennedjem for years; he lied to the guild about almost everything but his desert origins. He planned to die with Sennedjem but is talked down. Nefer's home is the desert too, she says, and she hopes his story shows another way of life there; she names Lesser Lord Kusanali as a reason the Sumeru of today is changing.
### LARGE +
Yelan's remarks that fireworks began as weapons against evil forces and wild beasts frame the event's theme: weapons and toys can be the same technology in different times, and the real question is who uses them and why. Chevreuse's verdict is that Ashvapati's crime lay not in researching weapons but in failing to use his skill to protect those he loved.

## characters
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
Nefer, who runs the Curatorium of Secrets, hires the Traveler and Paimon. Jahoda is the harried Voynich Guild manager and the event's reward character. Beidou, Navia, Clorinde, Chevreuse and Yelan carry the investigation, and Aino and Ineffa supply the defense bots. Ashvapati, Ati, Neb and Raya form the central family, and Dori is the Sumeru merchant who helps resolve it. Sennedjem is the antagonist, and Khaemwaset, Ashvapati's rescuer, appears only in flashback. Tarno is a messenger, and the ring's agents are nameless.
### LARGE +
Ashru appears with Nefer at the Curatorium. Other exhibitors on the roster include Liben, Lakkaberry, Wislawa, Nkrumah, Fassbender, Gongmiao, Huoxiang, Fengyun, Seahawk, Stone Stool and Zhiyao. The optional Tales of Many bring in Chasca, Citlali, Flins, Illuga, Alice, Nicole, Diluc and Varka.

## consequences
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
The ring's agents and Sennedjem are taken into custody by Fontaine's Special Security and Surveillance Patrol (Maison Gardiennage) to face a public trial; Yelan considers her objective met. Ashvapati is not charged by the Fontaine authorities and is expelled from the Voynich Guild by Nefer, who urges him to take the children back to the desert. He accepts the oasis gemstone for the children. The expo ends as an unusually memorable one in Nefer's judgement.

## placement
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### STANDARD
A Version 6.5 (Luna VI) flagship event, running 27 April to 14 May 2026 in three phases (27 April, 29 April and 1 May). Players needed Adventure Rank 20 and the Prologue Act III quest Song of the Dragon and Freedom; the Archon Quest True Moon was recommended first. The story is set in Nod-Krai and mentions Sumeru under Lesser Lord Kusanali. The Part I side quests are concurrent, and Parts II and III each begin two in-game days after the previous part.

## gameplay
@availability: limited
@version: 6.5
### LARGE
Game mechanics, not in-world fact. The event had three minigames, each unlocked by one of the Part I side quests: Thwack 'n Smack Defense Bots (a defense game using Aino's bots, tied to the Aino and Ineffa side quest), Pneumousia Barrage Countermeasure (an experimental shield that absorbs same-alignment projectiles, tied to Navia), and Fire Support Assault (guiding a Guizhong Ballista, tied to Beidou). Completing challenges raised Joint Exhibition Popularity, which could be spent toward an invitation to Jahoda; rewards included up to 970 Primogems and a Crown of Insight. Optional Tales of Many interactions unlocked as the story progressed.

## entry: Nod-Krai Trade Expo
@keys: Nod-Krai Trade Expo, Trade Expo
@keys2: Nefer, Jahoda, Voynich Guild, Nasha Town
@version: 6.5
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
An annual market held on the shoreline southeast of Nasha Town once shipping routes reopen, organised by the Voynich Guild and billed as the biggest "legal" black market in Teyvat. The town began as an unregulated black market, and the expo preserves that spirit of unrestricted trade. The guild takes a cut from seller and buyer and promises safety to all, so violence is forbidden, but deals struck outside the expo are not protected. This 6.5 edition was unusually large.
### LARGE +
Exhibitors included weapons makers (Beidou's ballista, the Spina's pneuma and ousia relay), toys and blueprints from the Krumkake Craftshop, a Natlan seller offering "Wayob fragments", a Fatui sergeant selling scrap muskets, a Mondstadt wine vendor, a curio dealer, and Dori with goods from the Hexenzirkel.

## entry: Ashvapati and Sennedjem
@keys: Ashvapati, Sennedjem, Khaemwaset
@keys2: Eremites, oasis, Ati, Neb, Raya, Nod-Krai Trade Expo
@version: 6.5
@availability: limited
@spoiler: major
### STANDARD
Ashvapati is a Voynich Guild member and guardian of Ati, Neb and Raya. By his own letter he is an expelled Kshahrewar scholar who was taken in by the Eremite chief Khaemwaset and built toys for children until Sennedjem, a trader who had left the oasis and then returned, convinced him to design Ruin Drake lures. Sennedjem sold the designs to enemies of the oasis, the camp was burned, and Ashvapati saved only three children. Sennedjem later became a rich arms supplier and, by his own claim, a Fontaine philanthropist. All of this is told by Ashvapati and Sennedjem themselves.

## entry: Ati, Neb and Raya
@keys: Ati, Neb, Raya
@keys2: Ashvapati, Jahoda, Nod-Krai Trade Expo
@version: 6.5
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
Three orphaned children, raised by Ashvapati, who run a toy booth at the expo. Neb loves building mechanisms and wants an Aino bot blueprint; Ati prefers practical purchases such as clothing; Raya hopes everyone can stop arguing. Jahoda promises to look after them. They are not involved in the arms ring, and in the end they ask Ashvapati to remain their uncle even after learning the whole truth.

## entry: Yelan and Chevreuse joint operation
@keys: joint operation, arms ring, ring of agents
@keys2: Yelan, Chevreuse, Nod-Krai
@version: 6.5
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
Yelan of Liyue's Ministry of Civil Affairs and Chevreuse of Fontaine's Special Security and Surveillance Patrol come to Nod-Krai after the same international arms-trading network, each carrying a trap product (Beidou's ballista as the bait). After Nefer's mediated deal they pool intelligence: the Traveler must be included in every move, and the expo's own rules must not be broken. They capture twenty agents and the buyer without casualties, then pursue Ashvapati as the best lead. Afterwards each returns to their duties; Chevreuse praises working together.

## entry: Dori's oasis gemstone
@keys: oasis gemstone, magical gemstone, Alice's gemstone
@keys2: Dori, Ashvapati, Nicole, Alice
@version: 6.5
@availability: limited
### STANDARD
A gemstone made by the mage Alice which, planted in the ground, grows a spring and then an oasis even in a desert. At the expo Dori sells it for the price Alice set, "whatever keeps the kids happy", which she fixes as what Ati, Neb and Raya earned. In the Tales of Many, Alice and Nicole test a pricing scheme paid in virtues like courage and kindness rather than Mora, which sold little.
